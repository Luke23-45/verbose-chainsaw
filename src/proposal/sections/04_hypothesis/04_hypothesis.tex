\section{Hypothesis}

The hypotheses are formulated within the accounting value-relevance framework.
H1 and H2 concern the baseline associations of EPS and BVPS with market price.
Under the residual-income valuation framework of \citet{ohlson1995}, fundamental
accounting variables are theoretically expected to exhibit positive associations
with share prices, but are evaluated empirically using two-tailed tests at the
5\% significance level without imposing directional constraints. H3 and H4
evaluate whether those associations differ between the pre- and
post-consolidation periods. Although international merger research suggests a
potential shift from earnings relevance toward book-value relevance
\citep{kwon2020}, that specific direction is not presumed a priori for Nepal.

\noindent H1: EPS is significantly associated with market price in the baseline
value-relevance model ($\beta_E \ne 0$).

\noindent H2: BVPS is significantly associated with market price in the baseline
value-relevance model ($\beta_B \ne 0$).

\noindent H3: The EPS--price association differs between the pre- and
post-consolidation periods ($\delta_E \ne 0$).

\noindent H4: The BVPS--price association differs between the pre- and
post-consolidation periods ($\delta_B \ne 0$).

\noindent The principal test evaluates the joint null hypothesis
$H_0: \delta_E = \delta_B = 0$, which tests whether the estimated
accounting-price associations differ across the two periods. These coefficients
are interpreted as differences in association across periods, not as causal
effects of mergers.
