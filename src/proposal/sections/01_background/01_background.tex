\section{Background of the study}

The stock market plays a vital role in economic development by promoting
capital formation and fostering sustainable economic growth. The trading of
securities facilitates capital allocation between savers and investors through
fund pooling, risk sharing, and wealth transfer \citep{mosley2008}.

In Nepal, the organized stock market began with the conversion of the Securities
Exchange Centre into Nepal Stock Exchange (NEPSE) Limited in 1993, with floor
trading commencing in 1994. NEPSE remains the sole stock exchange in the country
and has grown substantially in both market capitalization and the number of listed
securities \citep{nrb2023}.

Returns from equity investments vary with firm performance and stock price
movements driven by technical, fundamental, and market factors
\citep{bhattarai2014}. Firm-specific accounting fundamentals, principally
earnings per share (EPS), dividend per share (DPS), book value per share
(BVPS), and return on assets (ROA), have been widely examined in relation
to equity valuation both across emerging markets \citep{sharma2011} and
within the Nepalese commercial banking sector \citep{bhattarai2014,pradhan2016}.

The Nepalese banking sector has undergone substantial structural transformation
through the merger and acquisition wave of 2013--2020 and the major consolidation
of early 2023: Global IME Bank absorbed Bank of Kathmandu, Kumari Bank absorbed
Nepal Credit and Commerce Bank, Nepal Investment Bank merged with Mega Bank to form
Nepal Investment Mega Bank, and Himalayan Bank acquired Civil Bank
\citep{adhikari2023}. Across the broader financial sector, 239 banking and
financial institutions entered merger processes and 177 ceased to exist in their
own names, with the commercial-bank count falling from 32 in 2014 to 20 by
mid-2023. Sector-wide market price per share fell by roughly 46\% in the
periods following the mergers while earnings per share and dividend per share were
statistically unchanged \citep{adhikari2023,adhikari2023cbr}. Most existing
empirical studies on share prices rely on pre-merger datasets that do not reflect
the restructured banking environment. Furthermore, conflicting findings on ROA
and BVPS across recent studies underscore the need to re-examine the associations
between accounting fundamentals and share prices across a study window spanning
the periods before, during, and after the major 2023 consolidation.
