\section{Background of the study}

Equity valuation relates share prices to investors' expectations about future
cash flows and the information available about a firm. Accounting earnings,
book values, and dividends are therefore commonly examined as inputs to
valuation models \citep{miller1961,ohlson1995}.

In Nepal, the organized stock market developed from the Securities Exchange
Centre into Nepal Stock Exchange (NEPSE) Limited. NEPSE's own retrospective
records that trading began on 13 January 1994 and describes NEPSE as the
country's single stock market \citep{nepse2017}.

Returns from equity investments vary with firm performance and stock price
movements driven by technical, fundamental, and market factors
\citep{bhattarai2014}. Firm-specific accounting fundamentals, principally
earnings per share (EPS), dividend per share (DPS), book value per share
(BVPS), and return on assets (ROA), have been widely examined in relation
to equity valuation both across emerging markets \citep{sharma2011} and
within the Nepalese commercial banking sector \citep{bhattarai2014,pradhan2016}.

Nepal's commercial banking sector has been reshaped by mergers and acquisitions,
including several mergers that began joint operation in early 2023. Nepal Rastra
Bank's licensed-institution list records the post-merger operation dates and
lists 20 commercial banks as of mid-October 2023 \citep{nrb2023list}. A study of
seven commercial banks involved in mergers between 2013 and 2020 compared three
years before and after merger. In that study sample, mean market price per share
fell by 46.01\%; the result is evidence about those sampled earlier mergers, not
an estimate of the effect of the early-2023 mergers \citep{adhikari2023}. Recent
Nepalese studies also report mixed results for ROA and BVPS, including studies
whose observation periods extend through FY 2023/24 \citep{chhetri2023,
gyawali2022,joshi2023,pant2024,chaudhari2025,timalsina2026}. This proposal
therefore examines associations between accounting fundamentals and
post-disclosure prices over FY 2076/77 to FY 2081/82. It does not estimate the
causal effect of the mergers or test whether coefficients changed across
pre-merger and post-merger periods.
