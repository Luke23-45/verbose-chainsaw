\section{Brief review of the literature}

\subsection{Theoretical review}

Equity valuation relates share prices to expected future economic benefits and the information available to market participants. The residual-income valuation (RIV) framework developed in accounting valuation research by \citet{ohlson1995} and \citet{feltham1995} provides the fundamental theoretical foundation linking accounting numbers to equity market values. Under the clean-surplus relation ($BV_{t} = BV_{t-1} + NI_{t} - Div_{t}$), assuming suitable regularity and terminal conditions, the market value of common equity can be represented as the sum of current book value and the discounted present value of expected future residual income (abnormal earnings):
\begin{equation}
P_{t} = BV_{t} + \sum_{\tau=1}^{\infty} \frac{E_{t}[\mathit{NI}_{t+\tau} - r \cdot BV_{t+\tau-1}]}{(1+r)^{\tau}}
\label{eq:ohlson}
\end{equation}
where $BV_t$ is book equity at period $t$, $NI_{t+\tau}$ is net income in period $t+\tau$, and $r$ is the cost of equity capital. Under linear information dynamics, \citet{ohlson1995} demonstrates that share price can be expressed as a linear function of contemporaneous earnings per share (EPS) and book value per share (BVPS), establishing both accounting measures as core fundamental drivers of equity market value. BVPS represents the accumulated historical net worth per share, while EPS reflects current operating performance and future abnormal earning power.

A central theoretical question in financial accounting is whether the market's relative valuation of earnings and book value remains stable across major corporate reorganizations and structural transitions. Investigating the valuation effects of corporate reorganizations, \citet{kwon2020} examine changes in the value relevance of accounting information following mergers and acquisitions (M\&A). They show that structural corporate combinations alter the information environment: post-merger integration costs, accounting harmonization, and non-recurring consolidation adjustments can temporarily impair the persistence of reported earnings, leading investors to place greater valuation weight on balance-sheet net worth (book value) relative to current earnings. Similarly, \citet{collins1997} demonstrate that the relative value relevance of earnings and book values shifts dynamically across periods in response to changes in earnings quality and firm restructuring. These theoretical considerations motivate examining whether the associations between equity prices and accounting fundamentals in Nepal's commercial banking sector differed before and after the recent commercial banking consolidation wave.

\begin{sloppypar}
Classical valuation frameworks provide analytical benchmarks for understanding equity valuation and payout decisions. \citet{miller1961} establish the dividend-irrelevance theorem under frictionless capital markets and fixed investment policy, serving as a theoretical baseline rather than an empirical description of emerging markets. Dividend-capitalization models relax these assumptions: \citet{walter1963} emphasizes the internal rate of return relative to the cost of equity capital, while \citet{gordon1959} models equity value as a function of current dividends and perpetual growth. In markets characterized by information asymmetry between corporate insiders and external investors, signaling theory suggests that financial decisions and accounting disclosures convey credible private information when they are costly to imitate \citep{ross1977,bhattacharya1979,john1985}. Agency theory suggests that cash distributions mitigate free-cash-flow agency problems by limiting managerial discretion \citep{jensen1986}. In banking institutions, loan-portfolio opacity and regulatory capital constraints make the credibility and timing of financial reporting particularly salient for valuation \citep{morgan2002}.
\end{sloppypar}

The semi-strong form of the Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH) posits that asset prices reflect all publicly available information \citep{fama1970}. Seminal accounting studies by \citet{ball1968} and \citet{beaver1968} established that annual accounting disclosures possess substantial information content reflected in stock returns. Subsequent empirical research, notably on post-earnings-announcement drift \citep{bernard1989}, showed that market prices may adjust with a lag to published financial results. This underscores the critical importance of observation timing. In Nepal, using fiscal-year-end (Ashadh-end) closing prices introduces look-ahead bias because audited annual reports are prepared and published months after the fiscal year ends. Conversely, identifying individual, unverified announcement dates for each bank-year is prone to noise and measurement error in the absence of a centralized disclosure timestamp database. To address these timing concerns, this study relies on the statutory disclosure deadline established under Section 59(2) of the Banks and Financial Institutions Act (BAFIA), 2017, which requires licensed banks to publish audited annual financial statements within nine months of fiscal year-end, which this study operationalizes as the end of Chaitra (mid-April) \citep{bafia2017}. Observing market prices at a standardized window following this statutory deadline ensures temporal alignment without relying on fragmented disclosure dates.

Finally, the econometric specification requires careful treatment of price-derived ratios. Several empirical studies include the price-earnings (P/E) ratio alongside accounting fundamentals in regressions where market price per share (MPS) is the dependent variable. Because $\text{P/E} = \text{MPS}/\text{EPS}$, including P/E introduces a regressor that is algebraically constructed from the dependent variable itself, generating mechanical endogeneity, definitional overlap, and severe interpretative distortion. To preserve econometric integrity, this study excludes P/E and focuses exclusively on accounting fundamentals that are measured independently of current market prices.

\subsection{Empirical review}

International empirical literature provides extensive evidence that accounting fundamentals are associated with equity valuation, though the relative explanatory power of individual measures varies across institutional contexts. \citet{collins1997} investigated US firms over a forty-year period and found that while the combined value relevance of earnings and book values remained high, their relative weights shifted systematically. \citet{kwon2020} demonstrated that the value relevance of accounting numbers changes significantly after mergers and acquisitions, with book value relevance increasing relative to earnings relevance due to transient earnings noise during post-merger integration. In emerging capital markets, \citet{sharma2011} found that EPS, BVPS, and dividend per share (DPS) exhibited positive associations with equity prices in India. \citet{malhotra2013} observed positive associations for BVPS and EPS in the Indian NSE 100 index, while \citet{almumani2014} documented positive associations for EPS and BVPS among banks listed on the Amman Stock Exchange.

In the Nepalese capital market, empirical investigation into the relationship between accounting fundamentals and equity share prices has expanded alongside the development of the Nepal Stock Exchange (NEPSE). \citet{pradhan2003} examined listed Nepalese enterprises and found that cash dividends exhibited a stronger association with share prices than retained earnings. \citet{bhattarai2014} evaluated listed commercial banks over 2006--2014 using pooled cross-sectional regression, reporting positive associations for EPS and P/E, alongside an inverse dividend-yield relationship. \citet{pradhan2016} analyzed 14 commercial banks over 2002/03--2013/14, identifying bank size and EPS as major determinants of market valuation. \citet{bajracharya2019} evaluated ten commercial banks over 2006/07--2015/16, documenting positive associations for EPS and DPS, while ROA was statistically insignificant. \citet{silwal2019} examined ten commercial banks over 2065/66--2074/75 and reported that BVPS had the largest explanatory weight, followed by ROE and P/E.

Recent empirical studies on Nepalese commercial banks document notable divergence in findings, particularly regarding the statistical significance and directional effects of book value, profitability, and dividend measures. \citet{niroula2021} analyzed 18 commercial banks over 2015/16--2019/20, finding that EPS was significantly positive, whereas BVPS and ROE were negative and statistically insignificant. \citet{gyawali2022} evaluated ten commercial banks over 2017--2021 and observed positive associations for EPS and DPS, but an insignificant coefficient for ROA. In contrast, \citet{chhetri2023} examined 13 commercial banks over 2012--2022 using pooled OLS, finding that BVPS, ROA, and P/E were significantly positive, whereas EPS was statistically insignificant. \citet{joshi2023} studied seven commercial banks over 2012/13--2021/22 and reported positive, statistically significant coefficients for EPS, DPS, BVPS, and P/E.

Studies published in 2024 further highlight empirical divergence across specifications and measurement periods. \citet{lamsal2024} examined seven commercial banks and reported positive regression coefficients for EPS and P/E, but an insignificant negative coefficient for BVPS. \citet{giri2024} evaluated four commercial banks across 40 bank-year observations over FY 2070/71--2079/80, documenting positive correlations of market price with DPS, P/E, and net worth per share, while the bivariate EPS association was negligible. \citet{gurung2024} analyzed 16 commercial banks across 171 observations over 2007--2023 using stepwise regression, finding significant effects for EPS and BVPS, while DPS was statistically insignificant.

\begin{sloppypar}
Studies extending through FY 2023/24 provide evidence spanning Nepal's early-2023 banking consolidation wave. \citet{pant2024} investigated seven commercial banks over 2013/14--2023/24 and found positive significant coefficients for EPS and P/E, but insignificant negative coefficients for DPS and BVPS. \citet{chaudhari2025} examined ten commercial banks over 2014/15--2023/24, finding positive associations for EPS and DPS, but a statistically significant negative association for ROA. \citet{timalsina2026} evaluated 13 commercial banks over 2011/12--2023/24 and reported positive associations for EPS, DPS, and BVPS, whereas ROA was statistically insignificant. In a recent panel regression study, \citet{gurungdahal2025} examined ten commercial banks over 2013/14--2023/24 using bank fixed effects, finding statistically significant associations for dividend payout, EPS, BVPS, and ROA.

Studies published in 2026 contribute further empirical evidence. \citet{niraula2026} evaluated 16 commercial banks over 2019/20--2023/24, finding positive associations for capital adequacy and assets, but an insignificant coefficient for ROA. \citet{bhandari2026} examined all 19 NEPSE-listed commercial banks using census sampling and identified EPS as the primary determinant of share price, with positive effects for DPS and dividend payout. In regulatory and governance research, \citet{bhattarai2024} documented positive associations between capital adequacy and EPS, while \citet{dongol2024} examined corporate governance mechanisms and bank performance.
\end{sloppypar}

Regarding banking consolidation, \citet{adhikari2023} examined seven commercial banks merged between 2013 and 2020, documenting a 46.01\% decline in mean market share prices following mergers alongside stable EPS and a modest reduction in DPS. \citet{adhikari2023cbr} conducted a comparative financial performance analysis of merged banks. Nepal Rastra Bank records document that successive consolidations reduced the total commercial bank population to 20 institutions by mid-October 2023 \citep{nrb2023list}, with major commercial bank mergers commencing joint operations in early 2023.

In summary, empirical studies in Nepal consistently demonstrate that accounting fundamentals correlate with market prices, but the magnitude, sign, and statistical significance of individual measures vary substantially across studies. These differences stem from variations in sample composition, observation periods, model specifications (such as the inclusion or omission of P/E), and price observation timing. Importantly, conventional studies estimate average associations across their full sample periods without examining whether valuation relationships shifted before and after the consolidation wave.

\subsection{Research gap}

A critical review of the existing Nepalese literature identifies several key methodological and conceptual gaps that motivate the present study:

First, nearly all existing Nepalese studies employ pooled cross-sectional or panel regression designs that estimate static, sample-average relationships across their entire observation periods \citep{bhattarai2014,chhetri2023,pant2024,chaudhari2025,gurungdahal2025}. None of these studies explicitly tests whether the valuation relevance of fundamental accounting measures changed across structural events. The commercial banking consolidation wave restructured the capital base, asset scale, and reporting boundaries of several prominent commercial banks. Following the theoretical insights of \citet{kwon2020}, such restructuring can alter the market's relative valuation of earnings versus book values. Whether the associations of EPS and BVPS with market prices differed across the pre- and post-consolidation periods remains an unexamined empirical question in Nepal.

Second, a prevalent practice in existing Nepalese research is the inclusion of the price-earnings (P/E) ratio as an explanatory variable alongside accounting fundamentals in models predicting market price per share \citep{bhattarai2014,chhetri2023,joshi2023,pant2024,gurungdahal2025}. Because $\text{P/E} = \text{MPS}/\text{EPS}$, regressing MPS on P/E introduces a circular mathematical dependency, generating mechanical correlation that obscures the authentic valuation contribution of underlying accounting numbers. A defensible research design must exclude price-derived ratios and focus on accounting fundamentals that are determined independently of secondary market prices.

Third, prior studies frequently rely on ambiguous price timing, such as fiscal-year-end (Ashadh-end) closing prices or annual average prices. Fiscal-year-end prices precede the auditing and public dissemination of annual financial statements, violating the temporal sequence required for information content studies. Furthermore, attempting to reconstruct individual, ad hoc disclosure dates for every bank-year is prone to noise and unrecorded informal leakage in the Nepalese context. A standardized observation window operationalizing the statutory nine-month
reporting deadline under Section 59(2) of BAFIA (2017) at the end of Chaitra provides
an objective, locally grounded measurement rule. Furthermore, existing literature
overlooks entity transition timing: for FY 2078/79, annual accounts reflect
pre-merger standalone operations while post-deadline April 2023 prices capture
consolidated corporate valuation, necessitating explicit transition sensitivity testing.

Finally, while small sample sizes are common in single-sector studies, prior
research often overlooks econometric limitations such as residual degrees of
freedom and small-cluster inference pitfalls \citep{cameron2008clusters}.

The present study addresses these gaps by adopting the residual-income valuation framework of \citet{ohlson1995} and the post-merger value relevance framework of \citet{kwon2020}. It specifies a baseline panel model and a consolidation-period interaction model with bank and fiscal-year fixed effects, observing market prices at a standardized statutory reporting-deadline window ($MPS^{RD}$). By examining five purposively selected commercial banks over FY 2076/77--2081/82, the study provides focused empirical evidence on whether the value relevance of accounting information differed before and after the banking consolidation wave.
