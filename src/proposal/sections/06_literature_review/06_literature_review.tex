\section{Brief review of the literature}

\subsection{Conceptual review}

A share price represents the financial value at which a unit of ownership in a
publicly traded firm is exchanged on an organized stock market. In financial
economics, share price behavior is examined through several foundational
valuation paradigms. Under dividend-based valuation frameworks, expected future
cash distributions form an essential component of equity valuation.
Concurrently, accounting-based valuation approaches emphasize the fundamental
information contained in balance-sheet net asset backing (book value) and
earnings performance. In markets characterized by information asymmetry,
signaling theory suggests that published earnings and dividend distributions
convey credible signals regarding an institution's underlying financial
condition. Empirical studies on NEPSE suggest that equity returns and price
movements reflect a combination of these firm-specific financial fundamentals
and broader market dynamics \citep{bhattarai2014,mosley2008}.

Guided by these theoretical frameworks, this study examines four core
firm-specific accounting fundamentals as explanatory variables of market price
per share (MPS): earnings per share (EPS), dividend per share (DPS), book value
per share (BVPS), and return on assets (ROA). While earlier empirical studies
frequently incorporated the price-earnings (P/E) ratio as a regressor, P/E is
mathematically constructed using the dependent variable itself ($\text{P/E} =
\text{MPS} / \text{EPS}$). Including a regressor that embeds the dependent
variable creates a mechanical dependence on the dependent variable and is
therefore inappropriate as an explanatory regressor when MPS is the dependent
variable. Consequently, this study deliberately restricts its explanatory
regressors to financial accounting measures that exist independently of
secondary market price.

\subsubsection{Earnings per share}
Earnings per share (EPS) reflects the net profitability attributable to each
ordinary equity share, calculated as profit attributable to ordinary equity
holders divided by the weighted average number of ordinary shares outstanding
during the period. As a direct indicator of earning power, EPS is theoretically
expected to have a strong positive association with stock prices. This positive
relationship is widely corroborated both in international equity markets
\citep{malhotra2013,almumani2014} and across Nepalese commercial banking
literature \citep{gyawali2022,niroula2021}. At the regulatory level, Bhattarai
\citeyearpar{bhattarai2024} established that interest spread rates and capital
adequacy enhance bank EPS, while loan loss provisions and broad money growth
constrain it.

\subsubsection{Dividend per share}
Dividend per share (DPS) represents the actual cash return distributed to
shareholders per ordinary share. Under signaling theory, consistent dividend
payouts reduce information asymmetry and convey management's confidence in the
bank's sustained solvency and cash generation. Empirical studies of the Nepalese
banking sector frequently confirm DPS as a primary positive correlate of stock
prices \citep{joshi2023,gyawali2022}.

\subsubsection{Book value per share}
Book value per share (BVPS) indicates the net asset value attributable to each
ordinary share, calculated as total shareholders' equity divided by the number of
common shares outstanding. In banking institutions, BVPS provides an essential
measure of capital safety, asset backing, and downside protection. While Joshi et
al.\ \citeyearpar{joshi2023} observed BVPS to be a statistically significant
positive determinant, Chhetri \citeyearpar{chhetri2023} identified it as a
dominant valuation factor, whereas Pant et al.\ \citeyearpar{pant2024} documented
an insignificant association.

\subsubsection{Return on assets}
Return on assets (ROA) measures net income generated per unit of total assets,
serving as an operational indicator of managerial and asset-utilization
efficiency. Despite its theoretical prominence in corporate finance, empirical
evidence regarding ROA in the Nepalese banking sector remains highly
contradictory: Gyawali \citeyearpar{gyawali2022} found a positive but
statistically insignificant relationship, whereas Chaudhari
\citeyearpar{chaudhari2025} reported a statistically significant negative
coefficient, highlighting the necessity for further empirical resolution.

Two additional dimensions of the literature are relevant to the sector.
First, bank regulation and capital structure shape earnings, which in turn
influence share prices
\citep{bhattarai2024,dongol2024}. Second, the wave of mergers and acquisitions
that reshaped the Nepalese banking sector between 2013 and 2020 altered both
earnings and market price: sector-wide MPS fell by roughly 46\% in the
periods following the mergers, driven by capital-increment share issues, while
EPS and DPS were statistically unchanged \citep{adhikari2023,adhikari2023cbr}.

\subsection{Research gap}

The existing literature exhibits three key limitations. First, most studies are
confined to periods ending prior to the major 2023 consolidation wave, leaving
valuation behavior across the recent restructuring unexamined
\citep{adhikari2023,chaudhari2025}. Second, empirical results on ROA and BVPS
are directly contradictory: Chhetri \citeyearpar{chhetri2023} reports ROA as a
significant positive correlate, whereas Chaudhari \citeyearpar{chaudhari2025}
reports it as significantly negative, and Gyawali \citeyearpar{gyawali2022} as
positive but insignificant. On BVPS, Joshi et al.\ \citeyearpar{joshi2023} find
it significant while Pant et al.\ \citeyearpar{pant2024} find it negative and
insignificant. Third, several earlier specifications include P/E as an
explanatory variable alongside MPS \citep{bhattarai2014,chhetri2023}.
Because P/E is mechanically defined as MPS/EPS, the present study
excludes P/E from the regression specification to avoid direct
mathematical dependence between the dependent and explanatory variables.

This study addresses the identified empirical and specification issues by
examining a purposively selected sample of commercial banks across a six-year
period spanning before, during, and after the major 2023 consolidation
(FY 2076/77 to FY 2081/82). The study estimates a unified model of the
associations between EPS, DPS, BVPS, and ROA and post-disclosure equity prices,
while excluding valuation multiples that are mechanically constructed from
market price.
