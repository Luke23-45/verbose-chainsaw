\section{Brief review of the literature}

\subsection*{Theoretical review}

Equity valuation starts from the present value of expected dividends \citep{miller1961}. Under full information and fixed investment policy, the split between dividends and retention does not itself create value, which gives the proposal its neutral benchmark: any association between accounting numbers and price must come from what those numbers reveal about future cash generation, not from the payout mechanics alone.

Nepalese banking is a setting with pronounced information asymmetry between insiders and outside investors, so the signaling channel is central. When managers hold private information, costly observable choices convey it credibly \citep{ross1977}: dividends signal anticipated performance because a weak firm cannot mimic a sustained payout without cost \citep{bhattacharya1979}. Steady earnings and dividends therefore reduce asymmetry and move prices even before the cash itself matters, which is why earnings power and payout behavior belong in the same specification as information proxies rather than as cash-flow arithmetic.

The accounting anchor is the clean-surplus relation formalized by \citet{ohlson1995}: market value equals book value plus the present value of expected abnormal earnings, so earnings and book value enter valuation jointly and dividends displace price dollar-for-dollar while leaving the abnormal-earnings sequence unchanged. In banking terms, earnings per share carries the flow signal on current profitability, book value per share carries the stock signal on net-asset backing and downside protection, and dividend per share carries the costly signal on solvency and cash generation. Return on assets adds the efficiency margin --- net income per unit of assets --- through which regulation, capital structure, and asset use shape the earnings that later capitalize into price \citep{bhattarai2024}.

Earlier specifications often added the price-earnings ratio alongside market price, but $\text{P/E} = \text{MPS} / \text{EPS}$ embeds the dependent variable in the regressor and creates direct definitional dependence. This proposal therefore restricts regressors to accounting measures that exist independently of secondary-market price, and tests their associations with post-disclosure price without a mechanically constructed multiple on both sides of the equation.

\subsection*{Empirical review}

Recent work on Nepalese commercial banks is summarized in Table~\ref{tab:meta}. The evidence is extensive but contested on the two variables this proposal isolates, ROA and BVPS. Internationally, \citet{malhotra2013} link book value, earnings, and the earnings multiple to NSE prices with dividend yield inverse, and \citet{almumani2014} confirms earnings, book value, and the multiple as bank-price correlates in Amman. In Nepal, \citet{bhattarai2014} finds earnings and the multiple positive with yield inverse over 2006--2014, and \citet{pradhan2016} find earnings, return on assets, and size significant with GDP the key macro variable over 2002/03--2013/14.

\citet{bajracharya2019} report earnings, dividends, and the multiple significant with return on assets insignificant and inflation negative over 2006/07--2015/16. \citet{niroula2021} finds earnings and the multiple significantly positive with return on equity and book value negative and insignificant across 18 banks over 2015/16--2019/20. \citet{gyawali2022} finds earnings, dividends, and the multiple significantly positive with return on assets and GDP insignificant over 2017--2021. \citet{joshi2023} find earnings, dividends, the multiple, and book value all positive and significant over 2012/13--2021/22.

Against this run, \citet{chhetri2023} finds the multiple, book value, return on assets, and inflation significantly positive in a 13-bank 2012--2022 panel, whereas \citet{pant2024} find only earnings and the multiple significantly positive with dividends and book value insignificantly negative over 2013/14--2023/24. The most recent bank-year evidence sharpens the contradiction this proposal targets: \citet{chaudhari2025} reports earnings and dividends significantly positive with return on assets significantly negative over 2014/15--2023/24, while governance evidence links capital adequacy and payout to earnings with board independence negative at the 10\% level \citep{dongol2024}.

Two additional dimensions are relevant. Bank regulation and capital structure shape earnings, which in turn influence prices \citep{bhattarai2024,dongol2024}. The merger wave of 2013--2020 and the major 2023 consolidation altered both earnings and price: sector-wide MPS fell by roughly 46\% after the mergers, driven by capital-increment share issues, while EPS and DPS were statistically unchanged \citep{adhikari2023,adhikari2023cbr}.

\subsection*{Research gap}

Three limitations remain. First, most studies end before the major 2023 consolidation, leaving valuation across the restructuring unexamined \citep{adhikari2023,chaudhari2025}. Second, results on ROA and BVPS directly contradict: \citet{chhetri2023} reports ROA significantly positive, \citet{chaudhari2025} significantly negative, and \citet{gyawali2022} positive but insignificant; on BVPS, \citet{joshi2023} find it significant while \citet{pant2024} find it negative and insignificant. Third, several specifications include P/E alongside MPS \citep{bhattarai2014,chhetri2023}, which is defined as MPS/EPS. This study excludes P/E to avoid definitional dependence.

This study examines a purposively selected sample across FY 2076/77 to FY
2081/82, spanning the periods before, during, and after the major 2023 consolidation,
estimating a unified model of EPS, DPS, BVPS, and ROA against post-disclosure
equity prices while excluding valuation multiples constructed directly from
market prices.
