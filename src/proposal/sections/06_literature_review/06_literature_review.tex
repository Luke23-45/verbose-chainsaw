\section{Brief review of the literature}

\subsection{Conceptual review}

A share price is the price at which a unit of ownership in a company changes
hands on an organized exchange. It fluctuates with the balance between buying
and selling pressure, market volatility, macroeconomic conditions and investor
perception of the firm \citep{mosley2008,bhattarai2014}. Returns from equity
investment vary with firm performance and with market sentiment
\citep{bhattarai2014}.

This study examines the following five firm-specific measures as
determinants of market price per share.

\subsubsection{Earnings per share}
Earnings per share (EPS) indicates profitability attributable to each ordinary
share, computed as profit after preference dividend divided by the number of
ordinary shares outstanding. It is widely reported as positive and significant in
the Nepalese banking literature \citep{malhotra2013,almumani2014,gyawali2022,niroula2021}.
Bank-level regulation also matters: Bhattarai \citeyearpar{bhattarai2024} found
that weighted average spread rate and bank capital raise EPS, while base rate,
loan loss provision and broad money supply reduce it.

\subsubsection{Dividend per share}
Dividend per share (DPS) is total dividends paid divided by the number of shares
outstanding. It signals profitability, growth and the distribution policy of the
firm, and is frequently found significant in Nepalese studies
\citep{joshi2023,gyawali2022}.

\subsubsection{Price-earnings ratio}
The price-earnings (P/E) ratio is market price relative to earnings per share. It
is a valuation measure and a common pricing anchor for bank shares. It emerges
as positive and significant in several studies
\citep{malhotra2013,almumani2014,gyawali2022,niroula2021,chhetri2023}.

\subsubsection{Book value per share}
Book value per share (BVPS) is the per-share estimate of the minimum equity
value of a bank, after deducting preference equity. Joshi et al.\
\citeyearpar{joshi2023} report BVPS as significant though the weakest of the
four measures in their model, and Chhetri \citeyearpar{chhetri2023} finds it a
major positive determinant.

\subsubsection{Return on assets}
Return on assets (ROA) measures net income relative to total assets and indicates
the efficiency with which a bank uses its asset base. Findings on ROA are the
least consistent in the literature: Gyawali \citeyearpar{gyawali2022} reports a
positive but insignificant effect, while Chaudhari \citeyearpar{chaudhari2025}
finds a statistically significant negative effect.

Two further lines of evidence bear on the sector but not on share-price
determinants directly. First, bank regulation and capital structure shape
earnings, which in turn feed the pricing relationship
\citep{bhattarai2024,dongol2024}. Second, the wave of mergers and acquisitions
that reshaped the Nepalese banking sector between 2013 and 2020 altered both
earnings and market price: sector-wide MPS fell by roughly 46\% in the
periods following the mergers, driven by capital-increment share issues, while
EPS and DPS were statistically unchanged \citep{adhikari2023,adhikari2023cbr}.

\subsection{Research gap}

The existing literature has three key limitations. First, most studies are
confined to periods ending no later than 2021/22, and even the studies whose
windows cover the merger wave of 2013--2020 do not account for the structural
break in their specifications \citep{adhikari2023,chaudhari2025}. Second,
results on ROA and BVPS are directly contradictory: Chhetri
\citeyearpar{chhetri2023} reports ROA as a significant positive determinant,
whereas Chaudhari \citeyearpar{chaudhari2025} reports it as significantly
negative and Gyawali \citeyearpar{gyawali2022} as positive but insignificant.
On BVPS, Joshi et al.\ \citeyearpar{joshi2023} find it significant while Pant
et al.\ \citeyearpar{pant2024} find it negative and insignificant. Third,
samples are small and inconsistent, ranging from four to eighteen banks, making
the results difficult to generalize.

This study uses a sample of banks that exist as single legal entities through
the merger period, an observation window extending to the most recent fiscal
year, and a jointly estimated model retaining all five firm-specific measures
to resolve the conflicting ROA and BVPS findings within a single
specification.
