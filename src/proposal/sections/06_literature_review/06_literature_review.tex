\section{Brief review of the literature}

\subsection{Theoretical review}

Equity valuation provides a basis for examining reported accounting measures
in relation to share prices. Miller and Modigliani's dividend-irrelevance
result holds under restrictive assumptions: fixed investment policy and
frictionless capital markets. Under those assumptions, changing the payout does
not by itself change firm value \citep{miller1961}. This result is a benchmark,
not a direct prediction that a higher dividend per share must correspond to a
higher market price in Nepal.

Signaling theory offers a conditional reason why accounting disclosures and
dividend decisions may be associated with price. Ross (1977) models
financial-structure choices as signals when managers hold information
unavailable to investors. Bhattacharya (1979) develops a separate model in
which dividends can signal managers' expectations of future profitability under
specified information and signaling-cost assumptions. These theories do not establish that
every dividend increase signals stronger performance; they motivate examining
DPS alongside the other reported measures \citep{ross1977,bhattacharya1979}.

Ohlson's valuation model relates market value to current and future earnings,
book values, dividends, and other information, with the clean-surplus relation
as an accounting foundation \citep{ohlson1995}. In the present study, EPS
represents earnings on a per-share basis, BVPS represents book equity on a
per-share basis, DPS records the reported distribution for the fiscal year, and
ROA represents reported asset profitability. EPS and ROA share an earnings
numerator; DPS and BVPS are also related to earnings and retained earnings. The
regressors are therefore not assumed to be statistically independent. Their
overlap is one reason to assess multicollinearity and to interpret conditional
coefficients cautiously.

Several Nepalese studies include the price-earnings ratio (P/E) alongside
market price per share (MPS) as an explanatory variable
\citep{bhattarai2014,chhetri2023}. If P/E is calculated using the same MPS
observation that serves as the dependent variable, it contains that variable by
definition (P/E = MPS/EPS). The present model therefore excludes P/E to avoid
that direct mathematical coupling. This rationale applies to a P/E constructed
from the same price observation; it does not invalidate prior studies that may
have used a differently dated price.

\subsection{Empirical review}

International studies provide context but are not direct evidence for the
Nepalese banking sample. Research on NSE 100 firms in India and listed banks in
Jordan examines combinations of earnings, book value, dividends, and valuation
multiples, with findings that depend on the market, sample, and specification
\citep{malhotra2013,almumani2014}. Earlier Nepalese bank studies by Bhattarai
(2014) and Pradhan and Dahal (2016) also report associations between accounting
fundamentals and share prices, but their observation periods end before the
early-2023 merger wave \citep{bhattarai2014,pradhan2016}.

More recent Nepalese studies do not yield a uniform result for all variables.
Gyawali (2022), using ten banks over 2017--2021, reports a positive but
statistically insignificant ROA coefficient. Chhetri (2023) studies 13 banks
over 2012--2022 with pooled cross-sectional analysis and reports positive,
statistically significant results for BVPS and ROA; that paper also includes
P/E in a model with MPS as the outcome. Joshi et al. (2023) report a positive
BVPS association in their commercial-bank sample. These estimates differ in
sample, period, variable construction, and model specification, so they should
not be treated as estimates of one common effect
\citep{gyawali2022,chhetri2023,joshi2023}.

Two further Nepalese studies examine market price per share using related
accounting measures. Giri (2024) analyzes 40 bank-year observations for four
commercial banks over FY 2070/71--2079/80 using DPS, EPS, P/E, and net worth
per share. The article reports positive significant correlations of MPS with
DPS, P/E, and net worth per share, while describing the MPS--EPS association as
negligible. Gurung and Subedi (2024) analyze 171 yearly observations for 16
commercial banks over 2007--2023 using stepwise regression with EPS, DPS, BVPS,
P/E, and total assets. They report EPS, BVPS, and P/E as explanatory of MPS,
while DPS and total assets are not significant. Both studies add recent
evidence, again with different samples, periods, and specifications
\citep{giri2024,gurung2024}.

Studies with periods extending through FY 2023/24 provide evidence from the
post-consolidation period. Pant et al. (2024), using seven conveniently
selected banks, report that BVPS has a weak, slightly negative relationship
with MPS and does not significantly contribute to price variation. Chaudhari
(2025), using ten banks, reports a significantly negative ROA coefficient.
Timalsina (2026), using 13 banks through FY 2023/24, reports that ROA and GDP
growth are not statistically significant. Results remain mixed across these
samples, periods, and specifications
\citep{pant2024,chaudhari2025,timalsina2026}.

Merger evidence must also be distinguished by period and outcome. Adhikari,
Kavanagh, and Hampson (2023) compare financial ratios before and after mergers
for seven commercial banks involved in transactions from 2013 to 2020. Their
reported 46.01\% fall in mean MPS is specific to that sample and period, not an
estimate for the early-2023 mergers \citep{adhikari2023}. Other work examines
post-merger financial performance or governance rather than the
disclosure-timed share-price outcome used here
\citep{adhikari2023cbr,bhattarai2024,dongol2024}.

\subsection{Research gap}

The reviewed literature includes studies with observations through FY 2023/24,
so this proposal does not claim that post-consolidation evidence is absent. Its
bounded contribution is to estimate bivariate and conditional associations for a
target of five purposively selected bank series over FY 2076/77--2081/82 using a
pre-specified post-disclosure price rule: the NEPSE closing price on the first
trading day after the corresponding audited annual financial statements are
publicly disclosed through NEPSE. This provides a reproducible timing rule for
the proposed observations. The proposal does not claim that the rule is
unprecedented, that it resolves conflicting findings, or that it identifies
merger effects.

The analysis estimates bivariate associations and a joint pooled-OLS
specification for EPS, DPS, BVPS, and ROA with post-disclosure MPS. It excludes
P/E when that measure would be constructed from the same MPS observation used
as the dependent variable. Findings will be interpreted for the sampled bank
series only. The small sample, differing disclosure and price dates, and changes
in reporting boundaries around mergers limit interpretation.
