\section{Brief review of the literature}

\subsection{Theoretical review}

Equity valuation relates share prices to expected future economic benefits and the information available to market participants. The residual-income valuation (RIV) framework developed in accounting valuation research by \citet{ohlson1995} and \citet{feltham1995} provides the fundamental theoretical foundation linking accounting numbers to equity market values. Under the clean-surplus relation ($BV_{t} = BV_{t-1} + NI_{t} - Div_{t}$), assuming suitable regularity and terminal conditions, the market value of common equity can be represented as the sum of current book value and the discounted present value of expected future residual income (abnormal earnings):
\begin{equation}
P_{t} = BV_{t} + \sum_{\tau=1}^{\infty} \frac{E_{t}[\mathit{NI}_{t+\tau} - r \cdot BV_{t+\tau-1}]}{(1+r)^{\tau}}
\label{eq:ohlson}
\end{equation}
where $BV_t$ is book equity at period $t$, $NI_{t+\tau}$ is net income in period $t+\tau$, and $r$ is the cost of equity capital. The framework makes book value relevant as the current accounting anchor and earnings relevant insofar as they inform expectations of future residual income. The empirical price-level specification used in this proposal is a parsimonious value-relevance model motivated by this framework; it is not a direct estimate of the full residual-income valuation equation. EPS summarizes reported earnings per share for the period, while BVPS summarizes equity per share at the reporting date.

A relevant empirical question is whether investors' valuation weights on earnings and book value vary across structural changes. Using US data, \citet{kwon2020} find that investors generally place less weight on earnings and more on book value after acquisitions, with the pattern particularly strong after adoption of SFAS 141(R). This result motivates testing for a change in accounting--price associations, but it is not a direct prediction for Nepal: the institutional setting, accounting regime, merger context, and period differ. More broadly, \citet{collins1997} document changes over time in the relative value relevance of earnings and book values among US firms. Together, these studies support examining possible variation in the two associations while leaving their direction an empirical question in the Nepalese setting.

\begin{sloppypar}
Dividend policy is relevant only to the supplementary DPS analysis: the irrelevance result of \citet{miller1961} depends on restrictive market assumptions, while signaling and agency perspectives offer reasons payout decisions may convey information or constrain managerial discretion \citep{ross1977,bhattacharya1979,jensen1986}. These theories do not generate the proposal's primary hypotheses, which concern EPS and BVPS under the residual-income valuation framework. For banks, opacity in loan portfolios further makes the quality and timing of reported information salient \citep{morgan2002}.
\end{sloppypar}

The semi-strong form of the Efficient Market Hypothesis holds that prices reflect publicly available information \citep{fama1970}; earnings-announcement research also shows that market responses to accounting news can extend beyond the announcement date \citep{ball1968,beaver1968,bernard1989}. These findings make the relation between the accounting period, disclosure date, and observed price important. This is a theoretical benchmark; the proposal does not assume that NEPSE is semi-strong efficient. A fiscal-year-end price is temporally earlier than publication of the audited annual statements and therefore cannot be treated as a market price observed after those statements became public. This proposal uses a standardized price window at or after the statutory reporting deadline in Section 59(2) of the Banks and Financial Institutions Act, 2017, operationalized at Chaitra-end \citep{bafia2017}. The statute sets a publication requirement; it does not prove that each bank complied by the deadline. Accordingly, the measure is a deadline-window proxy, not a verified disclosure-date or event-time price, and the interpretation will remain conditional on the availability of bank-specific release-date evidence.

Finally, this proposal excludes the price-earnings (P/E) ratio from its specifications. Because $\text{P/E} = \text{MPS}/\text{EPS}$, including P/E when MPS is the outcome creates direct mathematical overlap between a regressor and the dependent variable, making its coefficient difficult to interpret as the separate association of an accounting fundamental with price. This is a scope and interpretation choice, not a claim that every prior study using P/E is invalid.

\subsection{Empirical review}

International value-relevance studies establish that the associations of earnings and book value with prices can vary across firms and periods, while evidence on merger-related changes comes from accounting regimes unlike Nepal's \citep{collins1997,kwon2020}. This literature motivates, but does not determine, the expected Nepalese results.

Nepalese studies commonly model bank share prices as outcomes and relate them to accounting measures and other bank-specific or macroeconomic variables. These studies therefore provide relevant evidence on accounting--price associations; the distinction for this proposal is that most focus on average price determinants rather than period-specific changes in valuation slopes. Findings differ across samples and specifications. For example, \citet{bhattarai2014} and \citet{chhetri2023} include P/E with accounting measures, while \citet{niroula2021} reports a positive EPS association but statistically insignificant negative coefficients for BVPS and ROE. In a four-bank, 40-observation panel, \citet{giri2024} reports positive correlations of price with DPS, P/E, and net worth per share but a negligible bivariate EPS association. Other studies report positive coefficients for EPS and BVPS, but differ on DPS, profitability, and the significance of those estimates \citep{joshi2023,lamsal2024,gurung2024,pant2024,chaudhari2025}.

Recent work confirms that this line of research remains active. \citet{gurungdahal2025} use bank fixed effects for ten banks over FY 2013/14--2023/24 and report significant associations for several financial indicators, including EPS and BVPS; their specification estimates average slopes over the sample rather than changes in those slopes across the early-2023 consolidation. \citet{bhandari2026} study 19 listed banks and report EPS as the strongest price correlate. \citet{niraula2026} examine firm-specific predictors of bank prices using a more recent five-year panel. Their questions and model specifications differ from the proposed test of whether EPS--price and BVPS--price associations changed across a defined structural period.

Nepalese merger research has primarily assessed financial performance before and after mergers. \citet{adhikari2023} compare seven banks around mergers completed during 2013--2020 using financial ratios and paired comparisons; they report improved liquidity and leverage, while profitability and shareholder-wealth ratios show no change or only marginal change. \citet{adhikari2023cbr} also examine pre- and post-merger financial performance, including sector-level comparisons. Neither study tests whether EPS--price and BVPS--price slopes changed across the early-2023 consolidation. \citet{acharya2026merger} extend this merger-performance literature with a comparison of merged banks and non-merged peers using difference-in-differences, supplemented by within-bank pre- and post-merger analyses and interaction models. They report heterogeneous post-merger outcomes and no statistically significant profitability advantage for merged banks relative to non-merged peers. Their focus is merger-related financial performance; it does not answer the present proposal's question about whether EPS--price and BVPS--price associations changed across the consolidation period.

\citet{bhattarai2024mps} analyze market price alongside accounting indicators over FY 2012/13--2022/23 in a pooled regression, reporting positive associations for EPS and DPS but no statistically significant BVPS association. Although that sample spans merger-related changes in the number of banks, the reported model does not interact EPS and BVPS with a period indicator to test for slope changes across the early-2023 consolidation. The separate EPS-focused regulatory study by \citet{bhattarai2024} and governance research by \citet{dongol2024} address related banking questions but do not test this value-relevance contrast. NRB records document that the early-2023 mergers reduced the commercial-bank count to 20 by mid-October 2023 \citep{nrb2023list}.

Taken together, the reviewed evidence supports the relevance of accounting information to Nepalese bank prices but also shows variation across indicators, samples, periods, and specifications. It does not establish in advance that either accounting association increased or decreased after the 2023 merger wave. The proposed study therefore treats the period comparison as an empirical, non-directional question and interprets it as an association difference rather than a merger effect.

\subsection{Research gap}

The reviewed literature leaves a focused question for this proposal. Price-determinant studies, including recent panel work, estimate average associations between bank prices and financial indicators. Nepalese merger studies, including recent comparative and difference-in-differences work, examine financial-performance outcomes around consolidation. In the studies reviewed here, we did not identify a test of whether the EPS--price and BVPS--price slopes differed across the early-2023 consolidation period for Nepalese commercial banks. The proposed interaction model addresses that question for five purposively selected bank series over FY 2076/77--2081/82. The design is exploratory and does not isolate a causal merger effect: common period changes, other contemporaneous events, a small purposive sample, and changes in the identity underlying some bank series constrain interpretation.

The price measure is a design choice intended to make the observation window reproducible. It uses the first eligible market-price observation on or after the Chaitra-end target, which operationalizes the statutory reporting deadline. The law does not confirm actual publication by each bank, and the prices are aggregator-sourced rather than independently verified NEPSE official closes. The study will report these limits and will not claim a verified post-disclosure market response. The FY 2078/79 accounting-price pairing also requires a sensitivity analysis because four banks' prices were observed after their mergers while that year's accounts reflect the pre-merger entities. The NMB series has a separate earlier acquisition-related timing mismatch, described in the methodology.
