\section{Research methodology}

\msub{1.1.1 Research design}
To address the research questions and achieve the stated objectives, this study
employs a descriptive and explanatory quantitative research design using
secondary panel data, in accordance with established social science methodology
\citep{pant2016}. The descriptive research design is utilized to describe the
distributional characteristics, central tendencies, and historical variations of
the study variables. Correlation and multiple regression analyses are employed
to examine the empirical associations between firm-specific accounting
fundamentals (EPS, DPS, BVPS, and ROA) and market price per share (MPS).

\msub{1.1.2 Population and sampling procedure}
The target population of the study comprises all 20 commercial banks operating
in Nepal as of mid-2023, following the extensive regulatory consolidation that
reduced the sector from 32 commercial banks in 2014. From this population, a
sample of 5 commercial banks is selected using a purposive (judgmental) sampling
technique. The selection criteria require: (i) representation of the prominent
commercial bank mergers that defined the early 2023 consolidation wave, (ii)
inclusion of an unmerged comparison bank (NMB Bank) to provide comparative
balance, and (iii) continuous availability of audited annual financial
statements and NEPSE trading records across the entire study period. Because the
sample is non-probabilistic and comprises 5 institutions, the empirical findings
are interpreted specifically for the sampled banks and serve as an indicative
benchmark rather than an automatic generalization across all 20 commercial
banks.

The sampled commercial banks, identified by their current legal corporate names,
are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Global IME Bank Limited (absorbed Bank of Kathmandu; joint operation commenced 9 January 2023; swap ratio 1:1)
  \item Himalayan Bank Limited (acquired Civil Bank; joint operation commenced 24 February 2023; swap ratio 100:80.28)
  \item Kumari Bank Limited (absorbed Nepal Credit and Commerce Bank; joint operation commenced 1 January 2023; swap ratio 1:1)
  \item Nepal Investment Mega Bank Limited (merger of Nepal Investment Bank and Mega Bank; joint operation commenced 11 January 2023; swap ratio 100:90)
  \item NMB Bank Limited (maintained independent operations without merger during the period)
\end{enumerate}
For merged banks, pre-merger observations represent the acquiring institution,
whereas post-merger observations represent the consolidated institution.
Consequently, the merger alters the economic scale and composition of the
bank-level observation, and this study does not interpret regression
coefficients as direct estimates of the causal effect of the mergers themselves.
The study covers a six-year observation period from FY 2076/77 to FY 2081/82,
spanning the pre- and post-consolidation phases of Nepal's commercial banking
sector, yielding 30 bank-year observations ($5\text{ sampled bank series}
\times 6\text{ fiscal years} = 30\text{ observations}$).

\msub{1.1.3 Nature and sources of data collection}
This study relies entirely on secondary financial and market data. Numerical
observations for the dependent variable (MPS) and the four independent variables
(EPS, DPS, BVPS, and ROA) are obtained from authoritative official sources,
including:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Audited annual reports and published financial statements of the sampled commercial banks;
  \item \textit{Banking and Financial Statistics} and supervision reports published by Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB);
  \item Trading records, official share price bulletins, and annual publications of the Nepal Stock Exchange (NEPSE) and the Securities Board of Nepal (SEBON);
  \item \textit{Economic Survey} published by the Ministry of Finance (MoF), Government of Nepal.
\end{itemize}
All extracted figures are systematically cross-checked across published sources
to verify numerical accuracy and consistency before statistical processing.

\msub{1.1.4 Research framework and definition of variables}
The conceptual framework illustrating the functional relationship between the
explanatory accounting fundamentals and the dependent variable is depicted in
Figure~\ref{fig:framework}. Market price per share (MPS) serves as the dependent
variable, while earnings per share (EPS), dividend per share (DPS), book value
per share (BVPS), and return on assets (ROA) serve as explanatory independent
variables. For each fiscal-year observation, MPS is measured at a pre-specified
trading date following publication and official submission of the corresponding
audited annual financial statements to NEPSE, ensuring that published accounting
information is fully available to the secondary market. The same timing rule is
applied consistently across all sampled banks and years.

\msub{1.1.5 Methods of analysis}
To examine the empirical relationships and evaluate the hypotheses formulated in
this study, both descriptive and inferential statistical techniques are applied.

\textbf{Model Specification:}
To examine the joint and conditional associations of all four accounting
fundamentals with market price per share, the following multiple linear
regression model is estimated:
\begin{equation}
\mathit{MPS}_{it} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \mathit{EPS}_{it} + \beta_2 \mathit{DPS}_{it} + \beta_3 \mathit{BVPS}_{it} + \beta_4 \mathit{ROA}_{it} + \varepsilon_{it}
\label{eq:main}
\end{equation}
where $\mathit{MPS}_{it}$ represents market price per share for bank $i$ in
period $t$; $\mathit{EPS}_{it}$, $\mathit{DPS}_{it}$, $\mathit{BVPS}_{it}$, and
$\mathit{ROA}_{it}$ denote earnings per share, dividend per share, book value
per share, and return on assets, respectively. The parameter $\beta_0$ represents
the intercept, $\beta_1$ through $\beta_4$ represent regression slope coefficients
capturing the estimated conditional association between each regressor and MPS
holding the other included variables constant, and $\varepsilon_{it}$ is the
stochastic disturbance term. In supplementary sensitivity checks, time-aligned
specifications pairing fiscal-year fundamentals with subsequent market prices
are evaluated to account for disclosure-lag effects.

\textbf{Statistical Tools and Estimation Procedure:}
The empirical analysis follows a structured analytical plan:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Descriptive Statistics:} Measures of central tendency and
    dispersion---including the mean, standard deviation, minimum, and
    maximum---are computed to examine the distributional properties, baseline
    levels, and variations of all variables across the panel.
  \item \textbf{Correlation Analysis:} Pearson correlation coefficients are
    estimated to determine the direction and degree of bivariate linear
    association between each accounting fundamental and MPS, and to provide
    preliminary inspection of collinearity among explanatory regressors.
  \item \textbf{Multiple Regression Analysis:} The multiple linear regression
    model (Eq.~\ref{eq:main}) is estimated using Ordinary Least Squares (OLS).
    The overall explanatory power is evaluated via the coefficient of
    determination ($R^2$) and adjusted $R^2$. Although the hypotheses specify
    positive directional associations, two-tailed $t$-tests at the 5\%
    significance level are used for conservative statistical inference, while
    the joint statistical significance of the regression model is tested using
    the $F$-test at the 5\% level of significance ($\alpha = 0.05$).
  \item \textbf{Diagnostic Tests:} Model assumptions are examined through
    diagnostic checks. Multicollinearity among regressors is evaluated using
    Variance Inflation Factors (VIF) and tolerance statistics, interpreted in
    conjunction with the correlation matrix rather than relying on an arbitrary
    single cutoff. Residual diagnostics include examinations for
    heteroskedasticity and screening for influential observations using Cook's
    distance. Given the very small number of sampled banks and short time
    dimension ($N=5, T=6$), pooled OLS is used as a baseline exploratory
    specification. Because observations are repeated within banks, inference is
    interpreted cautiously, and the dependence structure and small-sample
    limitations are explicitly acknowledged.
\end{enumerate}

All data organization, coding, and econometric estimations are conducted using
the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) and Microsoft Excel.
