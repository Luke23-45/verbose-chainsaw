\section{Research methodology}

\subsection{Research design}
This study uses a quantitative, descriptive and correlational design with
secondary data \citep{pant2016}. The design fits the research questions because
they describe observed values and estimate associations; the study does not
manipulate variables or identify causal effects.

Descriptive statistics summarize EPS, DPS, BVPS, ROA, and
$\mathit{MPS}^{post}$ by bank series and fiscal year. Pearson correlations
describe pairwise associations. Pooled ordinary least squares (OLS) estimates
the joint conditional associations specified in Eq.~\ref{eq:main}.

The target is 30 bank-year observations: five provisional bank series observed
over six fiscal years, FY 2076/77--2081/82. This is a short longitudinal
bank-year dataset, not a panel of five unchanged legal entities. Four series
cross a merger boundary, with earlier observations referring to the acquiring
institution and later observations referring to the consolidated institution.

Before finalizing the sample and estimating the model, a bank-by-year
feasibility screen will document, for every study year including FY 2081/82:
the audited financial-statement source, its NEPSE disclosure date, the first
subsequent trading date and closing price, each accounting measure, the source
and status of any dividend recommendation, and any share-capital action
affecting per-share comparability. The realized sample will include only
bank-years complete for all model variables. Unavailable or unverifiable
observations will be identified and reported, not imputed, and the regression
sample size will be stated. Pooled OLS is an
exploratory summary. Conventional OLS tests do not account for possible
within-series dependence or changes in reporting boundaries and will not be
treated as confirmatory evidence or as causal effects.

\subsection{Population and sampling procedure}
The target population for the post-consolidation sample frame is the 20
commercial banks listed by Nepal Rastra Bank as of mid-October 2023
\citep{nrb2023list}. From this population, a provisional
sample of five banks is specified using purposive (judgmental) sampling. The
selection criteria are: (i) representation of the prominent commercial bank
mergers that defined the early-2023 consolidation wave, (ii) inclusion of an
unmerged reference bank (NMB Bank) for descriptive context, and (iii)
verifiable audited annual financial statements, NEPSE trading records, and
accounting measures by their relevant observation dates across the entire
study period, subject to the feasibility screen described above.

If the screen shows that a selected series does not meet these criteria, the
final sample and the reason for any change will be reported before analysis.
Because the sample is non-probabilistic and comprises at most five institutions,
findings are interpreted for the sampled banks only.

The five candidate bank series, identified by their current legal corporate
names and subject to the feasibility screen, are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Global IME Bank Limited (absorbed Bank of Kathmandu, joint operation began 9 January 2023, swap ratio 1:1) \citep{gbime2022agm,nrb2023list}
  \item Himalayan Bank Limited (acquired Civil Bank, joint operation commenced 24 February 2023, swap ratio 100:80.28) \citep{hbl2023report}
  \item Kumari Bank Limited (absorbed Nepal Credit and Commerce Bank, joint operation commenced 1 January 2023, swap ratio 1:1) \citep{kumari2022sgm,nrb2023list}
  \item Nepal Investment Mega Bank Limited (merger of Nepal Investment Bank and Mega Bank, joint operation commenced 11 January 2023, swap ratio 100:90) \citep{nimb2023report}
  \item NMB Bank Limited (maintained independent operations without merger during the period) \citep{nrb2023list}
\end{enumerate}
For each merged bank series, pre-merger observations represent the acquiring
institution and post-merger observations represent the consolidated
institution. The fiscal year in which a merger began joint operation is a
transition year: the audited statement's reporting boundary will be recorded for
each bank-year, and that observation will be flagged rather than described as a
clean pre-merger or post-merger year. A source record will identify the
reporting institution and listed security for every observation. The merger
changes the scale and composition of a series; regression coefficients are not
interpreted as estimates of merger effects.

The study covers FY 2076/77--2081/82, spanning years before, during, and after
the early-2023 mergers, with a target of 30 observations subject to
verification. NMB Bank is included in the descriptive and pooled analyses as the
unmerged reference series. Because the model includes no merger indicator or
interaction, it does not estimate a merger-versus-non-merger comparison.

\subsection{Nature and sources of data collection}
This study relies entirely on secondary financial and market data. Bank
financial statements provide the accounting measures; NEPSE trading records
provide market prices. NRB, SEBON, and Ministry of Finance publications provide
regulatory and sector context and are used for cross-checking where they report
the same observation. Sources for each bank-year are recorded in the data file.
The principal sources are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Audited annual reports and published financial statements of the sampled commercial banks.
  \item \textit{Banking and Financial Statistics} and supervision reports published by Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB).
  \item NEPSE trading records and official share-price bulletins; SEBON annual reports and publications for market and regulatory context.
  \item \textit{Economic Survey} published by the Ministry of Finance (MoF), Government of Nepal.
\end{itemize}
Extracted figures are checked against the originating report and, where an
independent source reports the same measure, cross-checked before processing.
Differences are recorded and resolved using the originating audited report for
accounting measures and the NEPSE record for trading prices.

assets (ROA) are the explanatory variables.

Post-disclosure market price per share ($\mathit{MPS}^{\mathit{post}}$),
measured in Nepalese rupees (NPR), is the closing price on the first NEPSE
trading day with a calendar date strictly later than the NEPSE disclosure date
for the corresponding audited annual financial statements. The same rule is
applied to every sampled bank series and fiscal year. The filing date, trading
date, listed security, and source records are logged for each observation. The
NEPSE closing price recorded for that date is used; a retrospectively adjusted
price series is not substituted.

Any bonus issue, rights issue, split, or other share-capital action between
fiscal year-end and the price date is recorded. Accounting per-share measures
must use a share basis comparable with the listed security on the price date.
Audited restatements or source-documented share-basis conversions will be used
where available. If comparability cannot be established, the affected bank-year
is recorded as unavailable rather than adjusted using an undocumented rule.

Earnings per share (EPS), in NPR per ordinary share, is audited basic EPS as
reported in the annual financial statements or calculated from reported profit
attributable to ordinary equity holders and weighted-average ordinary shares
outstanding. Any audited retrospective restatement following a bonus issue or
share split is retained.

Dividend per share (DPS), in NPR per ordinary share, is the cash dividend per
share recommended for the corresponding fiscal year and publicly disclosed by
the MPS observation date. The recommendation is used whether or not it has
subsequently received shareholder approval or been distributed; later approval
or payment is not used because it was not necessarily known at the observation
date. A documented decision not to recommend a cash dividend is recorded as
zero. If the recommendation or its amount cannot be established by the MPS
date, DPS is unavailable. The source and recommendation status are logged.

Book value per share (BVPS), in NPR per ordinary share, is ordinary
shareholders' equity at fiscal year-end divided by ordinary shares outstanding
at that date. The audited reported value is used when its share basis is clear;
otherwise it is calculated from the audited statement.

Return on assets (ROA), in percent, is profit after tax divided by average
total assets (the arithmetic mean of opening and closing total assets),
multiplied by 100. The numerator and both asset balances are recorded for each
bank-year. In a merger transition year, the reporting entity and any
restatement of opening assets are recorded from the audited financial
statements; a value is treated as unavailable if the numerator and denominator
cannot be matched to the same reporting boundary. All explanatory variables use
these definitions; reported ratios using different definitions are retained only
as source checks and are not substituted into the analysis.

\subsection{Methods of analysis}
The analysis describes the observations and estimates the specified
associations. Because the sample contains repeated observations for only five
bank series, the regression and its conventional significance tests are treated
as exploratory rather than confirmatory.

To examine the joint and conditional associations of all four accounting
fundamentals with post-disclosure market price per share, the following multiple
linear regression model is specified:
\begin{equation}
\mathit{MPS}^{post}_{it} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \mathit{EPS}_{it} + \beta_2 \mathit{DPS}_{it} + \beta_3 \mathit{BVPS}_{it} + \beta_4 \mathit{ROA}_{it} + \varepsilon_{it}
\label{eq:main}
\end{equation}
where $\mathit{MPS}^{post}_{it}$ is the closing price for bank series $i$ in
fiscal year $t$, recorded on the pre-specified post-disclosure trading date.
$\mathit{EPS}_{it}$, $\mathit{DPS}_{it}$, $\mathit{BVPS}_{it}$, and
$\mathit{ROA}_{it}$ denote earnings per share, dividend per share, book value
per share, and return on assets, respectively. $\beta_0$ is the intercept;
$\beta_1$ through $\beta_4$ are slope coefficients for the conditional
associations, holding the other included variables constant.
$\varepsilon_{it}$ is the stochastic disturbance term.

The empirical analysis follows this protocol. Data suitability and model
assumptions are assessed before coefficient tests are interpreted:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Descriptive Statistics:} The mean, standard deviation, minimum,
    and maximum of all variables are reported by bank series and fiscal year.
    This presentation makes merger-linked reporting boundaries visible rather
    than treating observations as one unchanged institution.
  \item \textbf{Correlation Analysis:} Pearson correlations measure bivariate
    associations between each fundamental and $\mathit{MPS}^{post}$ and screen
    for strong pairwise associations among regressors.
  \item \textbf{Multiple Regression Analysis:} Eq.~\ref{eq:main} is estimated
    by pooled OLS as an exploratory baseline. The coefficient of determination
    ($R^2$) and adjusted $R^2$ describe in-sample fit. Individual coefficients
    are reported with two-tailed $t$-tests at the 5\% level ($\alpha=0.05$),
    and joint model significance is reported with an $F$-test at the 5\% level.
    These conventional tests are nominal because they do not adjust for
    possible within-series dependence. With only five series, bank-clustered
    standard errors are not used as an automatic correction. Coefficient signs,
    confidence intervals, and p-values are reported as exploratory summaries.
    The nominal tests do not establish causal effects, stable relationships, or
    sector-wide findings; hypotheses are not accepted or rejected as
    confirmatory conclusions. Prices are observed on bank-specific dates, so
    unmodeled market-wide conditions at those dates may also contribute to the
    estimated associations.
  \item \textbf{Diagnostic Tests:} Model assumptions are examined before
    interpreting results. Multicollinearity is evaluated with variance inflation
    factors (VIF) and tolerance statistics alongside the correlation matrix.
    Residual diagnostics include checks for heteroskedasticity (White or
    Breusch-Pagan test), influential observations (Cook's distance), and
    residual distribution (Q-Q plot). Residual patterns are inspected by bank
    series and fiscal year because repeated observations may be dependent. With
    only five series, cluster-based inference relies on very few clusters and is
    not treated as an automatic remedy \citep{cameron2008clusters}. These
    diagnostics can identify warning signs but cannot establish independence or
    validate inference for this short, merger-affected dataset. The small
    sample, transition-year reporting boundaries, and limits of conventional
    OLS inference are stated with the results. If data checks
    or diagnostics identify unresolved comparability, multicollinearity,
    heteroskedasticity, or influential-observation concerns, the affected
    coefficient tests will be omitted and the estimates will be presented
    descriptively with the concern stated.
\end{enumerate}

Data organization, coding, descriptive statistics, correlations, pooled OLS,
and the specified diagnostic checks are conducted using Microsoft Excel and the
Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS).
