\section{Research methodology}

\subsection{Research design}
This study adopts a quantitative, correlational, and comparative panel research design using secondary financial and market data \citep{pant2016}. The design is aligned with the research questions and objectives: it describes observed accounting fundamentals and share prices, estimates baseline value-relevance associations, and evaluates whether these associations differed before and after the banking consolidation wave. Because the researcher does not manipulate variables or assign institutions to treatments, the analysis is non-experimental and observational.

Descriptive statistics summarize earnings per share (EPS), book value per share (BVPS), supplementary variables (ROA, DPS), and the standardized reporting-deadline market price ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$) by bank series and fiscal year. Pearson correlation coefficients describe bivariate associations and screen for pairwise collinearity. The core empirical analysis employs a two-way fixed-effects panel regression framework, estimating a baseline value-relevance model (Model~1) and a consolidation-period interaction model (Model~2) with bank fixed effects ($\mu_i$) and fiscal-year fixed effects ($\lambda_t$).

The study targets 30 bank-year observations comprising five purposively selected
commercial bank series observed over six fiscal years, FY 2076/77--2081/82. The
preliminary price-availability screen found audited annual reports for all 30
bank-years, but trading suspensions during corporate restructuring make two
pre-consolidation prices unavailable within the defined freshness window: HBL FY
2077/78 (trading suspended until 6 June 2022) and NIMB FY 2077/78 (trading
suspended until 30 May 2022). The provisional estimation sample is therefore 28
bank-year observations: 13 classified as pre-consolidation (FY 2076/77--2078/79)
and 15 as post-consolidation (FY 2079/80--2081/82). The final sample size will be
confirmed after the planned source and data audit. Four of the five
sampled bank series crossed a merger boundary during FY 2079/80 (early 2023),
shifting reporting boundaries from the original acquiring entities to the
consolidated institutions. Following recent panel research on Nepalese commercial
banks \citep{gurungdahal2025}, fixed effects are used to account for
unobserved time-invariant bank heterogeneity. In this study, the interaction
coefficients estimate differences in accounting-price associations across
calendar periods rather than identifying causal effects of mergers.

Before final estimation, a structured bank-by-year source and data audit will
document the audited report, the Chaitra-end operationalization of the statutory
reporting deadline, the first eligible trading session on or after that target
within the five-calendar-day window, its ShareSansar-derived reported close,
the source for each price, the accounting measures, corporate actions affecting
share bases, and the status of any cash dividend recommendation. The audited
report's public-release date and supporting source will also be recorded where
verifiable. A preliminary screen
does not establish that every report was publicly available by the statutory
deadline; the planned audit will document any such uncertainty. Unverifiable or
trading-suspended prices will be excluded rather than replaced with stale prices.
Given the small provisional sample, inference will be treated as exploratory,
with estimation uncertainty and residual degrees of freedom reported.

\subsection{Population and sampling procedure}
The study's sampling frame is Nepalese commercial banks with listed ordinary
shares and usable annual-report and price records for FY 2076/77--2081/82. NRB's
mid-October 2023 directory listed 20 Class A commercial banks after the
consolidation wave; this is a historical sector snapshot, not the sample size
or a claim about the current bank count \citep{nrb2023list}. Five bank series
are selected purposively to cover four bank series involved in the major
early-2023 consolidations and one series not involved in that wave. The
resulting sample is designed for a
focused, exploratory comparison and is not statistically representative of
the full sector.

The specific selection criteria for the sampled banks are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item Commercial bank series involved in the major early-2023 consolidation wave;
  \item Inclusion of a comparison series not involved in the early-2023 merger wave; and
  \item Availability of audited annual reports for the six fiscal years FY 2076/77--2081/82 and market-price records that can be assessed under the five-calendar-day reporting-deadline window.
\end{enumerate}

The five purposively selected commercial bank series, identified by their current corporate names, are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Global IME Bank Limited (GBIME):} Absorbed Bank of Kathmandu Limited; joint operations commenced on 9 January 2023 (swap ratio 1:1) \citep{gbime2022agm,nrb2023list}.
  \item \textbf{Himalayan Bank Limited (HBL):} Acquired Civil Bank Limited; joint operations commenced on 24 February 2023 (swap ratio 100:80.28) \citep{hbl2023report}.
  \item \textbf{Kumari Bank Limited (KBL):} Absorbed Nepal Credit and Commerce Bank Limited; joint operations commenced on 1 January 2023 (swap ratio 1:1) \citep{kumari2022sgm,nrb2023list}.
  \item \textbf{Nepal Investment Mega Bank Limited (NIMB):} Amalgamation of Nepal Investment Bank Limited and Mega Bank Nepal Limited; joint operations commenced on 11 January 2023 (swap ratio 100:90) \citep{nimb2023report}.
  \item \textbf{NMB Bank Limited (NMB):} Did not participate in the early-2023 merger wave and is used as a comparison series, not as an untreated control. NMB commenced unified operations with Kanchan Development Bank on 23 August 2020. Accordingly, FY 2076/77 fundamentals precede that acquisition while the April 2021 price follows it; this is an additional entity-timing mismatch and will be examined by excluding the affected NMB bank-year in a sensitivity analysis \citep{nmb2021report}.
\end{enumerate}

For the four early-2023 merger series, observations before the mergers refer to the acquiring institutions and later observations refer to the combined entities. FY 2079/80 is treated as the merger-transition year; FY 2078/79 accounting measures for those four banks are paired with April 2023 prices observed after their mergers. Primary EPS values follow the current year's audited basic EPS; BVPS is recomputed from year-end ordinary equity and shares outstanding, with reported NAV used as a reconciliation check. Retrospectively restated EPS and bonus-adjusted BVPS are used only in separately identified sensitivity analyses. Findings are specific to these selected series.

\subsection{Nature and sources of data collection}
This study uses secondary financial and market data. Accounting data are
extracted from the banks' published audited reports. Market-price observations
come from a ShareSansar-derived daily-price dataset and are checked, where
available, against exact-date records from other aggregators. These checks do
not establish that the observations are official NEPSE closing prices.

\textbf{Accounting source documents:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Audited annual financial statements (Statements of Financial Position, Statements of Profit or Loss, and Statements of Changes in Equity) from the five sampled banks' published annual reports.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Market-price sources and verification:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item ShareSansar-derived daily records supply the reported close values \citep{sharesansar2026}. Exact-date Merolagani checks match 29 of the 30 candidate values by LTP; for NMB FY 2076/77 on 13 April 2021, the dataset/ShareSansar close is NPR 437.00 and Merolagani reports LTP NPR 436.00 \citep{merolagani2026}. The ShareSansar check is not independent of the dataset source.
  \item FinancialNotices reports the same Close values for all five FY 2081/82 observations \citep{financialnotices2026}. No primary NEPSE close record has been independently verified for the complete sample.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Contextual and regulatory sources:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB) publications, directives, and licensed institution directories;
  \item Securities Board of Nepal (SEBON) annual reports and capital market reviews; and
  \item Ministry of Finance (MoF), Government of Nepal, \textit{Economic Survey} reports.
\end{itemize}
Accounting figures are checked against the source banks' audited reports. Market-price source differences are retained and disclosed; accounting-document checks do not resolve price-source discrepancies.

\subsection{Research framework and definition of variables}
The conceptual research framework guiding this study is rooted in the accounting value-relevance model of \citet{ohlson1995} and the post-merger valuation framework of \citet{kwon2020}. It is depicted in Figure~\ref{fig:framework}. Market price per share at the reporting-deadline window ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$) is the dependent variable. Earnings per share (EPS) and book value per share (BVPS) are the primary independent variables. The consolidation period indicator ($\mathit{Post}_t$) serves as an interaction variable to examine whether the accounting-price slope coefficients differed across the consolidation period. Bank fixed effects ($\mu_i$) and fiscal-year fixed effects ($\lambda_t$) control for unobserved time-invariant bank characteristics and common macroeconomic shocks.

The operational definitions of all variables are as follows:

\textbf{Market Price per Share in the Reporting-Deadline Window ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$):} The dependent variable, measured in Nepalese Rupees (NPR), is the ShareSansar-derived reported close for ordinary shares on the first eligible trading date on or after Chaitra-end, the study's operationalization of the statutory nine-month audited-reporting deadline in Section 59(2) of the Banks and Financial Institutions Act, 2017 \citep{bafia2017}. The eligible observation must fall within five calendar days of the Chaitra-end target; if no eligible trade is recorded in that window, the observation is coded missing rather than carried forward. This is a standardized deadline-window proxy, not a price tied to each bank's actual disclosure date or an event-time market response. The legal requirement does not establish that each bank published its audited statements by that date; actual release dates and sources will be recorded where verifiable, and uncertain timing will limit interpretation as value relevance. Price-source checks and their limits are described above and in Table~\ref{tab:mps_rd}. Two candidate observations fail the freshness rule after merger-related suspensions: HBL FY 2077/78 (trading resumed 6 June 2022, 54 days after the target) and NIMB FY 2077/78 (30 May 2022, 47 days after the target). The preliminary screen therefore leaves 28 candidate observations, subject to the planned final audit.

\textbf{Earnings per Share (EPS):} Basic earnings per share, in NPR per ordinary share, extracted from each fiscal year's own audited Statement of Profit or Loss (the own-current reported basic EPS rule). EPS reflects net profit attributable to ordinary equity holders divided by the weighted-average number of ordinary shares outstanding during the fiscal year. Retrospective audited restatements reported in subsequent annual reports are retained for sensitivity analysis rather than overwriting original audited figures.

\textbf{Book Value per Share (BVPS):} Net worth per ordinary share, in NPR, calculated as total ordinary shareholders' equity attributable to equity holders of the bank at fiscal year-end divided by the number of ordinary shares outstanding at that date, recomputed from balance-sheet components with printed Net Asset Value (NAV) per share used as an audit reconciliation check.

\textbf{Consolidation Period Indicator ($\mathit{Post}_t$):} A binary temporal
indicator set to $1$ for observations belonging to the post-consolidation period
(FY 2079/80, 2080/81, and 2081/82) and $0$ for observations in the
pre-consolidation period (FY 2076/77, 2077/78, and 2078/79). Classification is
by the fiscal year of the accounting measures, not by the market-price
observation date. Thus FY 2078/79 remains in the pre-consolidation baseline even
though its April 2023 price observations followed the early-2023 mergers; this
entity-timing mismatch is assessed separately in the sensitivity analysis. FY
2079/80 is treated as the merger-transition fiscal year and is excluded in a
separate sensitivity analysis. The indicator interacts with EPS and BVPS to
test for slope differences across the specified periods.

\textbf{Supplementary / Robustness Variables:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textit{Return on Assets (ROA):} Profit after tax divided by average total assets, expressed as a percentage. Used in robustness checks as an alternative measure of operating profitability. Because EPS and ROA share an extremely high bivariate correlation ($r \approx 0.975$ on the clean sample), ROA is evaluated as an alternative substitute rather than an additive regressor.
  \item \textit{Dividend per Share (DPS):} Cash dividend per ordinary share, in NPR, recommended for the corresponding fiscal year that was publicly declared on or prior to the $\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$ observation date. Documented zero-dividend decisions are recorded as zero; unannounced or unverifiable recommendations are treated as missing.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Exclusion of Price-Derived Ratios:} The price-earnings (P/E) ratio is excluded from all specifications because $\text{P/E}=\text{MPS}/\text{EPS}$. Since it contains the dependent variable in its numerator, its inclusion would create direct mathematical overlap and make its coefficient difficult to interpret as the separate association of an accounting measure with price.

\subsection{Methods of analysis}
Data analysis is structured to describe the properties of the data, assess econometric assumptions, and test the research hypotheses using panel regression methods.

\subsubsection{Model Specification}
To evaluate the baseline value relevance of accounting fundamentals, Model~1 specifies a two-way fixed-effects panel regression:
\begin{equation}
\mathit{MPS}^{RD}_{it} = \alpha + \mu_i + \lambda_t + \beta_E \mathit{EPS}_{it} + \beta_B \mathit{BVPS}_{it} + \varepsilon_{it}
\label{eq:model1}
\end{equation}
where $i$ indexes the five commercial banks ($i = 1, \dots, 5$), $t$ indexes the six fiscal years ($t = 1, \dots, 6$), $\mu_i$ represents unobserved time-invariant bank fixed effects, $\lambda_t$ represents fiscal-year fixed effects, and $\varepsilon_{it}$ is the idiosyncratic disturbance term. The coefficients $\beta_E$ and $\beta_B$ capture the conditional associations of EPS and BVPS with the reporting-deadline market price.

To investigate whether the value relevance of accounting fundamentals differed before and after the consolidation wave, Model~2 introduces interaction terms with the consolidation indicator $\mathit{Post}_t$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathit{MPS}^{RD}_{it} ={}& \alpha + \mu_i + \lambda_t + \beta_E \mathit{EPS}_{it} + \beta_B \mathit{BVPS}_{it} \\
& + \delta_E (\mathit{EPS}_{it} \times \mathit{Post}_t) + \delta_B (\mathit{BVPS}_{it} \times \mathit{Post}_t) + \varepsilon_{it}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:model2}
\end{equation}
Because the specification includes a full set of fiscal-year fixed effects ($\lambda_t$), the standalone $\mathit{Post}_t$ indicator equals the sum of the fiscal-year indicator variables for FY 2079/80, FY 2080/81, and FY 2081/82. It is therefore perfectly collinear with the year fixed effects, is absorbed by them, and is not estimated separately.

For Model~2, the baseline valuation slopes during the pre-consolidation period are given by $\beta_E$ and $\beta_B$. The interaction coefficients $\delta_E$ and $\delta_B$ measure incremental slope shifts between the pre- and post-consolidation periods. Consequently, the post-consolidation slope for EPS is $(\beta_E + \delta_E)$, while for BVPS it is $(\beta_B + \delta_B)$.

\subsubsection{Hypothesis Testing and Robustness Protocol}
The analytical protocol proceeds through the following sequential steps:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Descriptive and Bivariate Analysis:} Mean, standard deviation, minimum, and maximum are computed for each variable across bank series and fiscal years. Pearson correlation coefficients and scatter matrices screen for bivariate associations and severe pairwise collinearity.
  \item \textbf{Estimation of Baseline Model (Model~1):} Evaluates Hypotheses~H1 ($\beta_E \ne 0$) and H2 ($\beta_B \ne 0$) using two-tailed tests at the 5\% significance level ($\alpha = 0.05$). Parameter estimates, standard errors, and 95\% confidence intervals are reported.
  \item \textbf{Estimation of Interaction Model (Model~2):} Evaluates H3 ($\delta_E \ne 0$) and H4 ($\delta_B \ne 0$) as two-sided coefficient tests at $\alpha = 0.05$, reporting estimates, standard errors, and 95\% confidence intervals. The primary omnibus test evaluates the joint null hypothesis:
  \begin{equation}
  H_0: \delta_E = \delta_B = 0
  \end{equation}
  using an HC3-based joint Wald $F$-test at the 5\% significance level. Rejection of $H_0$ provides evidence that at least one estimated accounting-price slope differs across the specified periods; it does not establish that consolidation caused the difference.
  \item \textbf{Robustness and Sensitivity Checks:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{ROA Specification:} EPS is replaced with ROA in Models~1 and 2 to evaluate sensitivity to an asset-scaled profitability measure.
    \item \textit{Dividend Specification:} In supplementary estimation, eligible DPS is included as an explanatory variable in Models~1 and 2 for bank-years where dividend declarations preceded the price window. On the same $N = 28$ observations, adding DPS would add one parameter and reduce Model~2's residual degrees of freedom from 14 to 13. Unavailable or unverifiable eligible DPS may further reduce the estimation sample.
    \item \textit{Transition and Entity-Timing Sensitivity:} Model~2 is re-estimated excluding (a) FY 2079/80 to assess sensitivity to the merger-transition year; (b) FY 2078/79, when the four merged banks' pre-merger fundamentals are paired with April 2023 prices after their mergers; and (c) NMB FY 2076/77, when pre-Kanchan fundamentals are paired with a post-acquisition April 2021 price. These exclusions are evaluated separately because simultaneous exclusions would further reduce an already small sample.
    \item \textit{Bonus Share Adjustment:} Model~2 is re-estimated with bonus-adjusted BVPS as a sensitivity check; the primary EPS series is unchanged.
    \item \textit{Restated EPS Sensitivity:} Model~2 is re-estimated using subsequent audited restatements of EPS where reported, using the restated values as published rather than applying a mechanical adjustment rule.
  \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Econometric Diagnostics and Inferential Caveats:}
  Diagnostic checks include Variance Inflation Factors (VIF) and tolerance statistics for multicollinearity, the Breusch-Pagan/Cook-Weisberg test for heteroskedasticity, Cook's distance for influential outliers, and Q-Q plots for residual normality.
  
  \begin{sloppypar}
  If the provisional $N = 28$ sample is confirmed, Model~2 estimates 14 parameters (one constant, 4 bank fixed effects, 5 year fixed effects, and 4 slope coefficients), leaving $28 - 14 = 14$ residual degrees of freedom. Cluster-robust inference is unreliable with so few bank clusters ($G = 5$) \citep{cameron2008clusters}. HC3 standard errors are used for heteroskedasticity; they do not account for arbitrary within-bank dependence over time and are not a correction for the small number of clusters. Accordingly, the tests are exploratory, and results are interpreted as differences in association across periods rather than causal merger effects.
  \end{sloppypar}
  
  Additionally, because fiscal-year fixed effects absorb the market-wide price level (macroeconomic trends and NEPSE index movements), slope parameters are identified through within-year cross-sectional variation among the sampled banks. To ensure full transparency regarding specification sensitivity, baseline two-way fixed-effects estimates are compared against pooled OLS and bank-only fixed-effects models.
\end{enumerate}

Data organization, audit cross-checking, and variable coding will be performed in Python (\textit{pandas}, \textit{numpy}). Econometric modeling, diagnostic testing, robust HC3 covariance estimation, and visualization will be executed using Python (\textit{statsmodels}, \textit{seaborn}, \textit{matplotlib}).
