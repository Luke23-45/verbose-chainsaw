\frontPageTitle{Abstract}

\vspace{0.1cm}

\setstretch{1.5}
\setlength{\parskip}{2pt}
This dissertation investigates the value relevance of financial accounting information, specifically earnings per share (EPS) and book value per share (BVPS), among Nepalese commercial banks, and evaluates whether these valuation relationships underwent a structural regime shift following the commercial banking consolidation wave of early 2023. Grounded in the residual income valuation (RIV) framework of Ohlson (1995) and clean-surplus accounting theory, the study addresses methodological limitations in prior Nepalese literature by eliminating circular price-to-earnings (P/E) regressors, reducing look-ahead bias via on-or-after-deadline prices under Section 59(2) of BAFIA ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$), and controlling for bank and temporal heterogeneity. Actual publication dates were verified in only 2 of 30 bank-years. A panel of five commercial banks is examined across six fiscal years (FY 2076/77--FY 2081/82, $N = 28$ clean bank-years under a 5-day freshness rule) using two-way fixed effects regressions with MacKinnon-White HC3 robust standard errors on Student's $t$ residual degrees of freedom.

In the baseline specification (Model 1), both earnings ($\hat{\beta}_E = 2.095, p = 0.616$) and book value ($\hat{\beta}_B = 0.219, p = 0.908$) exhibit positive but statistically insignificant slopes ($R^2 = 0.948$), with bank and year effects absorbing the cross-bank and cross-year variation. In the regime shift specification (Model 2), the pre-consolidation earnings slope was positive but insignificant ($\hat{\beta}_E = 5.577, p = 0.140$), while post-consolidation interactions compressed implied slopes to $2.453$ for EPS ($p = 0.298$) and $-1.658$ for BVPS ($p = 0.161$), neither statistically significant. The baseline joint Wald test fails to reject structural stability at the 5\% level under the pre-specified HC3 covariance ($F(2, 14) = 2.954, p = 0.085$), a result specific to that estimator. However, purging the entity-timing asymmetry in Specification R6 ($N = 23$) yields a statistically significant joint test ($F(2, 10) = 5.82, p = 0.021$ unadjusted, with Holm-adjusted $p = 0.190$ across nine tests). The evidence demonstrates that the regime shift is sensitivity-dependent rather than conclusive in the baseline, with post-merger capital dilution coinciding with compressed implied slopes. The study provides policy implications for regulatory asset quality reviews, trading halt limits, and centralized electronic corporate filings.
