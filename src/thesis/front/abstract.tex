\vspace{0.8cm}

\setstretch{1.5}
This dissertation investigates the value relevance of financial accounting information—specifically earnings per share (EPS) and book value per share (BVPS)—among prominent Nepalese commercial banks, and evaluates whether the valuation relationships underwent a structural regime shift across the landmark horizontal banking consolidation wave executed in early 2023 (mid-FY 2079/80). Grounded in the residual income valuation (RIV) framework of Ohlson (1995) and clean-surplus accounting theory, the study addresses critical methodological deficiencies in prior Nepalese valuation literature, including the circular inclusion of endogenous Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratios, look-ahead bias from fiscal-year-end pricing, and small-cluster downward bias. 

To ensure strict temporal causality, equity prices are measured immediately following the nine-month statutory annual reporting deadline operationalized at the end of Chaitra (mid-April) pursuant to Section 59(2) of the Banks and Financial Institutions Act, 2017 ($MPS^{RD}$). A purposive panel of five commercial banking series is examined across six fiscal years (FY 2076/77 through FY 2081/82): four merged institutions representing the early-2023 consolidation wave (GBIME, HBL, KBL, and NIMB) and one unmerged peer (NMB). Applying a 5-day freshness rule yields 28 realized bank-year observations (13 pre-consolidation, 15 post-consolidation); two observations (HBL and NIMB FY 2077/78) were excluded due to prolonged merger-related trading suspensions. Econometric estimation employs two-way fixed effects (absorbing unobserved bank heterogeneity and macro-year price movements) with MacKinnon-White HC3 robust covariance inference.

Descriptive statistics document a 33.80\% collapse in mean share price, a 40.11\% contraction in mean EPS, and a 76.90\% drop in cash dividends post-consolidation, while book value remained stable (+2.44\%). In the baseline model (Model~1), both fundamentals exhibit positive but statistically insignificant slopes ($\hat{\beta}_E = 2.095, p = 0.616; \hat{\beta}_B = 0.219, p = 0.908; R^2 = 0.948; \text{residual df} = 16$), demonstrating that macro-temporal fixed effects absorb market-wide valuation trends. In the interactive specification (Model~2), pre-consolidation pricing was driven primarily by earnings ($\hat{\beta}_E = 5.577, p = 0.140; \hat{\beta}_B = 0.100, p = 0.940$), but post-consolidation interactions shifted downward ($\hat{\delta}_E = -3.124, p = 0.448; \hat{\delta}_B = -1.758, p = 0.175$), compressing implied post-consolidation multiples to $2.453$ for EPS and $-1.658$ for BVPS. The principal joint Wald test indicates marginal significance at the 10\% level ($F(2, 14) = 2.954, p = 0.085$). 

Crucially, an eight-part robustness suite demonstrates that when the entity-mismatch year FY 2078/79 is excluded (Specification R6, $N = 23$), the joint regime shift test becomes \textbf{statistically significant at the 5\% level} ($F(2, 10) = 5.82, p = 0.021$), indicating a statistically significant structural difference in the valuation function once entity-timing distortions are eliminated. The findings reveal that post-merger credit-loss provisioning pressures and capital dilution coincided with compressed valuation multiples, while book value did not serve as an asset anchor. The study offers actionable recommendations for NRB, SEBON, bank executives, and investors regarding post-merger asset quality reviews, trading suspension reforms, and digital corporate disclosures.
