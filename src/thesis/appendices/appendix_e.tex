\chapter{Detailed Estimation Outputs}
\label{app:estimation_outputs}

This appendix prints the full estimation outputs behind Chapter IV. It adds no new specification and no new interpretation. Chapter III states the estimators and the fixed-effects structure in narrative form. Chapter IV reports the headline coefficients and the hypothesis decisions. This appendix prints every baseline coefficient, including every fixed-effect dummy, with HC3 standard errors, t-statistics, exact p-values, and 95 percent confidence intervals, together with the joint fixed-effect tests, the full eight-specification robustness suite including its fixed-effect dummies and joint tests, and the diagnostics and sensitivity checks. Estimates are computed from the stored analysis panel, not from the rounded print in Appendix B. Chapter IV prints the same headline coefficients to three decimals, so a last-digit difference between this appendix and Chapter IV is rounding.

\section{Estimation Audit}

The estimation sample is the 28-row sample built in Appendix D, Section D.5. Thirty bank-years were planned. Two are window-excluded: HBL FY 2077/78 and NIMB FY 2077/78, whose first valid prices fall 54 and 47 days after the reporting deadline, outside the five-day window. No other observation is excluded, and no value is imputed.

Parameters and residual degrees of freedom follow the dummy-variable design. Model 1 has 12 parameters: an intercept, 2 slopes, 4 bank dummies, and 5 year dummies, leaving 16 residual degrees of freedom. Model 2 adds the 2 regime-shift interactions for 14 parameters and 14 residual degrees of freedom. R4 adds cash DPS for 15 parameters and 13 degrees of freedom. R5 and R6 each drop one fiscal year and its dummy, giving 13 parameters and 10 degrees of freedom. All other robustness specifications carry 14 parameters and 14 degrees of freedom.

The reference bank is GBIME and the reference fiscal year is FY 2076/77, so the intercept is the GBIME FY 2076/77 baseline and each dummy measures a deviation from it. Estimation is ordinary least squares with two-way bank and fiscal-year fixed effects and MacKinnon-White HC3 robust standard errors. Test statistics use Student's t distribution on the residual degrees of freedom, and joint tests use the Wald F statistic under the HC3 covariance matrix. Within-R², printed in the baseline table notes, is the share of bank-and-year-adjusted variation explained by the slopes, where the adjusted variation is the residual variation left after the bank and year dummies alone.

Software and package versions are stated in Appendix D, Section D.6. No code is shared with this thesis, so this appendix is an audit record, not a replication package: it prints the full baseline and robustness estimates a reader needs to check the headline results, but it does not by itself rerun them. Precision follows the table notes: estimates, standard errors, and confidence limits to four decimals, t-statistics to two decimals, p-values to three decimals, and fit statistics to three decimals.

\section{Baseline Estimates}

Tables~\ref{tab:app_m1full} and~\ref{tab:app_m2full} print the full baseline estimates. Table~\ref{tab:app_fetests} prints the joint fixed-effect tests. The bank dummies are not significant at the 5 percent level in either model (Model 1 p = 0.280, Model 2 p = 0.080), while the year dummies are jointly significant in both models (p < 0.001) and all fixed effects jointly are significant in both models (p < 0.001). A year dummy measures the common price shift of its fiscal year relative to FY 2076/77 after accounting for EPS and BVPS, and a bank dummy measures the bank's average deviation from GBIME on the same basis.

\section{Diagnostics and Sensitivity}

Diagnostics for the baseline value-relevance model are summarized in Chapter IV, Table~\ref{tab:t6_diagnostics}: variance inflation factors of 1.16 for EPS and BVPS, a Breusch-Pagan LM statistic of 14.37 (p = 0.213), and a maximum Cook's distance of 0.552 (HBL FY 2076/77). Table~\ref{tab:app_flagged} lists all five observations that exceed the $4/N = 0.143$ flagging threshold in Model 1. Table~\ref{tab:app_m2flagged} lists the five observations that exceed the same threshold in Model 2; the largest is KBL FY 2076/77 at 0.4902, and the Breusch-Pagan LM statistic on Model 2 residuals is 14.54 on 13 degrees of freedom ($p = 0.337$).

Collinearity in Model 2. Variance inflation factors computed on the substantive regressors by the Chapter III method, auxiliary regressions on the slopes alone without fixed effects, are EPS 10.33, BVPS 2.28, $\delta_E$ 15.15, and $\delta_B$ 24.13. Three exceed the Chapter III screening threshold of 5. This collinearity is largely mechanical: each interaction equals its level variable in post-consolidation years and zero before, so the interactions necessarily correlate with their levels. It does not bias the coefficients, but it widens their standard errors, which the HC3 intervals already reflect, and it is why the regime-shift inference rests on the joint Wald test in Table~\ref{tab:app_robustfull} ($F = 2.954$, $p = 0.085$) rather than on the individual interaction t-statistics.

Table~\ref{tab:app_sensitivity} re-estimates Model 2 without each bank series in turn. Table~\ref{tab:app_loy} re-estimates Model 2 without each fiscal year in turn. The headline interactions keep their signs under every single-row deletion and every year deletion. Across bank deletions, only KBL reverses the earnings interaction. The joint test crosses the 5 percent level when NIMB FY 2078/79 is deleted ($p = 0.035$).
