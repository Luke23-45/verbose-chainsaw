\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:lit_intro}

The purpose of this chapter is to conduct a structured review of the theoretical, regulatory, and empirical literature pertinent to the valuation of equity securities and the value relevance of financial accounting information, with specific focus on commercial banking institutions undergoing structural consolidation.

This chapter establishes a direct link with the research problem and objectives articulated in Chapter~I. It is organized into four major substantive sections:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Section~\ref{sec:theoretical_review}: Theoretical Review} examines the foundational economic and financial accounting theories that govern equity valuation, residual income dynamics, post-merger information environments, market efficiency, signaling, agency conflicts, and the econometric hazards of price-derived valuation multiples.
\item \textbf{Section~\ref{sec:regulatory_framework}: Review of Legal, Regulatory, and Policy Framework in Nepal} reviews the institutional architecture governing the Nepalese banking sector and capital market, including the regulatory mandates of Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB), the statutory reporting obligations under the Banks and Financial Institutions Act (BAFIA), 2017, and the policy evolution of bank merger directives and capital expansion mandates.
\item \textbf{Section~\ref{sec:empirical_review}: Empirical Review} provides a comparative analysis of prior empirical studies, divided into international investigations across developed and emerging capital markets, foundational Nepalese banking studies, recent investigations of share price determinants (2021--2024), and contemporary literature spanning the early-2023 consolidation wave (2024--2026), synthesized through a detailed summary matrix.
\item \textbf{Section~\ref{sec:research_gap}: Research Gap} synthesizes the methodological, econometric, and conceptual shortcomings of the existing literature and explicitly articulates how the present dissertation addresses each identified gap.
\end{itemize}

\section{Theoretical Review}
\label{sec:theoretical_review}

\subsection{The Residual Income Valuation (RIV) Framework}
\label{subsec:ohlson_framework}

In financial economics and accounting theory, equity valuation has evolved from dividend-discounting formulations into models grounded in fundamental accounting numbers. The seminal theoretical breakthrough linking financial accounting disclosures directly to equity market values was established by \citet{ohlson1995} and extended by \citet{feltham1995}. The Residual Income Valuation (RIV) framework, often designated as the Edwards-Bell-Ohlson (EBO) model, reconceptualizes firm value by expressing the market value of common equity as the sum of currently recognized book value of equity and the present value of expected future residual income (abnormal earnings).

The mathematical foundation of the Ohlson framework rests on four interrelated structural building blocks:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The Present Value of Expected Dividends (PVED):}
Consistent with neoclassical asset pricing theory, the intrinsic market value of a firm's common equity at date $t$ ($P_t$) equals the discounted present value of all expected future net cash distributions (dividends, $D_{t+\tau}$) paid to equity holders:
\begin{equation}
P_t = \sum_{\tau=1}^{\infty} \frac{\mathbb{E}_t[D_{t+\tau}]}{(1 + r)^\tau}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbb{E}_t[\cdot]$ denotes the expectations operator conditioned on information available at date $t$, and $r$ represents the constant cost of equity capital (assumed strictly positive).

\item \textbf{The Clean Surplus Relation (CSR):}
The Ohlson model imposes the fundamental accounting condition of clean surplus accounting. Under CSR, all changes in the book value of equity ($BV_t$) between dates $t-1$ and $t$ must flow through the income statement as net income ($NI_t$), with the sole exception of transactions with equity holders in their capacity as owners (dividends paid, $D_t$, and capital contributions/withdrawals):
\begin{equation}
BV_t = BV_{t-1} + NI_t - D_t
\end{equation}
or equivalently:
\begin{equation}
D_t = NI_t - (BV_t - BV_{t-1})
\end{equation}
The clean surplus relation ensures accounting consistency by preventing firms from bypassing the statement of profit or loss through direct debits or credits to equity reserves.

\item \textbf{Residual Income (Abnormal Earnings) Formulation:}
Residual income (or abnormal earnings, denoted $NI_t^a$) represents the net income generated by the firm in excess of the normal return required on the beginning-of-period book equity capital:
\begin{equation}
NI_t^a = NI_t - r \cdot BV_{t-1}
\end{equation}
By substituting the clean surplus expression for dividends into the dividend-discount model, and assuming the standard regularity transversality condition that the discounted expected book value of equity approaches zero as the horizon approaches infinity ($\lim_{\tau \to \infty} (1+r)^{-\tau} \mathbb{E}_t[BV_{t+\tau}] = 0$), \citet{ohlson1995} demonstrates that the market value of equity can be expressed identically as:
\begin{equation}
P_t = BV_t + \sum_{\tau=1}^{\infty} \frac{\mathbb{E}_t[NI_{t+\tau}^a]}{(1 + r)^\tau} = BV_t + \sum_{\tau=1}^{\infty} \frac{\mathbb{E}_t[NI_{t+\tau} - r \cdot BV_{t+\tau-1}]}{(1 + r)^\tau}
\end{equation}

\item \textbf{Linear Information Dynamics and the Theoretical Valuation Equation:}
To translate this infinite-horizon residual income formulation into an empirical valuation equation containing contemporaneous accounting variables, \citet{ohlson1995} introduces linear information dynamics (LID). Specifically, current abnormal earnings ($NI_t^a$) and other non-accounting information ($v_t$) follow a stationary stochastic autoregressive process:
\begin{align}
NI_{t+1}^a &= \omega NI_t^a + v_t + \epsilon_{1,t+1} \\
v_{t+1} &= \gamma v_t + \epsilon_{2,t+1}
\end{align}
where the persistence parameters satisfy $0 \le \omega < 1$ and $0 \le \gamma < 1$, and $\epsilon_{1,t+1}, \epsilon_{2,t+1}$ are mean-zero disturbance terms.

Under these information dynamics, \citet{ohlson1995} demonstrates that the equilibrium market price of equity ($P_t$) solves as a closed-form linear function:
\begin{equation}
P_t = BV_t + \alpha_1 NI_t^a + \alpha_2 v_t
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_1 = \frac{\omega}{R_f - \omega}$, with $R_f = 1 + r$, and $\alpha_2 = \frac{R_f}{(R_f - \omega)(R_f - \gamma)}$.

Because abnormal earnings are defined with respect to beginning-of-period book value ($NI_t^a = NI_t - r \cdot BV_{t-1}$), the clean surplus relation ($BV_t = BV_{t-1} + NI_t - D_t$, or equivalently $BV_{t-1} = BV_t - NI_t + D_t$) can be substituted directly into the abnormal earnings expression:
\begin{equation}
NI_t^a = NI_t - r(BV_t - NI_t + D_t) = (1 + r)NI_t - r \cdot BV_t - r \cdot D_t
\end{equation}
Substituting this into the valuation equation yields:
\begin{equation}
P_t = BV_t + \alpha_1 \left[(1 + r)NI_t - r \cdot BV_t - r \cdot D_t\right] + \alpha_2 v_t
\end{equation}
Collecting terms on contemporaneous book value ($BV_t$) and net income ($NI_t$) yields the canonical structural form:
\begin{equation}
P_t = (1 - k) BV_t + k (\phi \cdot NI_t - D_t) + \alpha_2 v_t
\end{equation}
where $k = r \cdot \alpha_1 = \frac{r \omega}{R_f - \omega}$ and $\phi = \frac{R_f}{r} = \frac{1+r}{r}$.

Hence, the exact theoretical coefficient on contemporaneous book value of equity is:
\begin{equation}
(1 - k) = 1 - \frac{r \omega}{1 + r - \omega} = \frac{(1 + r)(1 - \omega)}{1 + r - \omega} > 0
\end{equation}
and the theoretical coefficient on contemporaneous net income is:
\begin{equation}
k \cdot \phi = \frac{\omega(1 + r)}{1 + r - \omega} \ge 0 \quad (\text{strictly positive for } \omega > 0)
\end{equation}

When moving from this analytical formulation to empirical testing, the accounting value-relevance literature follows \citet{collins1997} by estimating a linear approximation that omits the unobservable information term ($v_t$) and impounds dividend effects into the constant and error term, scaling variables on a per-share basis:
\begin{equation}
P_{it} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \text{EPS}_{it} + \beta_2 \text{BVPS}_{it} + \varepsilon_{it}
\end{equation}
where earnings per share ($\text{EPS}_{it}$) serves as the empirical proxy for contemporaneous earnings (with abnormal earning capacity impounded into the structural parameters), and book value per share ($\text{BVPS}_{it}$) serves as the empirical proxy for cumulative net resources.
\end{enumerate}

The Ohlson framework demonstrates that earnings and book value are not arbitrary ad hoc variables selected for regression convenience. Rather, they serve as theoretically motivated and complementary inputs in equity pricing. Book value represents the accumulated historical stock of equity net assets, providing an asset-backing floor, while earnings capture current operational flows and abnormal earning power. The theoretical coefficients are direct functions of earnings persistence ($\omega$). As earnings persistence increases, the valuation weight placed on earnings expands. Conversely, if earnings become transitory, noisy, or disrupted by non-recurring events, the persistence parameter $\omega$ declines, leading the market to shift its valuation weight toward balance-sheet net worth.

\subsection{Valuation Dynamics Across Corporate Reorganizations and M\&A}
\label{subsec:ma_valuation}

In the extant literature, an accounting amount is defined as value relevant if it has a predicted association with equity market values \citep{barth2001}. This dissertation adopts that definition: earnings per share and book value per share are value relevant to the extent that they predict the market price per share of Nepalese commercial banks. Whether such associations inform standard setting is itself contested: \citet{holthausen2001} conclude that value-relevance research offers little insight for standard setters, while \citet{barth2001} argue that it assesses how well accounting amounts reflect information used by equity investors and provides insights into questions of interest to standard setters.

A central theoretical question in accounting value relevance is whether the valuation multipliers attached to earnings and book values remain constant across major structural corporate reorganizations. In corporate amalgamations and acquisitions, the economic and reporting structure of the enterprise undergoes fundamental reconfiguration.

Investigating the valuation consequences of corporate combinations, \citet{kwon2020} examine changes in the value relevance of accounting information following mergers and acquisitions (M\&A). Drawing on their finding that investors shift valuation weight from earnings to book value after M\&A, the thesis identifies four channels through which corporate combinations alter the reporting environment:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Transitory Integration Costs and Earnings Noise:} Post-merger operational integration requires extensive administrative expenditures, harmonization of operating systems, restructuring of branch networks, and rationalization of redundant human resources. These integration expenses generate substantial transitory, non-recurring accounting charges that temporarily suppress reported net income.
\item \textbf{Accounting Harmonization and Portfolio Revaluation:} Merging disparate corporate entities requires harmonizing accounting policies across previously independent balance sheets. In commercial banking, this entails realigning loan classification standards, reassessing non-performing loans (NPLs), unifying impairment provisioning methodologies under NFRS 9 / IFRS 9, and recognizing purchase price allocation adjustments and goodwill.
\item \textbf{Earnings Persistence Impairment:} Because transitional accounting charges and post-merger adjustments are inherently temporary and do not reflect ongoing operational earning capacity, the time-series persistence of reported earnings ($\omega$) declines sharply in the immediate post-merger period.
\item \textbf{Shift Toward Balance-Sheet Anchoring:} Following the residual income logic of \citet{ohlson1995} and the empirical evidence of \citet{collins1997}, when earnings quality and persistence deteriorate due to non-recurring organizational shocks, rational equity investors discount current earnings as a noisy indicator of future performance. Consequently, market participants place greater valuation weight on the stock of net assets, book value per share (BVPS), which serves as an anchor of fundamental solvency and net worth.
\end{itemize}

\citet{collins1997} similarly demonstrate across a forty-one-year sequence of annual cross-sectional regressions that the relative value relevance of earnings versus book values shifts systematically: the incremental relevance of earnings declines while that of book value increases, driven by higher incidences of negative earnings, non-recurring charges, and intangible asset intensity. These theoretical insights provide the direct intellectual rationale for testing parameter shifts ($\delta_E, \delta_B$) across Nepal's 2023 banking consolidation wave.

\subsection{Neoclassical Valuation Benchmarks: Dividend Capitalization Models}
\label{subsec:dividend_models}

To provide analytical context to accounting valuation, classical financial theory formulated equity pricing around cash dividend distributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The Dividend Irrelevance Proposition:} \citet{miller1961} proved that in a frictionless capital market characterized by zero transaction costs, absence of corporate and personal taxes, symmetric information, and a given corporate investment policy, the dividend payout policy of a firm has no effect on its total market valuation or cost of capital. Firm value is determined solely by its investment decisions and the earning power of its assets. In emerging markets, however, frictionless market assumptions are fundamentally violated by capital market imperfections, asymmetric information, and regulatory mandates.
\item \textbf{Dividend Discount Formulations:} \citet{walter1963} relaxed frictionless assumptions by modelling share price as a function of the internal rate of return on retained earnings ($r_a$) relative to the cost of capital ($k_e$). Walter demonstrated that when $r_a > k_e$ (growth firms), retaining earnings maximizes share value, whereas when $r_a < k_e$, maximizing dividend payout enhances shareholder wealth. \citet{gordon1959} formulated the constant-growth dividend capitalization model:
\begin{equation}
P_0 = \frac{D_1}{k_e - g}
\end{equation}
positing that external investors discount retained earnings more heavily than current dividends due to risk aversion regarding uncertain future capital gains (the ``bird-in-the-hand'' hypothesis).
\end{itemize}

In the Nepalese commercial banking sector, dividend announcements, comprising both cash dividends and capitalized bonus shares, have historically served as important valuation signals. While the baseline valuation model follows \citet{collins1997} in focusing on earnings and book value, evaluating cash dividends (DPS) in an expanded specification serves as an empirical robustness test (Specification R4) to examine whether payout distributions alter the fundamental valuation coefficients.

\subsection{Information Asymmetry, Signaling, and Agency Theories in Banking}
\label{subsec:signaling_agency}

Financial institutions operate in an environment characterized by acute informational friction between bank managers, external equity investors, and regulatory bodies:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Signaling Theory:} \citet{ross1977}, \citet{bhattacharya1979}, and \citet{john1985} demonstrate that in capital markets plagued by information asymmetry, financial disclosures, dividend announcements, and corporate restructuring decisions serve as costly, credible signals conveying management's private expectations regarding future performance. Because weaker firms cannot afford to mimic costly dividend commitments or stringent audited reporting standards without courting financial distress, published audited accounting statements and declared dividends convey authentic fundamental value.
\item \textbf{Agency Theory:} \citet{jensen1976} formulate agency theory, and \citet{jensen1986} formulates the free cash flow theory, positing that corporate managers have incentives to accumulate resources and pursue empire-building acquisitions rather than returning excess capital to shareholders. In commercial banking, declared cash dividend distributions and transparent audited balance sheets mitigate agency conflicts by constraining managerial discretion and subjecting the institution to regular capital-market monitoring.
\item \textbf{Banking Asset Opacity and Prudential Regulation:} \citet{morgan2002} and \citet{flannery2004} establish that commercial banks are inherently more opaque than industrial corporations. Bank assets consist primarily of unquoted private commercial loans whose true quality and credit risk are shielded from public observation. This severe asset opacity makes statutory auditing, capital adequacy compliance, and standardized regulatory reporting deadlines indispensable for credible market valuation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Efficient Market Hypothesis and the Information Content of Disclosures}
\label{subsec:emh_pead}

The Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH), formulated by \citet{fama1970}, posits that asset prices in an efficient financial market fully reflect all available information. Under the semi-strong form of market efficiency, equity share prices adjust rapidly and unbiasedly to all publicly available information, including audited annual financial accounts, earnings releases, and dividend declarations.

Seminal empirical accounting research by \citet{ball1968} and \citet{beaver1968} established that published accounting earnings possess substantial information content: stock returns systematically adjust in the direction of unexpected earnings announcements. However, subsequent empirical research across global equity markets, most notably by \citet{bernard1989}, identified the phenomenon of \textit{post-earnings-announcement drift} (PEAD), wherein equity prices continue to adjust with a temporal lag to published accounting disclosures due to investor underreaction and information processing delays.

In frontier emerging markets such as the Nepal Stock Exchange (NEPSE), market efficiency is further constrained by illiquidity, retail-dominated trading, delayed dissemination of corporate filings, and, based on the disclosures examined for this study, the absence of a unified electronic corporate announcement timestamp registry. These realities introduce critical timing considerations into value-relevance research. If market prices are observed at fiscal-year-end (Ashadh-end / mid-July), they may precede the auditing, board adoption, NRB approval, and public release of annual reports, creating a temporal mismatch. Observing prices after the statutory reporting deadline provides a standardized rule, but it does not verify the public release date for every bank-year or establish when investors first received the information.

\subsection{Econometric Circularity of Price-Derived Multiples}
\label{subsec:pe_circularity}

A theoretical and econometric problem that recurs in empirical finance literature in Nepal is the erroneous inclusion of price-derived valuation multiples, specifically the Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratio, as explanatory independent variables in regressions where Market Price per Share (MPS) is the dependent variable.

The P/E ratio is algebraically defined as:
\begin{equation}
\text{P/E}_{it} = \frac{\text{MPS}_{it}}{\text{EPS}_{it}}
\end{equation}
Specifying an empirical regression model of the form:
\begin{equation}
\text{MPS}_{it} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \text{EPS}_{it} + \beta_2 \text{P/E}_{it} + \beta_3 \text{BVPS}_{it} + \varepsilon_{it}
\end{equation}
introduces a fatal structural circularity. The dependent variable ($\text{MPS}_{it}$) appears directly in the numerator of the explanatory variable ($\text{P/E}_{it}$). Mathematically:
\begin{equation}
\text{MPS}_{it} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \text{EPS}_{it} + \beta_2 \left(\frac{\text{MPS}_{it}}{\text{EPS}_{it}}\right) + \beta_3 \text{BVPS}_{it} + \varepsilon_{it}
\end{equation}
Rearranging this equation reveals that $\text{MPS}_{it}$ is being regressed on a function of itself. This specification generates severe mechanical endogeneity, violates the fundamental Gauss-Markov assumption of orthogonality between regressors and error terms ($\mathbb{E}[\text{P/E}_{it} \varepsilon_{it}] \ne 0$), creates artificial multi-collinearity, and inflates the coefficient of determination ($R^2$) through definitional overlap rather than economic explanation. Consequently, theoretical econometric integrity requires that all price-derived multiples be strictly excluded from value-relevance specifications, restricting explanatory regressors to pure accounting fundamentals that are determined independently of secondary market trading prices.

\section{Review of Legal, Regulatory, and Policy Framework in Nepal}
\label{sec:regulatory_framework}

The commercial banking sector and capital market of Nepal operate under a highly structured statutory and prudential regulatory regime:

\subsection{Evolution of Capital Market Regulation: SEC, NEPSE, and SEBON}
\label{subsec:nepse_history}

The institutional development of Nepal's organized capital market commenced with the establishment of the Securities Exchange Centre in 1976 (2033 BS) under the joint sponsorship of Nepal Rastra Bank and the Nepal Industrial Development Corporation (NIDC). The enactment of the Securities Exchange Act, 1983 provided the initial statutory foundation for securities transactions. In 1993, under financial sector liberalization programs, the Securities Exchange Centre was restructured into the Nepal Stock Exchange (NEPSE) Limited as the licensed operator of the secondary market, commencing formal floor trading on 13 January 1994 (29 Poush 2050 BS) \citep{nepse2017}. Simultaneously, the Securities Board of Nepal (SEBON) was constituted as the apex capital market regulator to oversee market integrity, protect investor interests, and regulate corporate disclosures \citep{nepse2017}.

\subsection{The Statutory Financial Reporting Framework: BAFIA 2017}
\label{subsec:bafia_framework}

Commercial banks in Nepal operate under the dual regulatory oversight of the Companies Act, 2006 (2063 BS) and the Banks and Financial Institutions Act, 2017 (2073 BS) (BAFIA), alongside prudential directives issued by Nepal Rastra Bank \citep{companiesact2006,bafia2017}.

Regarding financial reporting disclosure, Section 59 of BAFIA, 2017 establishes statutory submission and publication obligations for licensed institutions. Specifically, Section 59(2) delineates two distinct reporting duties:
\begin{enumerate}
\item First, a licensed bank must prepare its balance sheet and profit and loss account and submit them to Nepal Rastra Bank within three months of the completion of each financial year, publishing summary details for the information of the general public within that period as prescribed by NRB.
\item Second, the bank must publish its audited balance sheet and profit and loss account for the public in the prescribed format within nine months into the subsequent financial year \citep{bafia2017}.
\end{enumerate}

Because commercial bank fiscal years in Nepal terminate at Ashadh-end (mid-July), counting nine months into the subsequent financial year (from Shrawan through Chaitra) terminates at the end of Chaitra (mid-April). Operationalizing the price observation window at the first trading session on or after the end of Chaitra ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$) is the study's institutional operationalization of the statutory nine-month timeline under Section 59(2). In the two bank-years for which publication dates could be verified, commercial bank boards released audited annual highlights and announced dividend recommendations between Ashwin and Poush (October to January), with formal adoption at Annual General Meetings (AGMs) concluded prior to the end-of-Chaitra cutoff. For the remaining 28 bank-years, the actual public release date is not verified. Operationalizing the price observation window at the end of Chaitra provides a reproducible, legally anchored cutoff after the statutory deadline. This reduces date-selection ambiguity but does not verify the actual public release date for each bank-year or eliminate possible information leakage.

\subsection{NRB Capital Mandates and the Consolidation Directive}
\label{subsec:nrb_consolidation}

The structural consolidation of Nepalese commercial banks has been driven primarily by explicit central bank policy mandates:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The 2011 Merger Bylaws:} In 2011 (2068 BS), NRB promulgated the \textit{Bank and Financial Institutions Merger Bylaws}, introducing legal provisions, regulatory exemptions, and procedural guidelines for voluntary mergers \citep{nrb2011}.
\item \textbf{The 2015 Capital Expansion Mandate:} In its Monetary Policy for FY 2015/16 (2072/73 BS), NRB executed an aggressive systemic reform, mandating that all Class 'A' commercial banks quadruple their minimum paid-up equity capital from NPR 2.0 billion to NPR 8.0 billion by the end of FY 2073/74 (mid-July 2017) \citep{nrb2015}. To comply, commercial banks capitalized reserves through extensive bonus share distributions and executed heavy rights share issues, resulting in substantial share capital expansion and subsequent dilution of per-share earnings.
\item \textbf{The 2022--2023 Consolidation Wave:} Recognizing that capital expansion alone had not reduced the excessive number of banking institutions, NRB actively encouraged horizontal ``big mergers'' through moral suasion and regulatory forbearance (such as temporary leniency on Credit-to-Deposit ratios and branch overlapping) \citep{tht2023nrbmerger}. In addition, merging banks and financial institutions received tax reliefs under Section 47A of the Income Tax Act, 2058 \citep{incometaxact2058}.
\end{itemize}

This regulatory drive culminated in the systemic consolidation wave of early 2023 (mid-FY 2079/80), wherein major commercial banks executed statutory mergers and acquisitions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Global IME Bank Limited (GBIME):} Amalgamated with Bank of Kathmandu Limited (swap ratio 1:1) \citep{gbime2023report}, and unified commercial operations commenced on 9 January 2023 \citep{nrb2023bsr}.
\item \textbf{Himalayan Bank Limited (HBL):} Acquired Civil Bank Limited, and unified commercial operations commenced on 24 February 2023 (swap ratio 100:80.28) \citep{hbl2023report}.
\item \textbf{Kumari Bank Limited (KBL):} Amalgamated with Nepal Credit and Commerce Bank Limited (swap ratio 1:1) \citep{kumari2022sgm,kbl2023report}, and unified commercial operations commenced on 1 January 2023 \citep{nrb2023bsr}.
\item \textbf{Nepal Investment Mega Bank Limited (NIMB):} Formed via the amalgamation of Nepal Investment Bank Limited and Mega Bank Nepal Limited, and unified commercial operations commenced on 11 January 2023 (swap ratio 100:90) \citep{nimb2023report}.
\item \textbf{NMB Bank Limited (NMB):} Maintained continuous corporate operations without participating in the early-2023 consolidation wave, serving as the benchmark series. (Note: NMB had previously acquired Kanchan Development Bank Limited in August 2020, adding approximately NPR 0.50 billion in new shares issued to Kanchan shareholders at the 85\% swap ratio, alongside a 13\% bonus share capitalization, in FY 2077/78 \citep{nmb2021report}, but remained independent during the 2022--2023 wave).
\end{itemize}

By mid-October 2023 (Asoj 2080 BS), these amalgamations reduced the total commercial bank population in Nepal to exactly 20 Class 'A' institutions \citep{nrb2023list}.

\section{Empirical Review}
\label{sec:empirical_review}

\subsection{International Empirical Studies}
\label{subsec:intl_empirical}

Empirical accounting research across developed and emerging capital markets provides extensive documentation regarding the association between financial accounting disclosures and equity valuation:

\citet{collins1997} investigated the value relevance of earnings and book values in the United States over a forty-one-year period (1953--1993) across thousands of firm-year observations. Employing the \citet{ohlson1995} valuation framework, the authors addressed concerns that the transition from an industrialized to a service- and technology-oriented economy had degraded the relevance of historical accounting numbers. Their empirical findings demonstrated that the combined explanatory power ($R^2$) of earnings and book values had actually increased over the forty-one-year period. However, they observed a systematic shift in relative explanatory weights: the incremental value relevance of earnings declined, while the incremental value relevance of book value increased, driven by higher incidences of negative earnings, non-recurring charges, and intangible asset intensity.

\citet{kwon2020} investigated whether the value relevance of accounting information shifts following corporate mergers and acquisitions (M\&A) in the United States, using a panel spanning pre- and post-merger observations under SFAS 141(R). Their empirical results demonstrated that M\&A transactions significantly alter the relative valuation roles of earnings and book value. Specifically, during the post-merger integration period, the value relevance of earnings declined while the value relevance of book value per share increased markedly, with the shift particularly strong after the adoption of SFAS 141(R). The thesis interprets this shift as consistent with equity investors relying on balance-sheet net worth as a valuation anchor during corporate reorganization.

In emerging Asian equity markets, \citet{sharma2011} investigated the empirical relationship between accounting fundamentals and equity share prices across different industry groups in India over 1993/94 to 2008/09. The study found statistically significant associations between earnings per share (EPS), dividend per share (DPS), book value per share (BVPS), and share prices, with EPS and DPS identified as the strongest determinants. Sharma also tested the price-earnings ratio, dividend yield, dividend payout, and firm size (measured by sales and net worth) as explanatory variables. \citet{malhotra2013} examined 95 firms listed on the National Stock Exchange of India (NSE 100 index) from 2007 to 2012, demonstrating that book value per share, earnings per share, and price-earnings multiples significantly influenced market prices, while dividend yield had a significant inverse association. In the Middle East, \citet{almumani2014} evaluated listed commercial banks on the Amman Stock Exchange (ASE) over 2005--2011 using multivariate regression analysis, concluding that EPS, BVPS, and price-earnings ratios were the significant positive determinants of market price, with EPS showing the highest bivariate correlation ($r = 0.84$), while bank size had a significant inverse association. Dividend per share and dividend payout (DP) were also tested but had insignificant effects.

\subsection{Foundational Nepalese Empirical Literature (Early to Mid Studies)}
\label{subsec:foundational_nepal}

Empirical investigation into equity valuation determinants in Nepal originated alongside the formalization of NEPSE trading:

\citet{pradhan2003} conducted one of the earliest empirical evaluations of stock market behavior in Nepal, examining listed enterprises over 1994--1999. Employing descriptive and correlational techniques, Pradhan investigated the relative effects of cash dividends and retained earnings on stock prices. The study documented that cash dividends exhibited a stronger, more statistically significant positive association with market share prices than retained earnings, concluding that Nepalese equity investors exhibited strong cash-dividend preference in an environment characterized by institutional opacity and market volatility.

\citet{bhattarai2014} investigated the determinants of share prices across a sample of nine commercial banks (Nabil Bank, Nepal Investment Bank, Standard Chartered Bank Nepal, Everest Bank, Bank of Kathmandu, Machhapuchchhre Bank, Laxmi Bank, Kumari Bank, and Siddhartha Bank) over the period 2006 to 2014. Using pooled OLS regressions, Bhattarai evaluated fiscal-year-end closing market price per share against dividend payout ratio (DPR), dividend yield (DY), earnings per share (EPS), price-earnings (P/E) ratio, and bank size (SIZE). The study reported that EPS and P/E ratios exhibited statistically significant positive associations with market share prices, whereas dividend yield displayed an inverse relationship. However, as noted in Chapter~I, Bhattarai's specification explicitly included P/E as an explanatory regressor alongside MPS, inducing mechanical circularity.

\citet{pradhan2016} examined 14 commercial banks over the period 2002/03 to 2013/14 using a pooled panel regression framework. The authors analyzed the impact of firm size, earnings per share, dividend per share, price-earnings ratio, book value per share and return on assets on market equity valuation, alongside macroeconomic controls (GDP, inflation, money supply). Their empirical findings identified bank size as the most important determinant, with EPS, DPS, P/E, BVPS and ROA as major determining variables. Among the macroeconomic variables, GDP was a major determinant of share prices.

\citet{bajracharya2019} evaluated twelve commercial banks listed on NEPSE over the ten-year period from 2006/07 to 2015/16 (120 bank-year observations). Using panel data analysis (random effects, Hausman p = 0.818), the authors reported that earnings per share (EPS), dividend per share (DPS), and the price-earnings (P/E) ratio had a statistically significant positive effect on share prices, whereas return on assets (ROA) was statistically insignificant in the chosen random-effects specification (p = 0.0544), and the external factor inflation had a statistically significant negative effect. Bank rate (BR) and market capitalization (Ln MC) were also included as external factors.

\citet{silwal2019} evaluated ten commercial banks across 100 bank-year observations over FY 2065/66 to 2074/75 (2008/09--2017/18). Employing correlation and multiple regression analysis, the authors examined market price per share in relation to book value per share, return on equity, P/E ratio, dividend yield, and bank size. The study concluded that BVPS exerted the largest explanatory influence on stock prices, followed by P/E and ROE. The thesis reads this balance-sheet primacy as consistent with the investor benchmark shift following NRB's 2015 capital escalation mandate (Section~\ref{subsec:nrb_consolidation}).

\subsection{Empirical Evidence from Recent Nepalese Studies (2021--2024, Pre-Wave Panels)}
\label{subsec:recent_nepal}

Recent empirical research on Nepalese commercial banking valuation documents notable divergence in empirical findings across diverse samples and econometric specifications:

\citet{niroula2021} examined 18 commercial banks over the five-year period FY 2015/16 to 2019/20 (90 bank-year observations). Using pooled regression models, Niroula found that earnings per share had a statistically significant positive impact on market share prices, as did the P/E ratio and bank size. Conversely, book value per share (BVPS) and return on equity (ROE) exhibited negative coefficients that failed to attain statistical significance, highlighting growing empirical ambiguity regarding the pricing of balance-sheet net worth. Dividend yield (DY) and return on assets (ROA) were likewise included in the model but had negligible effects. Niroula's negative, insignificant BVPS coefficient sits at the opposite pole from \citet{silwal2019}'s finding that net asset backing was the primary benchmark for Nepalese bank investors, a divergence Section~\ref{subsec:synthesis_matrix} takes up.

\citet{gyawali2022} evaluated ten commercial banks listed on NEPSE over the five-year span 2017 to 2021 (50 observations). Employing descriptive statistics, Pearson correlation, and multiple regression analysis, Gyawali observed statistically significant positive relationships of share price with EPS, DPS, and the P/E ratio. However, the regression coefficient for return on assets (ROA) was statistically insignificant, suggesting that secondary equity markets discounted accounting return metrics in favor of per-share distributions. GDP had a positive but insignificant effect on share prices, while inflation had a negative and insignificant effect. Gyawali's statistically insignificant ROA coefficient contrasts directly with \citet{chaudhari2025}'s documented significant negative ROA association, illustrating the empirical instability of asset-productivity indicators across Nepalese banking panels.

\citet{chhetri2023} investigated 13 commercial banks over an eleven-year panel spanning 2012 to 2022 (143 bank-year observations) using a random-effects panel model (Hausman test p = 1.0000). Chhetri regressed market share price on EPS, P/E, BVPS, ROA, firm size (LNFS), inflation, GDP and broad money supply. The empirical results indicated that EPS, BVPS, ROA, P/E, firm size and inflation were positive and statistically significant determinants of market share prices (EPS p = 0.0062), while GDP and broad money supply were insignificant.

\citet{joshi2023} analyzed seven commercial banks across 70 bank-year observations over FY 2012/13 to 2021/22. Using correlation and linear panel regression techniques, the authors evaluated share price behavior against EPS, DPS, BVPS, and P/E ratios. Their findings indicated that all four explanatory variables exhibited statistically significant positive coefficients, with the P/E ratio and EPS demonstrating the highest explanatory power in predicting NEPSE market prices. Joshi et al.'s finding of uniformly positive and statistically significant coefficients across all four explanatory metrics is consistent with \citet{chhetri2023}, which likewise finds EPS and BVPS significant positive over a comparable observation window.

\citet{lamsal2024} evaluated seven commercial banks listed on NEPSE over FY 2011/12 to 2020/21, examining market price per share in relation to EPS, P/E, DPS, and BVPS. The correlation results showed significant positive associations between share prices and all four variables, strongest for DPS ($r = 0.846$, $p < 0.01$), and the multiple regression found EPS, DPS, and P/E significant positive (EPS $B = 19.307$, DPS $B = 14.486$, P/E $B = 32.308$, all $p = 0.001$), while BVPS was negative and insignificant ($B = -0.785$, $p = 0.097$). The paper's abstract and discussion claim an inverse DPS relationship, but that claim contradicts its own Tables 2 and 4, and the table evidence is followed here.

\citet{giri2024} conducted an empirical investigation into four commercial banks across 40 bank-year observations as stated in the paper (its ANOVA total degrees of freedom of 35 imply 36 observations entered the regression) over the decade FY 2070/71 to 2079/80 (2013/14--2022/23). Employing descriptive, correlational and multiple regression analysis, Giri evaluated share prices against DPS, EPS, P/E, and net worth per share (BVPS). The analysis revealed significant positive correlations between market price and DPS, P/E, and net worth per share, whereas the bivariate correlation with EPS was statistically negligible, reinforcing the argument that cash dividends and net asset backing overshadowed operational earnings among sampled banks.

\citet{gurung2024} evaluated 16 of the 19 listed commercial banks across 171 observations over the period 2007 to 2023, an unbalanced panel (16 banks over 17 years would yield 272 bank-years if balanced), using stepwise multiple regression analysis. The study found that earnings per share, book value per share, and the P/E ratio exerted statistically significant positive influences on commercial bank share prices, while dividend per share and total assets (TA) were statistically insignificant, demonstrating that market valuation was anchored in foundational balance-sheet and earning power rather than payout distributions.

\citet{bhattarai2024mps} analyzed market price alongside accounting indicators over FY 2012/13 to 2022/23 in a pooled regression covering 26 commercial banks before the merger wave and 19 after, excluding the fully government-owned Rastriya Banijya Bank. The study reported positive associations for EPS, DPS, and total assets with market price, a negative association for non-performing loans, and no statistically significant BVPS association. Although the sample spans merger-related changes in the number of banks, the reported model does not interact EPS and BVPS with a period indicator, so it does not test for slope changes across the early-2023 consolidation.

\subsection{Empirical Evidence Spanning the 2023 Consolidation Wave (2024--2026)}
\label{subsec:consolidation_studies}

The most recent body of empirical research has extended observation windows through FY 2024/25, directly capturing the post-consolidation operating environment:

\citet{pant2024} investigated seven commercial banks across 70 observations over the period 2013/14 to 2023/24, spanning the 2023 banking consolidation wave. Using multiple regression models, the authors regressed market price per share on EPS, DPS, BVPS, and P/E. The empirical findings demonstrated that EPS and P/E had statistically significant positive impacts on market share prices. Conversely, the coefficients for dividend per share and book value per share were negative and statistically insignificant. The thesis interprets this weak BVPS coefficient as consistent with merger-driven balance-sheet expansion during the consolidation wave, though Pant et al.\ do not test or discuss that mechanism. (The paper reports $N = 70$ despite describing an eleven-fiscal-year span as a ``ten-year period'', indicating seven bank-year observations are missing from the paper's data.)

\citet{chaudhari2025} examined ten commercial banks over 2014/15 to 2023/24 (100 bank-year observations, as the paper's methods and regression sections state 2014/15 with 100 observations despite the abstract mentioning 2013--14). Employing pooled OLS regression, Chaudhari analyzed market price per share against firm-specific variables (EPS, DPS, ROA, bank size) and macroeconomic factors (inflation, interest rates, GDP). The empirical results revealed strong, statistically significant positive coefficients for EPS and DPS. In contrast, return on assets (ROA) displayed a statistically significant \textit{negative} association with market share prices, while bank size, GDP and interest rates failed to attain statistical significance, and inflation was significant at the 5\% level ($p = 0.037$). Chaudhari concluded that equity investors penalized low-margin asset expansion while rewarding per-share earnings generation.

\citet{gurungdahal2025} investigated ten commercial banks listed on NEPSE over the eleven-year period FY 2013/14 to 2023/24 using a panel regression framework.\footnote{\citet{gurungdahal2025}, published in \textit{Pravaha} (vol.~31, no.~1, pp.~1--12), is distinct from the comprehensive panel study by \citet{dahal2025} in the \textit{Interdisciplinary Journal of Management and Social Sciences} (vol.~6, no.~1, pp.~252--261), which examined 209 bank-year observations across all listed commercial banks.} Among the studies reviewed, \citet{gurungdahal2025} provide the closest methodological precedent, because they applied the Hausman specification test and estimated a bank fixed-effects regression model to control for unobserved time-invariant bank heterogeneity. Their empirical results indicated that dividend payout ratio (DPR), P/E ratio, earnings per share, book value per share, return on assets, and stock return (SR, $\beta = 81.126$, p = 0.0346) exerted statistically significant impacts on market share prices. However, Gurung and Dahal estimated static, full-sample coefficients, omitting any formal interaction test of whether valuation parameters shifted before and after the 2023 consolidation wave.

\citet{dahal2025} examined 209 annual observations spanning FY 2013/14 to 2023/24 from all commercial banks listed on NEPSE, using descriptive statistics, correlation analysis, and multiple regression to relate market price per share to P/E, BVPS, dividend rate, earnings yield, and market-to-book ratio. They found significant positive relationships between market price and both P/E and BVPS, with market-to-book ratio, earnings yield, and dividend rate also positively correlated but less pronounced. Like most of the surveyed literature, the specification included the circular P/E regressor.

\citet{timalsina2026} evaluated 13 commercial banks over the thirteen-year panel FY 2011/12 to 2023/24 (169 bank-year observations). Using descriptive statistics, Pearson correlation, and panel regression analysis, Timalsina investigated the impact of firm-specific indicators (EPS, DPS, P/E, ROA, size) and macroeconomic factors (GDP, inflation, interest rates) on market share prices. The study's Hausman test ($p = 0.151$) favored the random-effects specification. The empirical findings revealed that EPS, DPS, P/E, firm size, and inflation had statistically significant positive associations with share prices, while interest rates exerted a significant negative effect. Return on assets (ROA) and GDP growth showed no statistically significant influence.

In the most contemporary literature, \citet{niraula2026} evaluated 16 commercial banks across 80 observations over FY 2019/20 to 2023/24 using balanced panel OLS regression. The study examined firm-specific determinants of market price per share, documenting statistically significant positive impacts for Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR) and total assets, and a positive effect for Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) significant only at the 10\% level, while ROA was positive but statistically insignificant. The weighted average interest rate spread (WAIRS) and the non-performing loan ratio (NPLR) had significant negative effects, and the credit-to-deposit ratio (CDR) had a negative effect significant only at the 10\% level. \citet{bhandari2026} executed a census study of all 19 NEPSE-listed commercial banks over FY 2014/15 to 2024/25, demonstrating that earnings indicators and dividend payout served as the primary determinants of market prices, while dividend yield had a negative impact. In governance and regulatory research, \citet{bhattarai2024} evaluated whether banking regulations (bank capital, loan loss provision, spread rate, broad money supply, base rate) affect earnings per share, while \citet{dongol2024} investigated corporate governance mechanisms and bank-specific factors on bank performance (earnings per share).

Regarding banking consolidation directly, \citet{adhikari2023} conducted an empirical examination of the stock and financial performance of seven commercial banks that underwent mergers between 2013 and 2020. The authors documented that post-merger market share prices fell by 46.01\% ($p = 0.000$), while dividend per share fell by 5.07\% ($p = 0.761$) and earnings per share were essentially unchanged (+0.12\%, $p = 0.992$), neither statistically significant. In a companion study, \citet{adhikari2023cbr} analyzed the comparative financial performance of merged commercial banks, finding that consolidation failed to generate immediate operational synergies or profitability improvements: liquidity and leverage ratios improved significantly, while profitability and shareholder-wealth ratios showed mixed or insignificant results.

\citet{acharya2026merger} extend this merger-performance literature with a comparison of merged banks and non-merged peers. They report heterogeneous post-merger outcomes and no consistent profitability gain for merged banks relative to non-merged peers. Their focus is merger-related financial performance. Like the Adhikari studies, it does not test whether EPS-price and BVPS-price slopes changed across the consolidation period.

\subsection{Synthesis of Empirical Literature}
\label{subsec:synthesis_matrix}

An evaluation of the international and domestic empirical literature reveals four thematic debates and methodological tensions that characterize commercial bank valuation:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The Earning Power versus Net Asset Value Valuation Anchor:}
A recurring empirical question in Nepalese commercial banking literature concerns whether market share prices are primarily driven by current operating profitability (EPS) or balance-sheet equity backing (BVPS). Among the fifteen studies in Table~\ref{tab:lit_synthesis} that test both EPS and BVPS, results diverge: for example, \citet{niroula2021} finds EPS significantly positive with BVPS negative and insignificant, while \citet{giri2024} finds net worth per share significant positive with EPS insignificant. In contrast, \citet{joshi2023}, \citet{gurung2024} and \citet{chhetri2023} find both positive and significant, and \citet{pant2024} replicates the Niroula pattern. Studies that report EPS as the predominant driver (\citet{bhandari2026} and \citet{chaudhari2025}) did not include BVPS in their models, and \citet{silwal2019}, which reports BVPS as the primary influence, did not test EPS, so those studies cannot support an EPS-versus-BVPS comparison. This divergence reflects institutional and temporal context: following NRB's 2015 fourfold paid-up capital mandate, equity bases expanded rapidly through bonus share capitalizations, temporarily elevating balance-sheet asset backing as an investor benchmark. However, as capital bases stabilized, operational earning capacity re-emerged as the primary valuation slope, until the 2022--2023 consolidation wave again restructured balance-sheet equity.

\item \textbf{Econometric Endogeneity and Mechanical Circularity in Valuation Multiples:}
A widespread methodological flaw across the surveyed literature is the routine inclusion of the Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratio as an independent explanatory variable in regressions modelling Market Price per Share (MPS) \citep{almumani2014,bajracharya2019,bhandari2026,bhattarai2014,chhetri2023,dahal2025,giri2024,gurung2024,gurungdahal2025,gyawali2022,joshi2023,lamsal2024,malhotra2013,niroula2021,pant2024,pradhan2016,sharma2011,silwal2019,timalsina2026}. Because $\text{P/E} \equiv \text{MPS} / \text{EPS}$, regressing market price on P/E introduces mechanical endogeneity and algebraic simultaneity bias, artificially inflating $R^2$ values and distorting true fundamental regression slopes. A defensible design therefore excludes price-derived ratios and restricts explanatory variables to fundamental accounting measures determined independently of secondary market share prices.

\item \textbf{Temporal Misalignment and Look-Ahead Bias in Price Measurement:}
\citet{bhattarai2014} measures equity share prices at fiscal-year-end (Ashadh-end / mid-July). Under Nepalese corporate reporting reality, audited annual financial accounts are not published at Ashadh-end. Instead, they require statutory auditing, NRB prudential clearance, and annual general meeting (AGM) adoption, typically spanning three to nine months into the subsequent fiscal year. Conditioning Ashadh-end market prices on accounting figures that were not publicly disclosed until months later violates the informational sequence of market efficiency, creating severe look-ahead bias and spurious associations.

\item \textbf{Panel Heterogeneity and the Static Pooling Assumption:}
With few exceptions, extant Nepalese banking studies employ pooled or static panel regressions across multi-year panels, implicitly assuming parameter homogeneity across disparate institutions and stability across time. While recent work by \citet{gurungdahal2025} introduces bank fixed effects to control for time-invariant bank heterogeneity, almost all existing studies estimate static, full-period average coefficients. Consequently, to the best of the researcher's knowledge and within the corpus of studies reviewed, no empirical study has evaluated whether fundamental valuation slopes shifted across the structural regime change induced by Nepal's 2023 commercial banking consolidation wave.
\end{itemize}

To synthesize the empirical literature directly informing this dissertation's econometric framework, Table~\ref{tab:lit_synthesis} presents a comparative synthesis of primary empirical studies. Selection is restricted to foundational theoretical frameworks and empirical studies whose dependent variable is market price per share (MPS) and which evaluate fundamental accounting indicators across commercial banks, with three foundational emerging-market benchmarks (\citet{sharma2011}, \citet{malhotra2013}, and \citet{almumani2014}) included because they establish the EPS/BVPS price relationship in comparable markets, and an early Nepalese study of listed firms generally, \citet{pradhan2003}, included as a benchmark. Studies of governance, capital adequacy regulation, or standalone financial performance (\citet{bhattarai2024}, \citet{dongol2024}, \citet{adhikari2023cbr}, and \citet{acharya2026merger}, none of which specifies market price per share as the dependent variable in the work reviewed here) are therefore excluded from the matrix.

\section{Research Gap}
\label{sec:research_gap}

A review of the theoretical foundations, regulatory frameworks, and empirical literature reveals five key conceptual, methodological, and econometric gaps that motivate this dissertation:

\subsection{The Consolidation Regime-Shift Gap}
Almost without exception, existing Nepalese banking studies employ pooled cross-sectional or static panel regression designs that estimate sample-average, full-period relationships \citep{bhattarai2014,chaudhari2025,chhetri2023,timalsina2026}. These models implicitly assume parameter constancy across time. Early 2023 witnessed five commercial banking combinations that altered reporting boundaries, asset bases, and capital structures. This study purposively samples four of those combinations alongside an unmerged benchmark \citep{nrb2023bsr}. Following the theoretical framework of \citet{kwon2020} and \citet{collins1997}, major corporate amalgamations impair earnings persistence through integration costs and loan harmonization, suggesting a possible shift in valuation weight toward balance-sheet equity. Despite this theoretical precedent, to the best of the researcher's knowledge and within the extensive corpus of studies reviewed, none has formulated an explicit regime-interaction econometric model to test (in a two-tailed framework) whether the valuation coefficients of EPS ($\beta_E$) and BVPS ($\beta_B$) differed between the pre-consolidation (FY 2076/77--2078/79) and post-consolidation (FY 2079/80--2081/82) periods.

\subsection{The Specification and Endogeneity Gap (Circularity of P/E Multiples)}
A widespread practice in the Nepalese empirical literature is the routine inclusion of the Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratio as an independent regressor in models predicting Market Price per Share (MPS) \citep{almumani2014,bajracharya2019,bhandari2026,bhattarai2014,chhetri2023,dahal2025,giri2024,gurung2024,gurungdahal2025,gyawali2022,joshi2023,lamsal2024,malhotra2013,niroula2021,pant2024,pradhan2016,sharma2011,silwal2019,timalsina2026}. As mathematically demonstrated in Section~\ref{subsec:pe_circularity}, because $\text{P/E} = \text{MPS} / \text{EPS}$, regressing MPS on P/E introduces a circular algebraic dependency, embedding the dependent variable directly inside the regressor. This induces severe mechanical endogeneity, inflates $R^2$ values spuriously, and invalidates statistical inference. A defensible research design must eliminate price-derived multiples and restrict explanatory variables to fundamental accounting measures determined independently of secondary market share prices.

\subsection{The Temporal Alignment and Look-Ahead Bias Gap}
Because audited annual financial statements are not prepared, audited, and published until months after Ashadh-end, pairing Ashadh-end prices with audited numbers creates severe look-ahead bias, violating temporal causality. Furthermore, attempting to reconstruct individual corporate announcement dates across 30 bank-years is prone to measurement error, informal leakage, and archival opacity. Existing literature lacks a standardized, institutionally grounded price-measurement rule. This study operationalizes Section 59(2) of BAFIA, 2017 at the end of Chaitra (mid-April), observing market prices at the first trading session on or after the end of Chaitra ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$). This provides a reproducible observation threshold after the statutory deadline, while leaving each bank-year's actual public release date and any earlier information leakage unverified.

\subsection{The Entity-Timing Transition Gap}
None of the empirical studies reviewed addresses the institutional transition timing of merged banks. For fiscal year 2078/79, annual financial reports represent pre-merger standalone operations, whereas by mid-April 2023, secondary market trading reflected the unified post-merger entities (mergers completed in January--February 2023). This creates an entity-timing mismatch between pre-merger accounting fundamentals and post-consolidation market valuations. Prior studies ignore this structural shock, risking biased parameter estimation. This study explicitly addresses entity transition timing through dedicated sensitivity exclusions.

\subsection{The Econometric and Small-Cluster Inference Gap}
Prior empirical research on Nepalese commercial banking routinely overlooks finite-sample degrees of freedom and the econometric hazards of small-cluster panel inference. In fixed-effects panel models with interactions, parameter estimation rapidly consumes degrees of freedom. Moreover, standard cluster-robust standard errors suffer from severe downward bias when the number of clusters is small (in this study, $G = 5$) \citep{cameron2008clusters}. This dissertation addresses this gap by disclosing exact residual degrees of freedom ($28 - 14 = 14$ df) and reporting the sensitivity of the joint test across covariance estimators, and framing findings as observed statistical associations rather than causal proofs, using MacKinnon-White HC3 as the heteroskedasticity-robust estimator. HC3 addresses heteroskedasticity, not within-bank correlation, so the within-bank independence assumption remains untested. The small-cluster limitation is handled by disclosing the degrees of freedom and reporting the sensitivity of the joint test across covariance estimators (Chapter~IV, Table~\ref{tab:cov_sensitivity}) and the wild-cluster bootstrap.

By synthesizing the \citet{ohlson1995} residual income valuation model with the post-merger value-relevance framework of \citet{kwon2020}, operationalizing the standardized BAFIA reporting deadline ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$), excluding circular P/E multiples, and executing a two-way fixed-effects panel model with seven robustness variants plus two baseline specifications, this dissertation is designed to address each of the identified gaps in the literature.
