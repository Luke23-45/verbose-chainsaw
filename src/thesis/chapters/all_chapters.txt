\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

\section{Background of the Study}
\label{sec:background}

A central question in financial economics and accounting valuation is whether the empirical relationship between published financial statement numbers and equity market prices remains stable across structural corporate reorganizations. At its theoretical core, the valuation of common equity connects secondary market share prices to investors' expectations regarding a firm's future economic earning power, cash-flow generation capability, and net asset backing, conditioned upon the public and private information available in the marketplace \citep{beaver1968,fama1970}. Among competing valuation architectures, the residual income valuation (RIV) framework pioneered by \citet{ohlson1995} and \citet{feltham1995} provides an analytically rigorous and mathematically closed foundation linking financial accounting numbers directly to equity share prices. Anchored in the clean-surplus relation---which dictates that the change in book value of equity equals contemporaneous net income minus net dividends---the Ohlson framework establishes that a firm's equity market value can be expressed as the sum of its currently recognized book value of equity and the discounted present value of expected future residual income (abnormal earnings). Under linear information dynamics, this theoretical formulation demonstrates that market prices can be represented as a linear function of contemporaneous earnings per share (EPS) and book value per share (BVPS). In this framework, BVPS serves as an accounting proxy for the cumulative historical net assets contributed by shareholders and retained from past earnings, whereas EPS captures current operational profitability and serves as the baseline indicator of the firm's forward-looking earning capacity \citep{collins1997}.

Empirical studies investigating the association between equity prices and published accounting fundamentals---collectively recognized as the accounting value-relevance literature---have provided extensive documentation across developed and emerging equity markets \citep{collins1997,almumani2014,malhotra2013,sharma2011}. In frontier and developing market environments, however, the informational capacity of equity markets is frequently challenged by structural friction, regulatory evolution, asymmetric information, illiquidity, and corporate restructuring. The Nepalese capital market presents a distinct and compelling setting for examining the empirical validity and temporal stability of accounting value relevance. The organized secondary securities market in Nepal originated with the establishment of the Securities Exchange Centre in 1976 (2033 BS), under the joint sponsorship of Nepal Rastra Bank and the Nepal Industrial Development Corporation (NIDC); the Securities Exchange Act, 1983 subsequently provided its statutory foundation.

In 1993, a major financial-sector reform initiative restructured the Centre into the Nepal Stock Exchange (NEPSE) Limited as the nation's sole organized stock exchange, concurrently establishing the Securities Board of Nepal (SEBON) as the apex statutory securities market regulator. Floor trading on NEPSE officially commenced on 13 January 1994 (29 Poush 2050 BS) \citep{nepse2017}. NEPSE is heavily dominated by the banking and financial institutions (BFIs) sector, with Class 'A' commercial banks accounting for more than 30\% of total market capitalization \citep{gurungdahal2025}.

Nepal's commercial banking sector has experienced profound structural, regulatory, and institutional transformations over the past two decades. Following the financial liberalization policies initiated in the mid-1980s, the number of licensed commercial banks expanded rapidly, reaching an apex of 32 licensed commercial banks alongside dozens of development banks and finance companies by fiscal year 2011/12 according to Nepal Rastra Bank statistics \citep{nrb2013stats}. Recognizing that severe institutional proliferation was outstripping supervisory capacity, inducing cutthroat lending competition, and creating systemic vulnerability, Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB)---the central bank and banking regulator---embarked on an aggressive, multi-phase consolidation drive. In 2011 (2068 BS), NRB introduced the \textit{Bank and Financial Institutions Merger Bylaws}, providing the initial regulatory architecture for voluntary corporate combinations \citep{nrb2011}. Subsequently, in its Monetary Policy for FY 2015/16 (2072/73 BS), NRB executed a historic fourfold capital expansion mandate, requiring all Class 'A' commercial banks to increase their minimum paid-up equity capital from NPR 2.0 billion to NPR 8.0 billion within a two-year regulatory window \citep{nrb2015}. Although banks initially satisfied this capital mandate primarily through bonus share capitalization and rights offerings, the persistent pressure for systemic consolidation culminated in the major banking consolidation wave of 2022--2023. Supported by regulatory incentives and moral suasion, commercial banks executed large-scale horizontal amalgamations and statutory acquisitions. By mid-October 2023 (Asoj 2080 BS), successive corporate consolidations reduced the commercial bank population to exactly 20 licensed institutions \citep{nrb2023list}.

A central theoretical and empirical issue in financial accounting is whether the valuation relationship between accounting fundamentals and equity market prices remains stable across major corporate reorganizations and structural transitions. As demonstrated in international M\&A literature, large-scale corporate mergers fundamentally restructure the corporate reporting entity: they alter the financial scale of the institution, introduce substantial post-merger integration costs, require accounting harmonization across legacy loan and deposit portfolios, generate non-recurring organizational adjustments, and expand the aggregate share capital base \citep{kwon2020}. Under such restructuring, reported accounting earnings can experience temporarily impaired persistence, with increased transitoriness, volatility, and noise, potentially leading equity investors to discount earnings and rely more heavily on balance-sheet equity (book value) as a valuation anchor \citep{collins1997}. In Nepal, four of the country's commercial banking series---Global IME Bank Limited (GBIME), Himalayan Bank Limited (HBL), Kumari Bank Limited (KBL), and Nepal Investment Mega Bank Limited (NIMB)---commenced joint operations following amalgamations and acquisitions between January and February 2023 (Poush--Falgun 2079 BS). Concurrently, established peer institution NMB Bank Limited (NMB) maintained operational continuity without participating in the early-2023 consolidation wave, thereby providing a contemporaneous benchmark series. Although NMB had previously acquired Kanchan Development Bank Limited in August 2020 (adding approximately NPR 0.50 billion in new shares issued to Kanchan shareholders at the 85\% swap ratio, alongside a 13\% bonus share distribution, in FY 2077/78) \citep{nmb2021report}, it did not undergo horizontal amalgamation during the 2022--2023 consolidation wave, remaining structurally stable throughout the focal restructuring divide.

Despite the profound economic and corporate restructuring induced by the 2023 consolidation wave, empirical research on Nepalese commercial banking valuation reviewed in Chapter II has predominantly focused on static, sample-average regressions spanning multi-year historical periods without explicitly evaluating whether the fundamental accounting-price relationships shifted across the consolidation divide \citep{bhattarai2014,chaudhari2025,chhetri2023,gurungdahal2025,joshi2023,pant2024,timalsina2026}. Furthermore, conventional literature has routinely suffered from methodological shortcomings, notably the circular inclusion of price-derived valuation ratios such as Price-to-Earnings (P/E) as explanatory regressors predicting market prices, and, in Bhattarai (2014), the reliance on fiscal-year-end (Ashadh-end) pricing dates that precede the public disclosure of audited financial accounts \citep{bhattarai2014}. This dissertation directly addresses these theoretical, institutional, and empirical gaps by evaluating the value relevance of accounting information (EPS and BVPS) before and after Nepal's 2023 banking consolidation wave across a longitudinal panel of five sampled commercial bank series over a six-year period (FY 2076/77 to FY 2081/82), using a standardized post-reporting-deadline pricing window grounded in Section 59(2) of the Banks and Financial Institutions Act, 2017 \citep{bafia2017}.

\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:problem_statement}

The valuation behavior of commercial bank equities in emerging capital markets is governed by a complex interplay between institutional regulation, financial disclosure credibility, corporate scale, and investor sentiment. In Nepal, empirical investigations examining the association between accounting indicators and equity share prices have documented extensive but divergent findings. Prior studies have evaluated diverse financial measures, including earnings per share, book value per share, dividend per share, return on assets, return on equity, and price-earnings ratios, yet their reported empirical conclusions diverge sharply regarding coefficient magnitudes, directional signs, and statistical significance.

For instance, regarding operating profitability, \citet{chhetri2023} examined 13 commercial banks over 2012--2022 using a random-effects panel model and reported a statistically significant positive association between return on assets (ROA) and market share price (MPS). Conversely, \citet{chaudhari2025} investigated ten commercial banks over 2014/15--2023/24 and documented a statistically significant \textit{negative} relationship between ROA and share prices, while \citet{gyawali2022} found a positive but statistically insignificant ROA coefficient across ten banks observed between 2017 and 2021. For book value per share, \citet{joshi2023} reported a strong, positive, statistically significant association with market price among seven commercial banks over 2012/13--2021/22, whereas \citet{pant2024} documented an insignificant negative coefficient for BVPS across seven commercial banks observed through FY 2023/24. Similarly, while \citet{bhandari2026} identified EPS as the predominant determinant of equity valuation across listed commercial banks, \citet{giri2024} found the bivariate EPS coefficient to be statistically negligible or secondary to dividend and net worth measures.

These empirical discrepancies stem fundamentally from differences in sample composition, observation periods, econometric specifications, and variable measurement timing. However, beyond empirical divergence across static studies, the existing literature reveals four critical conceptual and methodological problems that motivate the present dissertation:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The Absence of Consolidation Regime Shift Analysis:}
Published Nepalese studies reviewed in this dissertation predominantly employ pooled OLS or static panel regression designs that estimate unconditional, full-sample average coefficients across their entire observation spans \citep{bhattarai2014,chaudhari2025,timalsina2026}. These static models implicitly assume parameter stationarity---that the valuation slope attached to a rupee of earnings or book value remains identical throughout the sample period. However, between January and February 2023, five commercial banking combinations in recent Nepalese history commenced joint operations: Kumari Bank absorbed NCC Bank (1 January 2023); Global IME Bank absorbed Bank of Kathmandu (9 January 2023); Prabhu Bank acquired Century Commercial Bank (10 January 2023); Nepal Investment Bank amalgamated with Mega Bank Nepal to form NIMB (11 January 2023); and Himalayan Bank acquired Civil Bank (24 February 2023) \citep{nrb2023bsr}. These combinations fundamentally transformed the capital scale, loan portfolios, branch networks, and risk profiles of the surviving entities. International research demonstrates that structural corporate combinations alter the financial reporting environment, temporarily increasing earnings volatility while shifting investor attention toward balance-sheet net worth \citep{kwon2020}. No empirical study identified in the literature reviewed in Chapter II has formally evaluated whether the valuation coefficients of EPS and BVPS shifted between the pre-consolidation (FY 2076/77--2078/79) and post-consolidation (FY 2079/80--2081/82) periods using an explicit regime-interaction econometric framework.

\item \textbf{Econometric Endogeneity from Price-Derived Explanatory Variables:}
A common practice across studies reviewed in Chapter II is the inclusion of the price-earnings (P/E) ratio alongside fundamental accounting variables in regression models where Market Price per Share (MPS) serves as the dependent variable \citep{bhattarai2014,chhetri2023,giri2024,gurungdahal2025,joshi2023,pant2024}. Because the P/E ratio is algebraically defined as $\text{P/E} = \text{MPS} / \text{EPS}$, specifying a model of the form $\text{MPS}_{it} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \text{EPS}_{it} + \beta_2 \text{P/E}_{it} + \dots$ embeds the dependent variable directly within the numerator of one of its own explanatory regressors. This introduces mechanical endogeneity, circular causality, and spurious correlation that severely distort the estimated coefficients and invalidate standard hypothesis testing. A methodologically defensible accounting valuation study must strictly exclude price-derived multiples and focus exclusively on fundamental accounting variables that are determined independently of secondary market trading prices.

\item \textbf{Temporal Misalignment and Look-Ahead Bias in Price Measurement:}
A critical limitation in prior Nepalese valuation research concerns the timing of share price measurement. Bhattarai (2014) measures equity share prices at fiscal-year-end (Ashadh-end / mid-July) closing market prices to evaluate the relevance of financial statement numbers \citep{bhattarai2014}. Under Nepalese corporate and regulatory realities, commercial bank fiscal years terminate at Ashadh-end (mid-July), but audited annual financial statements are not prepared, audited by statutory auditors, approved by NRB, and published for months thereafter. Pairing Ashadh-end market prices with audited accounting figures that were not publicly known on that date creates severe look-ahead bias, violating the temporal causality required for information content studies \citep{ball1968,beaver1968,bernard1989}. Conversely, attempting to reconstruct individual, ad hoc corporate disclosure dates for 30 bank-year observations is fraught with measurement noise, selective informal leakage, and archival opacity. To resolve this temporal conundrum, an objective, legally grounded measurement rule is required. Section 59(2) of the Banks and Financial Institutions Act, 2017 (BAFIA) legally mandates that Class 'A' commercial banks publish their audited annual balance sheets and profit and loss accounts within nine months into the subsequent fiscal year---a statutory deadline operationalized at the end of Chaitra (mid-April) of the subsequent fiscal year \citep{bafia2017}. Observing market prices after this statutory threshold reduces the temporal mismatch between accounting figures and prices. It does not establish when each investor first learned the information or eliminate all possible look-ahead or information-leakage concerns.

\item \textbf{Neglect of Degrees of Freedom and Small-Cluster Econometric Fragility:}
Prior single-sector panel studies in Nepal frequently implement econometric estimation without disclosing or addressing the severe statistical constraints imposed by small cross-sectional dimensions. In fixed-effects panel models with interactive specifications, parameter proliferation rapidly consumes residual degrees of freedom. On a clean sample of $N = 28$ observations, estimating individual bank fixed effects, common fiscal-year fixed effects, and four slope coefficients leaves exactly $28 - 14 = 14$ residual degrees of freedom. Furthermore, standard cluster-robust sandwich standard errors perform poorly and exhibit substantial finite-sample downward bias when the number of clusters is small ($G = 5$) \citep{cameron2008clusters}. Prior empirical studies in Nepal reviewed in Chapter II have largely left these inferential hazards unaddressed, risking false discoveries and overstated statistical precision. Rigorous modeling requires explicit parameter accounting and the deployment of MacKinnon-White HC3 heteroskedasticity-robust inference, which addresses heteroskedasticity rather than within-bank correlation; the small-cluster constraint is addressed by disclosing residual degrees of freedom and reporting the sensitivity of the joint test to the covariance estimator, accompanied by transparent non-causal interpretation.
\end{itemize}

To address these empirical, methodological, and econometric problems, this study investigates three central research questions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Research Question 1:} Are fundamental accounting measures---specifically earnings per share (EPS) and book value per share (BVPS)---statistically associated with post-reporting-deadline market share prices ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$) among the selected Nepalese commercial banks?
\item \textbf{Research Question 2:} Did the estimated valuation associations of EPS and BVPS with market share prices differ significantly between the pre-consolidation period (FY 2076/77--2078/79) and the post-consolidation period (FY 2079/80--2081/82)?
\item \textbf{Research Question 3:} Are the baseline and consolidation-interaction findings robust to alternative operating profitability specifications, inclusion of eligible dividend disclosures, the econometric exclusion of structural transition periods, adjustment for bonus share capitalization, retrospective earnings restatements, and sensitivity analysis excluding the NMB FY 2076/77 bank-year with the Kanchan acquisition timing mismatch?
\end{itemize}

\section{Objectives of the Study}
\label{sec:objectives}

The overarching general objective of this dissertation is to examine the accounting value relevance of financial information and evaluate whether the empirical relationships between accounting fundamentals and equity market prices differed before and after the 2023 commercial banking consolidation wave across five purposively selected commercial bank series in Nepal.

To achieve this general objective, the study establishes the following specific objectives:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{To estimate the baseline value relevance} of earnings per share (EPS) and book value per share (BVPS) in relation to secondary market share prices measured at a standardized post-reporting-deadline window ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$) within a two-way fixed-effects panel regression framework.
\item \textbf{To test whether the empirical valuation slopes} of EPS ($\beta_E$) and BVPS ($\beta_B$) differed between the pre-consolidation period (FY 2076/77--2078/79) and the post-consolidation period (FY 2079/80--2081/82) by evaluating interaction parameters ($\delta_E, \delta_B$) and a joint Wald coefficient test.
\item \textbf{To evaluate the econometric robustness and stability} of the value-relevance estimates across alternative specifications, including:
\begin{itemize}
\item Substituting return on assets (ROA) for EPS as an asset-scaled profitability measure;
\item Incorporating eligible cash dividend per share (DPS) declarations into the valuation equation;
\item Sensitivity analysis excluding the FY 2079/80 merger transition year to verify results are not driven by immediate consolidation shock;
\item Sensitivity analysis excluding FY 2078/79 to address entity-timing mismatch between standalone fundamentals and consolidated trading prices;
\item Re-estimation using bonus-adjusted per-share metrics to account for share capital dilutions; and
\item Re-estimation using retrospective audited basic EPS restatements from subsequent annual reports; and
\item Sensitivity analysis excluding the NMB FY 2076/77 bank-year with the Kanchan acquisition timing mismatch.
\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\section{Hypotheses of the Study}
\label{sec:hypotheses}

The research hypotheses formulated in this dissertation are directly anchored in the residual income valuation theory of \citet{ohlson1995}, the post-merger value-relevance framework of \citet{kwon2020}, and empirical findings from emerging capital markets. Under Ohlson's (1995) theoretical model, both contemporaneous earnings and book equity are expected to correlate positively with market prices because they reflect, respectively, current operating efficiency and accumulated net asset value. However, following the methodological conventions of empirical accounting, all hypotheses are tested two-tailed at the 5\% level of significance ($\alpha = 0.05$) without imposing directional signs, thereby allowing the empirical data to support or fail to support the theoretical predictions.

The hypotheses are grouped into two categories corresponding to the baseline value-relevance model (Model 1) and the consolidation-interaction model (Model 2). Hypotheses $H_1$ and $H_2$ are stated for the baseline model (Model~1) and are re-examined in the consolidation-interaction model (Model~2), where the corresponding slopes are the pre-consolidation slopes; Chapter~V reports both evaluations. Research Question~3 has no corresponding hypothesis; it is answered by the robustness suite R3--R9 (Chapter~IV), not by a hypothesis test.

\subsection{Category A: Baseline Value Relevance Hypotheses (Model 1)}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Hypothesis 1 ($H_1$):}
\begin{itemize}
\item Null hypothesis ($H_{0,1}$): Earnings per share (EPS) is not significantly associated with secondary market share price at the reporting-deadline window in the baseline two-way fixed-effects model.
\begin{equation}
H_{0,1}: \beta_E = 0
\end{equation}
\item Alternative hypothesis ($H_{a,1}$): Earnings per share (EPS) is significantly associated with secondary market share price at the reporting-deadline window in the baseline two-way fixed-effects model.
\begin{equation}
H_{a,1}: \beta_E \ne 0
\end{equation}
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Hypothesis 2 ($H_2$):}
\begin{itemize}
\item Null hypothesis ($H_{0,2}$): Book value per share (BVPS) is not significantly associated with secondary market share price at the reporting-deadline window in the baseline two-way fixed-effects model.
\begin{equation}
H_{0,2}: \beta_B = 0
\end{equation}
\item Alternative hypothesis ($H_{a,2}$): Book value per share (BVPS) is significantly associated with secondary market share price at the reporting-deadline window in the baseline two-way fixed-effects model.
\begin{equation}
H_{a,2}: \beta_B \ne 0
\end{equation}
\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Category B: Consolidation-Period Regime Shift Hypotheses (Model 2)}
In corporate reorganizations, international research suggests that post-merger integration costs, portfolio revaluation, and organizational disruption may temporarily increase earnings noise, potentially leading investors to place relatively greater valuation weight on balance-sheet book value \citep{kwon2020}. However, whether this shift manifested in Nepal's emerging banking sector remains an open empirical question. Accordingly, the interaction hypotheses test for parameter differences across periods:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Hypothesis 3 ($H_3$):}
\begin{itemize}
\item Null hypothesis ($H_{0,3}$): The slope coefficient linking earnings per share to market share price does not differ significantly between the pre-consolidation and post-consolidation periods.
\begin{equation}
H_{0,3}: \delta_E = 0
\end{equation}
\item Alternative hypothesis ($H_{a,3}$): The slope coefficient linking earnings per share to market share price differs significantly between the pre-consolidation and post-consolidation periods.
\begin{equation}
H_{a,3}: \delta_E \ne 0
\end{equation}
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Hypothesis 4 ($H_4$):}
\begin{itemize}
\item Null hypothesis ($H_{0,4}$): The slope coefficient linking book value per share to market share price does not differ significantly between the pre-consolidation and post-consolidation periods.
\begin{equation}
H_{0,4}: \delta_B = 0
\end{equation}
\item Alternative hypothesis ($H_{a,4}$): The slope coefficient linking book value per share to market share price differs significantly between the pre-consolidation and post-consolidation periods.
\begin{equation}
H_{a,4}: \delta_B \ne 0
\end{equation}
\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

The overarching econometric evaluation of structural change across the banking consolidation wave is conducted via the joint null and alternative hypotheses:
\begin{equation}
H_{0,\text{joint}}: \delta_E = 0 \text{ and } \delta_B = 0 \quad \text{versus} \quad H_{a,\text{joint}}: \delta_E \ne 0 \text{ or } \delta_B \ne 0
\end{equation}
Rejecting this joint null hypothesis provides statistical evidence that the overall accounting-price valuation relationship differed across the consolidation period. In accordance with rigorous observational econometrics, non-rejection or rejection of these parameters is interpreted strictly as observed differences in statistical associations across calendar regimes, rather than as causal impacts of bank mergers.

\section{Rationale and Significance of the Study}
\label{sec:rationale}

This dissertation is justified on theoretical, methodological, empirical, and practical grounds. As Nepal's commercial banking sector emerges from the structural consolidation wave of 2022--2023, understanding how capital markets perceive and price accounting disclosures is critical for financial transparency, capital allocation, and regulatory design. The specific rationale and significance of this research include:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Theoretical and Conceptual Contribution:}
While the residual income valuation framework of \citet{ohlson1995} has been extensively tested in developed equity markets, its applicability in frontier environments undergoing rapid structural transformation remains underexplored. By synthesizing Ohlson's (1995) valuation model with the post-merger value-relevance framework of \citet{kwon2020}, this study provides a coherent theoretical structure for assessing how corporate amalgamations alter the relative informational roles of income-statement flows (earnings) versus balance-sheet stocks (book value). It grounds the empirical valuation of Nepalese banks within formal accounting valuation theory.

\item \textbf{Methodological Rigor and Standardization of Observation Timing:}
A major contribution of this study is the development and implementation of an institutionally grounded, reproducible price-measurement protocol ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$). By operationalizing the statutory nine-month reporting deadline under Section 59(2) of BAFIA at the end of Chaitra (mid-April), the study addresses the temporal flaw in Bhattarai (2014), which paired Ashadh-end market prices with financial reports published up to nine months later \citep{bhattarai2014}. In the two bank-years for which publication dates could be verified, audited financial highlights and dividend declarations were published between Ashwin and Poush (October to January), with formal AGM proceedings concluded prior to the end-of-Chaitra reporting deadline operationalized in this study; for the remaining 28 bank-years the actual public release date is not verified. Observing market prices at the first trading session on or after the Chaitra deadline means the accounts were legally required to be in the public domain under BAFIA Section 59(2) prior to price observation, while avoiding the measurement error and selective leakage inherent in attempting to reconstruct unverified corporate press-release timestamps across historical periods.

Furthermore, the explicit formulation of a 5-day freshness rule---under which trading-suspended observations are treated as missing rather than imputed from stale dates---maintains empirical transparency and eliminates stale price contamination.

\item \textbf{Econometric Integrity through Exclusion of Mechanical Multiples:}
This dissertation explicitly corrects the pervasive econometric specification error in Nepalese literature: the inclusion of P/E ratios alongside EPS in models predicting share prices. By excluding price-derived multiples and demonstrating the severe collinearity between EPS and ROA ($r = 0.975$, yielding a bivariate variance inflation factor of approximately 20), this study avoids the mechanical simultaneity bias inherent in price-deflated multiple regressions. It demonstrates why ROA must be evaluated as an alternative substitute rather than an additive regressor, preventing spurious statistical inferences.

\item \textbf{Practical Significance for Capital Market Stakeholders:}
The findings of this study provide actionable insights for diverse capital market participants:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Individual and Institutional Investors:} Informs investors on NEPSE regarding the comparative valuation relevance of earnings versus book values across structural consolidation periods, highlighting the valuation noise associated with transitional merger accounting.
\item \textbf{Commercial Bank Management and Boards of Directors:} Informs bank executives regarding the capital-market consequences of earnings volatility, dividend payout stability, and balance-sheet transparency following corporate amalgamations.
\item \textbf{Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB):} Offers the central bank empirical observations on how secondary equity markets evaluate capital and earnings metrics surrounding policy-driven bank consolidations, assisting in the design of future prudential capital and reporting guidelines.
\item \textbf{Securities Board of Nepal (SEBON) and NEPSE:} Highlights the critical importance of timely corporate disclosures, centralized announcement timestamp databases, and the market-distorting effects of prolonged merger-related trading suspensions.
\item \textbf{Academic Researchers:} Provides a documented, reproducible dataset and reproducibility appendix (Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}), rigorous two-way fixed-effects methodology, and robust finite-sample econometric protocols for future researchers investigating financial-sector dynamics in South Asia.
\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations of the Study}
\label{sec:limitations}

To maintain academic integrity and assist readers in interpreting the findings within their proper context, the methodological, institutional, and empirical boundaries of this research are explicitly acknowledged. In accordance with the \textit{Master's Degree Dissertation Guidelines (2019)} of the Faculty of Management, a clear distinction is maintained between the scope of the study (which defines its deliberate boundaries) and the limitations that arise within that scope:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Small Purposive Sample and Sectoral Generalizability:}
The study's scope is confined to five purposively selected Class 'A' commercial bank series observed across six fiscal years (FY 2076/77 to FY 2081/82). While these five institutions comprise four merged series from the central corporate combinations of the 2023 consolidation wave alongside an unmerged benchmark that did not participate in the wave, the sample cannot be generalized to the entire commercial banking industry, development banks, finance companies, or non-financial listed sectors. Findings apply directly to the selected institutions and their specific consolidation contexts.

\item \textbf{Realized Sample Attrition Due to Trading Suspensions:}
Although primary audited annual financial statements were complete for all 30 target bank-year observations, prolonged merger-related trading suspensions rendered two pre-consolidation market-price observations ineligible under the 5-day freshness window: Himalayan Bank FY 2077/78 (merger-related trading suspension until 6 June 2022; 54 days late) and Nepal Investment Bank FY 2077/78 (merger-related trading suspension until 30 May 2022; 47 days late). Consequently, following strict research protocol, these two observations are coded as missing rather than imputed from stale pre-suspension prices, reducing the clean estimation sample to $N = 28$ observations (13 pre-consolidation, 15 post-consolidation).

\item \textbf{Econometric Fragility and Finite-Sample Constraints:}
Estimating two-way fixed effects (bank fixed effects $\mu_i$ and fiscal-year fixed effects $\lambda_t$) in conjunction with four slope parameters on a sample of $N = 28$ observations leaves exactly $28 - 14 = 14$ residual degrees of freedom in Model 2. Furthermore, standard cluster-robust standard errors fail to perform reliably when the number of clusters is very small ($G = 5$) \citep{cameron2008clusters}. HC3 adjusts the estimated covariance for heteroskedasticity and leverage; it does not make small-sample inference automatically reliable. All statistical tests must therefore be interpreted as exploratory indicators rather than definitive asymptotic proofs.

\item \textbf{Estimator Dependence of the Headline Non-Rejection:}
The baseline joint test does not reject parameter stability at 5\% under the pre-specified HC3 covariance ($F(2,14)=2.954$, $p=0.085$), but the same test rejects at 5\% under the classical, HC0, HC1 and HC2 covariances for all eight tabulated joint tests R2--R9 (Chapter~IV, Table~\ref{tab:cov_sensitivity}); the baseline non-rejection is therefore specific to the HC3 estimator. Only R6 rejects at 5\% under HC3 (unadjusted $p=0.021$, Holm-adjusted $p=0.190$ across nine tests R2--R10).

\item \textbf{Statistical Power and Within-Bank Independence:}
No prospective power analysis was specified. A post hoc design-sensitivity calculation (Chapter~V) gives approximate detectable effects at 80\% power of 10.73 NPR for the Model~2 pre-period EPS slope, 12.06 NPR for the EPS interaction, and 3.71 NPR for the BVPS interaction, so the non-rejections do not rule out effects smaller than those magnitudes. In addition, the within-bank independence assumption remains untested: HC3 addresses heteroskedasticity, not within-bank correlation, and the five-bank panel is too small to support a reliable cluster-robust correction.

\item \textbf{Calendar-Period Operationalization vs. Causal Identification:}
The pre-consolidation (FY 2076/77--2078/79) and post-consolidation (FY 2079/80--2081/82) regimes are defined by calendar time. Consequently, the estimated interaction parameters ($\delta_E, \delta_B$) capture overall shifts in accounting-price associations across these temporal periods. These shifts reflect not only the internal corporate effects of banking consolidation, but also contemporaneous macroeconomic shifts, interest rate fluctuations, NEPSE broad-index cycles, and post-pandemic economic adjustments. The research design does not identify the isolated causal impact of mergers.

\item \textbf{Entity-Timing Mismatch in FY 2078/79:}
Because the four merging banks commenced joint operations in January--February 2023 (mid-FY 2079/80), their FY 2078/79 audited financial accounts represent pre-merger standalone operations, whereas their post-reporting-deadline market prices observed in mid-April 2023 reflect post-merger consolidated trading. Although this transition mismatch is retained in the baseline models to preserve sample continuity, it represents an unavoidable institutional friction that is explicitly evaluated via sensitivity exclusions.

\item \textbf{Institutional Reporting Compliance and Information Leakage:}
The standardized reporting window relies on the legal mandate under BAFIA Section 59(2) requiring publication within nine months into the subsequent fiscal year. Actual publication dates were verified in the reviewed records for only 2 of the 30 bank-years; for the remaining observations, the statutory deadline does not establish when the information became public. Any unrecorded informal information leakage prior to public announcements also remains unobserved. The five-day freshness cutoff is an operational rule, and its sensitivity is material: including the two delayed observations changes the Model 2 joint-test result to $F(2,16)=4.762$ ($p=0.024$). The excluded observations arose during prolonged merger-related suspensions, so the missingness mechanism cannot be assumed independent of the study context; its direction and form are not established here.
\end{itemize}

\section{Organization of the Study}
\label{sec:organization}

This dissertation is organized into five chapters, structured in accordance with the \textit{Master's Degree Dissertation Guidelines (2019)} of the Faculty of Management, Tribhuvan University. \section{Theoretical Review}
\label{sec:theoretical_review}

\subsection{The Residual Income Valuation (RIV) Framework}
\label{subsec:ohlson_framework}

In financial economics and accounting theory, equity valuation has evolved from dividend-discounting formulations into models grounded in fundamental accounting numbers. The seminal theoretical breakthrough linking financial accounting disclosures directly to equity market values was established by \citet{ohlson1995} and extended by \citet{feltham1995}. The Residual Income Valuation (RIV) framework---often designated as the Edwards-Bell-Ohlson (EBO) model---reconceptualizes firm value by expressing the market value of common equity as the sum of currently recognized book value of equity and the present value of expected future residual income (abnormal earnings).

The mathematical foundation of the Ohlson framework rests on four interrelated structural building blocks:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The Present Value of Expected Dividends (PVED):}
Consistent with neoclassical asset pricing theory, the intrinsic market value of a firm's common equity at date $t$ ($P_t$) equals the discounted present value of all expected future net cash distributions (dividends, $D_{t+\tau}$) paid to equity holders:
\begin{equation}
P_t = \sum_{\tau=1}^{\infty} \frac{\mathbb{E}_t[D_{t+\tau}]}{(1 + r)^\tau}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbb{E}_t[\cdot]$ denotes the expectations operator conditioned on information available at date $t$, and $r$ represents the constant cost of equity capital (assumed strictly positive).

\item \textbf{The Clean Surplus Relation (CSR):}
The Ohlson model imposes the fundamental accounting condition of clean surplus accounting. Under CSR, all changes in the book value of equity ($BV_t$) between dates $t-1$ and $t$ must flow through the income statement as net income ($NI_t$), with the sole exception of transactions with equity holders in their capacity as owners (dividends paid, $D_t$, and capital contributions/withdrawals):
\begin{equation}
BV_t = BV_{t-1} + NI_t - D_t
\end{equation}
or equivalently:
\begin{equation}
D_t = NI_t - (BV_t - BV_{t-1})
\end{equation}
The clean surplus relation ensures accounting consistency by preventing firms from bypassing the statement of profit or loss through direct debits or credits to equity reserves.

\item \textbf{Residual Income (Abnormal Earnings) Formulation:}
Residual income (or abnormal earnings, denoted $NI_t^a$) represents the net income generated by the firm in excess of the normal return required on the beginning-of-period book equity capital:
\begin{equation}
NI_t^a = NI_t - r \cdot BV_{t-1}
\end{equation}
By substituting the clean surplus expression for dividends into the dividend-discount model, and assuming the standard regularity transversality condition that the discounted expected book value of equity approaches zero as the horizon approaches infinity ($\lim_{\tau \to \infty} (1+r)^{-\tau} \mathbb{E}_t[BV_{t+\tau}] = 0$), \citet{ohlson1995} demonstrates that the market value of equity can be expressed identically as:
\begin{equation}
P_t = BV_t + \sum_{\tau=1}^{\infty} \frac{\mathbb{E}_t[NI_{t+\tau}^a]}{(1 + r)^\tau} = BV_t + \sum_{\tau=1}^{\infty} \frac{\mathbb{E}_t[NI_{t+\tau} - r \cdot BV_{t+\tau-1}]}{(1 + r)^\tau}
\end{equation}

\item \textbf{Linear Information Dynamics and the Theoretical Valuation Equation:}
To translate this infinite-horizon residual income formulation into an empirical valuation equation containing contemporaneous accounting variables, \citet{ohlson1995} introduces linear information dynamics (LID). Specifically, current abnormal earnings ($NI_t^a$) and other non-accounting information ($v_t$) follow a stationary stochastic autoregressive process:
\begin{align}
NI_{t+1}^a &= \omega NI_t^a + v_t + \epsilon_{1,t+1} \\
v_{t+1} &= \gamma v_t + \epsilon_{2,t+1}
\end{align}
where the persistence parameters satisfy $0 \le \omega < 1$ and $0 \le \gamma < 1$, and $\epsilon_{1,t+1}, \epsilon_{2,t+1}$ are mean-zero disturbance terms.

Under these information dynamics, \citet{ohlson1995} demonstrates that the equilibrium market price of equity ($P_t$) solves as a closed-form linear function:
\begin{equation}
P_t = BV_t + \alpha_1 NI_t^a + \alpha_2 v_t
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_1 = \frac{\omega}{R_f - \omega}$, with $R_f = 1 + r$, and $\alpha_2 = \frac{R_f}{(R_f - \omega)(R_f - \gamma)}$.

Because abnormal earnings are defined with respect to beginning-of-period book value ($NI_t^a = NI_t - r \cdot BV_{t-1}$), the clean surplus relation ($BV_t = BV_{t-1} + NI_t - D_t$, or equivalently $BV_{t-1} = BV_t - NI_t + D_t$) can be substituted directly into the abnormal earnings expression:
\begin{equation}
NI_t^a = NI_t - r(BV_t - NI_t + D_t) = (1 + r)NI_t - r \cdot BV_t - r \cdot D_t
\end{equation}
Substituting this into the valuation equation yields:
\begin{equation}
P_t = BV_t + \alpha_1 \left[(1 + r)NI_t - r \cdot BV_t - r \cdot D_t\right] + \alpha_2 v_t
\end{equation}
Collecting terms on contemporaneous book value ($BV_t$) and net income ($NI_t$) yields the canonical structural form:
\begin{equation}
P_t = (1 - k) BV_t + k (\phi \cdot NI_t - D_t) + \alpha_2 v_t
\end{equation}
where $k = r \cdot \alpha_1 = \frac{r \omega}{R_f - \omega}$ and $\phi = \frac{R_f}{r} = \frac{1+r}{r}$.

Hence, the exact theoretical coefficient on contemporaneous book value of equity is:
\begin{equation}
(1 - k) = 1 - \frac{r \omega}{1 + r - \omega} = \frac{(1 + r)(1 - \omega)}{1 + r - \omega} > 0
\end{equation}
and the theoretical coefficient on contemporaneous net income is:
\begin{equation}
k \cdot \phi = \frac{\omega(1 + r)}{1 + r - \omega} \ge 0 \quad (\text{strictly positive for } \omega > 0)
\end{equation}

When moving from this analytical formulation to empirical testing, the accounting value-relevance literature follows \citet{collins1997} by estimating a linear approximation that omits the unobservable information term ($v_t$) and impounds dividend effects into the constant and error term, scaling variables on a per-share basis:
\begin{equation}
P_{it} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \text{EPS}_{it} + \beta_2 \text{BVPS}_{it} + \varepsilon_{it}
\end{equation}
where earnings per share ($\text{EPS}_{it}$) serves as the empirical proxy for contemporaneous earnings (with abnormal earning capacity impounded into the structural parameters), and book value per share ($\text{BVPS}_{it}$) serves as the empirical proxy for cumulative net resources.
\end{enumerate}

The Ohlson framework demonstrates that earnings and book value are not arbitrary ad hoc variables selected for regression convenience; rather, they serve as theoretically motivated and complementary inputs in equity pricing. Book value represents the accumulated historical stock of equity net assets, providing an asset-backing floor, while earnings capture current operational flows and abnormal earning power. The theoretical coefficients are direct functions of earnings persistence ($\omega$). As earnings persistence increases, the valuation weight placed on earnings expands. Conversely, if earnings become transitory, noisy, or disrupted by non-recurring events, the persistence parameter $\omega$ declines, leading the market to shift its valuation weight toward balance-sheet net worth.

\subsection{Valuation Dynamics Across Corporate Reorganizations and M\&A}
\label{subsec:ma_valuation}

In the extant literature, an accounting amount is defined as value relevant if it has a predicted association with equity market values \citep{barth2001}. This dissertation adopts that definition: earnings per share and book value per share are value relevant to the extent that they predict the market price per share of Nepalese commercial banks. Whether such associations inform standard setting is itself contested: \citet{holthausen2001} conclude that value-relevance research offers little insight for standard setters, while \citet{barth2001} argue that it assesses how well accounting amounts reflect information used by equity investors and provides insights into questions of interest to standard setters.

A central theoretical question in accounting value relevance is whether the valuation multipliers attached to earnings and book values remain constant across major structural corporate reorganizations. In corporate amalgamations and acquisitions, the economic and reporting structure of the enterprise undergoes fundamental reconfiguration.

Investigating the valuation consequences of corporate combinations, \citet{kwon2020} examine changes in the value relevance of accounting information following mergers and acquisitions (M\&A). Drawing on their finding that investors shift valuation weight from earnings to book value after M\&A, the thesis identifies four channels through which corporate combinations alter the reporting environment:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Transitory Integration Costs and Earnings Noise:} Post-merger operational integration requires extensive administrative expenditures, harmonization of operating systems, restructuring of branch networks, and rationalization of redundant human resources. These integration expenses generate substantial transitory, non-recurring accounting charges that temporarily suppress reported net income.
\item \textbf{Accounting Harmonization and Portfolio Revaluation:} Merging disparate corporate entities requires harmonizing accounting policies across previously independent balance sheets. In commercial banking, this entails realigning loan classification standards, reassessing non-performing loans (NPLs), unifying impairment provisioning methodologies under NFRS 9 / IFRS 9, and recognizing purchase price allocation adjustments and goodwill.
\item \textbf{Earnings Persistence Impairment:} Because transitional accounting charges and post-merger adjustments are inherently temporary and do not reflect ongoing operational earning capacity, the time-series persistence of reported earnings ($\omega$) declines sharply in the immediate post-merger period.
\item \textbf{Shift Toward Balance-Sheet Anchoring:} Following the residual income logic of \citet{ohlson1995} and the empirical evidence of \citet{collins1997}, when earnings quality and persistence deteriorate due to non-recurring organizational shocks, rational equity investors discount current earnings as a noisy indicator of future performance. Consequently, market participants place greater valuation weight on the stock of net assets---book value per share (BVPS)---which serves as an anchor of fundamental solvency and net worth.
\end{itemize}

\citet{collins1997} similarly demonstrate across a forty-one-year sequence of annual cross-sectional regressions that the relative value relevance of earnings versus book values shifts systematically: the incremental relevance of earnings declines while that of book value increases, driven by higher incidences of negative earnings, non-recurring charges, and intangible asset intensity. These theoretical insights provide the direct intellectual rationale for testing parameter shifts ($\delta_E, \delta_B$) across Nepal's 2023 banking consolidation wave.

\subsection{Neoclassical Valuation Benchmarks: Dividend Capitalization Models}
\label{subsec:dividend_models}

To provide analytical context to accounting valuation, classical financial theory formulated equity pricing around cash dividend distributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The Dividend Irrelevance Proposition:} \citet{miller1961} proved that in a frictionless capital market characterized by zero transaction costs, absence of corporate and personal taxes, symmetric information, and a given corporate investment policy, the dividend payout policy of a firm has no effect on its total market valuation or cost of capital. Firm value is determined solely by its investment decisions and the earning power of its assets. In emerging markets, however, frictionless market assumptions are fundamentally violated by capital market imperfections, asymmetric information, and regulatory mandates.
\item \textbf{Dividend Discount Formulations:} \citet{walter1963} relaxed frictionless assumptions by modelling share price as a function of the internal rate of return on retained earnings ($r_a$) relative to the cost of capital ($k_e$). Walter demonstrated that when $r_a > k_e$ (growth firms), retaining earnings maximizes share value, whereas when $r_a < k_e$, maximizing dividend payout enhances shareholder wealth. \citet{gordon1959} formulated the constant-growth dividend capitalization model:
\begin{equation}
P_0 = \frac{D_1}{k_e - g}
\end{equation}
positing that external investors discount retained earnings more heavily than current dividends due to risk aversion regarding uncertain future capital gains (the ``bird-in-the-hand'' hypothesis).
\end{itemize}

In the Nepalese commercial banking sector, dividend announcements---comprising both cash dividends and capitalized bonus shares---have historically served as vital valuation signals. While the baseline valuation model follows \citet{collins1997} in focusing on earnings and book value, evaluating cash dividends (DPS) in an expanded specification serves as an essential empirical robustness test (Specification R4) to examine whether payout distributions alter the fundamental valuation coefficients.

\subsection{Information Asymmetry, Signaling, and Agency Theories in Banking}
\label{subsec:signaling_agency}

Financial institutions operate in an environment characterized by acute informational friction between bank managers, external equity investors, and regulatory bodies:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Signaling Theory:} \citet{ross1977}, \citet{bhattacharya1979}, and \citet{john1985} demonstrate that in capital markets plagued by information asymmetry, financial disclosures, dividend announcements, and corporate restructuring decisions serve as costly, credible signals conveying management's private expectations regarding future performance. Because weaker firms cannot afford to mimic costly dividend commitments or stringent audited reporting standards without courting financial distress, published audited accounting statements and declared dividends convey authentic fundamental value.
\item \textbf{Agency Theory:} \citet{jensen1976} formulate agency theory, and \citet{jensen1986} formulates the free cash flow theory, positing that corporate managers have incentives to accumulate resources and pursue empire-building acquisitions rather than returning excess capital to shareholders. In commercial banking, declared cash dividend distributions and transparent audited balance sheets mitigate agency conflicts by constraining managerial discretion and subjecting the institution to regular capital-market monitoring.
\item \textbf{Banking Asset Opacity and Prudential Regulation:} \citet{morgan2002} and \citet{flannery2004} establish that commercial banks are inherently more opaque than industrial corporations. Bank assets consist primarily of unquoted private commercial loans whose true quality and credit risk are shielded from public observation. This severe asset opacity makes statutory auditing, capital adequacy compliance, and standardized regulatory reporting deadlines indispensable for credible market valuation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Efficient Market Hypothesis and the Information Content of Disclosures}
\label{subsec:emh_pead}

The Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH), formulated by \citet{fama1970}, posits that asset prices in an efficient financial market fully reflect all available information. Under the semi-strong form of market efficiency, equity share prices adjust rapidly and unbiasedly to all publicly available information, including audited annual financial accounts, earnings releases, and dividend declarations.

Seminal empirical accounting research by \citet{ball1968} and \citet{beaver1968} established that published accounting earnings possess substantial information content: stock returns systematically adjust in the direction of unexpected earnings announcements. However, subsequent empirical research across global equity markets---most notably documented by \citet{bernard1989}---identified the phenomenon of \textit{post-earnings-announcement drift} (PEAD), wherein equity prices continue to adjust with a temporal lag to published accounting disclosures due to investor underreaction and information processing delays.

In frontier emerging markets such as the Nepal Stock Exchange (NEPSE), market efficiency is further constrained by illiquidity, retail-dominated trading, delayed dissemination of corporate filings, and---based on the disclosures examined for this study---the absence of a unified electronic corporate announcement timestamp registry. These realities introduce critical timing considerations into value-relevance research. If market prices are observed at fiscal-year-end (Ashadh-end / mid-July), they may precede the auditing, board adoption, NRB approval, and public release of annual reports, creating a temporal mismatch. Observing prices after the statutory reporting deadline provides a standardized rule, but it does not verify the public release date for every bank-year or establish when investors first received the information.

\subsection{Econometric Circularity of Price-Derived Multiples}
\label{subsec:pe_circularity}

A critical theoretical and econometric problem that pervades empirical finance literature in Nepal is the erroneous inclusion of price-derived valuation multiples---specifically the Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratio---as explanatory independent variables in regressions where Market Price per Share (MPS) is the dependent variable.

The P/E ratio is algebraically defined as:
\begin{equation}
\text{P/E}_{it} = \frac{\text{MPS}_{it}}{\text{EPS}_{it}}
\end{equation}
Specifying an empirical regression model of the form:
\begin{equation}
\text{MPS}_{it} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \text{EPS}_{it} + \beta_2 \text{P/E}_{it} + \beta_3 \text{BVPS}_{it} + \varepsilon_{it}
\end{equation}
introduces a fatal structural circularity. The dependent variable ($\text{MPS}_{it}$) appears directly in the numerator of the explanatory variable ($\text{P/E}_{it}$). Mathematically:
\begin{equation}
\text{MPS}_{it} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \text{EPS}_{it} + \beta_2 \left(\frac{\text{MPS}_{it}}{\text{EPS}_{it}}\right) + \beta_3 \text{BVPS}_{it} + \varepsilon_{it}
\end{equation}
Rearranging this equation reveals that $\text{MPS}_{it}$ is being regressed on a function of itself. This specification generates severe mechanical endogeneity, violates the fundamental Gauss-Markov assumption of orthogonality between regressors and error terms ($\mathbb{E}[\text{P/E}_{it} \varepsilon_{it}] \ne 0$), creates artificial multi-collinearity, and inflates the coefficient of determination ($R^2$) through definitional overlap rather than economic explanation. Consequently, theoretical econometric integrity requires that all price-derived multiples be strictly excluded from value-relevance specifications, restricting explanatory regressors to pure accounting fundamentals that are determined independently of secondary market trading prices.

\section{Review of Legal, Regulatory, and Policy Framework in Nepal}
\label{sec:regulatory_framework}

The commercial banking sector and capital market of Nepal operate under a highly structured statutory and prudential regulatory regime:

\subsection{Evolution of Capital Market Regulation: SEC, NEPSE, and SEBON}
\label{subsec:nepse_history}

The institutional development of Nepal's organized capital market commenced with the establishment of the Securities Exchange Centre in 1976 (2033 BS) under the joint sponsorship of Nepal Rastra Bank and the Nepal Industrial Development Corporation (NIDC). The enactment of the Securities Exchange Act, 1983 provided the initial statutory foundation for securities transactions. In 1993, under comprehensive financial sector liberalization programs, the Securities Exchange Centre was restructured into the Nepal Stock Exchange (NEPSE) Limited as the licensed operator of the secondary market, commencing formal floor trading on 13 January 1994 (29 Poush 2050 BS) \citep{nepse2017}. Simultaneously, the Securities Board of Nepal (SEBON) was constituted as the apex capital market regulator to oversee market integrity, protect investor interests, and regulate corporate disclosures \citep{nepse2017}.

\subsection{The Statutory Financial Reporting Framework: BAFIA 2017}
\label{subsec:bafia_framework}

Commercial banks in Nepal operate under the dual regulatory oversight of the Companies Act, 2006 (2063 BS) and the Banks and Financial Institutions Act, 2017 (2073 BS) (BAFIA), alongside prudential directives issued by Nepal Rastra Bank \citep{companiesact2006,bafia2017}.

Regarding financial reporting disclosure, Section 59 of BAFIA, 2017 establishes statutory submission and publication obligations for licensed institutions. Specifically, Section 59(2) delineates two distinct reporting duties:
\begin{enumerate}
\item First, a licensed bank must prepare its balance sheet and profit and loss account and submit them to Nepal Rastra Bank within three months of the completion of each financial year, publishing summary details for the information of the general public within that period as prescribed by NRB; and
\item Second, the bank must publish its audited balance sheet and profit and loss account for the public in the prescribed format within nine months into the subsequent financial year \citep{bafia2017}.
\end{enumerate}

Because commercial bank fiscal years in Nepal terminate at Ashadh-end (mid-July), counting nine months into the subsequent financial year (from Shrawan through Chaitra) terminates at the end of Chaitra (mid-April). Operationalizing the price observation window at the first trading session on or after the end of Chaitra ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$) is the study's institutional operationalization of the statutory nine-month timeline under Section 59(2). In the two bank-years for which publication dates could be verified, commercial bank boards released audited annual highlights and announced dividend recommendations between Ashwin and Poush (October to January), with formal adoption at Annual General Meetings (AGMs) concluded prior to the end-of-Chaitra cutoff; for the remaining 28 bank-years the actual public release date is not verified. Operationalizing the price observation window at the end of Chaitra provides a reproducible, legally anchored cutoff after the statutory deadline. This reduces date-selection ambiguity but does not verify the actual public release date for each bank-year or eliminate possible information leakage.

\subsection{NRB Capital Mandates and the Consolidation Directive}
\label{subsec:nrb_consolidation}

The structural consolidation of Nepalese commercial banks has been driven primarily by explicit central bank policy mandates:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The 2011 Merger Bylaws:} In 2011 (2068 BS), NRB promulgated the \textit{Bank and Financial Institutions Merger Bylaws}, introducing legal provisions, regulatory exemptions, and procedural guidelines for voluntary mergers \citep{nrb2011}.
\item \textbf{The 2015 Capital Expansion Mandate:} In its Monetary Policy for FY 2015/16 (2072/73 BS), NRB executed an aggressive systemic reform, mandating that all Class 'A' commercial banks quadruple their minimum paid-up equity capital from NPR 2.0 billion to NPR 8.0 billion by the end of FY 2073/74 (mid-July 2017) \citep{nrb2015}. To comply, commercial banks capitalized reserves through extensive bonus share distributions and executed heavy rights share issues, resulting in substantial share capital expansion and subsequent dilution of per-share earnings.
\item \textbf{The 2022--2023 Consolidation Wave:} Recognizing that capital expansion alone had not reduced the excessive number of banking institutions, NRB actively encouraged horizontal ``big mergers'' through moral suasion and regulatory forbearance (such as temporary leniency on Credit-to-Deposit ratios and branch overlapping) \citep{tht2023nrbmerger}; merging banks and financial institutions also received tax reliefs under Section 47A of the Income Tax Act, 2058 \citep{incometaxact2058}.
\end{itemize}

This regulatory drive culminated in the systemic consolidation wave of early 2023 (mid-FY 2079/80), wherein major commercial banks executed statutory mergers and acquisitions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Global IME Bank Limited (GBIME):} Amalgamated with Bank of Kathmandu Limited (swap ratio 1:1) \citep{gbime2023report}; unified commercial operations commenced on 9 January 2023 \citep{nrb2023bsr}.
\item \textbf{Himalayan Bank Limited (HBL):} Acquired Civil Bank Limited; unified commercial operations commenced on 24 February 2023 (swap ratio 100:80.28) \citep{hbl2023report}.
\item \textbf{Kumari Bank Limited (KBL):} Amalgamated with Nepal Credit and Commerce Bank Limited (swap ratio 1:1) \citep{kumari2022sgm,kbl2023report}; unified commercial operations commenced on 1 January 2023 \citep{nrb2023bsr}.
\item \textbf{Nepal Investment Mega Bank Limited (NIMB):} Formed via the amalgamation of Nepal Investment Bank Limited and Mega Bank Nepal Limited; unified commercial operations commenced on 11 January 2023 (swap ratio 100:90) \citep{nimb2023report}.
\item \textbf{NMB Bank Limited (NMB):} Maintained continuous corporate operations without participating in the early-2023 consolidation wave, serving as the benchmark series. (Note: NMB had previously acquired Kanchan Development Bank Limited in August 2020, adding approximately NPR 0.50 billion in new shares issued to Kanchan shareholders at the 85\% swap ratio, alongside a 13\% bonus share capitalization, in FY 2077/78 \citep{nmb2021report}, but remained independent during the 2022--2023 wave).
\end{itemize}

By mid-October 2023 (Asoj 2080 BS), these amalgamations reduced the total commercial bank population in Nepal to exactly 20 Class 'A' institutions \citep{nrb2023list}.

\section{Empirical Review}
\label{sec:empirical_review}

\subsection{International Empirical Studies}
\label{subsec:intl_empirical}

Empirical accounting research across developed and emerging capital markets provides extensive documentation regarding the association between financial accounting disclosures and equity valuation:

\citet{collins1997} investigated the value relevance of earnings and book values in the United States over a forty-one-year period (1953--1993) across thousands of firm-year observations. Employing the \citet{ohlson1995} valuation framework, the authors addressed concerns that the transition from an industrialized to a service- and technology-oriented economy had degraded the relevance of historical accounting numbers. Their empirical findings demonstrated that the combined explanatory power ($R^2$) of earnings and book values had actually increased over the forty-one-year period. However, they observed a systematic shift in relative explanatory weights: the incremental value relevance of earnings declined, while the incremental value relevance of book value increased, driven by higher incidences of negative earnings, non-recurring charges, and intangible asset intensity.

\citet{kwon2020} investigated whether the value relevance of accounting information shifts following corporate mergers and acquisitions (M\&A) in the United States, using a panel spanning pre- and post-merger observations under SFAS 141(R). Their empirical results demonstrated that M\&A transactions significantly alter the relative valuation roles of earnings and book value. Specifically, during the post-merger integration period, the value relevance of earnings declined while the value relevance of book value per share increased markedly, with the shift particularly strong after the adoption of SFAS 141(R). The thesis interprets this shift as consistent with equity investors relying on balance-sheet net worth as a valuation anchor during corporate reorganization.

In emerging Asian equity markets, \citet{sharma2011} investigated the empirical relationship between accounting fundamentals and equity share prices across different industry groups in India over 1993/94 to 2008/09. The study found statistically significant associations between earnings per share (EPS), dividend per share (DPS), book value per share (BVPS), and share prices, with EPS and DPS identified as the strongest determinants. Sharma also tested the price-earnings ratio, dividend yield, dividend payout, and firm size (measured by sales and net worth) as explanatory variables. \citet{malhotra2013} examined 95 firms listed on the National Stock Exchange of India (NSE 100 index) from 2007 to 2012, demonstrating that book value per share, earnings per share, and price-earnings multiples significantly influenced market prices, while dividend yield had a significant inverse association. In the Middle East, \citet{almumani2014} evaluated listed commercial banks on the Amman Stock Exchange (ASE) over 2005--2011 using multivariate regression analysis, concluding that EPS, BVPS, and price-earnings ratios were the significant positive determinants of market price, with EPS showing the highest bivariate correlation ($r = 0.84$), while bank size had a significant inverse association. Dividend per share and dividend payout (DP) were also tested but had insignificant effects.

\subsection{Foundational Nepalese Empirical Literature (Early to Mid Studies)}
\label{subsec:foundational_nepal}

Empirical investigation into equity valuation determinants in Nepal originated alongside the formalization of NEPSE trading:

\citet{pradhan2003} conducted one of the earliest empirical evaluations of stock market behavior in Nepal, examining listed enterprises over 1994--1999. Employing descriptive and correlational techniques, Pradhan investigated the relative effects of cash dividends and retained earnings on stock prices. The study documented that cash dividends exhibited a stronger, more statistically significant positive association with market share prices than retained earnings, concluding that Nepalese equity investors exhibited strong cash-dividend preference in an environment characterized by institutional opacity and market volatility.

\citet{bhattarai2014} investigated the determinants of share prices across a sample of nine commercial banks (Nabil Bank, Nepal Investment Bank, Standard Chartered Bank Nepal, Everest Bank, Bank of Kathmandu, Machhapuchchhre Bank, Laxmi Bank, Kumari Bank, and Siddhartha Bank) over the period 2006 to 2014. Using pooled OLS regressions, Bhattarai evaluated fiscal-year-end closing market price per share against dividend payout ratio (DPR), dividend yield (DY), earnings per share (EPS), price-earnings (P/E) ratio, and bank size (SIZE). The study reported that EPS and P/E ratios exhibited statistically significant positive associations with market share prices, whereas dividend yield displayed an inverse relationship. However, as noted in Chapter~I, Bhattarai's specification explicitly included P/E as an explanatory regressor alongside MPS, inducing mechanical circularity.

\citet{pradhan2016} examined 14 commercial banks over the period 2002/03 to 2013/14 using a pooled panel regression framework. The authors analyzed the impact of firm size, earnings per share, dividend per share, price-earnings ratio, book value per share and return on assets on market equity valuation, alongside macroeconomic controls (GDP, inflation, money supply). Their empirical findings identified bank size as the most important determinant, with EPS, DPS, P/E, BVPS and ROA as major determining variables; among the macroeconomic variables, GDP was a major determinant of share prices.

\citet{bajracharya2019} evaluated twelve commercial banks listed on NEPSE over the ten-year period from 2006/07 to 2015/16 (120 bank-year observations). Using panel data analysis (random effects, Hausman p = 0.818), the authors reported that earnings per share (EPS), dividend per share (DPS), and the price-earnings (P/E) ratio had a statistically significant positive effect on share prices, whereas return on assets (ROA) was statistically insignificant in the chosen random-effects specification (p = 0.0544), and the external factor inflation had a statistically significant negative effect. Bank rate (BR) and market capitalization (Ln MC) were also included as external factors.

\citet{silwal2019} evaluated ten commercial banks across 100 bank-year observations over FY 2065/66 to 2074/75 (2008/09--2017/18). Employing correlation and multiple regression analysis, the authors examined market price per share in relation to book value per share, return on equity, P/E ratio, dividend yield, and bank size. The study concluded that BVPS exerted the largest explanatory influence on stock prices, followed by P/E and ROE. The thesis reads this balance-sheet primacy as consistent with the investor benchmark shift following NRB's 2015 capital escalation mandate (Section~\ref{subsec:nrb_consolidation}).

\subsection{Empirical Evidence from Recent Nepalese Studies (2021--2024, Pre-Wave Panels)}
\label{subsec:recent_nepal}

Recent empirical research on Nepalese commercial banking valuation documents notable divergence in empirical findings across diverse samples and econometric specifications:

\citet{niroula2021} examined 18 commercial banks over the five-year period FY 2015/16 to 2019/20 (90 bank-year observations). Using pooled regression models, Niroula found that earnings per share had a statistically significant positive impact on market share prices, as did the P/E ratio and bank size. Conversely, book value per share (BVPS) and return on equity (ROE) exhibited negative coefficients that failed to attain statistical significance, highlighting growing empirical ambiguity regarding the pricing of balance-sheet net worth. Dividend yield (DY) and return on assets (ROA) were likewise included in the model but had negligible effects. Niroula's negative, insignificant BVPS coefficient sits at the opposite pole from \citet{silwal2019}'s finding that net asset backing was the primary benchmark for Nepalese bank investors, a divergence Section~\ref{subsec:synthesis_matrix} takes up.

\citet{gyawali2022} evaluated ten commercial banks listed on NEPSE over the five-year span 2017 to 2021 (50 observations). Employing descriptive statistics, Pearson correlation, and multiple regression analysis, Gyawali observed statistically significant positive relationships of share price with EPS, DPS, and the P/E ratio. However, the regression coefficient for return on assets (ROA) was statistically insignificant, suggesting that secondary equity markets discounted accounting return metrics in favor of per-share distributions. GDP had a positive but insignificant effect on share prices, while inflation had a negative and insignificant effect. Gyawali's statistically insignificant ROA coefficient contrasts directly with \citet{chaudhari2025}'s documented significant negative ROA association, illustrating the empirical instability of asset-productivity indicators across Nepalese banking panels.

\citet{chhetri2023} investigated 13 commercial banks over an eleven-year panel spanning 2012 to 2022 (143 bank-year observations) using a random-effects panel model (Hausman test p = 1.0000). Chhetri regressed market share price on EPS, P/E, BVPS, ROA, firm size (LNFS), inflation, GDP and broad money supply. The empirical results indicated that EPS, BVPS, ROA, P/E, firm size and inflation were positive and statistically significant determinants of market share prices (EPS p = 0.0062), while GDP and broad money supply were insignificant.

\citet{joshi2023} analyzed seven commercial banks across 70 bank-year observations over FY 2012/13 to 2021/22. Using correlation and linear panel regression techniques, the authors evaluated share price behavior against EPS, DPS, BVPS, and P/E ratios. Their findings indicated that all four explanatory variables exhibited statistically significant positive coefficients, with the P/E ratio and EPS demonstrating the highest explanatory power in predicting NEPSE market prices. Joshi et al.'s finding of uniformly positive and statistically significant coefficients across all four explanatory metrics is consistent with \citet{chhetri2023}, which likewise finds EPS and BVPS significant positive over a comparable observation window.

\citet{lamsal2024} evaluated seven commercial banks listed on NEPSE over FY 2011/12 to 2020/21, examining market price per share in relation to EPS, P/E, DPS, and BVPS. The correlation results showed significant positive associations between share prices and all four variables, strongest for DPS ($r = 0.846$, $p < 0.01$), and the multiple regression found EPS, DPS, and P/E significant positive (EPS $B = 19.307$, DPS $B = 14.486$, P/E $B = 32.308$, all $p = 0.001$), while BVPS was negative and insignificant ($B = -0.785$, $p = 0.097$). The paper's abstract and discussion claim an inverse DPS relationship, but that claim contradicts its own Tables 2 and 4, and the table evidence is followed here.

\citet{giri2024} conducted an empirical investigation into four commercial banks across 40 bank-year observations as stated in the paper (its ANOVA total degrees of freedom of 35 imply 36 observations entered the regression) over the decade FY 2070/71 to 2079/80 (2013/14--2022/23). Employing descriptive, correlational and multiple regression analysis, Giri evaluated share prices against DPS, EPS, P/E, and net worth per share (BVPS). The analysis revealed significant positive correlations between market price and DPS, P/E, and net worth per share, whereas the bivariate correlation with EPS was statistically negligible, reinforcing the argument that cash dividends and net asset backing overshadowed operational earnings among sampled banks.

\citet{gurung2024} evaluated 16 of the 19 listed commercial banks across 171 observations over the period 2007 to 2023, an unbalanced panel (16 banks over 17 years would yield 272 bank-years if balanced), using stepwise multiple regression analysis. The study found that earnings per share, book value per share, and the P/E ratio exerted statistically significant positive influences on commercial bank share prices, while dividend per share and total assets (TA) were statistically insignificant, demonstrating that market valuation was anchored in foundational balance-sheet and earning power rather than payout distributions.

\citet{bhattarai2024mps} analyzed market price alongside accounting indicators over FY 2012/13 to 2022/23 in a pooled regression covering 26 commercial banks before the merger wave and 19 after, excluding the fully government-owned Rastriya Banijya Bank. The study reported positive associations for EPS, DPS, and total assets with market price, a negative association for non-performing loans, and no statistically significant BVPS association. Although the sample spans merger-related changes in the number of banks, the reported model does not interact EPS and BVPS with a period indicator, so it does not test for slope changes across the early-2023 consolidation.

\subsection{Empirical Evidence Spanning the 2023 Consolidation Wave (2024--2026)}
\label{subsec:consolidation_studies}

The most recent body of empirical research has extended observation windows through FY 2024/25, directly capturing the post-consolidation operating environment:

\citet{pant2024} investigated seven commercial banks across 70 observations over the period 2013/14 to 2023/24, spanning the 2023 banking consolidation wave. Using multiple regression models, the authors regressed market price per share on EPS, DPS, BVPS, and P/E. The empirical findings demonstrated that EPS and P/E had statistically significant positive impacts on market share prices. Conversely, the coefficients for dividend per share and book value per share were negative and statistically insignificant. The thesis interprets this weak BVPS coefficient as consistent with merger-driven balance-sheet expansion during the consolidation wave, though Pant et al.\ do not test or discuss that mechanism. (The paper reports $N = 70$ despite describing an eleven-fiscal-year span as a ``ten-year period''; seven bank-year observations are missing from the paper's data.)

\citet{chaudhari2025} examined ten commercial banks over 2014/15 to 2023/24 (100 bank-year observations; the paper's abstract states 2013--14, but its methods and regression sections state 2014/15 with 100 observations, which is followed here). Employing pooled OLS regression, Chaudhari analyzed market price per share against firm-specific variables (EPS, DPS, ROA, bank size) and macroeconomic factors (inflation, interest rates, GDP). The empirical results revealed strong, statistically significant positive coefficients for EPS and DPS. In contrast, return on assets (ROA) displayed a statistically significant \textit{negative} association with market share prices, while bank size, GDP and interest rates failed to attain statistical significance; inflation was significant at the 5\% level ($p = 0.037$). Chaudhari concluded that equity investors penalized low-margin asset expansion while rewarding per-share earnings generation.

\citet{gurungdahal2025} investigated ten commercial banks listed on NEPSE over the eleven-year period FY 2013/14 to 2023/24 using a panel regression framework.\footnote{\citet{gurungdahal2025}, published in \textit{Pravaha} (vol.~31, no.~1, pp.~1--12), is distinct from the comprehensive panel study by \citet{dahal2025} in the \textit{Interdisciplinary Journal of Management and Social Sciences} (vol.~6, no.~1, pp.~252--261), which examined 209 bank-year observations across all listed commercial banks.} Among the studies reviewed, \citet{gurungdahal2025} provide the closest methodological precedent, because they applied the Hausman specification test and estimated a bank fixed-effects regression model to control for unobserved time-invariant bank heterogeneity. Their empirical results indicated that dividend payout ratio (DPR), P/E ratio, earnings per share, book value per share, return on assets, and stock return (SR, $\beta = 81.126$, p = 0.0346) exerted statistically significant impacts on market share prices. However, Gurung and Dahal estimated static, full-sample coefficients, omitting any formal interaction test of whether valuation parameters shifted before and after the 2023 consolidation wave.

\citet{dahal2025} examined 209 annual observations spanning FY 2013/14 to 2023/24 from all commercial banks listed on NEPSE, using descriptive statistics, correlation analysis, and multiple regression to relate market price per share to P/E, BVPS, dividend rate, earnings yield, and market-to-book ratio. They found significant positive relationships between market price and both P/E and BVPS, with market-to-book ratio, earnings yield, and dividend rate also positively correlated but less pronounced. Like most of the surveyed literature, the specification included the circular P/E regressor.

\citet{timalsina2026} evaluated 13 commercial banks over the thirteen-year panel FY 2011/12 to 2023/24 (169 bank-year observations). Using descriptive statistics, Pearson correlation, and panel regression analysis, Timalsina investigated the impact of firm-specific indicators (EPS, DPS, P/E, ROA, size) and macroeconomic factors (GDP, inflation, interest rates) on market share prices. The study's Hausman test ($p = 0.151$) favored the random-effects specification. The empirical findings revealed that EPS, DPS, P/E, firm size, and inflation had statistically significant positive associations with share prices, while interest rates exerted a significant negative effect. Return on assets (ROA) and GDP growth showed no statistically significant influence.

In the most contemporary literature, \citet{niraula2026} evaluated 16 commercial banks across 80 observations over FY 2019/20 to 2023/24 using balanced panel OLS regression. The study examined firm-specific determinants of market price per share, documenting statistically significant positive impacts for Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR) and total assets, and a positive effect for Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) significant only at the 10\% level, while ROA was positive but statistically insignificant; the weighted average interest rate spread (WAIRS) and the non-performing loan ratio (NPLR) had significant negative effects, and the credit-to-deposit ratio (CDR) a negative effect significant only at the 10\% level. \citet{bhandari2026} executed a census study of all 19 NEPSE-listed commercial banks over FY 2014/15 to 2024/25, demonstrating that earnings indicators and dividend payout served as the primary determinants of market prices, while dividend yield had a negative impact. In governance and regulatory research, \citet{bhattarai2024} evaluated whether banking regulations (bank capital, loan loss provision, spread rate, broad money supply, base rate) affect earnings per share, while \citet{dongol2024} investigated corporate governance mechanisms and bank-specific factors on bank performance (earnings per share).

Regarding banking consolidation directly, \citet{adhikari2023} conducted an empirical examination of the stock and financial performance of seven commercial banks that underwent mergers between 2013 and 2020. The authors documented that post-merger market share prices fell by 46.01\% ($p = 0.000$), while dividend per share fell by 5.07\% ($p = 0.761$) and earnings per share were essentially unchanged (+0.12\%, $p = 0.992$), neither statistically significant. In a companion study, \citet{adhikari2023cbr} analyzed the comparative financial performance of merged commercial banks, finding that consolidation failed to generate immediate operational synergies or profitability improvements: liquidity and leverage ratios improved significantly, while profitability and shareholder-wealth ratios showed mixed or insignificant results.

\citet{acharya2026merger} extend this merger-performance literature with a comparison of merged banks and non-merged peers. They report heterogeneous post-merger outcomes and no consistent profitability gain for merged banks relative to non-merged peers. Their focus is merger-related financial performance; like the Adhikari studies, it does not test whether EPS-price and BVPS-price slopes changed across the consolidation period.

\subsection{Synthesis of Empirical Literature}
\label{subsec:synthesis_matrix}

A critical evaluation of the international and domestic empirical literature reveals four major thematic debates and methodological tensions that characterize commercial bank valuation:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The Earning Power versus Net Asset Value Valuation Anchor:}
A central empirical debate across Nepalese commercial banking literature concerns whether market share prices are primarily driven by current operating profitability (EPS) or balance-sheet equity backing (BVPS). Among the fifteen studies in Table~\ref{tab:lit_synthesis} that test both EPS and BVPS, results diverge: for example, \citet{niroula2021} finds EPS significantly positive with BVPS negative and insignificant, while \citet{giri2024} finds net worth per share significant positive with EPS insignificant; by contrast, \citet{joshi2023}, \citet{gurung2024} and \citet{chhetri2023} find both positive and significant, and \citet{pant2024} replicates the Niroula pattern. Studies that report EPS as the predominant driver---\citet{bhandari2026} and \citet{chaudhari2025}---did not include BVPS in their models, and \citet{silwal2019}, which reports BVPS as the primary influence, did not test EPS, so those studies cannot support an EPS-versus-BVPS comparison. This divergence reflects institutional and temporal context: following NRB's 2015 fourfold paid-up capital mandate, equity bases expanded rapidly through bonus share capitalizations, temporarily elevating balance-sheet asset backing as an investor benchmark. However, as capital bases stabilized, operational earning capacity re-emerged as the primary valuation slope, until the 2022--2023 consolidation wave again restructured balance-sheet equity.

\item \textbf{Econometric Endogeneity and Mechanical Circularity in Valuation Multiples:}
A pervasive methodological flaw across the surveyed literature is the routine inclusion of the Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratio as an independent explanatory variable in regressions modelling Market Price per Share (MPS) \citep{almumani2014,bajracharya2019,bhandari2026,bhattarai2014,chhetri2023,dahal2025,giri2024,gurung2024,gurungdahal2025,gyawali2022,joshi2023,lamsal2024,malhotra2013,niroula2021,pant2024,pradhan2016,sharma2011,silwal2019,timalsina2026}. Because $\text{P/E} \equiv \text{MPS} / \text{EPS}$, regressing market price on P/E introduces mechanical endogeneity and algebraic simultaneity bias, artificially inflating $R^2$ values and distorting true fundamental regression slopes. A defensible design therefore excludes price-derived ratios and restricts explanatory variables to fundamental accounting measures determined independently of secondary market share prices.

\item \textbf{Temporal Misalignment and Look-Ahead Bias in Price Measurement:}
\citet{bhattarai2014} measures equity share prices at fiscal-year-end (Ashadh-end / mid-July). Under Nepalese corporate reporting reality, audited annual financial accounts are not published at Ashadh-end; they require statutory auditing, NRB prudential clearance, and annual general meeting (AGM) adoption, typically spanning three to nine months into the subsequent fiscal year. Conditioning Ashadh-end market prices on accounting figures that were not publicly disclosed until months later violates the informational sequence of market efficiency, creating severe look-ahead bias and spurious associations.

\item \textbf{Panel Heterogeneity and the Static Pooling Assumption:}
With few exceptions, extant Nepalese banking studies employ pooled or static panel regressions across multi-year panels, implicitly assuming parameter homogeneity across disparate institutions and stability across time. While recent work by \citet{gurungdahal2025} introduces bank fixed effects to control for time-invariant bank heterogeneity, almost all existing studies estimate static, full-period average coefficients. Consequently, to the best of the researcher's knowledge and within the corpus of studies reviewed, no empirical study has evaluated whether fundamental valuation slopes shifted across the structural regime change induced by Nepal's 2023 commercial banking consolidation wave.
\end{itemize}

To synthesize the empirical literature directly informing this dissertation's econometric framework, Table~\ref{tab:lit_synthesis} presents a comparative synthesis of primary empirical studies. Selection is restricted to foundational theoretical frameworks and empirical studies whose dependent variable is market price per share (MPS) and which evaluate fundamental accounting indicators across commercial banks, with three foundational emerging-market benchmarks---\citet{sharma2011}, \citet{malhotra2013}, and \citet{almumani2014}---included because they establish the EPS/BVPS price relationship in comparable markets, and an early Nepalese study of listed firms generally, \citet{pradhan2003}, included as a benchmark. Studies of governance, capital adequacy regulation, or standalone financial performance---\citet{bhattarai2024}, \citet{dongol2024}, \citet{adhikari2023cbr}, and \citet{acharya2026merger}, none of which specifies market price per share as the dependent variable in the work reviewed here---are therefore excluded from the matrix.

\section{Research Gap}
\label{sec:research_gap}

A review of the theoretical foundations, regulatory frameworks, and empirical literature reveals five key conceptual, methodological, and econometric gaps that motivate this dissertation:

\subsection{The Consolidation Regime-Shift Gap}
Almost without exception, existing Nepalese banking studies employ pooled cross-sectional or static panel regression designs that estimate sample-average, full-period relationships \citep{bhattarai2014,chaudhari2025,chhetri2023,timalsina2026}. These models implicitly assume parameter constancy across time. Early 2023 witnessed five commercial banking combinations that altered reporting boundaries, asset bases, and capital structures. This study purposively samples four of those combinations alongside an unmerged benchmark \citep{nrb2023bsr}. Following the theoretical framework of \citet{kwon2020} and \citet{collins1997}, major corporate amalgamations impair earnings persistence through integration costs and loan harmonization, suggesting a possible shift in valuation weight toward balance-sheet equity. Despite this theoretical precedent, to the best of the researcher's knowledge and within the extensive corpus of studies reviewed, none has formulated an explicit regime-interaction econometric model to test (in a two-tailed framework) whether the valuation coefficients of EPS ($\beta_E$) and BVPS ($\beta_B$) differed between the pre-consolidation (FY 2076/77--2078/79) and post-consolidation (FY 2079/80--2081/82) periods.

\subsection{The Specification and Endogeneity Gap (Circularity of P/E Multiples)}
A pervasive practice in the Nepalese empirical literature is the routine inclusion of the Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratio as an independent regressor in models predicting Market Price per Share (MPS) \citep{almumani2014,bajracharya2019,bhandari2026,bhattarai2014,chhetri2023,dahal2025,giri2024,gurung2024,gurungdahal2025,gyawali2022,joshi2023,lamsal2024,malhotra2013,niroula2021,pant2024,pradhan2016,sharma2011,silwal2019,timalsina2026}. As mathematically demonstrated in Section~\ref{subsec:pe_circularity}, because $\text{P/E} = \text{MPS} / \text{EPS}$, regressing MPS on P/E introduces a circular algebraic dependency, embedding the dependent variable directly inside the regressor. This induces severe mechanical endogeneity, inflates $R^2$ values spuriously, and invalidates statistical inference. A defensible research design must eliminate price-derived multiples and restrict explanatory variables to fundamental accounting measures determined independently of secondary market share prices.

\subsection{The Temporal Alignment and Look-Ahead Bias Gap}
Because audited annual financial statements are not prepared, audited, and published until months after Ashadh-end, pairing Ashadh-end prices with audited numbers creates severe look-ahead bias, violating temporal causality. Furthermore, attempting to reconstruct individual corporate announcement dates across 30 bank-years is prone to measurement error, informal leakage, and archival opacity. Existing literature lacks a standardized, institutionally grounded price-measurement rule. This study operationalizes Section 59(2) of BAFIA, 2017 at the end of Chaitra (mid-April), observing market prices at the first trading session on or after the end of Chaitra ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$). This provides a reproducible observation threshold after the statutory deadline, while leaving each bank-year's actual public release date and any earlier information leakage unverified.

\subsection{The Entity-Timing Transition Gap}
None of the empirical studies reviewed addresses the institutional transition timing of merged banks. For fiscal year 2078/79, annual financial reports represent pre-merger standalone operations, whereas by mid-April 2023, secondary market trading reflected the unified post-merger entities (mergers completed in January--February 2023). This creates an entity-timing mismatch between pre-merger accounting fundamentals and post-consolidation market valuations. Prior studies ignore this structural shock, risking biased parameter estimation. This study explicitly addresses entity transition timing through dedicated sensitivity exclusions.

\subsection{The Econometric and Small-Cluster Inference Gap}
Prior empirical research on Nepalese commercial banking routinely overlooks finite-sample degrees of freedom and the econometric hazards of small-cluster panel inference. In fixed-effects panel models with interactions, parameter estimation rapidly consumes degrees of freedom. Moreover, standard cluster-robust standard errors suffer from severe downward bias when the number of clusters is small (in this study, $G = 5$) \citep{cameron2008clusters}. This dissertation addresses this gap by disclosing exact residual degrees of freedom ($28 - 14 = 14$ df) and reporting the sensitivity of the joint test across covariance estimators, and framing findings as observed statistical associations rather than causal proofs, using MacKinnon-White HC3 as the heteroskedasticity-robust estimator. HC3 addresses heteroskedasticity, not within-bank correlation, so the within-bank independence assumption remains untested; the small-cluster limitation is handled by disclosing the degrees of freedom and reporting the sensitivity of the joint test across covariance estimators (Chapter~IV, Table~\ref{tab:cov_sensitivity}) and the wild-cluster bootstrap.

By synthesizing the \citet{ohlson1995} residual income valuation model with the post-merger value-relevance framework of \citet{kwon2020}, operationalizing the standardized BAFIA reporting deadline ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$), excluding circular P/E multiples, and executing a rigorous two-way fixed-effects panel model with seven robustness variants plus two baseline specifications, this dissertation is designed to address each of the identified gaps in the literature.

\chapter{Research Methodology}

\section{Introduction}
Scientific research in empirical finance and financial accounting is distinguished by the formalization, transparency, and reproducibility of its methodological design. This chapter sets out the comprehensive research methodology employed to investigate the value relevance of accounting information across Nepal's 2023 commercial banking consolidation wave. Structured in accordance with the \textit{Master's Degree Dissertation Guidelines (2019)} of the Faculty of Management, Tribhuvan University, this chapter is organized into five interrelated sections:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Section 3.2: Research Design} specifies the quantitative, correlational, comparative, and longitudinal two-way fixed-effects panel design.
    \item \textbf{Section 3.3: Population, Sample, and Sampling Procedure} defines the commercial banking population, justifies purposive sampling, presents the institutional profiles of the five bank series, and defines the 5-day freshness rule.
    \item \textbf{Section 3.4: Nature and Sources of Data, and Data Collection Procedure} details audited financial statement inputs, market price series, regulatory sources, and reconciliation protocols.
    \item \textbf{Section 3.5: Research Framework and Definition of Variables} delineates the conceptual framework, operational variable definitions ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$, EPS, BVPS, $\mathit{Post}_t$, ROA, DPS), and the exclusion of circular P/E multiples.
    \item \textbf{Section 3.6: Methods of Analysis and Econometric Specification} formulates the baseline (Model~1) and regime-shift (Model~2) panel models, MacKinnon-White HC3 robust inference, finite-sample degrees of freedom accounting, diagnostic tests, and the nine-specification estimation and robustness suite (R1--R2 baselines plus seven robustness variants R3--R9).
\end{itemize}

\section{Research Design}
This study adopts a quantitative, correlational, and comparative longitudinal panel research design using secondary financial accounting and capital market data \citep{pant2016}. The design is explicitly aligned with the research questions and objectives formulated in Chapter~I:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Quantitative and Correlational:} The study operationalizes fundamental accounting metrics---earnings per share (EPS), book value per share (BVPS), return on assets (ROA), and dividend per share (DPS)---and evaluates their statistical associations with secondary market equity closing prices ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$) using parametric correlation matrices and linear regression estimation.
    \item \textbf{Comparative and Longitudinal Panel:} The research design tracks five commercial banking institutions across six consecutive fiscal years, spanning FY 2076/77 (2019/20) through FY 2081/82 (2024/25). This longitudinal dimension allows the study to compare valuation relationships across two distinct calendar and institutional regimes: the pre-consolidation period (FY 2076/77--2078/79, $n = 13$ clean observations) and the post-consolidation period (FY 2079/80--2081/82, $n = 15$ clean observations).
    \item \textbf{Observational and Non-Experimental:} Because the researcher does not manipulate corporate financial decisions, assign institutions to merger treatments, or intervene in market price formation, the investigation is non-experimental and observational. In accordance with rigorous econometric principles, parameter estimates are interpreted as conditional statistical associations rather than as causal merger impacts.
    \item \textbf{Two-Way Fixed-Effects Framework:} To mitigate potential omitted-variable bias, the core empirical architecture implements a two-way fixed-effects panel model incorporating unobserved time-invariant bank fixed effects ($\mu_i$) and common fiscal-year fixed effects ($\lambda_t$). Bank fixed effects control for persistent differences in institutional reputation, corporate governance, risk appetite, and historical branch networks. Fiscal-year fixed effects absorb market-wide macroeconomic shocks, shifts in monetary policy, inflation cycles, and broad NEPSE equity index movements. The estimated slopes are conditional on both sets of fixed effects; this description does not imply simple double demeaning.
    \item \textbf{Exploratory Finite-Sample Inference:} Recognizing the small cross-sectional dimension ($N = 5$ banking series, $G = 5$ clusters), the research design explicitly disclaims asymptotic certainty. Inference is structured around MacKinnon-White HC3 heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors with transparent reporting of finite-sample degrees of freedom, providing an exploratory, honest assessment of parameter uncertainty.
\end{itemize}

This study uses only published secondary data (audited annual reports and public price series); no human participants were involved.

\section{Population, Sample, and Sampling Procedure}

\subsection{Population of the Study}
The target population for this study comprises all commercial banks licensed by Nepal Rastra Bank as Class `A' financial institutions operating in the Federal Democratic Republic of Nepal. Over the past decade, successive consolidation mandates executed by NRB reduced the commercial banking sector from 32 institutions in FY 2011/12 \citep{nrb2013stats} to 20 commercial banks by mid-October 2023 (Asoj 2080 BS) \citep{nrb2023list}. These 20 licensed institutions constitute the population of the modern Nepalese commercial banking industry. Of these 20 licensed institutions, 19 are listed on NEPSE \citep{bhandari2026}; the one unlisted institution is Rastriya Banijya Bank Limited \citep{nrb2023list}.

\subsection{Sampling Method and Selection Criteria}
The study uses a purposive sample of four selected merged bank series and one unmerged benchmark. In terms of institutional count, the five sampled banking series represent exactly 25.0\% (5 of 20) of all licensed Class `A' commercial banks in Nepal following the 2023 consolidation wave \citep{nrb2023list}. Four of the five Class `A' commercial bank combinations executed during the early-2023 wave are represented in the sample. The NEPSE source records Prabhu Bank's acquisition of Century Commercial Bank \citep{nrb2023bsr}. Prabhu Bank/Century was not included among the selected cases; no separate data-based exclusion criterion is claimed. Because purposive sampling restricts institutional coverage to these five series rather than executing an exhaustive sector-wide census, empirical findings apply directly to the sampled institutions; aggregate Class `A' sector asset and market-capitalization shares are not claimed or evaluated.

The specific sampling criteria applied to select the institutions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Participation in the 2023 Consolidation Wave:} Commercial bank series that executed horizontal statutory amalgamations or major corporate acquisitions that commenced joint commercial operations during the early-2023 consolidation wave (January--February 2023 / mid-FY 2079/80).
    \item \textbf{Benchmark Series Continuity:} Inclusion of an established Class `A' commercial bank that did not participate in the early-2023 consolidation wave, providing a contemporaneous, unmerged benchmark series to differentiate sector-wide market trends from merger-specific structural shifts.
    \item \textbf{Longitudinal Data Availability and Regulatory Compliance:} Availability of complete, audited annual financial statements across all six fiscal years (FY 2076/77 to FY 2081/82) prepared in accordance with Nepal Financial Reporting Standards (NFRS) and submitted to Nepal Rastra Bank.
    \item \textbf{Secondary Market Listing and Trading Activity:} Active listing on the Nepal Stock Exchange (NEPSE), with row-level trading freshness applied as detailed in Section~\ref{subsec:sample_realized} (under which two bank-years with extended trading halts exceeding the 5-day post-deadline freshness window are coded as missing).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Sample Size, Missingness Protocol, and Realized Observations}
\label{subsec:sample_realized}
The research protocol targets 30 potential bank-year observations, comprising five commercial bank series tracked over six fiscal years (FY 2076/77 to FY 2081/82). Primary audited annual financial reports are complete for all 30 bank-years ($100\%$ accounting coverage).

However, in secondary equity pricing, corporate restructuring frequently induces prolonged trading halts. To ensure empirical integrity, the study enforces a strict \textbf{five-day calendar freshness rule}:
\begin{quote}
\textit{If a security was suspended from trading or recorded zero valid transactions within a five-day calendar window following the study's operationalized reporting deadline (end of Chaitra / mid-April), the observation is coded as missing and excluded from the regression sample. It is never imputed from stale pre-suspension prices.}
\end{quote}

The five-day length is an un-derived operational choice; no substantive rationale for five days rather than another window is recorded in this thesis. The choice is consequential for the headline result: including the two delayed observations changes the Model~2 joint test to $F(2,16)=4.762$ ($p=0.024$), against $F(2,14)=2.954$ ($p=0.085$) in the 28-row sample (Section~3.5.2).

Two bank-year observations fail this freshness rule due to prolonged merger-related trading suspensions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Himalayan Bank Limited (HBL) FY 2077/78:} Trading in HBL shares on NEPSE was suspended from 13 May 2021, when the two banks signed a merger memorandum of understanding; the proposed bilateral merger with Nepal Investment Bank was then rejected by HBL shareholders at its 29th AGM in January 2022, and trading remained suspended until 6 June 2022 (Jestha 2079 BS), representing a resumption delay of 54 days beyond the target deadline of 13 April 2022.
    \item \textbf{Nepal Investment Mega Bank Limited (NIMB, formerly NIB) FY 2077/78:} Trading in NIB shares was concurrently suspended under the same merger process, resuming on 30 May 2022 (Jestha 2079 BS), representing a resumption delay of 47 days beyond the 13 April 2022 deadline.
\end{itemize}

Consequently, these two observations are coded as missing, leaving a realized clean estimation sample of \textbf{$N = 28$ observations} (13 pre-consolidation bank-years and 15 post-consolidation bank-years).

\section{Nature and Sources of Data, and Data Collection Procedure}

\subsection{Nature of Data}
This dissertation relies exclusively on quantitative secondary data extracted from audited financial disclosures and capital market data aggregators. All accounting variables are compiled strictly on a standalone Bank-level basis rather than consolidated Group accounts, consistent with the regulatory return framework, in which a licensed commercial bank's capital adequacy and prudential metrics are computed at the standalone Bank level rather than for the consolidated Group. Standalone Bank balance-sheet identities ($\text{Total Assets} - \text{Total Liabilities} - \text{Total Equity} = 0$) hold across all 30 bank-year observations within a $\le 1$ NPR rounding tolerance. In the audited Global IME Bank Group statements, Group total equity (NPR 29,463.85 million in FY 2076/77) exceeds equity attributable to equity holders (NPR 29,252.57 million) by the non-controlling interest (NPR 211.28 million); the parser's Group total-equity field fell back to the attributable figure, so the raw extraction does not carry a separate Group total. Regardless, Group-level figures are never used in panel regressions as all empirical estimations rely strictly on standalone Bank accounts, where non-controlling interest is nil and total equity equals attributable equity.

The empirical dataset comprises two primary streams of financial information:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Audited Financial Accounting Data:} Comprehensive annual financial metrics---specifically basic earnings per share, total equity attributable to equity holders, ordinary shares outstanding, profit after tax, average total assets, and recommended cash dividends per share.
    \item \textbf{Secondary Market Trading Data:} Daily equity transaction records---specifically closing market share prices, trade dates, and trading volume. Trading status was inferred from daily transaction records and press accounts; NEPSE's own suspension notices were not obtained (see Appendix~D, Section D.8).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Sources of Data Collection}
To ensure institutional credibility and empirical accuracy, data sources are bifurcated into primary empirical inputs and contextual regulatory publications:

\textbf{Primary Empirical Data Sources:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Audited Annual Financial Reports:} Statements of Financial Position (Balance Sheets), Statements of Profit or Loss (Income Statements), Statements of Changes in Equity, and accompanying Notes to Accounts extracted directly from the published annual reports of Global IME Bank, Himalayan Bank, Kumari Bank, Nepal Investment Mega Bank, and NMB Bank.
    \item \textit{Capital Market Data Aggregators:} Secondary closing market share prices retrieved from established capital market aggregators (ShareSansar, Merolagani, and FinancialNotices), cross-checked against a second aggregator (Merolagani last traded prices for all 30 dataset price cells; FinancialNotices Close for the five FY 2081/82 rows).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Contextual and Regulatory Data Sources:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB):} Annual Banking and Financial Statistics and licensed financial institution directories (the population counts in Section~3.3) and the Bank and Financial Institutions Merger Bylaws (the regulatory architecture for the consolidation wave, cited in Chapter~I).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Data Audit, Extraction, and Reconciliation Protocol}
All secondary data were processed through a multi-stage audit and verification protocol to ensure that every value was extracted from the audited report, recomputed where the definition requires it, and flagged where recomputation disagreed with a printed figure, rather than overwritten:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Primary Audit Verification:} All fundamental accounting variables (EPS, equity, ordinary shares, net profit, total assets, DPS) were manually extracted from original audited annual reports and verified across balance sheet and income statement schedules.
    \item \textbf{Reconciliation Against Aggregators:} Figures compiled from financial aggregators were cross-referenced against original audited PDF reports. In all instances of discrepancy, figures were resolved strictly in favor of the originating audited financial accounts.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Aggregator Price Reconciliation:} Dataset prices (28 clean observation-date prices plus the two delayed resumption prices, which were subsequently excluded from estimation under the five-day freshness rule) were compared against Merolagani LTPs (and FinancialNotices for FY 2081/82). Out of these 30 dataset cells, 29 demonstrated perfect zero-variance matching; for NMB FY 2076/77, the dataset (ShareSansar) recorded a closing price of NPR 437.00, whereas Merolagani reported a last traded price (LTP) of NPR 436.00. The NPR 437.00 value was retained on the basis of ShareSansar's full daily record (open 432, high 439, low 428, close 437.00); because ShareSansar is the dataset's primary source, this does not represent independent verification, and the official NEPSE closing price for 13 April 2021 remains unverified against primary exchange floor archives.
    \item \textbf{Trading Status and Freshness Verification:} Daily trading status was screened across the calendar window following the reporting deadline to identify trading halts, record dates, and resumption sessions.
    \item \textbf{Prior-Year Comparative Audit and Continuity:} Prior-year comparative figures reported in subsequent annual reports were audited against own-year reports. Five balance-sheet restatements arising from mid-year mergers and accounting alignments were documented: Global IME Bank FY 2078/79 (NPR 718.44 million; restated previous column in the GBIME FY 2078/79 report, Statement of Financial Position, PDF pp.~83--84), Himalayan Bank FY 2080/81 (Civil Bank merger, NPR 1,877.08 million; restated previous column in the HBL FY 2080/81 report, Statement of Financial Position, PDF pp.~54--55), Kumari Bank FY 2079/80 (NCC Bank merger, NPR 1,047.39 million; restated previous column in the KBL FY 2079/80 report, Statement of Financial Position, PDF p.~42), Kumari Bank FY 2077/78 (derivative line alignment, NPR -7,369.69 million; restated previous column in the KBL FY 2077/78 report, Statement of Financial Position, PDF p.~34, against the own-current KBL FY 2076/77 report, PDF p.~49), and a minor asset reclassification in Kumari Bank FY 2078/79 (NPR 9.59 million, or 0.005\%; restated previous column in the KBL FY 2078/79 report, Statement of Financial Position, PDF p.~32, against the own-current KBL FY 2077/78 report, PDF p.~34) — each labelled by the annual report whose previous-year comparative column carries the restatement, with Statement of Financial Position pages from Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}, Table~\ref{tab:app_locators}. Under the study's empirical design, each observation is populated from its own contemporaneous audited financial report (the own-current rule) to preserve the exact information set available to market participants at the statutory reporting deadline.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Research Framework and Definition of Variables}

\subsection{Conceptual Framework}
The conceptual framework guiding this research synthesizes the residual income valuation theory of \citet{ohlson1995} and the post-merger valuation model of \citet{kwon2020}. It is depicted in Figure~\ref{fig:framework}.

\input{figures/fig_framework}

In this framework, secondary market closing price at the reporting deadline ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$) serves as the outcome variable. Earnings per share (EPS) and book value per share (BVPS) are the primary independent fundamental regressors. The consolidation period indicator ($\mathit{Post}_t$) acts as a temporal moderating indicator, interacting with EPS and BVPS to test for structural parameter shifts ($\delta_E, \delta_B$) across the consolidation wave. Bank fixed effects ($\mu_i$) and fiscal-year fixed effects ($\lambda_t$) control for time-invariant institutional heterogeneity and market-wide macroeconomic trends.

\subsection{Operational Definition and Measurement of Variables}

\subsubsection{Dependent Variable}
\textbf{Market Price per Share at Reporting Deadline ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$):}
The closing secondary market trading price for ordinary shares, measured in Nepalese Rupees (NPR), recorded on the first trading session on the Nepal Stock Exchange (NEPSE) on or after the statutory nine-month audited annual reporting publication deadline established under Section 59(2) of the Banks and Financial Institutions Act, 2017 \citep{bafia2017}.

Operational measurement is established at the end of Chaitra (mid-April) of the subsequent fiscal year. If the deadline falls on a weekend, public holiday, or trading halt, price is recorded on the first active trading session within a 5-day calendar window. Observations exceeding the 5-day window are coded as missing. This rule provides a consistent cutoff after the statutory deadline, but does not verify the public release date for every bank-year or rule out earlier information leakage. Disclosure dates were investigated only for the five FY 2081/82 bank-years, of which two could be settled and three were held pending; for the remaining 25 bank-years no disclosure date is recorded. The five-day cutoff is consequential: including the two delayed observations changes the Model~2 joint test to $F(2,16)=4.762$ ($p=0.024$), compared with $F(2,14)=2.954$ ($p=0.085$) in the 28-row sample.

\subsubsection{Primary Independent Variables}
\textbf{Earnings per Share (EPS):}
Basic earnings per ordinary share, expressed in NPR, extracted from each bank's own audited Statement of Profit or Loss (the own-current reported basic EPS rule). EPS is calculated as net profit after tax attributable to ordinary equity holders divided by the weighted-average number of ordinary shares outstanding during the fiscal year:
\begin{equation}
\mathit{EPS}_{it} = \frac{\text{Net Profit Attributable to Equity Holders}_{it}}{\text{Weighted Average Number of Ordinary Shares}_{it}}
\label{eq:eps_def}
\end{equation}
Retrospective audited restatements reported in subsequent annual reports are retained for sensitivity analysis rather than overwriting original audited figures, preserving the exact information set available to market participants at the reporting date. In corporate combination years (notably FY 2079/80), the weighted-average share denominator reflects pre- and post-merger issuance weights in accordance with NAS 33 / NFRS, whereas BVPS uses ending ordinary shares outstanding. Consequently, reported basic EPS in the consolidation transition year reflects blended share counts (e.g., Global IME Bank reported EPS of 22.06 vs.\ PAT divided by year-end shares of 18.71; NIMB 13.92 vs.\ 10.90; HBL 9.18 vs.\ 7.22), while BVPS consistently reflects the enlarged post-merger capital base. The same share-base divergence also arises in FY 2076/77: Global IME Bank reported EPS of 17.99 against PAT divided by year-end shares of 15.33 following its merger with Janata Bank Nepal Ltd (completed 5 December 2019) and acquisition of Hathway Finance Company, and Kumari Bank reported EPS of 12.08 against 9.25 following its acquisition of Deva Bikas Bank (joint operations from 28 Ashad 2077 BS).

\textbf{Book Value per Share (BVPS):}
Net asset value per ordinary share, expressed in NPR, reflecting the net worth backing of each ordinary equity share at fiscal year-end. BVPS is recomputed directly from audited Statement of Financial Position components as total ordinary shareholders' equity attributable to equity holders divided by the total number of ordinary shares outstanding at fiscal year-end:
\begin{equation}
\mathit{BVPS}_{it} = \frac{\text{Total Equity Attributable to Ordinary Equity Holders}_{it}}{\text{Total Number of Ordinary Shares Outstanding at Year-End}_{it}}
\label{eq:bvps_def}
\end{equation}
Printed Net Asset Value (NAV) per share reported in bank financial highlights is used as an audit reconciliation check against recomputed balance-sheet net worth. Recomputed BVPS is adopted in all empirical regressions to preserve exact decimal precision; minor variances of 0.08 to 0.40 NPR across six NIMB observations arise because printed NAV highlights in those annual reports were rounded to whole integers (190.70 vs.\ 191.00; 198.28 vs.\ 198.00; 185.67 vs.\ 186.00; 162.92 vs.\ 163.00; 176.31 vs.\ 176.00; 190.40 vs.\ 190.00), whereas two further observations exhibit unrounded decimal differences that remain flagged as unresolved reporting variances: HBL FY 2076/77 (187.67 recomputed vs.\ 187.72 printed, gap 0.05) and NMB Bank FY 2081/82 (175.18 recomputed vs.\ 174.49 printed, gap 0.69).

\subsubsection{Moderating / Interaction Variable}
\textbf{Consolidation Period Indicator ($\mathit{Post}_t$):}
A binary temporal indicator variable reflecting the structural regime shift of the early-2023 banking consolidation wave:
\begin{equation}
\mathit{Post}_t = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{for pre-consolidation fiscal years (FY 2076/77, 2077/78, 2078/79)} \\ 1 & \text{for post-consolidation fiscal years (FY 2079/80, 2080/81, 2081/82)} \end{cases}
\label{eq:post_def}
\end{equation}
This variable interacts with EPS ($\mathit{EPS}_{it} \times \mathit{Post}_t$) and BVPS ($\mathit{BVPS}_{it} \times \mathit{Post}_t$) to estimate parameter differences ($\delta_E, \delta_B$) across regimes.

\subsubsection{Supplementary and Robustness Variables}
\textbf{Return on Assets (ROA):}
Operating profitability expressed as net profit after tax divided by average total assets, expressed as a percentage:
\begin{equation}
\mathit{ROA}_{it} = \left(\frac{\text{Profit After Tax}_{it}}{\text{Average Total Assets}_{it}}\right) \times 100
\label{eq:roa_def}
\end{equation}
Average total assets is the mean of the current and previous fiscal year-end total assets.
Evaluated in Specification R3 as an asset-scaled substitute for EPS. Because EPS and ROA correlate at $r = 0.975$ (yielding a bivariate variance inflation factor of $1 / (1 - r^2) \approx 20$), they are never co-included in the same regression to prevent severe multicollinearity.

\textbf{Dividend per Share (DPS):}
Cash dividend recommended per ordinary share, expressed in NPR, for the corresponding fiscal year. Evaluated in Specification R4, including only eligible cash dividends that were officially declared on or prior to the $\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$ observation date. Documented zero-dividend decisions are recorded as zero; unannounced dividends are treated as missing in the operational rule. In the realized analysis sample ($N = 28$), all 28 observations possess confirmed declarations or documented nil distributions (zero cash dividend), meaning no DPS observation is missing in the realized estimation dataset.

\subsubsection{Strict Exclusion of Price-Derived Multiples}
\textbf{Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio:}
The Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratio is strictly excluded from all regression models. As mathematically proved in Section~\ref{subsec:pe_circularity}, because $\text{P/E} = \text{MPS} / \text{EPS}$, regressing MPS on P/E introduces circular algebraic endogeneity that biases coefficients and spuriously inflates explanatory power.

The codebook definitions governing every variable in the panel are collected in Table~\ref{tab:app_codebook} of Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}.

\section{Methods of Analysis and Econometric Specification}
The empirical protocol is executed through descriptive statistics, bivariate correlation diagnostics, fixed-effects panel regressions, finite-sample inference adjustments, and a nine-specification suite comprising the two baseline models (R1--R2) and seven robustness variants (R3--R9).

\subsection{Model 1: Baseline Value Relevance Model}
To evaluate the baseline value relevance of accounting fundamentals (Hypotheses~$H_1$ and $H_2$), Model~1 specifies a two-way fixed-effects panel regression:
\begin{equation}
\mathit{MPS}^{RD}_{it} = \alpha + \mu_i + \lambda_t + \beta_E \mathit{EPS}_{it} + \beta_B \mathit{BVPS}_{it} + \varepsilon_{it}
\label{eq:fe_model1}
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $i = 1, \dots, 5$ indexes the five commercial bank series;
    \item $t = 1, \dots, 6$ indexes the six fiscal years;
    \item $\mathit{MPS}^{RD}_{it}$ is the secondary market closing price observed at the post-reporting-deadline window;
    \item $\mathit{EPS}_{it}$ is basic earnings per share from own audited SOPL;
    \item $\mathit{BVPS}_{it}$ is recomputed book value per share at fiscal year-end;
    \item $\alpha$ is the constant baseline intercept;
    \item $\mu_i$ represents unobserved time-invariant bank fixed effects, estimated via 4 bank dummy variables with Global IME Bank Limited (GBIME) serving as the omitted reference bank;
    \item $\lambda_t$ represents common fiscal-year fixed effects, estimated via 5 fiscal-year dummy variables with FY 2076/77 serving as the omitted reference year;
    \item $\beta_E$ and $\beta_B$ are the baseline valuation slopes for EPS and BVPS;
    \item $\varepsilon_{it}$ is the idiosyncratic disturbance term.
\end{itemize}

In Model~1, the parameter count comprises 1 intercept, 4 bank dummy coefficients, 5 fiscal-year dummy coefficients, and 2 slope parameters, totaling $K = 12$ estimated parameters. On the realized sample of $N = 28$ observations, Model~1 retains $N - K = 28 - 12 = 16$ residual degrees of freedom.

\subsection{Model 2: Consolidation-Period Regime Shift Model}
To test whether accounting value relevance differed before and after the banking consolidation wave (Hypotheses~$H_3$ and $H_4$), Model~2 introduces interaction terms between accounting fundamentals and the consolidation indicator:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathit{MPS}^{RD}_{it} ={}& \alpha + \mu_i + \lambda_t + \beta_E \mathit{EPS}_{it} + \beta_B \mathit{BVPS}_{it} \\
& + \delta_E (\mathit{EPS}_{it} \times \mathit{Post}_t) + \delta_B (\mathit{BVPS}_{it} \times \mathit{Post}_t) + \varepsilon_{it}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:fe_model2}
\end{equation}
where reference categories remain identically defined (GBIME as reference bank for $\mu_i$, FY 2076/77 as reference fiscal year for $\lambda_t$).

\textbf{Econometric Note on Standalone Post Absorption:}
Because Model~2 includes a full set of fiscal-year fixed effects ($\lambda_t$), the standalone consolidation indicator ($\mathit{Post}_t$) is a deterministic linear combination of the post-consolidation year dummy indicators:
\begin{equation}
\mathit{Post}_t = D_{2079/80} + D_{2080/81} + D_{2081/82}
\end{equation}
where $D_t$ represents the post-consolidation fiscal-year dummy indicators. Including a standalone $\mathit{Post}_t$ regressor alongside full year fixed effects would create perfect exact collinearity (dummy variable trap). Consequently, the main effect of $\mathit{Post}_t$ is fully absorbed by the fiscal-year fixed effects and is not estimated separately.

\textbf{Interpretation of Slope Parameters:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Pre-Consolidation Valuation Slopes:} Represented directly by the baseline coefficients:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{Slope}_{\text{EPS, Pre}} = \beta_E, \quad \text{Slope}_{\text{BVPS, Pre}} = \beta_B
    \end{equation}
    \item \textit{Incremental Regime Shifts:} Measured by the interaction coefficients:
    \begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\text{EPS}} = \delta_E, \quad \Delta_{\text{BVPS}} = \delta_B
    \end{equation}
    \item \textit{Post-Consolidation Valuation Slopes:} Calculated as linear combinations:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{Slope}_{\text{EPS, Post}} = \beta_E + \delta_E, \quad \text{Slope}_{\text{BVPS, Post}} = \beta_B + \delta_B
    \end{equation}
    \item \textit{Principal Hypothesis Test:} Evaluated via the joint null hypothesis of zero parameter shift:
    \begin{equation}
    H_0: \delta_E = \delta_B = 0
    \end{equation}
    tested using a joint Wald $F$-test with 2 numerator degrees of freedom under the robust covariance matrix.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Econometric Estimation, Degrees of Freedom, and Robust Inference}
Estimation is performed by Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) with explicit dummy variables for bank and fiscal-year fixed effects:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Degrees of Freedom Accounting:} On the realized clean sample of $N = 28$ observations, Model~2 estimates 14 parameters:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item 1 constant intercept ($\alpha$)
        \item 4 bank fixed effects ($\mu_i$, with GBIME as reference)
        \item 5 fiscal-year fixed effects ($\lambda_t$, with FY 2076/77 as reference)
        \item 2 baseline slopes ($\beta_E, \beta_B$)
        \item 2 interaction slopes ($\delta_E, \delta_B$)
    \end{itemize}
    \begin{equation}
    \text{Residual Degrees of Freedom} = N - K = 28 - 14 = 14 \text{ df}
    \end{equation}
    In supplementary models incorporating DPS (Specification R4), estimating the additional slope parameter $\beta_{\text{DPS}}$ increases the parameter count to $K = 15$, reducing residual degrees of freedom from 14 to $28 - 15 = 13$ df.
    \item \textbf{MacKinnon-White HC3 Robust Standard Errors and Reference Distribution:} Cluster-robust covariance estimators are distinct from HC3 and can have unreliable finite-sample behavior when the number of clusters is small ($G = 5$) \citep{cameron2008clusters}. Standard errors, $t$-statistics, and confidence intervals are computed using \citet{mackinnon1985} HC3 heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{HC3} = (X'X)^{-1} \left( \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{e_i^2}{(1 - h_{ii})^2} x_i x_i' \right) (X'X)^{-1}
    \label{eq:hc3}
    \end{equation}
    where $h_{ii} = x_i (X'X)^{-1} x_i'$ represents the leverage of observation $i$. Dividing squared residuals by $(1 - h_{ii})^2$ adjusts the covariance estimate for leverage. This adjustment does not remove influential observations or guarantee reliable finite-sample inference. All $t$-statistics, $p$-values, and parameter confidence intervals are referred to Student's $t$ distribution on residual degrees of freedom ($t(14)$ for Model~2, $t(16)$ for Model~1); given the small sample, inference remains uncertain. The joint-test conclusion is sensitive to the covariance estimator: Chapter IV reports the joint Wald tests under the classical, HC0, HC1, HC2 and HC3 covariances (Table~\ref{tab:cov_sensitivity}). HC3 is retained as the designated estimator on the grounds stated above---pre-specification in the proposal and its small-sample properties---not because the alternative estimators agree.
    Within-bank serial independence of errors is also assumed by the reported covariance calculations; it is not directly tested here. The five-bank panel is too small to support a reliable cluster-robust correction, so this assumption remains a limitation. If within-bank residuals are positively autocorrelated, the HC3 standard errors understate the true sampling variation and the reported p-values are understated, so the tests over-reject; the reported non-rejections are correspondingly conservative; no serial-correlation diagnostic (Durbin--Watson, Wooldridge, or HAC/cluster-robust comparison) is reported, so this possibility cannot be excluded. As a small-sample sensitivity check on the joint Wald decisions, a wild-cluster bootstrap in the spirit of \citet{cameron2008clusters} is also run: the null is imposed by fitting Model~1, its residuals are resampled by bank with Rademacher weights, and the HC3 Wald statistic is recomputed on each bootstrap sample. With five banks there are only $2^5 = 32$ distinct sign patterns, so all 32 are enumerated and the bootstrap $p$-values are multiples of $1/32$; the smallest attainable $p$-value is therefore $2/32 = 0.0625$, and the check cannot produce a 5\% rejection for any specification.
    \item \textbf{Goodness of Fit:} The reported $R^2$ is the least-squares dummy-variable fit, $R^2 = 1 - \text{SSR}_{\text{LSDV}} / \text{SST}$, where $\text{SSR}_{\text{LSDV}}$ is the residual sum of squares of the full model including fixed effects. Within-$R^2$ isolates the contribution of the substantive slopes and interactions: $\text{Within-}R^2 = 1 - \text{SSR}_{\text{LSDV}} / \text{SSR}_{\text{dummies}}$, where $\text{SSR}_{\text{dummies}}$ is the residual sum of squares from a regression on the bank and fiscal-year dummies alone. It is therefore not the one-way within-$R^2$ of a single fixed effect, and it is not computed by demeaning the data.
\end{itemize}

Full coefficient estimates, including every fixed-effect dummy and the joint fixed-effect tests, are printed in Appendix E, Tables~\ref{tab:app_m1full}--\ref{tab:app_fetests}, and full robustness estimates in Tables~\ref{tab:app_robustfull}--\ref{tab:app_robust_fetests}.

\subsection{Econometric Diagnostic Battery}
To assess econometric validity, the estimation protocol executes four diagnostic checks:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Multicollinearity:} Variance Inflation Factors (VIF) and tolerance statistics ($1/\text{VIF}$) computed on substantive regressors. The attention threshold is $\text{VIF} > 5$; it is a screen, not a pass/fail validity test. Model~1 EPS and BVPS VIFs are approximately $1.16$. Model~2 VIFs are $10.33$ (EPS), $2.28$ (BVPS), $15.15$ (EPS $\times$ Post), and $24.13$ (BVPS $\times$ Post). Fixed-effect dummies are excluded from these VIF calculations. On the full fitted design, including the fixed-effect dummies, the VIFs are $5.70$ (EPS) and $12.32$ (BVPS) for Model~1, and $14.82$ (EPS), $12.46$ (BVPS), $27.34$ (EPS $\times$ Post) and $259.32$ (BVPS $\times$ Post) for Model~2, with the largest year-dummy VIF $118.21$ (Chapter~IV, Table~\ref{tab:t6_diagnostics} note).
    \item \textbf{Heteroskedasticity:} The Koenker robust form of the Breusch-Pagan Lagrange Multiplier test (statsmodels \texttt{robust=True}) is evaluated against the null hypothesis of constant error variance ($H_0: \sigma_i^2 = \sigma^2$); the classical variant is also reported because it differs for Model~1.
    \item \textbf{Influential Outliers:} Cook's Distance ($D_i$) computed for each observation and evaluated against the conventional finite-sample threshold $D_i > 4/n = 4/28 \approx 0.143$. The count of flagged observations depends on the threshold: $D_i > 1$ flags none in either model, while $4/(n-K-1)$ flags 3 in Model~1 and 2 in Model~2, against 5 each at $4/n$.
    \item \textbf{Residual Normality:} Normal Q-Q plots and supplementary Shapiro-Wilk tests assess residual shape. The Q-Q plots are broadly linear through the center but show lower-tail departures; Shapiro-Wilk rejects normality for Model~1 ($W=0.925$, $p=0.045$) and Model~2 ($W=0.922$, $p=0.038$). These diagnostics flag a limitation of the small-sample $t/F$ reference distributions; they do not test the validity of the HC3 covariance estimator.
\end{itemize}

No prospective power analysis was specified. As a post hoc design-sensitivity calculation, a two-sided test at $\alpha=0.05$ and 80\% power using the fitted HC3 standard errors and residual-$df$ $t$ reference gives approximate single-coefficient minimum detectable effects of 10.73 NPR for the Model~2 pre-period EPS slope ($df=14$), 12.06 NPR for $\delta_E$ ($df=14$), and 3.71 NPR for $\delta_B$ ($df=14$). These are detectable-effect thresholds under the stated model, not observed power and not evidence that smaller effects are absent. With five banks the effective sample size is below 28, so these minimum detectable effects are optimistic upper bounds. Accordingly, failure to reject a null is not evidence that the corresponding effect is zero; estimates and confidence intervals remain uncertain within this design's precision.

\subsection{The Nine-Specification Estimation and Robustness Suite}
To verify that empirical inferences are not artifacts of specific modelling choices, the study estimates a structured nine-specification suite comprising the two baseline models and seven robustness variants (Specifications R1 to R9):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{R1 (Model 1 Baseline):} Baseline two-way fixed-effects model without interaction terms ($n = 28$).
    \item \textbf{R2 (Model 2 Baseline):} Full two-way fixed-effects model with consolidation interaction terms ($n = 28$).
    \item \textbf{R3 (ROA Substitution):} Replaces EPS with return on assets (ROA) to evaluate sensitivity to an asset-scaled operational profitability metric ($n = 28$).
    \item \textbf{R4 (Dividend Specification):} Incorporates eligible cash dividend per share (DPS) declarations into Model~2, noting degrees of freedom decrease to 13 ($n = 28$).
    \item \textbf{R5 (Excluding Transition Year):} Re-estimates Model~2 excluding FY 2079/80 ($n = 23$) to verify results are not driven by immediate consolidation shock.
    \item \textbf{R6 (Excluding Entity-Timing Mismatch):} Re-estimates Model~2 excluding FY 2078/79 ($n = 23$) to eliminate the mismatch where standalone fundamentals were paired with post-merger April 2023 prices. In both R5 and R6 the excluded fiscal year also removes its fiscal-year dummy, so the parameter count falls from $K = 14$ to $K = 13$ and residual degrees of freedom are $23 - 13 = 10$.
    \item \textbf{R7 (Bonus Share Capitalization Adjustment):} Re-estimates Model~2 using bonus-adjusted book value per share ($\mathit{BVPS}_{\text{adj}} = \mathit{BVPS} / (1 + \text{bonus ratio})$) to evaluate parameter stability against share dilutions ($n = 28$). The bonus ratio is the board-proposed bonus share percentage for that fiscal year, taken from the board proposal in the annual report (Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}, Table~\ref{tab:app_locators} dividend pages; values in Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}, Table~\ref{tab:app_bonus_ratios}), expressed as a percentage of paid-up capital and divided by 100 to give a proportion. Because the bonus ratio represents that fiscal year's proposed bonus issued in the subsequent fiscal year, $\mathit{BVPS}_{\text{adj}}$ represents a pro-forma forward capitalization adjustment rather than a restated year-end book value. Only BVPS is adjusted; EPS and price remain on the original share base, so this is a one-sided rescaling of BVPS, not a full bonus-dilution adjustment of the valuation relation.
    \item \textbf{R8 (Retrospective Restated EPS):} Re-estimates Model~2 using retrospective audited basic EPS restatements from subsequent annual reports ($n = 28$). Specifically, two bank-year observations in the analyzed sample exhibit retrospective restatements: Global IME Bank FY 2076/77 (reported 17.99, restated 15.33) and NMB Bank FY 2077/78 (reported 16.66, restated 14.76). A third restatement in the broader 30-row dataset, Himalayan Bank FY 2077/78 (reported 28.07, restated 27.60), falls within a trading-suspension window and is excluded from the regression sample; thus, it does not affect the R8 estimates.
    \item \textbf{R9 (Excluding Kanchan-Mismatched Bank-Year):} Re-estimates Model~2 excluding the single bank-year NMB FY 2076/77 ($n = 27$, residual degrees of freedom 13) whose pre-acquisition fundamentals are paired with a post-acquisition April 2021 price following the Kanchan Development Bank acquisition, to verify results are not driven by that timing mismatch. The parallel Kumari Bank FY 2076/77 case, whose April 2021 price reflects the combined bank following the Deva Bikas Bank acquisition while joint operations began on 28 Ashad 2077 BS (three to four days prior to the fiscal year-end), is not excluded here: its year-end balance sheet, and hence BVPS, already reflects the combined bank, whereas the Kanchan acquisition closed after the fiscal year-end, leaving NMB's entire FY 2076/77 fundamentals pre-acquisition.
    \item \textbf{R10 (Excluding Both Kanchan- and Deva-Mismatched Bank-Years):} Re-estimates Model~2 excluding both NMB FY 2076/77 and Kumari Bank FY 2076/77 ($n = 26$, residual degrees of freedom 12) as a supplementary post-hoc sensitivity check. While KBL's year-end balance sheet incorporates Deva Bikas Bank, its annual income statement reflects only three to four days of post-acquisition operating flows against an April 2021 market price that follows nine months of unified operations. Evaluating the combined exclusion provides an empirical check on partial-year flow exposure.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Specification Comparison}
To ensure transparency regarding how fixed effects absorb market-wide variation, baseline two-way fixed-effects estimates are compared directly against:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pooled OLS:} Neglects bank and year heterogeneity, reflecting conventional static literature.
    \item \textbf{Bank Fixed-Effects Only:} Absorbs cross-bank heterogeneity but retains longitudinal macroeconomic price variation.
\end{itemize}

Data extraction, audit reconciliation, and panel assembly are executed in Python (\texttt{pandas}, \texttt{numpy}). Econometric modelling, robust HC3 covariance estimation, diagnostic testing, and visualization are executed using Python (\texttt{statsmodels}, \texttt{seaborn}, \texttt{matplotlib}). Software versions are those in the analysis requirements: \texttt{statsmodels} 0.14.6 (pinned); \texttt{pandas} 2.2 or later, \texttt{numpy} 1.26 or later, \texttt{scipy} 1.11 or later, \texttt{seaborn} 0.13 or later, \texttt{matplotlib} 3.8 or later, and \texttt{PyYAML} 6.0 or later.

\section{Introduction}
This chapter presents the empirical findings, econometric evaluations, and theoretical interpretations resulting from the investigation into the value relevance of accounting information across the 2023 Nepalese commercial banking consolidation wave. The primary objective is to evaluate whether fundamental accounting numbers---specifically earnings per share (EPS) and book value per share (BVPS)---exhibit a statistically discernible association with secondary market equity prices observed on or after statutory annual reporting deadlines ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$), and whether the pricing relations attached to these accounting measures underwent a structural regime shift following the large-scale horizontal amalgamations executed in early 2023.

In accordance with the Faculty of Management, Tribhuvan University \textit{Master's Degree Dissertation Guidelines} (2019, pp.~14--15), the chapter is organized into two major substantive components: \textbf{Results} and \textbf{Discussion}.

The \textbf{Results} section (Section~4.2) details the empirical outcomes derived from the five sampled banking series over the six-year observation period (FY 2076/77 to FY 2081/82, corresponding to Gregorian years 2019/20 through 2024/25). The presentation proceeds through seven structured subsections:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Descriptive Statistics} (Section~4.2.1), summarizing the distributional properties, central tendencies, and dispersions of the primary valuation variables across the full panel and comparative pre- and post-consolidation sub-periods;
    \item \textit{Correlation Analysis and Multicollinearity Assessment} (Section~4.2.2), examining bivariate linear associations, verifying collinearity boundaries between EPS and return on assets (ROA), and reviewing visual dispersion patterns;
    \item \textit{Baseline Value Relevance: Model 1 Estimation} (Section~4.2.3), reporting the estimation of Model~1 under two-way fixed effects with MacKinnon-White HC3 robust standard errors on Student's $t$ residual degrees of freedom;
    \item \textit{Impact of Consolidation on Value Relevance: Model 2 Estimation} (Section~4.2.4), detailing the estimation of Model~2, capturing pre-consolidation valuation slopes, regime interaction coefficients ($\delta_E, \delta_B$), implied post-consolidation net slopes, and the principal joint Wald test of parameter stability;
    \item \textit{Hypothesis Testing} (Section~4.2.5), evaluating the empirical findings against the four operational research hypotheses ($H_1, H_2, H_3, H_4$) formulated in Chapter~I;
    \item \textit{Robustness and Sensitivity Analyses (R1 through R9)} (Section~4.2.6), executing a nine-specification suite (R1--R2 baselines plus seven robustness variants R3--R9) to probe the sensitivity of baseline estimates to profitability definitions, inclusion of eligible dividend disclosures, entity-timing mismatches, transition-year volatility, bonus share dilutive adjustments, and restated basic earnings; and
    \item \textit{Econometric Diagnostics and Specification Comparison} (Section~4.2.7), evaluating variance inflation factors (VIF), Breusch-Pagan heteroskedasticity tests, Cook's distance influence metrics, residual normality, and comparing pooled OLS, bank fixed effects, and two-way fixed effects specifications.
\end{enumerate}

For analytical transparency and audit traceability, the analytical figures embedded in this chapter are computed from the dataset printed in Appendix~B.

The \textbf{Discussion} section (Section~4.3) evaluates, synthesizes, and critically interprets these empirical findings in the context of foundational valuation theory and contemporary empirical literature. It examines:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The structural behavior of accounting valuation slopes in the pre-consolidation regime (Section~4.3.1);
    \item Economic and institutional interpretations of the downward shift in the earnings slope and the attenuation of the book value slope post-consolidation (Section~4.3.2);
    \item Theoretical reconciliation with the \citet{ohlson1995} residual income valuation framework, clean-surplus accounting, dividend signaling, and banking opacity theories (Section~4.3.3);
    \item Comparative alignment and divergence with prior Nepalese and emerging-market empirical literature (Section~4.3.4); and
    \item Methodological, institutional, and regulatory implications stemming from transition-year friction and statutory reporting deadlines under Section 59(2) of the Banks and Financial Institutions Act, 2017 (Section~4.3.5).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Results}

\subsection{Descriptive Statistics}
To establish the empirical baseline of the panel, Table~\ref{tab:t1_descriptives} reports the descriptive statistics---sample size ($n$), mean, standard deviation (SD), minimum, and maximum---for the dependent variable ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$) and explanatory accounting fundamentals (EPS, BVPS, DPS, and ROA) across the full analysis sample ($N = 28$) as well as the partitioned pre-consolidation ($n = 13$) and post-consolidation ($n = 15$) sub-periods.

The descriptive statistics in Table~\ref{tab:t1_descriptives} reveal substantial economic shifts across the pre- and post-consolidation periods:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Market Price ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$):} The full-sample mean closing share price at the reporting deadline was NPR 254.34, with a wide range spanning from NPR 136.90 to NPR 520.00. Across sub-periods, share prices experienced a substantial secular decline. In the pre-consolidation era (FY 2076/77--2078/79), the mean market price stood at NPR 310.59 (SD = 119.46), reaching a high of NPR 520.00 for HBL in FY 2076/77. Following the consolidation wave, the mean market price contracted to NPR 205.60 (SD = 33.45), representing an average price erosion of 33.81\% (NPR 105.00 per share). Concurrently, cross-sectional dispersion in share prices contracted substantially, with standard deviation falling by 72.00\% (from 119.46 to 33.45). This price compression is consistent with broader macroeconomic tightening in Nepal and a general market repricing of commercial bank equities toward a narrower valuation band.
    \item \textbf{Earnings per Share (EPS):} Across the full sample, EPS averaged NPR 14.05, ranging from NPR 0.02 (Kumari Bank in FY 2080/81) to NPR 27.60 (HBL in FY 2076/77). Between regimes, mean EPS declined from NPR 17.90 pre-consolidation to NPR 10.72 post-consolidation---a contraction of 40.13\%. Its standard deviation increased from 3.99 to 6.53 (+63.95\%), and its coefficient of variation rose from 22.3\% to 61.0\%. These are descriptive changes and do not identify their cause.
    \item \textbf{Book Value per Share (BVPS):} In marked contrast to market prices and earnings, book value per share exhibited relative stability across regimes. The full-sample mean BVPS was NPR 160.18 (SD = 17.37), bounded between NPR 132.71 and NPR 190.70. Across sub-periods, mean BVPS rose slightly from NPR 158.11 (SD = 19.10) pre-consolidation to NPR 161.97 (SD = 16.18) post-consolidation---an increase of 2.45\% (NPR 3.87), while its standard deviation declined by 15.30\%. This descriptive pattern does not identify its cause.
    \item \textbf{Dividend per Share (DPS):} Cash dividend distributions declined. In the pre-consolidation period, all sampled banks distributed cash dividends, yielding a mean DPS of NPR 6.06 (SD = 3.56; ranging from NPR 2.00 to NPR 12.50). In the post-consolidation era, mean DPS was NPR 1.40 (SD = 2.97), a 76.90\% reduction; its median was zero because 12 of 15 post-consolidation bank-years recorded no cash dividend. DPS SD declined by 16.62\%, while its coefficient of variation rose from 58.8\% to 212.2\%.
    \item \textbf{Return on Assets (ROA):} Mean operating profitability fell across the structural divide. Full-sample mean ROA stood at 0.99\% (SD = 0.47\%). Pre-consolidation ROA averaged 1.31\% (SD = 0.22\%, range 0.90\% to 1.79\%), whereas post-consolidation ROA averaged 0.70\% (SD = 0.44\%, range 0.00\% to 1.51\%)---a 46.38\% decline; the SD rose by 97.49\% and the coefficient of variation rose from 16.9\% to 62.2\%.

\subsection{Baseline Value Relevance: Model 1 Estimation}
To address Research Question~1 and provide an empirical benchmark of accounting value relevance prior to evaluating regime shifts, Model~1 estimates the static Ohlson valuation equation across the entire six-year panel:
\begin{equation}
\mathit{MPS}^{RD}_{it} = \alpha + \mu_i + \lambda_t + \beta_E \mathit{EPS}_{it} + \beta_B \mathit{BVPS}_{it} + \varepsilon_{it}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha$ is the constant baseline intercept, $\mu_i$ represents unobserved time-invariant bank fixed effects (with GBIME serving as reference bank), and $\lambda_t$ represents common fiscal-year fixed effects (with FY 2076/77 serving as reference year). Standard errors, $t$-statistics, $p$-values, and confidence intervals are estimated using \citet{mackinnon1985} HC3 heteroskedasticity-consistent standard errors evaluated on Student's $t$ distribution with residual degrees of freedom ($\text{df} = 16$).

Table~\ref{tab:t3_model1} reports the parameter estimates for Model~1, with MacKinnon-White HC3 robust standard errors in parentheses beneath each estimate; $t$-statistics, $p$-values, and 95\% confidence intervals are reported in the text.

\subsection{Impact of Consolidation on Value Relevance: Model 2 Estimation}
To evaluate Research Question~2 and directly test whether the 2023 consolidation wave altered the valuation relationship between accounting fundamentals and equity prices, Model~2 introduces consolidation interaction terms into the two-way fixed effects framework:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathit{MPS}^{RD}_{it} ={}& \alpha + \mu_i + \lambda_t + \beta_E \mathit{EPS}_{it} + \beta_B \mathit{BVPS}_{it} \\
& + \delta_E (\mathit{EPS}_{it} \times \mathit{Post}_t) + \delta_B (\mathit{BVPS}_{it} \times \mathit{Post}_t) + \varepsilon_{it}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\mathit{Post}_t = 1$ for the post-consolidation fiscal years (FY 2079/80, 2080/81, and 2081/82) and $\mathit{Post}_t = 0$ for the pre-consolidation fiscal years (FY 2076/77, 2077/78, and 2078/79). Reference categories remain identical (GBIME for $\mu_i$, FY 2076/77 for $\lambda_t$).

Because common fiscal-year fixed effects ($\lambda_t$) are fully specified, the stand-alone indicator $\mathit{Post}_t$ is an exact linear combination of the fiscal-year dummies ($\mathit{Post}_t = D_{2079/80} + D_{2080/81} + D_{2081/82}$); consequently, the main effect of $\mathit{Post}_t$ is perfectly absorbed by the year fixed effects and cannot be separately estimated. The pre-consolidation valuation slopes are captured by $\beta_E$ and $\beta_B$, the regime interaction coefficients are captured by $\delta_E$ and $\delta_B$, and the implied post-consolidation valuation slopes are given by the linear combinations $(\beta_E + \delta_E)$ and $(\beta_B + \delta_B)$.

The empirical estimates in Table~\ref{tab:t4_model2} do not provide statistically significant evidence regarding structural changes in valuation dynamics:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Pre-Consolidation Valuation Regime:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Prior to the 2023 consolidation wave, the estimated valuation slope for earnings per share was $\beta_E = 5.577$ (HC3 SE = 3.560, $t = 1.57, p = 0.140$). In economic magnitude, a one-rupee increase in EPS is associated with a 5.58-rupee increase in price, conditional on book value and the fixed effects. This estimate is not statistically significant at the 5\% level ($p = 0.140$ on $t(14)$), reflecting the wide confidence interval ([-2.059, 13.213]) resulting from small sample degrees of freedom and conservative HC3 standard errors.
        \item The pre-consolidation slope for book value per share was $\beta_B = 0.100$ (HC3 SE = 1.306, $t = 0.08, p = 0.940$). The estimate is close to zero, but its uncertainty does not establish that book value had no incremental association with price.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Consolidation Regime Interaction Shifts:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item The earnings interaction coefficient is negative: $\delta_E = -3.124$ (HC3 SE = 4.003, $t = -0.78, p = 0.448$). Its 95\% HC3 confidence interval is $[-11.710, 5.462]$, which includes no shift and shifts in either direction; the point estimate does not establish a post-consolidation discount.
        \item The book value interaction coefficient is negative: $\delta_B = -1.758$ (HC3 SE = 1.231, $t = -1.43, p = 0.175$), with a 95\% HC3 confidence interval of $[-4.398, 0.882]$. This imprecise point estimate does not establish an erosion in the book-value slope.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Implied Post-Consolidation Slopes:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Combining the baseline slope and interaction shift yields an implied post-consolidation earnings slope of $\beta_E + \delta_E = 2.453$ (HC3 SE = 2.269, $t = 1.08, p = 0.298$), with a 95\% HC3 confidence interval of $[-2.412, 7.319]$. The point estimate is lower than the pre-consolidation estimate, but the difference is not statistically significant.
        \item The implied post-consolidation slope for book value is negative: $\beta_B + \delta_B = -1.658$ (HC3 SE = 1.120, $t = -1.48, p = 0.161$). Its 95\% confidence interval, $[-4.059, 0.744]$, includes zero, so the estimate does not establish a negative association.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Principal Joint Wald Test of Regime Shift:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Because corporate combinations simultaneously alter multiple accounting dimensions, testing interaction coefficients individually can fail to capture multi-parameter structural shifts. The principal econometric test is the joint Wald $F$-test evaluating the composite null hypothesis that both interaction terms are simultaneously zero ($H_0: \delta_E = 0 \text{ and } \delta_B = 0$).
        \item The joint test yields $F(2, 14) = 2.954$ with $p = 0.085$. The null hypothesis is not rejected at the designated 5\% level under HC3. The unadjusted result is below 10\%, but remains suggestive rather than conclusive. A post hoc design-sensitivity calculation gives approximate detectable effects at 80\% power of 10.73 NPR for the pre-period EPS slope, 12.06 NPR for the EPS interaction, and 3.71 NPR for the BVPS interaction, so these non-rejections do not rule out effects smaller than those magnitudes.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Parameter Accounting and Residual Degrees of Freedom:}
    Model~2 estimates 1 constant intercept, 4 bank fixed effects (GBIME reference), 5 fiscal-year fixed effects (FY 2076/77 reference), 2 main slope parameters ($\beta_E, \beta_B$), and 2 interaction parameters ($\delta_E, \delta_B$), consuming a total of 14 degrees of freedom. On $N = 28$ observations, this leaves exactly $28 - 14 = 14$ residual degrees of freedom.
\end{enumerate}

Detailed evaluations of individual hypotheses:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 1 ($H_1$): Value Relevance of Earnings per Share:}
    \textit{Hypothesis Statement:} There is a statistically significant relationship between earnings per share and market share price ($H_0: \beta_E = 0$ vs.\ $H_1: \beta_E \ne 0$).
    In Model~1, the overall EPS slope is $\hat{\beta}_E = 2.095$ with an HC3 standard error of $4.096$ ($t = 0.51, p = 0.616$). In Model~2, the pre-consolidation EPS slope is estimated at $\hat{\beta}_E = 5.577$ with an HC3 standard error of $3.560$ ($t = 1.57, p = 0.140$). In both models, the point estimates are positive, but because $p > 0.05$ and the standard errors are large, the study fails to reject the null hypothesis of no statistical association at the 5\% significance level.
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 2 ($H_2$): Value Relevance of Book Value per Share:}
    \textit{Hypothesis Statement:} There is a statistically significant relationship between book value per share and market share price ($H_0: \beta_B = 0$ vs.\ $H_1: \beta_B \ne 0$).
    In Model~1, the overall slope is $\hat{\beta}_B = 0.219$ ($t = 0.12, p = 0.908$). In Model~2, the pre-consolidation BVPS slope is $\hat{\beta}_B = 0.100$ with an HC3 standard error of $1.306$ ($t = 0.08, p = 0.940$). Because $p \gg 0.05$ across both specifications, the study fails to reject the null hypothesis. Prior to consolidation, the book value slope is imprecisely estimated (95\% CI $[-2.700, 2.901]$), so the study cannot establish whether book value had a negligible, positive, or negative association with price beyond earnings and fixed effects.
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 3 ($H_3$): Structural Shift in Earnings Value Relevance:}
    \textit{Hypothesis Statement:} The value relevance of earnings per share underwent a statistically significant shift following the 2023 consolidation wave ($H_0: \delta_E = 0$ vs.\ $H_1: \delta_E \ne 0$).
    The interaction coefficient is $\hat{\delta}_E = -3.124$ with an HC3 standard error of $4.003$ ($t = -0.78, p = 0.448$). Although the point estimate indicates a substantial downward shift in the earnings slope, the individual coefficient is not statistically significant at conventional thresholds. The study fails to reject the null hypothesis of no structural shift in EPS value relevance when evaluated in isolation.
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 4 ($H_4$): Structural Shift in Book Value Relevance:}
    \textit{Hypothesis Statement:} The value relevance of book value per share underwent a statistically significant shift following the 2023 consolidation wave ($H_0: \delta_B = 0$ vs.\ $H_1: \delta_B \ne 0$).
    The interaction coefficient is $\hat{\delta}_B = -1.758$ with an HC3 standard error of $1.231$ ($t = -1.43, p = 0.175$). Although the negative shift reduces the implied post-consolidation slope to $-1.658$, the interaction term does not achieve statistical significance at the 5\% level. The study fails to reject the null hypothesis of no structural shift in BVPS value relevance when evaluated in isolation.
    \item \textbf{Principal Joint Structural Test:}
    Evaluating the joint composite hypothesis $H_0: \delta_E = \delta_B = 0$ under the pre-specified HC3 covariance yields $F(2,14)=2.954$ ($p=0.085$). The test does not reject at 5\% under HC3; the unadjusted result is below 10\% but remains suggestive rather than conclusive. The same test rejects at 5\% under the classical ($F=9.573$, $p=0.002$), HC0 ($F=12.654$, $p=0.001$), HC1 ($F=6.327$, $p=0.011$) and HC2 ($F=6.243$, $p=0.012$) covariances, and this pattern holds for all eight joint tests R2--R9 (Table~\ref{tab:cov_sensitivity}). The baseline non-rejection is therefore specific to the HC3 estimator.
\end{itemize}

The sensitivity analysis yields critical econometric and substantive insights:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Operating Profitability Substitution (R3):} Substituting ROA (PAT divided by average total assets) for EPS yields an operating profitability slope of $83.760$ (HC3 SE = 79.852, $p = 0.312$) and an interaction shift of $\delta_{ROA} = -43.570$ (HC3 SE = 83.671, $p = 0.611$). The book value interaction remains negative ($\delta_B = -1.901, \text{SE} = 1.290, p = 0.163$), and the joint Wald test yields $F(2, 14) = 2.46$ ($p = 0.121$). This is consistent with the observed valuation dynamics not being an artifact of per-share earnings scaling, though no substantive coefficient in this specification is statistically significant and it does not establish fundamental operational repricing.
    \item \textbf{Dividend Incorporation (R4):} Augmenting Model~2 with declared cash dividends per share (DPS) produces an earnings slope of $\hat{\beta}_E = 6.183$ (SE = 4.508, $p = 0.193$) and a book value slope of $\hat{\beta}_B = -0.642$ (SE = 1.378, $p = 0.649$). The interaction shifts remain consistently negative ($\delta_E = -4.072, \text{SE} = 5.151, p = 0.443; \delta_B = -1.770, \text{SE} = 1.335, p = 0.208$), and the joint Wald $F$ is $2.77$ ($p = 0.100$). Because adding DPS consumes an additional parameter, residual degrees of freedom decrease from 14 to exactly 13 on $N = 28$. DPS enters as an additive control. An interacted DPS variant (DPS $\\times$ Post) was also estimated; the joint regime-shift test is $F(2,12)=2.85$ ($p=0.097$), the same non-rejection as the additive R4, so the additive specification is retained.
    \item \textbf{Transition-Year Exclusion (R5):} Dropping the initial post-merger integration year (FY 2079/80, $N = 23, \text{residual df} = 10$) to eliminate one-off transition accounting friction preserves the structural pattern: $\hat{\beta}_E = 5.993$ (SE = 4.635, $p = 0.225$), $\hat{\delta}_E = -2.578$ (SE = 5.521, $p = 0.650$), $\hat{\delta}_B = -1.880$ (SE = 1.419, $p = 0.215$), with joint Wald $F(2, 10) = 2.41$ ($p = 0.139$). This pattern is consistent with the interpretation that the negative interaction shifts are not solely driven by temporary integration write-offs in the immediate post-merger fiscal year. The implied post-consolidation slopes are $\hat{\beta}_E + \hat{\delta}_E = 3.415$ (SE $= 3.891$, $p = 0.401$) for EPS and $\hat{\beta}_B + \hat{\delta}_B = -2.081$ (SE $= 1.969$, $p = 0.315$) for BVPS, both insignificant and the same signs as the baseline.
    \item \textbf{Entity-Timing Mismatch Exclusion (R6) --- Sensitivity Result:} Specification R6 excludes all five observations from FY 2078/79 ($N = 23$, residual $df=10$), including NMB, whose series did not have this 2023 merger mismatch. Excluding only the four mismatched bank observations produces numerically identical estimates because the remaining NMB observation is absorbed by the year effect. The four-row variant is, however, numerically degenerate for inference: the remaining NMB FY 2078/79 observation is the only observation in its year, so it has leverage 1.0 and a residual of approximately zero, and the HC3 standard errors of the year dummies reach approximately 205. The joint test is unchanged ($F(2,10)=5.818$, $p=0.021$), but the covariance matrix in that variant is not reliable. FY 2078/79 represents an entity-timing asymmetry for the four merged banks: audited statements published at the end of Chaitra 2079 BS reflected standalone, pre-merger banking operations, whereas prices observed on 13 April 2023 reflected the expanded post-merger entities.
    When this entity-mismatch fiscal year is excluded, the unadjusted joint Wald test is below 5\%: $F(2, 10) = 5.82, p = 0.021$, compared with the 5\% critical value of approximately 4.103; a 29.5\% decrease in the observed statistic would remove the raw rejection. This is the only unadjusted rejection among the nine joint tests R2--R10 under HC3; Holm adjustment across that family gives $p = 0.190$ ($p = 0.169$ across R2--R9). The R6 result therefore remains a sensitivity finding and does not provide multiplicity-adjusted evidence of a regime shift. In the R6 sample, dropping any single bank leaves the joint test far from rejection ($F = 0.615$--$1.309$, $p = 0.329$--$0.572$), so the R6 rejection does not survive a leave-one-bank perturbation.
    In this FY 2078/79-excluded specification, the book value interaction shift is estimated at $\hat{\delta}_B = -2.611$ with an HC3 standard error of $1.517$ ($t = -1.72, p = 0.116$ on $t(10)$), while the earnings interaction is $\hat{\delta}_E = -2.436$ (HC3 SE = 4.755, $t = -0.51, p = 0.620$). Neither individual interaction is significant at the 10\% level, and R5 at the same sample size and residual degrees of freedom does not reject ($F(2,10)=2.41$, $p=0.139$). R6 is an unadjusted sensitivity result. The implied post-consolidation slopes are $\hat{\beta}_E + \hat{\delta}_E = 1.900$ (SE $= 2.649$, $p = 0.490$) and $\hat{\beta}_B + \hat{\delta}_B = -1.809$ (SE $= 1.357$, $p = 0.212$), both insignificant and the same signs as the baseline; the joint rejection therefore does not imply significant post-consolidation slopes.
    \item \textbf{Bonus Capitalization Adjustment (R7):} Adjusting book value only for subsequent bonus share dilution ($\mathit{BVPS}_{\text{adj}} = \mathit{BVPS} / (1 + \text{bonus ratio})$, with EPS and price left on the original share base) yields estimates that are qualitatively similar to baseline but not identical: $\hat{\beta}_E = 5.775$ (SE = 4.145, $p = 0.185$), $\hat{\delta}_E = -4.043$ (SE = 4.446, $p = 0.379$), $\hat{\delta}_B = -1.533$ (SE = 1.595, $p = 0.353$), with Wald $F(2, 14) = 2.37$ ($p = 0.130$). The BVPS slope rises from 0.100 to 0.153 and the joint $F$ falls by about 20\%; the check is therefore a one-sided rescaling of BVPS, not a full bonus-dilution adjustment of the valuation relation.
    \item \textbf{Restated Earnings per Share (R8):} Substituting the basic EPS values printed as restated in later annual reports for GBIME FY 2076/77 and NMB FY 2077/78 leaves the baseline conclusions intact: $\hat{\beta}_E = 5.256$ (SE = 3.016, $p = 0.103$), $\hat{\delta}_E = -2.860$ (SE = 3.807, $p = 0.465$), $\hat{\delta}_B = -1.812$ (SE = 1.223, $p = 0.161$), with Wald $F(2, 14) = 2.92$ ($p = 0.087$).
    \item \textbf{Kanchan-Mismatched Bank-Year Exclusion (R9):} Dropping the single bank-year whose pre-acquisition fundamentals are paired with a post-acquisition price (NMB FY 2076/77, $N = 27$, residual df = 13) leaves the baseline pattern unchanged: $\hat{\beta}_E = 6.000$ (SE = 7.028, $p = 0.409$), $\hat{\delta}_E = -3.445$ (SE = 6.598, $p = 0.610$), $\hat{\delta}_B = -1.741$ (SE = 1.477, $p = 0.259$), with joint Wald $F(2, 13) = 1.79$ ($p = 0.205$). The Kanchan timing mismatch does not drive the baseline finding of no significant joint shift. HBL FY 2076/77 is not covered by R9; the single-observation leave-one-out aggregates are reported in the note to Table~\ref{tab:app_sensitivity} in Appendix E (joint $p$ ranging 0.035--0.291 across the 28 deletions), but the HBL FY 2076/77-specific deletion is not printed there.
\end{itemize}

A wild-cluster bootstrap sensitivity check was run on the two joint Wald decisions. The null was imposed by fitting Model~1, its residuals were resampled by bank with Rademacher weights, and the HC3 Wald statistic was recomputed on each of the 32 enumerated sign patterns. For Model~2 the bootstrap $p$-value is $0.0625$ (2 of 32 patterns), below the HC3 $p = 0.085$ but still above 0.05; for R6 it is $0.0625$ (2 of 32), above the HC3 $p = 0.021$. Only the R6 decision flips under the bootstrap, and that flip hangs on a single pattern out of 32. With 32 patterns the smallest attainable $p$-value is $2/32 = 0.0625$, so this bootstrap cannot produce a 5\% rejection for any specification; the R6 flip is therefore mechanically forced, not evidence about cluster-robust inference. The check is inconclusive at $G = 5$: it does not overturn the HC3 inference, and it provides no information about whether the joint-test decisions would survive a cluster-robust alternative.

Two additional price-window sensitivities show that the sample rule affects the joint-test decision. Re-including the two observed delayed prices gives $F(2,16)=4.762$ ($p=0.024$; $N=30$). In a stale-price carry-forward scenario, using the last prices in the panel-series files for HBL (NPR 487) and NIB/NIMB (NPR 453), both dated 12 May 2021, gives $F(2,16)=3.870$ ($p=0.043$). The candidate price table also records an alternative reported pair, HBL NPR 484 and NIB NPR 460, dated 13 May 2021; using that pair gives $F(2,16)=3.821$ ($p=0.044$). The difference reflects an unresolved one-day/source discrepancy. Both pairs are roughly a year before the April 2022 target, so these are stale-price scenarios, not observations meeting the five-day rule. In the $N=30$ model with the observed delayed prices, the pre-period BVPS estimate rises from 0.100 to 0.709; excluding the delayed rows therefore attenuates that slope in this sensitivity comparison. The delayed observations coincide with merger-related trading gaps, but their missingness mechanism and its direction cannot be established from these cases.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Value Relevance of Accounting Fundamentals in the Pre-Consolidation Regime}
As reported in Table~\ref{tab:t4_model2}, in the pre-consolidation regime (FY 2076/77 through FY 2078/79), the estimated earnings slope was $\hat{\beta}_E = 5.577$ ($p = 0.140$ on $t(14)$), whereas the book-value slope was $\hat{\beta}_B = 0.100$ ($p = 0.940$ on $t(14)$).

These parameter estimates are not statistically significant at the 5\% level and are imprecise; their relative magnitudes do not establish the valuation behavior of the Nepalese secondary banking market prior to the 2023 consolidation wave:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Relative Point Estimates:} The earnings point estimate ($5.58$) is larger than the BVPS estimate ($0.10$), but both are statistically insignificant and have wide uncertainty. These estimates do not establish that investors prioritized earnings or that earnings served as the primary valuation benchmark.
    \item \textbf{Book Value Estimate:} The BVPS point estimate is close to zero, but its confidence interval is wide; the estimate does not establish that book value had no incremental pricing association. The reasons investors may have assigned to this estimate cannot be identified from the regression.
\end{itemize}

\subsection[Regime Shift: Earnings and Book Value Slopes Post-Consolidation]{Regime Shift: Earnings and Book Value Slopes Post-\\Consolidation}
As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:t4_model2}, following the consolidation wave of early 2023, the point estimates show an attenuation of the implied slopes that is not statistically significant, with negative interaction coefficients ($\hat{\delta}_E = -3.124, p = 0.448; \hat{\delta}_B = -1.758, p = 0.175$) and attenuated implied post-consolidation slopes ($\hat{\beta}_E + \hat{\delta}_E = 2.453, p = 0.298; \hat{\beta}_B + \hat{\delta}_B = -1.658, p = 0.161$).

Because neither individual interaction term achieves statistical significance at conventional thresholds, and the joint Wald test in the baseline sample is marginally significant at 10\% ($p = 0.085$), these patterns are interpreted not as proven causal effects, but as suggestive structural tendencies consistent with several economic and institutional mechanisms operating in Nepal's post-merger banking environment:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Asset Quality Pressures and Loan Provisioning (Plausible Mechanism):} The horizontal amalgamations executed in 2023 required surviving commercial banks to assimilate legacy loan portfolios from acquired institutions. One plausible mechanism is that, following consolidation, commercial banks experienced rising non-performing loans, driven by broad macroeconomic deceleration, construction sector stress, and stricter loan-loss provisioning requirements. No non-performing-loan, provisioning, or macroeconomic variable is estimated in this study, so this mechanism is a hypothesis, not a finding. To the extent that credit-loss charges expanded, reported accounting earnings exhibited higher volatility; whether that volatility reflects transitoriness was not measured. Equity investors may have recognized that reported net income was increasingly sensitive to provisioning assumptions, leading to a discount on reported earnings. This mechanism is consistent with the point-estimate attenuation of the earnings slope, though direct asset quality variables are not estimated in the econometric model.
    \item \textbf{Capital Expansion and Share Supply:} Amalgamations significantly expanded the paid-up equity base of surviving banks through share swap agreements. The influx of newly created equity shares diluted per-share earnings (mean EPS contracted by 40.13\% post-consolidation, from NPR 17.90 to NPR 10.72) and expanded the secondary market float. Market participants may have factored this enlarged equity supply into their pricing models, anticipating slower growth in per-share metrics (see Chapter~I, Section~1.1; Chapter~III, Section~3.3).
    \item \textbf{Dividend Cessation and Information Loss:} In the pre-consolidation period, regular cash dividends provided an observable benchmark for equity valuations (mean DPS = NPR 6.06). Following consolidation, regulatory capital conservation guidelines and heightened provisioning mandates coincided with widespread cash dividend suspensions (mean DPS fell to NPR 1.40, with 12 of 15 post-consolidation bank-years recording zero cash dividends). The withdrawal of regular dividend payments removed an observable cash flow signal. This mechanism is a theoretical hypothesis, not a corroborated finding: the bivariate DPS--price correlation in this sample is near zero ($r = 0.082$, $p = 0.679$; Table~\ref{tab:t2_correlations}), so the sample provides no evidence of a dividend--price link.
    \item \textbf{Interpreting the Negative Book Value Point Estimate:} The negative implied slope for book value per share ($\hat{\beta}_B + \hat{\delta}_B = -1.658$, HC3 SE = 1.120, $t = -1.48, p = 0.161$) is statistically insignificant under the Student's $t(14)$ distribution. It cannot therefore be presented as an established empirical law or as evidence that book equity increases were associated with lower prices. Possible explanations based on asset quality or capital structure remain hypotheses; these variables were not estimated in the model.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Reconciling with Theoretical Frameworks}
The empirical findings can be evaluated against foundational theoretical models in accounting and financial economics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Divergence and Resonance with \citet{kwon2020}:}
    Comparing our results with the post-merger valuation framework of \citet{kwon2020} reveals an important divergence alongside a shared insight:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Where our results resonate:} In line with Kwon and Wang, our findings are consistent with the perspective that corporate amalgamations can introduce accounting noise and structural disruption that attenuate the valuation relevance of earnings. Following major mergers, financial statement comparability is disrupted and historical earnings predictability may diminish; the point estimate for the earnings shift is negative ($\hat{\delta}_E = -3.124$) but statistically insignificant ($p = 0.448$), so it does not establish that equity markets discount reported earnings.
        \item \textit{Where our results diverge:} In Kwon and Wang's framework, when earnings become noisy following mergers, book value may assume an anchoring role. In this sample, the book-value slope point estimate changes from $\hat{\beta}_B=0.100$ to an implied post-consolidation estimate of $-1.658$, but the post estimate is statistically insignificant ($p=0.161$). The result does not establish whether book value served as an asset anchor.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The \citet{ohlson1995} Residual Income Framework:}
    The Ohlson model specifies firm equity value as the sum of contemporaneous book value and capitalized expected future abnormal earnings:
    \begin{equation}
    P_t = B_t + \sum_{\tau=1}^{\infty} R^{-\tau} \mathbb{E}_t [\tilde{x}_{t+\tau}^a]
    \end{equation}
    Our baseline estimates in the pre-consolidation period display positive point estimates for both components ($\hat{\beta}_E = 5.58, \hat{\beta}_B = 0.10$). However, the post-consolidation regime exposes the limitations of linear valuation models during periods of banking restructuring. When clean-surplus accounting is complicated by merger reserve adjustments, swap accounting, and dividend halts, the linear pricing model is estimated imprecisely, producing wide confidence intervals and attenuated point estimates; a structural breakdown of the pricing relation is not established.
    \item \textbf{Dividend Signaling Theory \citep{bhattacharya1979,john1985}:}
    Under dividend signaling theory, cash distributions provide a costly and credible signal verifying reported earnings quality. In the Nepalese banking sector, where retail equity investors face informational opacity regarding loan classifications, cash dividends historically functioned as an observable verification of accounting profits. When regulatory capital constraints coincided with the elimination of cash dividends post-consolidation, the absence of this verification signal coincided with the attenuation of the earnings slope; the sample itself shows no dividend--price association ($r = 0.082$, $p = 0.679$), so this is a theoretical mechanism, not a corroborated finding.
    \item \textbf{Banking Opacity and Regulatory Forbearance \citep{morgan2002,flannery2004}:}
    Commercial banks are recognized in financial economics as opaque institutions due to the proprietary nature of loan assets and the discretion inherent in loan provisioning. In Nepal, regulatory forbearance provisions---allowing consolidated banks extended timelines to meet regulatory ratios---may have increased informational opacity. When market participants cannot independently verify loan portfolio quality, they may discount reported accounting metrics, attenuating valuation slopes.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Comparative Context with Nepalese and International Empirical Studies}
Evaluating the empirical findings of this dissertation against prior literature reveals important methodological and empirical distinctions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Methodological Differences with Prior Nepalese Studies:}
    Prior empirical literature on Nepalese commercial banks has predominantly reported statistically significant positive coefficients for accounting fundamentals \citep{bhattarai2014,joshi2023}. In contrast, our two-way fixed effects baseline yields statistically insignificant slopes ($\hat{\beta}_E = 2.09, p = 0.616; \hat{\beta}_B = 0.22, p = 0.908$). This divergence reflects four critical methodological factors:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Exclusion of Circular Price-to-Earnings Multiples:} Several prior Nepalese studies included the Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratio as an explanatory regressor predicting share price \citep{bhattarai2014,chhetri2023,giri2024,joshi2023,pant2024}. As demonstrated mathematically in Chapter~II, because $\text{P/E} = \mathit{MPS} / \mathit{EPS}$, regressing $\mathit{MPS}$ on P/E introduces the dependent variable into the regressor, generating mechanical circularity and inflated statistical significance. This dissertation strictly excludes P/E.
        \item \textit{Omission of Macro-Year Fixed Effects in Pooled Models:} Prior studies frequently relied on pooled OLS, omitting fiscal-year dummies. As Table~\ref{tab:spec_comp} illustrates, pooled OLS yields an EPS point estimate of $4.882$ compared with $2.095$ in two-way fixed effects. Year fixed effects absorb common fiscal-year price variation, but the two-way LSDV slope is conditional on both bank and year indicators and is not a purely within-year estimate. The pooled estimate is imprecise ($t=1.58$, $p=0.128$), as is the Model~1 estimate; this comparison does not establish that omitted year shocks explain the pooled literature's results.
        \item \textit{Standardized Reporting-Deadline Measurement:} Bhattarai (2014) measured share prices at fiscal year-end (Ashadh-end / mid-July), before audited reports were published. This study measures prices on or after the statutory deadline under Section 59(2) of BAFIA, operationalized at the end of Chaitra (mid-April) \citep{bafia2017}. This reduces temporal mismatch but does not verify the public release date for each bank-year or eliminate possible information leakage.
        \item \textit{Sample Harmonization:} In reconciling prior samples, \citet{bhattarai2014} analyzed nine commercial banks over 2006--2014, while \citet{joshi2023} analyzed seven commercial banks over 2012/13--2021/22. Both examined pre-consolidation periods under pooled structures; whether that pooling explains their higher reported significance is not established by this study's comparison, since the pooled EPS slope here is itself insignificant ($p = 0.128$).
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Alignment with Recent Methodological Studies:}
    Our findings closely parallel more recent Nepalese studies that document valuation instability:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \citet{chaudhari2025} examined ten commercial banks over 2014/15--2023/24 without P/E circularity and documented strong, statistically significant positive coefficients for EPS and DPS, a statistically significant negative association for ROA, while among the macroeconomic variables inflation was significant at the 5\% level ($p = 0.037$) and interest rates and GDP were insignificant.
        \item \citet{pant2024} investigated seven commercial banks through FY 2023/24 and documented a negative, statistically insignificant BVPS coefficient, consistent with our post-consolidation findings.
        \item \citet{timalsina2026} evaluated 13 commercial banks over FY 2011/12 to 2023/24 and documented statistically significant positive associations of EPS, DPS, P/E, firm size, and inflation with share prices, with interest rates exerting a significant negative effect.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Emerging Market Literature Context:}
    In broader emerging and frontier markets, \citet{almumani2014} in Jordan found that EPS, BVPS, and price-earnings ratios were the significant positive determinants of market price, with EPS showing the highest bivariate correlation ($r = 0.84$), and \citet{malhotra2013} in India found that book value per share, earnings per share, and price-earnings multiples significantly influenced market prices. These studies report an EPS/BVPS price relationship in comparable markets, though neither tested whether value relevance changes across a consolidation regime.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Methodological Context: Transition Dynamics and Structural Discontinuities}
The sensitivity analyses conducted in Section~\ref{subsec:robustness_suite} provide important methodological insights for evaluating corporate restructuring in frontier capital markets:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Entity-Boundary Timing and Sensitivity Check R6:}
    The most salient sensitivity finding arises from specification R6, which excludes FY 2078/79 ($N = 23, \text{residual df} = 10$). In that specification, the joint Wald test of regime shift achieves statistical significance at the 5\% level:
    \begin{equation}
    F(2, 10) = 5.82, \quad p = 0.021
    \end{equation}
    This result reflects an entity-timing sensitivity: FY 2078/79 accounts represent pre-merger operations while April 2023 prices reflect post-merger entities. The baseline joint test does not reject at 5\% ($p=0.085$). Excluding the year yields an unadjusted $p=0.021$, but the result is not significant after Holm adjustment across the nine joint tests ($p=0.190$). It is reported as an association across calendar regimes, not as a causal merger effect.
    \item \textbf{The 5-Day Freshness Rule and Trading Suspensions:}
    The exclusion of HBL and NIMB for FY 2077/78 reflects extended merger-related trading suspensions. The two observations exceed the five-day operational cutoff and are omitted without imputation. Because their delays coincide with merger-related suspensions, the exclusion may be informative; including them changes the baseline Model~2 joint test to $F(2,16)=4.762$ ($p=0.024$), compared with $F(2,14)=2.954$ ($p=0.085$) in the 28-row sample.
    \item \textbf{BAFIA Section 59(2) Reporting Deadlines as an Empirical Anchor:}
    Anchoring share price observations to the nine-month statutory reporting deadline under Section 59(2) of BAFIA, operationalized at the end of Chaitra (mid-April), provided a reproducible observation rule. It reduces date-selection ambiguity but does not verify each bank-year public release date or eliminate possible information leakage.
\end{itemize}

In summary, the estimated slopes are consistent with lower earnings and book-value associations after consolidation, but the estimates are imprecise and the baseline joint test does not reject at 5\% under HC3. The unadjusted R6 result is not significant after Holm adjustment across the nine joint tests and depends on the sample definition. These analyses do not establish a causal merger effect.

\section{Summary of the Study}

\subsection{Research Overview, Problem, and Objectives}
The valuation of common equity lies at the intersection of accounting disclosure, corporate governance, and capital market efficiency. Under the residual income valuation (RIV) framework established by \citet{ohlson1995} and \citet{feltham1995}, market equity value is formalized as a linear function of contemporaneous balance-sheet equity (book value per share, capturing cumulative resources) and operational profitability (earnings per share, capturing abnormal forward-looking earning capacity), anchored in the clean-surplus accounting relation.

In emerging and frontier capital markets such as Nepal, the empirical stability of these accounting valuation relationships is routinely challenged by institutional friction, regulatory shifts, information asymmetry, and structural corporate reorganizations. The commercial banking sector in Nepal represents the dominant institutional pillar of the Nepal Stock Exchange (NEPSE), accounting for more than 30\% of total market capitalization \citep{gurungdahal2025}. Over the past decade, Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB) pursued aggressive regulatory policies to consolidate the banking industry, culminating in the 2022--2023 consolidation wave during which major commercial banks executed large-scale horizontal amalgamations and statutory acquisitions.

Despite the landmark scale of this corporate restructuring, existing empirical research on Nepalese banking equity valuation exhibited substantial theoretical, methodological, and econometric deficiencies:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Almost all published Nepalese studies employed static, full-sample pooled or one-way random-effects regressions, implicitly assuming parameter stability and failing to test whether corporate combinations altered valuation slopes across the pre- and post-consolidation regimes \citep{chaudhari2025,chhetri2023,timalsina2026}; \citet{gurungdahal2025} estimated a bank fixed-effects model rather than a pooled regression, but likewise did not test a regime shift;
    \item Empirical models frequently introduced the circular Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratio as an independent regressor predicting market share price (MPS), embedding the dependent variable within its own explanatory matrix and generating severe mechanical endogeneity and spurious correlation \citep{bhattarai2014,giri2024,joshi2023,pant2024};
    \item Bhattarai (2014) measured share prices at fiscal year-end (Ashadh-end / mid-July), long before audited annual financial statements were completed and published, inducing severe look-ahead bias and violating temporal causality \citep{bhattarai2014}; and
    \item Prior panel estimations routinely ignored the finite-sample constraints of small cross-sectional clusters, risking false discoveries and overstated statistical precision through inappropriate covariance estimators. This study's own cluster count is $G = 5$.
\end{itemize}

To resolve these empirical gaps, this dissertation addressed three core research questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Are fundamental accounting measures (EPS and BVPS) statistically associated with secondary market share prices observed on or after statutory annual reporting deadlines ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$)?
    \item Did the valuation associations of EPS and BVPS differ between the pre-consolidation period (FY 2076/77--2078/79) and the post-consolidation period (FY 2079/80--2081/82)?
    \item How sensitive are the estimated valuation coefficients to transition-year effects, entity-timing mismatches, operating profitability substitutions, inclusion of eligible dividend disclosures, bonus share adjustments, restated earnings, and sensitivity analysis excluding the NMB FY 2076/77 bank-year with the Kanchan acquisition timing mismatch?
\end{itemize}

Correspondingly, the study set out to: (i) evaluate the baseline value relevance of EPS and BVPS under a standardized post-reporting-deadline pricing framework; (ii) quantify structural shifts in valuation slopes across the 2023 consolidation wave using an interactive panel specification; and (iii) execute a comprehensive nine-specification estimation and robustness suite (R1--R2 baselines plus seven robustness variants R3--R9) to establish the empirical boundary conditions of accounting valuation slopes in Nepal.

\subsection{Methodology and Research Design Synthesis}
To execute this investigation with rigorous econometric discipline, the study employed a longitudinal panel design spanning six fiscal years (FY 2076/77 through FY 2081/82, corresponding to 2019/20 through 2024/25). The target population encompassed all commercial banks licensed by Nepal Rastra Bank as Class `A' financial institutions. A purposive sample of five commercial banking series was selected:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Four amalgamated banking series representing the major corporate combinations of the early-2023 wave: Global IME Bank Limited (GBIME, absorbed Bank of Kathmandu), Himalayan Bank Limited (HBL, acquired Civil Bank), Kumari Bank Limited (KBL, absorbed NCC Bank), and Nepal Investment Mega Bank Limited (NIMB, amalgamation of Nepal Investment Bank and Mega Bank Nepal); and
    \item One benchmark banking series: NMB Bank Limited (NMB), which maintained continuous corporate operations throughout the 2023 wave without participating in horizontal combinations (note: NMB acquired Kanchan Development Bank Limited in August 2020, adding approximately Nepalese rupees (NPR) 0.50 billion in new shares issued to Kanchan shareholders at the 85\% swap ratio, alongside a 13\% bonus share distribution, in FY 2077/78, while its corporate identity remained continuous through the 2023 consolidation).
\end{itemize}

To reduce temporal mismatch between accounting periods and price observations, market prices were observed on or after the statutory annual financial reporting deadline. Section 59(2) of the Banks and Financial Institutions Act, 2017 (BAFIA) legally mandates that commercial banks publish their audited annual balance sheets and profit and loss accounts within nine months into the subsequent fiscal year---operationalized at the end of Chaitra (mid-April) of the subsequent fiscal year \citep{bafia2017}. The dependent variable, $\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$, was defined as the closing market price on the first trading session on or after this statutory threshold, subject to a strict 5-day freshness rule.

Under this 5-day rule, 28 clean bank-year observations were realized out of an intended balanced panel of 30 bank-years (13 pre-consolidation and 15 post-consolidation observations). Two observations were excluded and dropped rather than imputed: HBL FY 2077/78 (trading resumed 54 days late on 6 June 2022) and NIMB FY 2077/78 (trading resumed 47 days late on 30 May 2022), both resulting from prolonged merger-related secondary market trading suspensions around the April 2022 deadline.

Econometric estimation was structured around two core models:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Model 1 (Baseline Value Relevance):} Specified as a two-way fixed effects panel regression:
    \begin{equation}
    \mathit{MPS}^{RD}_{it} = \alpha + \mu_i + \lambda_t + \beta_E \mathit{EPS}_{it} + \beta_B \mathit{BVPS}_{it} + \varepsilon_{it}
    \end{equation}
    where $\mu_i$ captures unobserved bank fixed effects (with GBIME as reference) and $\lambda_t$ absorbs common fiscal-year macroeconomic shocks and NEPSE-wide price cycles (with FY 2076/77 as reference). Residual degrees of freedom were exact: $28 - 12 = 16$.
    \item \textbf{Model 2 (Regime Shift Interaction):} Specified to estimate structural changes across the early-2023 consolidation wave:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathit{MPS}^{RD}_{it} ={}& \alpha + \mu_i + \lambda_t + \beta_E \mathit{EPS}_{it} + \beta_B \mathit{BVPS}_{it} \\
    & + \delta_E (\mathit{EPS}_{it} \times \mathit{Post}_t) + \delta_B (\mathit{BVPS}_{it} \times \mathit{Post}_t) + \varepsilon_{it}
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathit{Post}_t = 1$ for post-consolidation years (FY 2079/80--2081/82) and 0 for pre-consolidation years (FY 2076/77--2078/79). The main effect of $\mathit{Post}_t$ was fully absorbed by the fiscal-year fixed effects ($\lambda_t$). Residual degrees of freedom were exact: $28 - 14 = 14$.
\end{itemize}

Because the five-bank panel is too small to support a reliable cluster-robust correction, all inferential statistical tests ($t$-tests, confidence intervals, and the principal joint Wald $F$-test of $H_0: \delta_E = \delta_B = 0$) were conducted using the heteroskedasticity-consistent (HC3) covariance estimator of \citet{mackinnon1985} evaluated on Student's $t$ residual degrees of freedom. HC3 addresses heteroskedasticity, not within-bank correlation, so the within-bank independence assumption remains untested.

\subsection{Summary of Major Empirical Findings}
The empirical analysis yielded several substantive findings regarding the statistical properties, baseline relevance, regime stability, and sensitivity of accounting fundamentals. The underlying dataset is printed in Appendix~\ref{app:analysis_dataset}, and the full estimation outputs behind these findings are printed in Appendix~\ref{app:estimation_outputs}.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Descriptive Shifts Across Regimes:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Market Price Compression:} Full sample mean $\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$ stood at NPR 254.34. Across regimes, mean share price dropped by 33.81\% from NPR 310.59 pre-consolidation to NPR 205.60 post-consolidation, while cross-sectional standard deviation contracted by 72.00\% (from 119.46 to 33.45), documenting a substantial market-wide price compression.
        \item \textit{Earnings Deterioration and Dispersion:} Mean EPS contracted by 40.13\% from NPR 17.90 pre-consolidation to NPR 10.72 post-consolidation. Its SD increased by 63.95\% (3.99 to 6.53), and its coefficient of variation rose from 22.3\% to 61.0\%.
        \item \textit{Dividend Reduction:} Mean DPS fell by 76.90\% (NPR 6.06 to NPR 1.40); its post-consolidation median was zero, with 12 of 15 observations recording no cash dividend. The DPS SD declined 16.62\%, while its coefficient of variation rose from 58.8\% to 212.2\%.
        \item \textit{Book Value Stability:} Mean BVPS increased by 2.45\% from NPR 158.11 to NPR 161.97, while its SD declined by 15.30\% (NPR 19.10 to NPR 16.18). These descriptive changes do not identify their cause.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Correlation and Multicollinearity Boundaries:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Operating profitability (ROA) correlated almost perfectly with EPS ($r = 0.975, p < 0.01$), confirming that simultaneous inclusion would generate severe multicollinearity (variance inflation factor (VIF) $\approx 20$). Hence, ROA was preserved strictly as an isolated substitute.
        \item EPS and BVPS have a moderate bivariate correlation ($r = 0.372, p = 0.051$). Model~1 substantive-regressor VIFs are approximately 1.16. Model~2 VIFs are 10.33 (EPS), 2.28 (BVPS), 15.15 (EPS $\times$ Post), and 24.13 (BVPS $\times$ Post), warranting caution in interpreting individual coefficients. On the full fitted design the VIFs are 5.70 and 12.32 (Model~1) and 14.82, 12.46, 27.34 and 259.32 (Model~2).
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Baseline Value Relevance (Model 1):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item In the baseline two-way fixed effects model, the estimated earnings coefficient was $\hat{\beta}_E = 2.095$ (HC3 SE = 4.096, $t = 0.51, p = 0.616$ on $t(16)$) and the book value coefficient was $\hat{\beta}_B = 0.219$ (HC3 SE = 1.858, $t = 0.12, p = 0.908$ on $t(16)$).
        \item Both coefficients were positive, consistent with valuation theory, but neither was statistically significant at the 5\% level. The imprecision comes from the bank fixed effects, not the year effects: with year effects only, the earnings slope is 2.185 (SE 1.260, $p = 0.098$), and adding bank effects raises its standard error to 4.096, so the bank effects absorb the cross-bank variation that identifies the slope. The model yielded $R^2 = 0.948$ (Adjusted $R^2 = 0.912$); the sharp increase from $R^2 = 0.136$ in pooled ordinary least squares (OLS) to $0.919$ with year effects alone ($0.948$ with bank effects added) illustrates how much price variation common fiscal-year effects absorb.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Regime Shift and Implied-Slope Compression (Model 2):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Pre-Consolidation Valuation:} The earnings slope estimate was $\hat{\beta}_E = 5.577$ (HC3 SE = 3.560, $p = 0.140$), and the book value slope estimate was $\hat{\beta}_B = 0.100$ (HC3 SE = 1.306, $p = 0.940$). The earnings point estimate is larger, but both estimates are imprecise; their relative magnitude does not establish which measure was more value relevant.
        \item \textit{Regime Interaction Shifts:} The post-consolidation interaction terms were negative: $\hat{\delta}_E = -3.124$ (HC3 SE = 4.003, $t = -0.78, p = 0.448$) and $\hat{\delta}_B = -1.758$ (HC3 SE = 1.231, $t = -1.43, p = 0.175$).
        \item \textit{Implied Post-Consolidation Slopes:} The implied post-consolidation point estimates are $\hat{\beta}_E + \hat{\delta}_E = 2.453$ ($p = 0.298$) and $\hat{\beta}_B + \hat{\delta}_B = -1.658$ ($p = 0.161$). These estimates are imprecise and do not establish a decline in either slope or a negative book-value association.
        \item \textit{Principal Joint Wald Test:} The joint test of parameter stability ($H_0: \delta_E = \delta_B = 0$) yielded $F(2, 14) = 2.954$ ($p = 0.085$) under the pre-specified HC3 covariance. Under the designated 5\% threshold, the baseline model does not reject parameter stability under HC3, though it achieves marginal significance at the 10\% level. The same test rejects at 5\% under the classical, HC0, HC1 and HC2 covariances for all eight specifications R2--R9 (Chapter IV, Table~\ref{tab:cov_sensitivity}), so the baseline non-rejection is specific to the HC3 estimator.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Robustness Suite Insights and the Entity-Timing Sensitivity:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item The robustness suite (R1--R10) did not reject parameter stability at the 5\% level across ROA substitution (R3), cash dividend inclusion (R4, residual $\text{df} = 13$), transition-year removal (R5), bonus share dilution (R7), restated basic earnings (R8), Kanchan-mismatched bank-year removal (R9, residual $\text{df} = 13$), and supplementary partial-year Deva removal (R10, residual $\text{df} = 12$), except under Specification R6, whose unadjusted joint Wald test rejected at 5\%; this is the only raw rejection among nine joint tests R2--R10 under HC3 and the Holm-adjusted p-value is 0.190 ($p = 0.169$ across R2--R9).
        \item \textit{Entity-Timing Sensitivity (Specification R6):} When FY 2078/79 was excluded to address the entity-timing asymmetry (standalone pre-merger fundamentals paired with April 2023 trading prices for the four merged banks), the unadjusted joint test was below 5\%:
        \begin{equation}
        F(2, 10) = 5.82, \quad p = 0.021 \quad (N = 23)
        \end{equation}
        with $\hat{\delta}_B = -2.611$ (HC3 SE = 1.517, $t = -1.72, p = 0.116$ on $t(10)$). Neither interaction is individually significant. R5, which has the same sample size and residual degrees of freedom, does not reject (F(2,10)=2.41, p=0.139). R6 is an unadjusted sensitivity result; its Holm-adjusted p-value is 0.190 ($p = 0.169$ across R2--R9). In the R6 sample, dropping any single bank leaves the joint test far from rejection ($F = 0.615$--$1.309$, $p = 0.329$--$0.572$), so the R6 rejection does not survive a leave-one-bank perturbation.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Specification Comparisons and Omitted Variable Bias:}
    Pooled OLS produced an EPS slope estimate of $4.882$ ($R^2=0.136$), compared with $2.095$ in the two-way fixed-effects model ($R^2=0.948$). The pooled estimate is imprecise ($p=0.128$), and the raw $R^2$ values include the variation accounted for by the fixed effects; they do not isolate the contribution of the accounting regressors.
\end{itemize}

No prospective power analysis was specified. A post hoc design-sensitivity calculation using two-sided $\alpha=0.05$, 80\% power, fitted HC3 standard errors, and the residual-$df$ $t$ reference gives approximate detectable effects of 10.73 NPR for the Model~2 pre-period EPS slope, 12.06 NPR for the EPS interaction, and 3.71 NPR for the BVPS interaction. These thresholds show that the non-rejections do not rule out effects smaller than those magnitudes; they are not observed-power estimates.

\section{Conclusions}

Based on the empirical findings, econometric diagnostics, and theoretical evaluations conducted in this dissertation, the following major conclusions, direct answers to research questions, and formal hypothesis decisions are drawn:

\subsection{Direct Answers to Research Questions}
In direct response to the three research questions formulated in Chapter~I:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Answer to Research Question 1 (Baseline Value Relevance):} The baseline two-way fixed-effects model does not find statistically significant EPS or BVPS associations with the price measure ($p = 0.616$ for EPS; $p = 0.908$ for BVPS). Including year fixed effects accounts for common macro-year price variation and raises model $R^2$; this comparison does not establish that omitted year shocks account for the explanatory power of pooled studies.
    \item \textbf{Answer to Research Question 2 (Consolidation Regime Shift):} The estimated interaction coefficients are negative ($\hat{\delta}_E=-3.124$, $\hat{\delta}_B=-1.758$), but neither is statistically significant. The baseline joint test does not reject parameter stability at 5\% ($F=2.954$, $p=0.085$); the point estimates alone do not establish downward compression.
    \item \textbf{Answer to Research Question 3 (Sensitivity and Boundary Conditions):} The unadjusted R6 joint test is below 5\% ($F(2,10)=5.82$, $p=0.021$), but this is the only raw rejection among nine tests R2--R10 under HC3. Its Holm-adjusted $p$-value is 0.190 ($p = 0.169$ across R2--R9). The remaining specifications do not reject at 5\% under HC3; the evidence is sensitivity-dependent.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Regime Differences in Accounting Valuation Slopes}
The pre-consolidation point estimates were $\hat{\beta}_E=5.58$ and $\hat{\beta}_B=0.10$; the implied post-consolidation point estimates were 2.45 and $-1.66$. These estimates are imprecise and do not establish earnings dominance, a decline in either slope, or a negative book-value association. The baseline joint test does not reject at 5\% ($F=2.954$, $p=0.085$). The R6 result is an unadjusted sensitivity finding and is not significant after Holm adjustment across the nine joint tests (adjusted $p=0.190$; $p = 0.169$ across R2--R9). Results are reported as associations across calendar periods, not as an established causal merger impact.

\subsection{Behavior of Book Value and Earnings Slopes Across Regimes}
Under classical valuation theory \citep{ohlson1995,collins1997}, disrupted earnings may increase the valuation role of book value. The negative post-consolidation BVPS point estimate ($\hat{\beta}_B + \hat{\delta}_B=-1.658$) is statistically insignificant ($p=0.161$), so these data do not establish whether this theoretical substitution occurred or how market participants treated expanded book equity.

\subsection{Econometric Discipline: Specification Sensitivity and Macro-Year Variation}
This dissertation highlights the influence of econometric specification in evaluating accounting value relevance. Pooled and two-way fixed-effects estimates differ, but raw $R^2$ includes variation absorbed by fixed effects and should not be interpreted as the contribution of accounting fundamentals alone. Slope estimates are conditional on both bank and fiscal-year fixed effects; they are not obtained by simple double demeaning. P/E is excluded because it embeds the dependent price in its numerator. The estimated accounting associations remain uncertain in this small sample.

\subsection{Archival Timing and Data Integrity in Frontier Market Research}
The sensitivity analysis shows dependence on sample and entity-boundary definitions. Excluding FY 2078/79 in R6 yielded an unadjusted joint test of $F(2,10)=5.82$ ($p=0.021$), but across the nine joint tests R2--R10 its Holm-adjusted $p$-value is 0.190 ($p = 0.169$ across R2--R9); the observed F is 41.8\% above the 5\% critical value. The five-day cutoff is an operational rule. Including the two delayed observations changes the baseline joint test to $F(2,16)=4.762$ ($p=0.024$). Carrying forward the 12 May 2021 panel-series prices for HBL/NIB ($487/453$) gives $F(2,16)=3.870$ ($p=0.043$); using the alternative 13 May reported pair ($484/460$) gives $F(2,16)=3.821$ ($p=0.044$). Both pairs are roughly a year before the April 2022 target and are stale-price scenarios, not observations satisfying the freshness rule. In the $N=30$ model with the observed delayed prices, the pre-period BVPS estimate is 0.709 compared with 0.100 in the baseline. These sensitivities show that the sample rule is consequential. A wild-cluster bootstrap of the two joint Wald tests, enumerating all 32 Rademacher sign patterns at five banks, gives $p = 0.0625$ for both the baseline and R6, flipping only the R6 decision on a single pattern; with 32 patterns the smallest attainable $p$-value is $2/32 = 0.0625$, so the flip is mechanically forced and the check is inconclusive at this cluster count and does not overturn the HC3 inference.

As summarized in Table~\ref{tab:hyp_decisions}, the empirical evaluations lead to the following formal decisions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 1 ($H_1$: Value Relevance of EPS):} Evaluated via the null hypothesis $H_0: \beta_E = 0$. In the baseline two-way fixed effects Model~1, the earnings slope was $\hat{\beta}_E = 2.095$ ($t = 0.51, p = 0.616$ on $t(16)$); in Model~2, the pre-consolidation earnings slope was $\hat{\beta}_E = 5.577$ ($t = 1.57, p = 0.140$ on $t(14)$). Because both $p$-values exceed the 5\% significance threshold, the study fails to reject the null hypothesis $H_0$. Accounting earnings do not exhibit a statistically significant association with post-reporting-deadline market prices in the two-way fixed-effects specification, where bank effects absorb the cross-bank variation that identifies the slope.
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 2 ($H_2$: Value Relevance of BVPS):} Evaluated via the null hypothesis $H_0: \beta_B = 0$. In Model~1, the book value slope was $\hat{\beta}_B = 0.219$ ($t = 0.12, p = 0.908$ on $t(16)$); in Model~2, the pre-consolidation book value slope was $\hat{\beta}_B = 0.100$ ($t = 0.08, p = 0.940$ on $t(14)$). Failing to attain statistical significance at the 5\% level, the evidence fails to reject $H_0$. Book value per share shows no statistically significant incremental pricing effect in the baseline specification.
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 3 ($H_3$: Structural Regime Shift in EPS Slope):} Evaluated via the null hypothesis $H_0: \delta_E = 0$ in Model~2. The estimated interaction parameter was $\hat{\delta}_E = -3.124$ ($t = -0.78, p = 0.448$ on $t(14)$). Because $p > 0.05$, the study fails to reject $H_0$. The individual compression in the earnings valuation slope across the 2023 consolidation wave is not statistically significant.
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 4 ($H_4$: Structural Regime Shift in BVPS Slope):} Evaluated via the null hypothesis $H_0: \delta_B = 0$ in Model~2. The estimated interaction parameter was $\hat{\delta}_B = -1.758$ ($t = -1.43, p = 0.175$ on $t(14)$). Because $p > 0.05$, the study fails to reject $H_0$. The individual shift in the book value valuation slope is not statistically significant at the 5\% level.
    \item \textbf{Joint Test of Structural Regime Shift:} Evaluated via the joint null hypothesis $H_0: \delta_E = \delta_B = 0$. In baseline Model~2, the Wald test yielded $F(2,14)=2.954$ ($p=0.085$), failing to reject at 5\%. R6 yielded an unadjusted $F(2,10)=5.82$ ($p=0.021$); it is the only raw rejection among nine tests R2--R10 under HC3 and its Holm-adjusted $p$-value is 0.190 ($p = 0.169$ across R2--R9). The evidence for a regime shift is therefore sensitivity-dependent and not conclusive.
\end{itemize}

Two limits stated in Chapter~I bear on how far these conclusions travel. First, this purposive five-series sample includes four selected combinations from the early-2023 consolidation wave and one unmerged benchmark. The fifth combination, Prabhu Bank/Century, was not selected, and no separate data-based exclusion criterion is claimed. The findings therefore describe the sampled institutions and do not establish results for the omitted Prabhu/Century combination or the full commercial banking population; they cannot be generalized to development banks, finance companies, or non-financial listed sectors. Second, actual publication dates were verified in the reviewed records for only 2 of the 30 bank-years; for the remaining observations, the statutory deadline does not establish when the information became public, so the post-reporting-deadline pricing protocol rests on the deadline as an operational rule rather than on verified publication in each case.

\section{Implications of the Study}

The findings and methodological insights of this dissertation offer targeted, actionable implications partitioned across three institutional domains:

\subsection{Implications for Regulatory Authorities (NRB, SEBON, and NEPSE)}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Scrutiny of Post-Merger Asset Quality and Provisioning Transparency (NRB):}
    The imprecise point estimates suggest a downward compression in the earnings slope and no positive book value pricing anchor post-consolidation. Whether this coincides with market caution regarding the asset quality of merged commercial banks is a hypothesis: no asset-quality variable is estimated in this study. Given that uncertainty, NRB's supervisory departments could prioritize rigorous on-site asset quality reviews (AQRs) across consolidated entities. Standardized, transparent impairment testing of loan books assimilated during amalgamations is essential to restore market confidence in reported financial accounts.
    \item \textbf{Evaluation of Mandatory Consolidation Policies (NRB):}
    While consolidation mandates reduced the licensed commercial bank population from 32 in FY 2011/12 to 20 by mid-October 2023, the imprecise estimates do not establish that horizontal amalgamations were accompanied by substantial earnings transitoriness---no transitoriness parameter was estimated---or by an attenuation of the valuation slopes, since neither interaction is statistically significant. Policy frameworks should not assume that structural combinations automatically translate into improved financial stability or higher equity valuations in the short to medium term, because financial stability was not measured in this study.
    \item \textbf{Transparency in Capital Distribution Directives (NRB):}
    The sharp reduction in cash dividend distributions post-consolidation removed an observable cash flow signal for bank equities, though DPS was never shown to anchor valuation (its correlation with price is $r = 0.082$, $p = 0.679$). NRB should provide clear, forward-looking guidelines regarding dividend distribution criteria and counter-cyclical capital buffer expectations, helping market participants formulate realistic cash flow projections.
    \item \textbf{Reforming Secondary Market Trading Suspension Practices (SEBON and NEPSE):}
    A critical institutional friction identified in this study was the extended merger-related trading suspensions, which froze secondary market trading for up to 54 days past statutory reporting deadlines. SEBON and NEPSE should enforce trading continuity during corporate combinations, restricting trading halts to at most a few sessions around share swap execution. Continuous price discovery is vital to maintain liquidity and avoid information distortion.
    \item \textbf{Standardizing Corporate Disclosure Timelines and Digital Access (SEBON and NEPSE):}
    Under Section 59(2) of BAFIA, commercial banks are legally mandated to publish audited financial statements within nine months into the subsequent fiscal year \citep{bafia2017}. To mitigate the risk of fragmented corporate disclosures and ensure uniform public access, SEBON and NEPSE should operationalize a centralized electronic disclosure portal where audited annual reports, quarterly Nepal Financial Reporting Standards (NFRS) financials, and merger swap audit reports are uploaded instantaneously with certified timestamps.
    \item \textbf{Discouraging Endogenous Ratios in Equity Research (SEBON and NEPSE):}
    Given the historical use of endogenous price-earnings multiples in local equity research and academic literature, regulatory education frameworks should promote sound financial modelling practices that avoid regressing share price on price-derived ratios.
    \item \textbf{Standardizing NFRS Restatement Disclosures (Accounting Standard Setters):}
    The Accounting Standards Board (ASB) of Nepal and the Institute of Chartered Accountants of Nepal (ICAN) could consider enforcing uniform reporting of bonus-adjusted basic EPS and diluted EPS across consecutive annual reports; this study does not establish that current reporting is non-uniform, so the recommendation rests on transparency grounds rather than on a documented deficiency.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Implications for Commercial Bank Executives and Boards of Directors}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Prioritizing Post-Merger Operational and Credit Integration:}
    The observed earnings dispersion post-consolidation is a descriptive change whose cause is not identified by this study. To the extent that dispersion reflects integration friction, executive leadership may benefit from focusing on internal operational harmonization, including the integration of management information systems, core banking software, and credit underwriting standards; the results do not establish that these specific actions are necessary.
    \item \textbf{Transparent Disclosure of Restructuring Adjustments:}
    Given the uncertainty around the post-consolidation book-value association, commercial bank management should enhance voluntary footnote disclosures regarding purchase price allocations, recognized goodwill, restructuring provisions, and reserve transfers. Granular disclosures regarding asset quality can help alleviate investor skepticism.
    \item \textbf{Communication of Sustainable Capital Policies:}
    Bank executives should pursue sustainable capital management strategies that balance regulatory capital adequacy maintenance with transparent, predictable dividend distribution policies.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Implications for Equity Investors, Financial Analysts, and Market Participants}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Scrutinizing Book Value Composition:}
    Equity investors and analysts in Nepal should exercise caution when using unadjusted book value per share as a safety metric for consolidated banks. In the post-merger banking era, reported book equity may encompass capital issued against stressed assets or restructuring costs, though no balance-sheet composition variable was estimated to establish this. Fundamental analysis should examine the underlying quality of net assets, adjusting for unamortized goodwill and loan provisioning levels.
    \item \textbf{Recognizing Earnings Transitoriness in Merged Institutions:}
    Market participants should account for the fact that reported accounting earnings in the immediate years following major amalgamations can exhibit heightened volatility; whether that volatility reflects transitoriness due to credit-loss charges and integration expenses was not measured. Valuation models should use normalized operational metrics rather than headline basic EPS.
    \item \textbf{Incorporating Macro-Temporal Factors:}
    Financial analysts must recognize that common fiscal-year shocks exert a dominant influence on bank share prices (year fixed effects raise $R^2$ from 0.136 to 0.919); the specific channels---systemic liquidity, policy rates, broad market sentiment---are not identified by this study, since no macroeconomic variable is in any model. Valuation frameworks should incorporate macro-temporal factors rather than relying solely on static accounting slopes.
\end{itemize}

\section{Recommendations and Directions for Future Research}

\subsection{Policy and Managerial Recommendations}
Based on the synthesized conclusions and institutional implications, the following specific recommendations are offered:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Mandatory Post-Merger Asset Quality Disclosures:} Given that the recovery performance, provisioning coverage, and write-offs of acquired legacy loan portfolios are unmeasured in this study and therefore an acknowledged evidence gap, Nepal Rastra Bank could consider mandating that commercial banks completing horizontal amalgamations publish a semi-annual ``Asset Quality and Integration Disclosure Report'' for three years post-merger, detailing those items.
    \item \textbf{Limiting Trading Suspensions During Corporate Restructuring:} The Securities Board of Nepal and NEPSE should establish regulatory ceilings that restrict secondary market trading halts during corporate combinations to at most a few consecutive trading sessions around formal share swap execution.
    \item \textbf{Operationalizing a Centralized Digital Filings Repository:} SEBON should establish an open-access, machine-readable digital filings portal requiring all listed financial institutions to upload audited financial statements, board resolutions, and statutory disclosures concurrently with their public release.
    \item \textbf{Publishing Core Operational Earnings Metrics:} Commercial bank management and equity research institutions should report normalized operating earnings per share alongside statutory NFRS basic EPS, distinguishing ongoing operational performance from non-recurring merger adjustments and regulatory reserve transfers. No non-recurring merger adjustment or regulatory reserve transfer is quantified in this study, so the recommendation is a transparency proposal, not a response to a measured deficiency.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Avenues for Future Academic Inquiry}
While this dissertation has established an econometric baseline for evaluating accounting value relevance across the 2023 consolidation wave, several fruitful avenues remain open for future scholarly research:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Methodological Rigor and Econometric Specification in Frontier Markets:}
    This dissertation demonstrates the critical importance of rigorous panel econometric specifications in Nepalese accounting research. Graduate researchers and academic institutions should eliminate mathematically circular regressors (such as P/E predicting MPS), incorporate two-way fixed effects to control for unobserved bank and temporal heterogeneity, and employ finite-sample robust inference methods such as MacKinnon-White HC3 on Student's $t$ residual degrees of freedom.
    \item \textbf{Extension to Long-Horizon Post-Consolidation Panels:}
    This study examined the post-consolidation regime across three initial fiscal years (FY 2079/80 through FY 2081/82). As additional financial years elapse, future research should re-estimate the interactive valuation models over longer post-merger horizons (5 to 10 years) to evaluate whether valuation slopes change once integration is fully completed.
    \item \textbf{Expansion to the Full Commercial Banking Population:}
    Future studies should expand the cross-sectional sample to encompass all 19 NEPSE-listed commercial banks, employing difference-in-differences (DiD) panel frameworks that compare aggressively merged banking series against a cohort of organically grown or minimally consolidated peer institutions, if such a cohort can be identified among the 19 listed banks.
    \item \textbf{Investigation of Non-Financial Disclosure and Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) Metrics:}
    Subsequent research should examine the value relevance of non-financial indicators, corporate governance metrics, digital banking investments, and Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) disclosures among Nepalese financial institutions, assessing whether non-accounting disclosures provide incremental pricing power.
    \item \textbf{Intraday Event-Study Methodologies:}
    Building upon the panel findings of this study, future inquiries could employ high-frequency, intraday event-study methodologies to capture the immediate market reaction surrounding statutory board dividend announcements, AGM approvals, and quarterly earnings releases, quantifying the speed and informational efficiency of NEPSE's price discovery mechanism.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Sectoral Valuation Comparative Inquiries:}
    Finally, comparative studies should investigate accounting value relevance across divergent sectors of the Nepalese economy, contrasting the commercial banking industry with insurance companies, hydropower developers, microfinance institutions, and manufacturing corporations to evaluate how divergent regulatory environments shape the pricing of earnings and book value.
\end{itemize}
