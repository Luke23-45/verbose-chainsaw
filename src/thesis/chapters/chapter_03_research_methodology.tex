\chapter{Research Methodology}

\section{Introduction}
Scientific research in empirical finance and financial accounting is distinguished by the formalization, transparency, and reproducibility of its methodological design. This chapter sets out the research methodology employed to investigate the value relevance of accounting information across Nepal's 2023 commercial banking consolidation wave. Structured in accordance with the \textit{Master's Degree Dissertation Guidelines (2019)} of the Faculty of Management, Tribhuvan University, this chapter is organized into five interrelated sections:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Section 3.2: Research Design} specifies the quantitative, correlational, comparative, and longitudinal two-way fixed-effects panel design.
    \item \textbf{Section 3.3: Population, Sample, and Sampling Procedure} defines the commercial banking population, justifies purposive sampling, presents the institutional profiles of the five bank series, and defines the 5-day freshness rule.
    \item \textbf{Section 3.4: Nature and Sources of Data, and Data Collection Procedure} details audited financial statement inputs, market price series, regulatory sources, and reconciliation protocols.
    \item \textbf{Section 3.5: Research Framework and Definition of Variables} delineates the conceptual framework, operational variable definitions ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$, EPS, BVPS, $\mathit{Post}_t$, ROA, DPS), and the exclusion of circular P/E multiples.
    \item \textbf{Section 3.6: Methods of Analysis and Econometric Specification} formulates the baseline (Model~1) and regime-shift (Model~2) panel models, MacKinnon-White HC3 robust inference, finite-sample degrees of freedom accounting, diagnostic tests, and the nine-specification estimation and robustness suite (R1--R2 baselines plus seven robustness variants R3--R9).
\end{itemize}

\section{Research Design}
This study adopts a quantitative, correlational, and comparative longitudinal panel research design using secondary financial accounting and capital market data \citep{pant2016}. The design is explicitly aligned with the research questions and objectives formulated in Chapter~I:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Quantitative and Correlational:} The study operationalizes fundamental accounting metrics, namely earnings per share (EPS), book value per share (BVPS), return on assets (ROA), and dividend per share (DPS), and evaluates their statistical associations with secondary market equity closing prices ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$) using parametric correlation matrices and linear regression estimation.
    \item \textbf{Comparative and Longitudinal Panel:} The research design tracks five commercial banking institutions across six consecutive fiscal years, spanning FY 2076/77 (2019/20) through FY 2081/82 (2024/25). This longitudinal dimension allows the study to compare valuation relationships across two distinct calendar and institutional regimes: the pre-consolidation period (FY 2076/77--2078/79, $n = 13$ clean observations) and the post-consolidation period (FY 2079/80--2081/82, $n = 15$ clean observations).
    \item \textbf{Observational and Non-Experimental:} Because the researcher does not manipulate corporate financial decisions, assign institutions to merger treatments, or intervene in market price formation, the investigation is non-experimental and observational. In accordance with standard econometric principles, parameter estimates are interpreted as conditional statistical associations rather than as causal merger impacts.
    \item \textbf{Two-Way Fixed-Effects Framework:} To mitigate potential omitted-variable bias, the core empirical architecture implements a two-way fixed-effects panel model incorporating unobserved time-invariant bank fixed effects ($\mu_i$) and common fiscal-year fixed effects ($\lambda_t$). Bank fixed effects control for persistent differences in institutional reputation, corporate governance, risk appetite, and historical branch networks. Fiscal-year fixed effects absorb market-wide macroeconomic shocks, shifts in monetary policy, inflation cycles, and broad NEPSE equity index movements. The estimated slopes are conditional on both sets of fixed effects. This description does not imply simple double demeaning.
    \item \textbf{Exploratory Finite-Sample Inference:} Recognizing the small cross-sectional dimension ($N = 5$ banking series, $G = 5$ clusters), the research design explicitly disclaims asymptotic certainty. Inference is structured around MacKinnon-White HC3 heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors with transparent reporting of finite-sample degrees of freedom, providing an exploratory, honest assessment of parameter uncertainty.
\end{itemize}

This study uses only published secondary data (audited annual reports and public price series), and no human participants were involved.

\section{Population, Sample, and Sampling Procedure}

\subsection{Population of the Study}
The target population for this study comprises all commercial banks licensed by Nepal Rastra Bank as Class `A' financial institutions operating in the Federal Democratic Republic of Nepal. Over the past decade, successive consolidation mandates executed by NRB reduced the commercial banking sector from 32 institutions in FY 2011/12 \citep{nrb2013stats} to 20 commercial banks by mid-October 2023 (Asoj 2080 BS) \citep{nrb2023list}. These 20 licensed institutions constitute the population of the modern Nepalese commercial banking industry. Of these 20 licensed institutions, 19 are listed on NEPSE \citep{bhandari2026}, while the one unlisted institution is Rastriya Banijya Bank Limited \citep{nrb2023list}.

\subsection{Sampling Method and Selection Criteria}
The study uses a purposive sample of four selected merged bank series and one unmerged benchmark. In terms of institutional count, the five sampled banking series represent exactly 25.0\% (5 of 20) of all licensed Class `A' commercial banks in Nepal following the 2023 consolidation wave \citep{nrb2023list}. Four of the five Class `A' commercial bank combinations executed during the early-2023 wave are represented in the sample, while the fifth was Prabhu Bank's acquisition of Century Commercial Bank \citep{nrb2023bsr}. Prabhu Bank/Century was not included among the selected cases, and no separate data-based exclusion criterion is claimed. Because purposive sampling restricts institutional coverage to these five series rather than executing an exhaustive sector-wide census, empirical findings apply directly to the sampled institutions, and aggregate Class `A' sector asset and market-capitalization shares are not claimed or evaluated.

The specific sampling criteria applied to select the institutions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Participation in the 2023 Consolidation Wave:} Commercial bank series that executed horizontal statutory amalgamations or major corporate acquisitions that commenced joint commercial operations during the early-2023 consolidation wave (January--February 2023 / mid-FY 2079/80).
    \item \textbf{Benchmark Series Continuity:} Inclusion of an established Class `A' commercial bank that did not participate in the early-2023 consolidation wave, providing institutional continuity across the 2023 reporting boundary alongside the four consolidated series.
    \item \textbf{Longitudinal Data Availability and Regulatory Compliance:} Availability of complete, audited annual financial statements across all six fiscal years (FY 2076/77 to FY 2081/82) prepared in accordance with Nepal Financial Reporting Standards (NFRS) and submitted to Nepal Rastra Bank.
    \item \textbf{Secondary Market Listing and Trading Activity:} Active listing on the Nepal Stock Exchange (NEPSE), with row-level trading freshness applied as detailed in Section~\ref{subsec:sample_realized} (under which two bank-years with extended trading halts exceeding the 5-day post-deadline freshness window are coded as missing).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Sample Size, Missingness Protocol, and Realized Observations}
\label{subsec:sample_realized}
The research protocol targets 30 potential bank-year observations, comprising five commercial bank series tracked over six fiscal years (FY 2076/77 to FY 2081/82). Primary audited annual financial reports are complete for all 30 bank-years ($100\%$ accounting coverage).

However, in secondary equity pricing, corporate restructuring frequently induces prolonged trading halts. To ensure empirical integrity, the study enforces a strict \textbf{five-day calendar freshness rule}:
\begin{quote}
\textit{If a security was suspended from trading or recorded zero valid transactions within a five-day calendar window following the study's operationalized reporting deadline (end of Chaitra / mid-April), the observation is coded as missing and excluded from the regression sample. It is never imputed from stale pre-suspension prices.}
\end{quote}

The five-day length is an un-derived operational choice, as no substantive rationale for five days rather than another window is recorded in this thesis. The choice is consequential for the headline result: including the two delayed observations changes the Model~2 joint test to $F(2,16)=4.762$ ($p=0.024$), against $F(2,14)=2.954$ ($p=0.085$) in the 28-row sample (Section~3.5.2).

Two bank-year observations fail this freshness rule due to prolonged merger-related trading suspensions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Himalayan Bank Limited (HBL) FY 2077/78:} Trading in HBL shares on NEPSE was suspended from 13 May 2021, when the two banks signed a merger memorandum of understanding. The proposed bilateral merger with Nepal Investment Bank was then rejected by HBL shareholders at its 29th AGM in January 2022, and trading remained suspended until 6 June 2022 (Jestha 2079 BS), representing a resumption delay of 54 days beyond the target deadline of 13 April 2022.
    \item \textbf{Nepal Investment Mega Bank Limited (NIMB, formerly NIB) FY 2077/78:} Trading in NIB shares was concurrently suspended under the same merger process, resuming on 30 May 2022 (Jestha 2079 BS), representing a resumption delay of 47 days beyond the 13 April 2022 deadline.
\end{itemize}

Consequently, these two observations are coded as missing, leaving a realized clean estimation sample of \textbf{$N = 28$ observations} (13 pre-consolidation bank-years and 15 post-consolidation bank-years).

\section{Nature and Sources of Data, and Instrument of Data Collection}

\subsection{Nature of Data}
This dissertation relies exclusively on quantitative secondary data extracted from audited financial disclosures and capital market data aggregators. All accounting variables are compiled strictly on a standalone Bank-level basis rather than consolidated Group accounts, consistent with the regulatory return framework, in which a licensed commercial bank's capital adequacy and prudential metrics are computed at the standalone Bank level rather than for the consolidated Group. Standalone Bank balance-sheet identities ($\text{Total Assets} - \text{Total Liabilities} - \text{Total Equity} = 0$) hold across all 30 bank-year observations within a $\le 1$ NPR rounding tolerance. In the audited Global IME Bank Group statements, Group total equity (NPR 29,463.85 million in FY 2076/77) exceeds equity attributable to equity holders (NPR 29,252.57 million) by the non-controlling interest (NPR 211.28 million). The parser's Group total-equity field fell back to the attributable figure, so the raw extraction does not carry a separate Group total. Regardless, Group-level figures are never used in panel regressions as all empirical estimations rely strictly on standalone Bank accounts, where non-controlling interest is nil and total equity equals attributable equity.

The empirical dataset comprises two primary streams of financial information:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Audited Financial Accounting Data:} Annual financial metrics, specifically basic earnings per share, total equity attributable to equity holders, ordinary shares outstanding, profit after tax, average total assets, and recommended cash dividends per share.
    \item \textbf{Secondary Market Trading Data:} Daily equity transaction records, specifically closing market share prices, trade dates, and trading volume. Trading status was inferred from daily transaction records and press accounts, as NEPSE's own suspension notices were not obtained (see Appendix~D, Section D.8).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Sources of Data Collection}
To ensure institutional credibility and empirical accuracy, data sources are bifurcated into primary empirical inputs and contextual regulatory publications:

\textbf{Primary Empirical Data Sources:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Audited Annual Financial Reports:} Statements of Financial Position (Balance Sheets), Statements of Profit or Loss (Income Statements), Statements of Changes in Equity, and accompanying Notes to Accounts extracted directly from the published annual reports of Global IME Bank, Himalayan Bank, Kumari Bank, Nepal Investment Mega Bank, and NMB Bank.
    \item \textit{Capital Market Data Aggregators:} Secondary closing market share prices retrieved from established capital market aggregators (ShareSansar, Merolagani, and FinancialNotices), cross-checked against a second aggregator (Merolagani last traded prices for all 30 dataset price cells, and FinancialNotices Close for the five FY 2081/82 rows).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Contextual and Regulatory Data Sources:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB):} Annual Banking and Financial Statistics and licensed financial institution directories (the population counts in Section~3.3) and the Bank and Financial Institutions Merger Bylaws (the regulatory architecture for the consolidation wave, cited in Chapter~I).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Instrument of Data Collection: Structured Financial Data Register}
Because this dissertation relies on quantitative secondary data extracted from corporate financial disclosures and capital market feeds, the instrument of data collection is operationalized as a standardized, structured financial data extraction register. The instrument defines an explicit coding matrix with fixed bank-year observation rows and standardized schedule columns: (i) statutory filing and AGM endorsement locators, (ii) secondary trading session calendar dates and suspension intervals, (iii) Statement of Financial Position components (ordinary share capital, general reserves, retained earnings, total equity attributable to equity holders), (iv) Statement of Profit or Loss metrics (net profit after tax, reported basic earnings per share), (v) corporate dividend distribution records (cash dividend per share, bonus share distribution ratio), and (vi) secondary market closing price at the reporting deadline ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$). The structured instrument and its complete line-item verification register are published in Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility} (Table~\ref{tab:app_register}).

\subsection{Data Audit, Extraction, and Reconciliation Protocol}
All secondary data were processed through a multi-stage audit and verification protocol to ensure that every value was extracted from the audited report, recomputed where the definition requires it, and flagged where recomputation disagreed with a printed figure, rather than overwritten:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Primary Audit Verification:} All fundamental accounting variables (EPS, equity, ordinary shares, net profit, total assets, DPS) were manually extracted from original audited annual reports and verified across balance sheet and income statement schedules.
    \item \textbf{Reconciliation Against Aggregators:} Figures compiled from financial aggregators were cross-referenced against original audited PDF reports. In all instances of discrepancy, figures were resolved strictly in favor of the originating audited financial accounts.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Aggregator Price Reconciliation:} Dataset prices (28 clean observation-date prices plus the two delayed resumption prices, which were subsequently excluded from estimation under the five-day freshness rule) were compared against Merolagani LTPs (and FinancialNotices for FY 2081/82). Out of these 30 dataset cells, 29 demonstrated perfect zero-variance matching. For NMB FY 2076/77, the dataset (ShareSansar) recorded a closing price of NPR 437.00, whereas Merolagani reported a last traded price (LTP) of NPR 436.00. The NPR 437.00 value was retained on the basis of ShareSansar's full daily record (open 432, high 439, low 428, close 437.00). Because ShareSansar is the dataset's primary source, this does not represent independent verification, and the official NEPSE closing price for 13 April 2021 remains unverified against primary exchange floor archives.
    \item \textbf{Trading Status and Freshness Verification:} Daily trading status was screened across the calendar window following the reporting deadline to identify trading halts, record dates, and resumption sessions.
    \item \textbf{Prior-Year Comparative Audit and Continuity:} Prior-year comparative figures reported in subsequent annual reports were audited against own-year reports. Five balance-sheet restatements arising from mid-year mergers and accounting alignments were documented: Global IME Bank FY 2078/79 (NPR 718.44 million, restated previous column in the GBIME FY 2078/79 report, Statement of Financial Position, PDF pp.~83--84), Himalayan Bank FY 2080/81 (Civil Bank merger, NPR 1,877.08 million, restated previous column in the HBL FY 2080/81 report, Statement of Financial Position, PDF pp.~54--55), Kumari Bank FY 2079/80 (NCC Bank merger, NPR 1,047.39 million, restated previous column in the KBL FY 2079/80 report, Statement of Financial Position, PDF p.~42), Kumari Bank FY 2077/78 (derivative line alignment, NPR -7,369.69 million, restated previous column in the KBL FY 2077/78 report, Statement of Financial Position, PDF p.~34, against the own-current KBL FY 2076/77 report, PDF p.~49), and a minor asset reclassification in Kumari Bank FY 2078/79 (NPR 9.59 million, or 0.005\%, restated previous column in the KBL FY 2078/79 report, Statement of Financial Position, PDF p.~32, against the own-current KBL FY 2077/78 report, PDF p.~34), each labelled by the annual report whose previous-year comparative column carries the restatement, with Statement of Financial Position pages from Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}, Table~\ref{tab:app_locators}. Under the study's empirical design, each observation is populated from its own contemporaneous audited financial report (the own-current rule) to preserve the exact information set available to market participants at the statutory reporting deadline.
\end{enumerate}

The full provenance record for every value in this study, including the source register and the price verification trail, is printed in Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility} (Tables~\ref{tab:app_register}--\ref{tab:app_statute}).

\section{Research Framework and Definition of Variables}

\subsection{Conceptual Framework}
The conceptual framework guiding this research synthesizes the residual income valuation theory of \citet{ohlson1995} and the post-merger valuation model of \citet{kwon2020}. It is depicted in Figure~\ref{fig:framework}.

\input{figures/fig_framework}

In this framework, secondary market closing price at the reporting deadline ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$) serves as the outcome variable. Earnings per share (EPS) and book value per share (BVPS) are the primary independent fundamental regressors. The consolidation period indicator ($\mathit{Post}_t$) acts as a temporal moderating indicator, interacting with EPS and BVPS to test for structural parameter shifts ($\delta_E, \delta_B$) across the consolidation wave. Bank fixed effects ($\mu_i$) and fiscal-year fixed effects ($\lambda_t$) control for time-invariant institutional heterogeneity and market-wide macroeconomic trends.

\subsection{Operational Definition and Measurement of Variables}

\subsubsection{Dependent Variable}
\textbf{Market Price per Share at Reporting Deadline ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$):}
The closing secondary market trading price for ordinary shares, measured in Nepalese Rupees (NPR), recorded on the first trading session on the Nepal Stock Exchange (NEPSE) on or after the statutory nine-month audited annual reporting publication deadline established under Section 59(2) of the Banks and Financial Institutions Act, 2017 \citep{bafia2017}.

Operational measurement is established at the end of Chaitra (mid-April) of the subsequent fiscal year. If the deadline falls on a weekend, public holiday, or trading halt, price is recorded on the first active trading session within a 5-day calendar window. Observations exceeding the 5-day window are coded as missing. This rule provides a consistent cutoff after the statutory deadline, but does not verify the public release date for every bank-year or rule out earlier information leakage. Disclosure dates were investigated only for the five FY 2081/82 bank-years, of which two could be settled and three were held pending. For the remaining 25 bank-years, no disclosure date is recorded. The five-day cutoff is consequential: including the two delayed observations changes the Model~2 joint test to $F(2,16)=4.762$ ($p=0.024$), compared with $F(2,14)=2.954$ ($p=0.085$) in the 28-row sample.

\subsubsection{Primary Independent Variables}
\textbf{Earnings per Share (EPS):}
Basic earnings per ordinary share, expressed in NPR, extracted from each bank's own audited Statement of Profit or Loss (the own-current reported basic EPS rule). EPS is calculated as net profit after tax attributable to ordinary equity holders divided by the weighted-average number of ordinary shares outstanding during the fiscal year:
\begin{equation}
\mathit{EPS}_{it} = \frac{\text{Net Profit Attributable to Equity Holders}_{it}}{\text{Weighted Average Number of Ordinary Shares}_{it}}
\label{eq:eps_def}
\end{equation}
Retrospective audited restatements reported in subsequent annual reports are retained for sensitivity analysis rather than overwriting original audited figures, preserving the exact information set available to market participants at the reporting date. In corporate combination years (notably FY 2079/80), the weighted-average share denominator reflects pre- and post-merger issuance weights in accordance with NAS 33 / NFRS, whereas BVPS uses ending ordinary shares outstanding. Consequently, reported basic EPS in the consolidation transition year reflects blended share counts (e.g., Global IME Bank reported EPS of 22.06 vs.\ PAT divided by year-end shares of 18.71, NIMB 13.92 vs.\ 10.90, and HBL 9.18 vs.\ 7.22), while BVPS consistently reflects the enlarged post-merger capital base. The same share-base divergence also arises in FY 2076/77: Global IME Bank reported EPS of 17.99 against PAT divided by year-end shares of 15.33 following its merger with Janata Bank Nepal Ltd (completed 5 December 2019) and acquisition of Hathway Finance Company, and Kumari Bank reported EPS of 12.08 against 9.25 following its acquisition of Deva Bikas Bank (joint operations from 28 Ashad 2077 BS).

\textbf{Book Value per Share (BVPS):}
Net asset value per ordinary share, expressed in NPR, reflecting the net worth backing of each ordinary equity share at fiscal year-end. BVPS is recomputed directly from audited Statement of Financial Position components as total ordinary shareholders' equity attributable to equity holders divided by the total number of ordinary shares outstanding at fiscal year-end:
\begin{equation}
\mathit{BVPS}_{it} = \frac{\text{Total Equity Attributable to Ordinary Equity Holders}_{it}}{\text{Total Number of Ordinary Shares Outstanding at Year-End}_{it}}
\label{eq:bvps_def}
\end{equation}
Printed Net Asset Value (NAV) per share reported in bank financial highlights is used as an audit reconciliation check against recomputed balance-sheet net worth. Recomputed BVPS is adopted in all empirical regressions to preserve exact decimal precision. Minor variances of 0.08 to 0.40 NPR across six NIMB observations arise because printed NAV highlights in those annual reports were rounded to whole integers (190.70 vs.\ 191.00, 198.28 vs.\ 198.00, 185.67 vs.\ 186.00, 162.92 vs.\ 163.00, 176.31 vs.\ 176.00, and 190.40 vs.\ 190.00), whereas two further observations exhibit unrounded decimal differences that remain flagged as unresolved reporting variances: HBL FY 2076/77 (187.67 recomputed vs.\ 187.72 printed, gap 0.05) and NMB Bank FY 2081/82 (175.18 recomputed vs.\ 174.49 printed, gap 0.69).

\subsubsection{Moderating / Interaction Variable}
\textbf{Consolidation Period Indicator ($\mathit{Post}_t$):}
A binary temporal indicator variable reflecting the structural regime shift of the early-2023 banking consolidation wave:
\begin{equation}
\mathit{Post}_t = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{for pre-consolidation fiscal years (FY 2076/77, 2077/78, 2078/79)} \\ 1 & \text{for post-consolidation fiscal years (FY 2079/80, 2080/81, 2081/82)} \end{cases}
\label{eq:post_def}
\end{equation}
This variable interacts with EPS ($\mathit{EPS}_{it} \times \mathit{Post}_t$) and BVPS ($\mathit{BVPS}_{it} \times \mathit{Post}_t$) to estimate parameter differences ($\delta_E, \delta_B$) across regimes.

\subsubsection{Supplementary and Robustness Variables}
\textbf{Return on Assets (ROA):}
Operating profitability expressed as net profit after tax divided by average total assets, expressed as a percentage:
\begin{equation}
\mathit{ROA}_{it} = \left(\frac{\text{Profit After Tax}_{it}}{\text{Average Total Assets}_{it}}\right) \times 100
\label{eq:roa_def}
\end{equation}
Average total assets is the mean of the current and previous fiscal year-end total assets.
Evaluated in Specification R3 as an asset-scaled substitute for EPS. Because EPS and ROA correlate at $r = 0.975$ (yielding a bivariate variance inflation factor of $1 / (1 - r^2) \approx 20$), they are never co-included in the same regression to prevent severe multicollinearity.

\textbf{Dividend per Share (DPS):}
Cash dividend recommended per ordinary share, expressed in NPR, for the corresponding fiscal year. Evaluated in Specification R4, including only eligible cash dividends that were officially declared on or prior to the $\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$ observation date. Documented zero-dividend decisions are recorded as zero, while unannounced dividends are treated as missing in the operational rule. In the realized analysis sample ($N = 28$), all 28 observations possess confirmed declarations or documented nil distributions (zero cash dividend), meaning no DPS observation is missing in the realized estimation dataset.

\subsubsection{Strict Exclusion of Price-Derived Multiples}
\textbf{Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio:}
The Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratio is strictly excluded from all regression models. As mathematically proved in Section~\ref{subsec:pe_circularity}, because $\text{P/E} = \text{MPS} / \text{EPS}$, regressing MPS on P/E introduces circular algebraic endogeneity that biases coefficients and spuriously inflates explanatory power.

The codebook definitions governing every variable in the panel are collected in Table~\ref{tab:app_codebook} of Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}.

\section{Methods of Analysis and Econometric Specification}
The empirical protocol is executed through descriptive statistics, bivariate correlation diagnostics, fixed-effects panel regressions, finite-sample inference adjustments, and a nine-specification suite comprising the two baseline models (R1--R2) and seven robustness variants (R3--R9).

\subsection{Model 1: Baseline Value Relevance Model}
To evaluate the baseline value relevance of accounting fundamentals (Hypotheses~$H_1$ and $H_2$), Model~1 specifies a two-way fixed-effects panel regression:
\begin{equation}
\mathit{MPS}^{RD}_{it} = \alpha + \mu_i + \lambda_t + \beta_E \mathit{EPS}_{it} + \beta_B \mathit{BVPS}_{it} + \varepsilon_{it}
\label{eq:fe_model1}
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $i = 1, \dots, 5$ indexes the five commercial bank series.
    \item $t = 1, \dots, 6$ indexes the six fiscal years.
    \item $\mathit{MPS}^{RD}_{it}$ is the secondary market closing price observed at the post-reporting-deadline window.
    \item $\mathit{EPS}_{it}$ is basic earnings per share from own audited SOPL.
    \item $\mathit{BVPS}_{it}$ is recomputed book value per share at fiscal year-end.
    \item $\alpha$ is the constant baseline intercept.
    \item $\mu_i$ represents unobserved time-invariant bank fixed effects, estimated via 4 bank dummy variables with Global IME Bank Limited (GBIME) serving as the omitted reference bank.
    \item $\lambda_t$ represents common fiscal-year fixed effects, estimated via 5 fiscal-year dummy variables with FY 2076/77 serving as the omitted reference year.
    \item $\beta_E$ and $\beta_B$ are the baseline valuation slopes for EPS and BVPS.
    \item $\varepsilon_{it}$ is the idiosyncratic disturbance term.
\end{itemize}

In Model~1, the parameter count comprises 1 intercept, 4 bank dummy coefficients, 5 fiscal-year dummy coefficients, and 2 slope parameters, totaling $K = 12$ estimated parameters. On the realized sample of $N = 28$ observations, Model~1 retains $N - K = 28 - 12 = 16$ residual degrees of freedom.

\subsection{Model 2: Consolidation-Period Regime Shift Model}
To test whether accounting value relevance differed before and after the banking consolidation wave (Hypotheses~$H_3$ and $H_4$), Model~2 introduces interaction terms between accounting fundamentals and the consolidation indicator:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathit{MPS}^{RD}_{it} ={}& \alpha + \mu_i + \lambda_t + \beta_E \mathit{EPS}_{it} + \beta_B \mathit{BVPS}_{it} \\
& + \delta_E (\mathit{EPS}_{it} \times \mathit{Post}_t) + \delta_B (\mathit{BVPS}_{it} \times \mathit{Post}_t) + \varepsilon_{it}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:fe_model2}
\end{equation}
where reference categories remain identically defined (GBIME as reference bank for $\mu_i$, FY 2076/77 as reference fiscal year for $\lambda_t$).

\textbf{Econometric Note on Standalone Post Absorption:}
Because Model~2 includes a full set of fiscal-year fixed effects ($\lambda_t$), the standalone consolidation indicator ($\mathit{Post}_t$) is a deterministic linear combination of the post-consolidation year dummy indicators:
\begin{equation}
\mathit{Post}_t = D_{2079/80} + D_{2080/81} + D_{2081/82}
\end{equation}
where $D_t$ represents the post-consolidation fiscal-year dummy indicators. Including a standalone $\mathit{Post}_t$ regressor alongside full year fixed effects would create perfect exact collinearity (dummy variable trap). Consequently, the main effect of $\mathit{Post}_t$ is fully absorbed by the fiscal-year fixed effects and is not estimated separately.

\textbf{Interpretation of Slope Parameters:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Pre-Consolidation Valuation Slopes:} Represented directly by the baseline coefficients:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{Slope}_{\text{EPS, Pre}} = \beta_E, \quad \text{Slope}_{\text{BVPS, Pre}} = \beta_B
    \end{equation}
    \item \textit{Incremental Regime Shifts:} Measured by the interaction coefficients:
    \begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\text{EPS}} = \delta_E, \quad \Delta_{\text{BVPS}} = \delta_B
    \end{equation}
    \item \textit{Post-Consolidation Valuation Slopes:} Calculated as linear combinations:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{Slope}_{\text{EPS, Post}} = \beta_E + \delta_E, \quad \text{Slope}_{\text{BVPS, Post}} = \beta_B + \delta_B
    \end{equation}
    \item \textit{Principal Hypothesis Test:} Evaluated via the joint null hypothesis of zero parameter shift:
    \begin{equation}
    H_0: \delta_E = \delta_B = 0
    \end{equation}
    tested using a joint Wald $F$-test with 2 numerator degrees of freedom under the robust covariance matrix.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Econometric Estimation, Degrees of Freedom, and Robust Inference}
Estimation is performed by Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) with explicit dummy variables for bank and fiscal-year fixed effects:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Degrees of Freedom Accounting:} On the realized clean sample of $N = 28$ observations, Model~2 estimates 14 parameters:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item 1 constant intercept ($\alpha$)
        \item 4 bank fixed effects ($\mu_i$, with GBIME as reference)
        \item 5 fiscal-year fixed effects ($\lambda_t$, with FY 2076/77 as reference)
        \item 2 baseline slopes ($\beta_E, \beta_B$)
        \item 2 interaction slopes ($\delta_E, \delta_B$)
    \end{itemize}
    \begin{equation}
    \text{Residual Degrees of Freedom} = N - K = 28 - 14 = 14 \text{ df}
    \end{equation}
    In supplementary models incorporating DPS (Specification R4), estimating the additional slope parameter $\beta_{\text{DPS}}$ increases the parameter count to $K = 15$, reducing residual degrees of freedom from 14 to $28 - 15 = 13$ df.
    \item \textbf{MacKinnon-White HC3 Robust Standard Errors and Reference Distribution:} Cluster-robust covariance estimators are distinct from HC3 and can have unreliable finite-sample behavior when the number of clusters is small ($G = 5$) \citep{cameron2008clusters}. Standard errors, $t$-statistics, and confidence intervals are computed using \citet{mackinnon1985} HC3 heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{HC3} = (X'X)^{-1} \left( \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{e_i^2}{(1 - h_{ii})^2} x_i x_i' \right) (X'X)^{-1}
    \label{eq:hc3}
    \end{equation}
    where $h_{ii} = x_i (X'X)^{-1} x_i'$ represents the leverage of observation $i$. Dividing squared residuals by $(1 - h_{ii})^2$ adjusts the covariance estimate for leverage. This adjustment does not remove influential observations or guarantee reliable finite-sample inference. All $t$-statistics, $p$-values, and parameter confidence intervals are referred to Student's $t$ distribution on residual degrees of freedom ($t(14)$ for Model~2, $t(16)$ for Model~1). Given the small sample, inference remains uncertain. The joint-test conclusion is sensitive to the covariance estimator: Chapter IV reports the joint Wald tests under the classical, HC0, HC1, HC2 and HC3 covariances (Table~\ref{tab:cov_sensitivity}). HC3 is retained as the designated estimator on the grounds stated above (pre-specification in the proposal and its small-sample properties), not because the alternative estimators agree.
    Within-bank serial independence of errors is also assumed by the reported covariance calculations, and it is not directly tested here. The five-bank panel is too small to support a reliable cluster-robust correction, so this assumption remains a limitation. If within-bank residuals are positively autocorrelated, the HC3 standard errors understate the true sampling variation and the reported p-values are understated, so the tests over-reject, and the reported non-rejections are correspondingly conservative. Furthermore, no serial-correlation diagnostic (Durbin--Watson, Wooldridge, or HAC/cluster-robust comparison) is reported, so this possibility cannot be excluded. As a small-sample sensitivity check on the joint Wald decisions, a wild-cluster bootstrap in the spirit of \citet{cameron2008clusters} is also run: the null is imposed by fitting Model~1, its residuals are resampled by bank with Rademacher weights, and the HC3 Wald statistic is recomputed on each bootstrap sample. With five banks there are only $2^5 = 32$ distinct sign patterns, so all 32 are enumerated and the bootstrap $p$-values are multiples of $1/32$. The smallest attainable $p$-value is therefore $2/32 = 0.0625$, and the check cannot produce a 5\% rejection for any specification.
    \item \textbf{Goodness of Fit:} The reported $R^2$ is the least-squares dummy-variable fit, $R^2 = 1 - \text{SSR}_{\text{LSDV}} / \text{SST}$, where $\text{SSR}_{\text{LSDV}}$ is the residual sum of squares of the full model including fixed effects. Within-$R^2$ isolates the contribution of the substantive slopes and interactions: $\text{Within-}R^2 = 1 - \text{SSR}_{\text{LSDV}} / \text{SSR}_{\text{dummies}}$, where $\text{SSR}_{\text{dummies}}$ is the residual sum of squares from a regression on the bank and fiscal-year dummies alone. It is therefore not the one-way within-$R^2$ of a single fixed effect, and it is not computed by demeaning the data.
\end{itemize}

Full coefficient estimates, including every fixed-effect dummy and the joint fixed-effect tests, are printed in Appendix E, Tables~\ref{tab:app_m1full}--\ref{tab:app_fetests}, and full robustness estimates in Tables~\ref{tab:app_robustfull}--\ref{tab:app_robust_fetests}.

\subsection{Econometric Diagnostic Protocol}
The estimation protocol uses four diagnostic procedures, and their empirical results are reported in Chapter~IV:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Multicollinearity:} Variance Inflation Factors (VIF) and tolerance statistics ($1/\text{VIF}$) are computed for the substantive regressors. The attention threshold is $\text{VIF} > 5$, serving as a screening benchmark rather than a pass/fail validity test. The primary VIF screen excludes fixed-effect dummies, while full-design VIFs are reported separately for transparency.
    \item \textbf{Heteroskedasticity:} The Koenker robust form of the Breusch-Pagan Lagrange Multiplier test (statsmodels \texttt{robust=True}) is evaluated against the null hypothesis of constant error variance ($H_0: \sigma_i^2 = \sigma^2$). The classical Breusch-Pagan variant is also reported as a comparison.
    \item \textbf{Influential Observations:} Cook's distance ($D_i$) is computed for each observation. The conventional finite-sample screening threshold $D_i > 4/n$ is used to identify cases for review, but it is not treated as an automatic exclusion rule.
    \item \textbf{Residual Normality:} Normal Q-Q plots and supplementary Shapiro-Wilk tests are used to assess residual distributional shape. These diagnostics inform interpretation of finite-sample $t/F$ reference distributions, without testing the validity of the HC3 covariance estimator.
\end{itemize}

No prospective power analysis was specified. A post hoc design-sensitivity calculation using fitted HC3 standard errors and residual-degrees-of-freedom $t$ reference values is reported in Chapter~IV. Its minimum detectable effects are thresholds under the stated model, not observed power or evidence that smaller effects are absent. The calculation does not model within-bank serial dependence, which remains untested, so its precision estimates should be interpreted cautiously. Failure to reject a null is not evidence that the corresponding effect is zero.

\subsection{The Nine-Specification Estimation and Robustness Suite}
To verify that empirical inferences are not artifacts of specific modelling choices, the study estimates a structured nine-specification suite comprising the two baseline models and seven robustness variants (Specifications R1 to R9):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{R1 (Model 1 Baseline):} Baseline two-way fixed-effects model without interaction terms ($n = 28$).
    \item \textbf{R2 (Model 2 Baseline):} Full two-way fixed-effects model with consolidation interaction terms ($n = 28$).
    \item \textbf{R3 (ROA Substitution):} Replaces EPS with return on assets (ROA) to evaluate sensitivity to an asset-scaled operational profitability metric ($n = 28$).
    \item \textbf{R4 (Dividend Specification):} Incorporates eligible cash dividend per share (DPS) declarations into Model~2, noting degrees of freedom decrease to 13 ($n = 28$).
    \item \textbf{R5 (Excluding Transition Year):} Re-estimates Model~2 excluding FY 2079/80 ($n = 23$) to verify results are not driven by immediate consolidation shock.
    \item \textbf{R6 (Excluding Entity-Timing Mismatch):} Re-estimates Model~2 excluding FY 2078/79 ($n = 23$) to eliminate the mismatch where standalone fundamentals were paired with post-merger April 2023 prices. In both R5 and R6 the excluded fiscal year also removes its fiscal-year dummy, so the parameter count falls from $K = 14$ to $K = 13$ and residual degrees of freedom are $23 - 13 = 10$.
    \item \textbf{R7 (Bonus Share Capitalization Adjustment):} Re-estimates Model~2 using bonus-adjusted book value per share ($\mathit{BVPS}_{\text{adj}} = \mathit{BVPS} / (1 + \text{bonus ratio})$) to evaluate parameter stability against share dilutions ($n = 28$). The bonus ratio is the board-proposed bonus share percentage for that fiscal year, taken from the board proposal in the annual report (Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}, Table~\ref{tab:app_locators} dividend pages, and values in Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}, Table~\ref{tab:app_bonus_ratios}), expressed as a percentage of paid-up capital and divided by 100 to give a proportion. Because the bonus ratio represents that fiscal year's proposed bonus issued in the subsequent fiscal year, $\mathit{BVPS}_{\text{adj}}$ represents a pro-forma forward capitalization adjustment rather than a restated year-end book value. Only BVPS is adjusted, while EPS and price remain on the original share base, so this is a one-sided rescaling of BVPS rather than a full bonus-dilution adjustment of the valuation relation.
    \item \textbf{R8 (Retrospective Restated EPS):} Re-estimates Model~2 using retrospective audited basic EPS restatements from subsequent annual reports ($n = 28$). Specifically, two bank-year observations in the analyzed sample exhibit retrospective restatements: Global IME Bank FY 2076/77 (reported 17.99, restated 15.33) and NMB Bank FY 2077/78 (reported 16.66, restated 14.76). A third restatement in the broader 30-row dataset, Himalayan Bank FY 2077/78 (reported 28.07, restated 27.60), falls within a trading-suspension window and is excluded from the regression sample, and thus does not affect the R8 estimates.
    \item \textbf{R9 (Excluding Kanchan-Mismatched Bank-Year):} Re-estimates Model~2 excluding the single bank-year NMB FY 2076/77 ($n = 27$, residual degrees of freedom 13) whose pre-acquisition fundamentals are paired with a post-acquisition April 2021 price following the Kanchan Development Bank acquisition, to verify results are not driven by that timing mismatch. The parallel Kumari Bank FY 2076/77 case, whose April 2021 price reflects the combined bank following the Deva Bikas Bank acquisition while joint operations began on 28 Ashad 2077 BS (three to four days prior to the fiscal year-end), is not excluded here: its year-end balance sheet, and hence BVPS, already reflects the combined bank, whereas the Kanchan acquisition closed after the fiscal year-end, leaving NMB's entire FY 2076/77 fundamentals pre-acquisition.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Supplementary Post-Hoc Sensitivity Check (R10):}
In addition to the pre-planned nine-specification suite (R1--R9), R10 re-estimates Model~2 excluding both NMB FY 2076/77 and Kumari Bank FY 2076/77 ($n = 26$, residual degrees of freedom 12). KBL's joint operations with Deva Bikas began on 28 Ashad 2077 BS, shortly before fiscal year-end. Its year-end balance sheet incorporates the combined entity, while the annual income statement reflects only a brief post-acquisition operating period. The annual report does not specify the exact EPS denominator treatment for that period. Excluding this observation alongside NMB FY 2076/77 provides a supplementary check on partial-year flow exposure. Specification R10 is post hoc and is not part of the pre-planned R1--R9 suite.

\subsection{Specification Comparison}
To ensure transparency regarding how fixed effects absorb market-wide variation, baseline two-way fixed-effects estimates are compared directly against:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pooled OLS:} Neglects bank and year heterogeneity, reflecting conventional static literature.
    \item \textbf{Bank Fixed-Effects Only:} Absorbs cross-bank heterogeneity but retains longitudinal macroeconomic price variation.
\end{itemize}
