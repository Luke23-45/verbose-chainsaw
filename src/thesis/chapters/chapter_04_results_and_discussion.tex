\chapter{Results and Discussion}

\section{Introduction}
This chapter presents the empirical findings, econometric evaluations, and theoretical interpretations resulting from the investigation into the value relevance of accounting information across the 2023 Nepalese commercial banking consolidation wave. The primary objective is to evaluate whether fundamental accounting numbers---specifically earnings per share (EPS) and book value per share (BVPS)---exhibit a statistically discernible association with secondary market equity prices observed on or after statutory annual reporting deadlines ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$), and whether the pricing relations attached to these accounting measures underwent a structural regime shift following the large-scale horizontal amalgamations executed in early 2023.

In accordance with the Faculty of Management, Tribhuvan University \textit{Master's Degree Dissertation Guidelines} (2019, pp.~14--15), the chapter is organized into two major substantive components: \textbf{Results} and \textbf{Discussion}.

The \textbf{Results} section (Section~4.2) details the empirical outcomes derived from the five sampled banking series over the six-year observation period (FY 2076/77 to FY 2081/82, corresponding to Gregorian years 2019/20 through 2024/25). The presentation proceeds through seven structured subsections:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Descriptive Statistics} (Section~4.2.1), summarizing the distributional properties, central tendencies, and dispersions of the primary valuation variables across the full panel and comparative pre- and post-consolidation sub-periods;
    \item \textit{Correlation Analysis and Multicollinearity Assessment} (Section~4.2.2), examining bivariate linear associations, verifying collinearity boundaries between EPS and return on assets (ROA), and reviewing visual dispersion patterns;
    \item \textit{Baseline Value Relevance: Model 1 Estimation} (Section~4.2.3), reporting the estimation of Model~1 under two-way fixed effects with MacKinnon-White HC3 robust standard errors on Student's $t$ residual degrees of freedom;
    \item \textit{Impact of Consolidation on Value Relevance: Model 2 Estimation} (Section~4.2.4), detailing the estimation of Model~2, capturing pre-consolidation valuation slopes, regime interaction coefficients ($\delta_E, \delta_B$), implied post-consolidation net slopes, and the principal joint Wald test of parameter stability;
    \item \textit{Hypothesis Testing} (Section~4.2.5), evaluating the empirical findings against the four operational research hypotheses ($H_1, H_2, H_3, H_4$) formulated in Chapter~I;
    \item \textit{Robustness and Sensitivity Analyses (R1 through R9)} (Section~4.2.6), executing a nine-specification suite (R1--R2 baselines plus seven robustness variants R3--R9) to probe the sensitivity of baseline estimates to profitability definitions, inclusion of eligible dividend disclosures, entity-timing mismatches, transition-year volatility, bonus share dilutive adjustments, and restated basic earnings; and
    \item \textit{Econometric Diagnostics and Specification Comparison} (Section~4.2.7), evaluating variance inflation factors (VIF), Breusch-Pagan heteroskedasticity tests, Cook's distance influence metrics, residual normality, and comparing pooled OLS, bank fixed effects, and two-way fixed effects specifications.
\end{enumerate}

For analytical transparency and audit traceability, the analytical figures embedded in this chapter are computed from the dataset printed in Appendix~B.

The \textbf{Discussion} section (Section~4.3) evaluates, synthesizes, and critically interprets these empirical findings in the context of foundational valuation theory and contemporary empirical literature. It examines:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The structural behavior of accounting valuation slopes in the pre-consolidation regime (Section~4.3.1);
    \item Economic and institutional interpretations of the downward shift in the earnings slope and the attenuation of the book value slope post-consolidation (Section~4.3.2);
    \item Theoretical reconciliation with the \citet{ohlson1995} residual income valuation framework, clean-surplus accounting, dividend signaling, and banking opacity theories (Section~4.3.3);
    \item Comparative alignment and divergence with prior Nepalese and emerging-market empirical literature (Section~4.3.4); and
    \item Methodological, institutional, and regulatory implications stemming from transition-year friction and statutory reporting deadlines under Section 59(2) of the Banks and Financial Institutions Act, 2017 (Section~4.3.5).
\end{enumerate}

The descriptive statistics in Table~\ref{tab:t1_descriptives} reveal substantial economic shifts across the pre- and post-consolidation periods:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Market Price ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$):} The full-sample mean closing share price at the reporting deadline was NPR 254.34, with a wide range spanning from NPR 136.90 to NPR 520.00. Across sub-periods, share prices experienced a substantial secular decline. In the pre-consolidation era (FY 2076/77--2078/79), the mean market price stood at NPR 310.59 (SD = 119.46), reaching a high of NPR 520.00 for HBL in FY 2076/77. Following the consolidation wave, the mean market price contracted to NPR 205.60 (SD = 33.45), representing an average price erosion of 33.81\% (NPR 105.00 per share). Concurrently, cross-sectional dispersion in share prices contracted substantially, with standard deviation falling by 72.00\% (from 119.46 to 33.45). This price compression is consistent with broader macroeconomic tightening in Nepal and a general market repricing of commercial bank equities toward a narrower valuation band.
    \item \textbf{Earnings per Share (EPS):} Across the full sample, EPS averaged NPR 14.05, ranging from NPR 0.02 (Kumari Bank in FY 2080/81) to NPR 27.60 (HBL in FY 2076/77). Between regimes, mean EPS declined from NPR 17.90 pre-consolidation to NPR 10.72 post-consolidation---a contraction of 40.13\%. Its standard deviation increased from 3.99 to 6.53 (+63.95\%), and its coefficient of variation rose from 22.3\% to 61.0\%. These are descriptive changes and do not identify their cause.
    \item \textbf{Book Value per Share (BVPS):} In marked contrast to market prices and earnings, book value per share exhibited relative stability across regimes. The full-sample mean BVPS was NPR 160.18 (SD = 17.37), bounded between NPR 132.71 and NPR 190.70. Across sub-periods, mean BVPS rose slightly from NPR 158.11 (SD = 19.10) pre-consolidation to NPR 161.97 (SD = 16.18) post-consolidation---an increase of 2.45\% (NPR 3.87), while its standard deviation declined by 15.30\%. This descriptive pattern does not identify its cause.
    \item \textbf{Dividend per Share (DPS):} Cash dividend distributions declined. In the pre-consolidation period, all sampled banks distributed cash dividends, yielding a mean DPS of NPR 6.06 (SD = 3.56; ranging from NPR 2.00 to NPR 12.50). In the post-consolidation era, mean DPS was NPR 1.40 (SD = 2.97), a 76.90\% reduction; its median was zero because 12 of 15 post-consolidation bank-years recorded no cash dividend. DPS SD declined by 16.62\%, while its coefficient of variation rose from 58.8\% to 212.2\%.
    \item \textbf{Return on Assets (ROA):} Mean operating profitability fell across the structural divide. Full-sample mean ROA stood at 0.99\% (SD = 0.47\%). Pre-consolidation ROA averaged 1.31\% (SD = 0.22\%, range 0.90\% to 1.79\%), whereas post-consolidation ROA averaged 0.70\% (SD = 0.44\%, range 0.00\% to 1.51\%)---a 46.38\% decline; the SD rose by 97.49\% and the coefficient of variation rose from 16.9\% to 62.2\%.
\end{itemize}

The correlation matrix reveals several key empirical patterns:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Unconditional Price Associations:} In raw, unadjusted bivariate correlations, $\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$ exhibits a positive correlation with EPS ($r = 0.358, p = 0.061$) and BVPS ($r = 0.217, p = 0.268$). Descriptively, bank-years with higher earnings and profitability tended to pay higher cash dividends; these are bivariate associations only and do not establish that earnings cause dividends.

\subsection{Baseline Value Relevance: Model 1 Estimation}
To address Research Question~1 and provide an empirical benchmark of accounting value relevance prior to evaluating regime shifts, Model~1 estimates the static Ohlson valuation equation across the entire six-year panel:
\begin{equation}
\mathit{MPS}^{RD}_{it} = \alpha + \mu_i + \lambda_t + \beta_E \mathit{EPS}_{it} + \beta_B \mathit{BVPS}_{it} + \varepsilon_{it}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha$ is the constant baseline intercept, $\mu_i$ represents unobserved time-invariant bank fixed effects (with GBIME serving as reference bank), and $\lambda_t$ represents common fiscal-year fixed effects (with FY 2076/77 serving as reference year). Standard errors, $t$-statistics, $p$-values, and confidence intervals are estimated using \citet{mackinnon1985} HC3 heteroskedasticity-consistent standard errors evaluated on Student's $t$ distribution with residual degrees of freedom ($\text{df} = 16$).

Table~\ref{tab:t3_model1} reports the parameter estimates for Model~1, with MacKinnon-White HC3 robust standard errors in parentheses beneath each estimate; $t$-statistics, $p$-values, and 95\% confidence intervals are reported in the text.

The baseline results in Table~\ref{tab:t3_model1} provide important empirical insights:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Coefficient Magnitudes and Signs:} Both accounting fundamentals exhibit positive coefficients: $\beta_E = 2.095$ and $\beta_B = 0.219$. In economic terms, this suggests that across the full observation span, each additional NPR 1.00 of reported earnings per share is associated with an incremental NPR 2.09 in market share price at the reporting deadline ($\mathit{MPS}^{RD}$), while each additional NPR 1.00 of book equity per share is associated with an incremental NPR 0.22 in market price, holding unobserved bank characteristics and common macro-temporal shocks constant.
    \item \textbf{Statistical Significance under Robust Inference:} Neither slope coefficient achieves statistical significance at conventional levels. For EPS, the $t$-statistic is $0.51$ ($p = 0.616$ on $t(16)$), and the 95\% HC3 confidence interval spans widely from NPR $-6.587$ to NPR $10.777$. For BVPS, the $t$-statistic is $0.12$ ($p = 0.908$ on $t(16)$), with a 95\% confidence interval spanning from NPR $-3.720$ to NPR $4.158$.
    \item \textbf{Goodness of Fit and Degrees of Freedom:} The model exhibits a high coefficient of determination ($R^2 = 0.948$, Adjusted $R^2 = 0.912$). However, as demonstrated in the specification comparisons in Section~\ref{subsec:diagnostics_spec}, this high explanatory power is almost entirely driven by the two-way fixed effects structure---specifically the fiscal-year dummies ($\lambda_t$), which absorb the substantial macro-market repricing of NEPSE over this period. Accounting for 1 constant intercept, 4 bank dummy parameters, 5 fiscal-year dummy parameters, and 2 slope regressors consumes 12 degrees of freedom, leaving exactly $28 - 12 = 16$ residual degrees of freedom.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Impact of Consolidation on Value Relevance: Model 2 Estimation}
To evaluate Research Question~2 and directly test whether the 2023 consolidation wave altered the valuation relationship between accounting fundamentals and equity prices, Model~2 introduces consolidation interaction terms into the two-way fixed effects framework:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathit{MPS}^{RD}_{it} ={}& \alpha + \mu_i + \lambda_t + \beta_E \mathit{EPS}_{it} + \beta_B \mathit{BVPS}_{it} \\
& + \delta_E (\mathit{EPS}_{it} \times \mathit{Post}_t) + \delta_B (\mathit{BVPS}_{it} \times \mathit{Post}_t) + \varepsilon_{it}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\mathit{Post}_t = 1$ for the post-consolidation fiscal years (FY 2079/80, 2080/81, and 2081/82) and $\mathit{Post}_t = 0$ for the pre-consolidation fiscal years (FY 2076/77, 2077/78, and 2078/79). Reference categories remain identical (GBIME for $\mu_i$, FY 2076/77 for $\lambda_t$).

Because common fiscal-year fixed effects ($\lambda_t$) are fully specified, the stand-alone indicator $\mathit{Post}_t$ is an exact linear combination of the fiscal-year dummies ($\mathit{Post}_t = D_{2079/80} + D_{2080/81} + D_{2081/82}$); consequently, the main effect of $\mathit{Post}_t$ is perfectly absorbed by the year fixed effects and cannot be separately estimated. The pre-consolidation valuation slopes are captured by $\beta_E$ and $\beta_B$, the regime interaction coefficients are captured by $\delta_E$ and $\delta_B$, and the implied post-consolidation valuation slopes are given by the linear combinations $(\beta_E + \delta_E)$ and $(\beta_B + \delta_B)$.

The empirical estimates in Table~\ref{tab:t4_model2} do not provide statistically significant evidence regarding structural changes in valuation dynamics:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Pre-Consolidation Valuation Regime:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Prior to the 2023 consolidation wave, the estimated valuation slope for earnings per share was $\beta_E = 5.577$ (HC3 SE = 3.560, $t = 1.57, p = 0.140$). In economic magnitude, a one-rupee increase in EPS is associated with a 5.58-rupee increase in price, conditional on book value and the fixed effects. This estimate is not statistically significant at the 5\% level ($p = 0.140$ on $t(14)$), reflecting the wide confidence interval ([-2.059, 13.213]) resulting from small sample degrees of freedom and conservative HC3 standard errors.
        \item The pre-consolidation slope for book value per share was $\beta_B = 0.100$ (HC3 SE = 1.306, $t = 0.08, p = 0.940$). The estimate is close to zero, but its uncertainty does not establish that book value had no incremental association with price.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Consolidation Regime Interaction Shifts:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item The earnings interaction coefficient is negative: $\delta_E = -3.124$ (HC3 SE = 4.003, $t = -0.78, p = 0.448$). Its 95\% HC3 confidence interval is $[-11.710, 5.462]$, which includes no shift and shifts in either direction; the point estimate does not establish a post-consolidation discount.
        \item The book value interaction coefficient is negative: $\delta_B = -1.758$ (HC3 SE = 1.231, $t = -1.43, p = 0.175$), with a 95\% HC3 confidence interval of $[-4.398, 0.882]$. This imprecise point estimate does not establish an erosion in the book-value slope.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Implied Post-Consolidation Slopes:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Combining the baseline slope and interaction shift yields an implied post-consolidation earnings slope of $\beta_E + \delta_E = 2.453$ (HC3 SE = 2.269, $t = 1.08, p = 0.298$), with a 95\% HC3 confidence interval of $[-2.412, 7.319]$. The point estimate is lower than the pre-consolidation estimate, but the difference is not statistically significant.
        \item The implied post-consolidation slope for book value is negative: $\beta_B + \delta_B = -1.658$ (HC3 SE = 1.120, $t = -1.48, p = 0.161$). Its 95\% confidence interval, $[-4.059, 0.744]$, includes zero, so the estimate does not establish a negative association.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Principal Joint Wald Test of Regime Shift:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Because corporate combinations simultaneously alter multiple accounting dimensions, testing interaction coefficients individually can fail to capture multi-parameter structural shifts. The principal econometric test is the joint Wald $F$-test evaluating the composite null hypothesis that both interaction terms are simultaneously zero ($H_0: \delta_E = 0 \text{ and } \delta_B = 0$).
        \item The joint test yields $F(2, 14) = 2.954$ with $p = 0.085$. The null hypothesis is not rejected at the designated 5\% level under HC3. The unadjusted result is below 10\%, but remains suggestive rather than conclusive. A post hoc design-sensitivity calculation gives approximate detectable effects at 80\% power of 10.73 NPR for the pre-period EPS slope, 12.06 NPR for the EPS interaction, and 3.71 NPR for the BVPS interaction, so these non-rejections do not rule out effects smaller than those magnitudes.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Parameter Accounting and Residual Degrees of Freedom:}
    Model~2 estimates 1 constant intercept, 4 bank fixed effects (GBIME reference), 5 fiscal-year fixed effects (FY 2076/77 reference), 2 main slope parameters ($\beta_E, \beta_B$), and 2 interaction parameters ($\delta_E, \delta_B$), consuming a total of 14 degrees of freedom. On $N = 28$ observations, this leaves exactly $28 - 14 = 14$ residual degrees of freedom.
\end{enumerate}

Detailed evaluations of individual hypotheses:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 1 ($H_1$): Value Relevance of Earnings per Share:}
    \textit{Hypothesis Statement:} There is a statistically significant relationship between earnings per share and market share price ($H_0: \beta_E = 0$ vs.\ $H_1: \beta_E \ne 0$).
    In Model~1, the overall EPS slope is $\hat{\beta}_E = 2.095$ with an HC3 standard error of $4.096$ ($t = 0.51, p = 0.616$). In Model~2, the pre-consolidation EPS slope is estimated at $\hat{\beta}_E = 5.577$ with an HC3 standard error of $3.560$ ($t = 1.57, p = 0.140$). In both models, the point estimates are positive, but because $p > 0.05$ and the standard errors are large, the study fails to reject the null hypothesis of no statistical association at the 5\% significance level.
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 2 ($H_2$): Value Relevance of Book Value per Share:}
    \textit{Hypothesis Statement:} There is a statistically significant relationship between book value per share and market share price ($H_0: \beta_B = 0$ vs.\ $H_1: \beta_B \ne 0$).
    In Model~1, the overall slope is $\hat{\beta}_B = 0.219$ ($t = 0.12, p = 0.908$). In Model~2, the pre-consolidation BVPS slope is $\hat{\beta}_B = 0.100$ with an HC3 standard error of $1.306$ ($t = 0.08, p = 0.940$). Because $p \gg 0.05$ across both specifications, the study fails to reject the null hypothesis. Prior to consolidation, the book value slope is imprecisely estimated (95\% CI $[-2.700, 2.901]$), so the study cannot establish whether book value had a negligible, positive, or negative association with price beyond earnings and fixed effects.
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 3 ($H_3$): Structural Shift in Earnings Value Relevance:}
    \textit{Hypothesis Statement:} The value relevance of earnings per share underwent a statistically significant shift following the 2023 consolidation wave ($H_0: \delta_E = 0$ vs.\ $H_1: \delta_E \ne 0$).
    The interaction coefficient is $\hat{\delta}_E = -3.124$ with an HC3 standard error of $4.003$ ($t = -0.78, p = 0.448$). Although the point estimate indicates a substantial downward shift in the earnings slope, the individual coefficient is not statistically significant at conventional thresholds. The study fails to reject the null hypothesis of no structural shift in EPS value relevance when evaluated in isolation.
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 4 ($H_4$): Structural Shift in Book Value Relevance:}
    \textit{Hypothesis Statement:} The value relevance of book value per share underwent a statistically significant shift following the 2023 consolidation wave ($H_0: \delta_B = 0$ vs.\ $H_1: \delta_B \ne 0$).
    The interaction coefficient is $\hat{\delta}_B = -1.758$ with an HC3 standard error of $1.231$ ($t = -1.43, p = 0.175$). Although the negative shift reduces the implied post-consolidation slope to $-1.658$, the interaction term does not achieve statistical significance at the 5\% level. The study fails to reject the null hypothesis of no structural shift in BVPS value relevance when evaluated in isolation.
    \item \textbf{Principal Joint Structural Test:}
    Evaluating the joint composite hypothesis $H_0: \delta_E = \delta_B = 0$ under the pre-specified HC3 covariance yields $F(2,14)=2.954$ ($p=0.085$). The test does not reject at 5\% under HC3; the unadjusted result is below 10\% but remains suggestive rather than conclusive. The same test rejects at 5\% under the classical ($F=9.573$, $p=0.002$), HC0 ($F=12.654$, $p=0.001$), HC1 ($F=6.327$, $p=0.011$) and HC2 ($F=6.243$, $p=0.012$) covariances, and this pattern holds for all eight joint tests R2--R9 (Table~\ref{tab:cov_sensitivity}). The baseline non-rejection is therefore specific to the HC3 estimator.
\end{itemize}

The sensitivity analysis yields critical econometric and substantive insights:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Operating Profitability Substitution (R3):} Substituting ROA (PAT divided by average total assets) for EPS yields an operating profitability slope of $83.760$ (HC3 SE = 79.852, $p = 0.312$) and an interaction shift of $\delta_{ROA} = -43.570$ (HC3 SE = 83.671, $p = 0.611$). The book value interaction remains negative ($\delta_B = -1.901, \text{SE} = 1.290, p = 0.163$), and the joint Wald test yields $F(2, 14) = 2.46$ ($p = 0.121$). This is consistent with the observed valuation dynamics not being an artifact of per-share earnings scaling, though no substantive coefficient in this specification is statistically significant and it does not establish fundamental operational repricing.
    \item \textbf{Dividend Incorporation (R4):} Augmenting Model~2 with declared cash dividends per share (DPS) produces an earnings slope of $\hat{\beta}_E = 6.183$ (SE = 4.508, $p = 0.193$) and a book value slope of $\hat{\beta}_B = -0.642$ (SE = 1.378, $p = 0.649$). The interaction shifts remain consistently negative ($\delta_E = -4.072, \text{SE} = 5.151, p = 0.443; \delta_B = -1.770, \text{SE} = 1.335, p = 0.208$), and the joint Wald $F$ is $2.77$ ($p = 0.100$). Because adding DPS consumes an additional parameter, residual degrees of freedom decrease from 14 to exactly 13 on $N = 28$. DPS enters as an additive control. An interacted DPS variant (DPS $\\times$ Post) was also estimated; the joint regime-shift test is $F(2,12)=2.85$ ($p=0.097$), the same non-rejection as the additive R4, so the additive specification is retained.
    \item \textbf{Transition-Year Exclusion (R5):} Dropping the initial post-merger integration year (FY 2079/80, $N = 23, \text{residual df} = 10$) to eliminate one-off transition accounting friction preserves the structural pattern: $\hat{\beta}_E = 5.993$ (SE = 4.635, $p = 0.225$), $\hat{\delta}_E = -2.578$ (SE = 5.521, $p = 0.650$), $\hat{\delta}_B = -1.880$ (SE = 1.419, $p = 0.215$), with joint Wald $F(2, 10) = 2.41$ ($p = 0.139$). This pattern is consistent with the interpretation that the negative interaction shifts are not solely driven by temporary integration write-offs in the immediate post-merger fiscal year. The implied post-consolidation slopes are $\hat{\beta}_E + \hat{\delta}_E = 3.415$ (SE $= 3.891$, $p = 0.401$) for EPS and $\hat{\beta}_B + \hat{\delta}_B = -2.081$ (SE $= 1.969$, $p = 0.315$) for BVPS, both insignificant and the same signs as the baseline.
    \item \textbf{Entity-Timing Mismatch Exclusion (R6) --- Sensitivity Result:} Specification R6 excludes all five observations from FY 2078/79 ($N = 23$, residual $df=10$), including NMB, whose series did not have this 2023 merger mismatch. Excluding only the four mismatched bank observations produces numerically identical estimates because the remaining NMB observation is absorbed by the year effect. The four-row variant is, however, numerically degenerate for inference: the remaining NMB FY 2078/79 observation is the only observation in its year, so it has leverage 1.0 and a residual of approximately zero, and the HC3 standard errors of the year dummies reach approximately 205. The joint test is unchanged ($F(2,10)=5.818$, $p=0.021$), but the covariance matrix in that variant is not reliable. FY 2078/79 represents an entity-timing asymmetry for the four merged banks: audited statements published at the end of Chaitra 2079 BS reflected standalone, pre-merger banking operations, whereas prices observed on 13 April 2023 reflected the expanded post-merger entities.
    When this entity-mismatch fiscal year is excluded, the unadjusted joint Wald test is below 5\%: $F(2, 10) = 5.82, p = 0.021$, compared with the 5\% critical value of approximately 4.103; a 29.5\% decrease in the observed statistic would remove the raw rejection. This is the only unadjusted rejection among the nine joint tests R2--R10 under HC3; Holm adjustment across that family gives $p = 0.190$ ($p = 0.169$ across R2--R9). The R6 result therefore remains a sensitivity finding and does not provide multiplicity-adjusted evidence of a regime shift. In the R6 sample, dropping any single bank leaves the joint test far from rejection ($F = 0.615$--$1.309$, $p = 0.329$--$0.572$), so the R6 rejection does not survive a leave-one-bank perturbation.
    In this FY 2078/79-excluded specification, the book value interaction shift is estimated at $\hat{\delta}_B = -2.611$ with an HC3 standard error of $1.517$ ($t = -1.72, p = 0.116$ on $t(10)$), while the earnings interaction is $\hat{\delta}_E = -2.436$ (HC3 SE = 4.755, $t = -0.51, p = 0.620$). Neither individual interaction is significant at the 10\% level, and R5 at the same sample size and residual degrees of freedom does not reject ($F(2,10)=2.41$, $p=0.139$). R6 is an unadjusted sensitivity result. The implied post-consolidation slopes are $\hat{\beta}_E + \hat{\delta}_E = 1.900$ (SE $= 2.649$, $p = 0.490$) and $\hat{\beta}_B + \hat{\delta}_B = -1.809$ (SE $= 1.357$, $p = 0.212$), both insignificant and the same signs as the baseline; the joint rejection therefore does not imply significant post-consolidation slopes.
    \item \textbf{Bonus Capitalization Adjustment (R7):} Adjusting book value only for subsequent bonus share dilution ($\mathit{BVPS}_{\text{adj}} = \mathit{BVPS} / (1 + \text{bonus ratio})$, with EPS and price left on the original share base) yields estimates that are qualitatively similar to baseline but not identical: $\hat{\beta}_E = 5.775$ (SE = 4.145, $p = 0.185$), $\hat{\delta}_E = -4.043$ (SE = 4.446, $p = 0.379$), $\hat{\delta}_B = -1.533$ (SE = 1.595, $p = 0.353$), with Wald $F(2, 14) = 2.37$ ($p = 0.130$). The BVPS slope rises from 0.100 to 0.153 and the joint $F$ falls by about 20\%; the check is therefore a one-sided rescaling of BVPS, not a full bonus-dilution adjustment of the valuation relation.
    \item \textbf{Restated Earnings per Share (R8):} Substituting the basic EPS values printed as restated in later annual reports for GBIME FY 2076/77 and NMB FY 2077/78 leaves the baseline conclusions intact: $\hat{\beta}_E = 5.256$ (SE = 3.016, $p = 0.103$), $\hat{\delta}_E = -2.860$ (SE = 3.807, $p = 0.465$), $\hat{\delta}_B = -1.812$ (SE = 1.223, $p = 0.161$), with Wald $F(2, 14) = 2.92$ ($p = 0.087$).
    \item \textbf{Kanchan-Mismatched Bank-Year Exclusion (R9):} Dropping the single bank-year whose pre-acquisition fundamentals are paired with a post-acquisition price (NMB FY 2076/77, $N = 27$, residual df = 13) leaves the baseline pattern unchanged: $\hat{\beta}_E = 6.000$ (SE = 7.028, $p = 0.409$), $\hat{\delta}_E = -3.445$ (SE = 6.598, $p = 0.610$), $\hat{\delta}_B = -1.741$ (SE = 1.477, $p = 0.259$), with joint Wald $F(2, 13) = 1.79$ ($p = 0.205$). The Kanchan timing mismatch does not drive the baseline finding of no significant joint shift. HBL FY 2076/77 is not covered by R9; the single-observation leave-one-out aggregates are reported in the note to Table~\ref{tab:app_sensitivity} in Appendix E (joint $p$ ranging 0.035--0.291 across the 28 deletions), but the HBL FY 2076/77-specific deletion is not printed there.
\end{itemize}

A wild-cluster bootstrap sensitivity check was run on the two joint Wald decisions. The null was imposed by fitting Model~1, its residuals were resampled by bank with Rademacher weights, and the HC3 Wald statistic was recomputed on each of the 32 enumerated sign patterns. For Model~2 the bootstrap $p$-value is $0.0625$ (2 of 32 patterns), below the HC3 $p = 0.085$ but still above 0.05; for R6 it is $0.0625$ (2 of 32), above the HC3 $p = 0.021$. Only the R6 decision flips under the bootstrap, and that flip hangs on a single pattern out of 32. With 32 patterns the smallest attainable $p$-value is $2/32 = 0.0625$, so this bootstrap cannot produce a 5\% rejection for any specification; the R6 flip is therefore mechanically forced, not evidence about cluster-robust inference. The check is inconclusive at $G = 5$: it does not overturn the HC3 inference, and it provides no information about whether the joint-test decisions would survive a cluster-robust alternative.

Two additional price-window sensitivities show that the sample rule affects the joint-test decision. Re-including the two observed delayed prices gives $F(2,16)=4.762$ ($p=0.024$; $N=30$). In a stale-price carry-forward scenario, using the last prices in the panel-series files for HBL (NPR 487) and NIB/NIMB (NPR 453), both dated 12 May 2021, gives $F(2,16)=3.870$ ($p=0.043$). The candidate price table also records an alternative reported pair, HBL NPR 484 and NIB NPR 460, dated 13 May 2021; using that pair gives $F(2,16)=3.821$ ($p=0.044$). The difference reflects an unresolved one-day/source discrepancy. Both pairs are roughly a year before the April 2022 target, so these are stale-price scenarios, not observations meeting the five-day rule. In the $N=30$ model with the observed delayed prices, the pre-period BVPS estimate rises from 0.100 to 0.709; excluding the delayed rows therefore attenuates that slope in this sensitivity comparison. The delayed observations coincide with merger-related trading gaps, but their missingness mechanism and its direction cannot be established from these cases.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Value Relevance of Accounting Fundamentals in the Pre-Consolidation Regime}
As reported in Table~\ref{tab:t4_model2}, in the pre-consolidation regime (FY 2076/77 through FY 2078/79), the estimated earnings slope was $\hat{\beta}_E = 5.577$ ($p = 0.140$ on $t(14)$), whereas the book-value slope was $\hat{\beta}_B = 0.100$ ($p = 0.940$ on $t(14)$).

These parameter estimates are not statistically significant at the 5\% level and are imprecise; their relative magnitudes do not establish the valuation behavior of the Nepalese secondary banking market prior to the 2023 consolidation wave:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Relative Point Estimates:} The earnings point estimate ($5.58$) is larger than the BVPS estimate ($0.10$), but both are statistically insignificant and have wide uncertainty. These estimates do not establish that investors prioritized earnings or that earnings served as the primary valuation benchmark.
    \item \textbf{Book Value Estimate:} The BVPS point estimate is close to zero, but its confidence interval is wide; the estimate does not establish that book value had no incremental pricing association. The reasons investors may have assigned to this estimate cannot be identified from the regression.
\end{itemize}

\subsection[Regime Shift: Earnings and Book Value Slopes Post-Consolidation]{Regime Shift: Earnings and Book Value Slopes Post-\\Consolidation}
As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:t4_model2}, following the consolidation wave of early 2023, the point estimates show an attenuation of the implied slopes that is not statistically significant, with negative interaction coefficients ($\hat{\delta}_E = -3.124, p = 0.448; \hat{\delta}_B = -1.758, p = 0.175$) and attenuated implied post-consolidation slopes ($\hat{\beta}_E + \hat{\delta}_E = 2.453, p = 0.298; \hat{\beta}_B + \hat{\delta}_B = -1.658, p = 0.161$).

Because neither individual interaction term achieves statistical significance at conventional thresholds, and the joint Wald test in the baseline sample is marginally significant at 10\% ($p = 0.085$), these patterns are interpreted not as proven causal effects, but as suggestive structural tendencies consistent with several economic and institutional mechanisms operating in Nepal's post-merger banking environment:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Asset Quality Pressures and Loan Provisioning (Plausible Mechanism):} The horizontal amalgamations executed in 2023 required surviving commercial banks to assimilate legacy loan portfolios from acquired institutions. One plausible mechanism is that, following consolidation, commercial banks experienced rising non-performing loans, driven by broad macroeconomic deceleration, construction sector stress, and stricter loan-loss provisioning requirements. No non-performing-loan, provisioning, or macroeconomic variable is estimated in this study, so this mechanism is a hypothesis, not a finding. To the extent that credit-loss charges expanded, reported accounting earnings exhibited higher volatility; whether that volatility reflects transitoriness was not measured. Equity investors may have recognized that reported net income was increasingly sensitive to provisioning assumptions, leading to a discount on reported earnings. This mechanism is consistent with the point-estimate attenuation of the earnings slope, though direct asset quality variables are not estimated in the econometric model.
    \item \textbf{Capital Expansion and Share Supply:} Amalgamations significantly expanded the paid-up equity base of surviving banks through share swap agreements. The influx of newly created equity shares diluted per-share earnings (mean EPS contracted by 40.13\% post-consolidation, from NPR 17.90 to NPR 10.72) and expanded the secondary market float. Market participants may have factored this enlarged equity supply into their pricing models, anticipating slower growth in per-share metrics (see Chapter~I, Section~1.1; Chapter~III, Section~3.3).
    \item \textbf{Dividend Cessation and Information Loss:} In the pre-consolidation period, regular cash dividends provided an observable benchmark for equity valuations (mean DPS = NPR 6.06). Following consolidation, regulatory capital conservation guidelines and heightened provisioning mandates coincided with widespread cash dividend suspensions (mean DPS fell to NPR 1.40, with 12 of 15 post-consolidation bank-years recording zero cash dividends). The withdrawal of regular dividend payments removed an observable cash flow signal. This mechanism is a theoretical hypothesis, not a corroborated finding: the bivariate DPS--price correlation in this sample is near zero ($r = 0.082$, $p = 0.679$; Table~\ref{tab:t2_correlations}), so the sample provides no evidence of a dividend--price link.
    \item \textbf{Interpreting the Negative Book Value Point Estimate:} The negative implied slope for book value per share ($\hat{\beta}_B + \hat{\delta}_B = -1.658$, HC3 SE = 1.120, $t = -1.48, p = 0.161$) is statistically insignificant under the Student's $t(14)$ distribution. It cannot therefore be presented as an established empirical law or as evidence that book equity increases were associated with lower prices. Possible explanations based on asset quality or capital structure remain hypotheses; these variables were not estimated in the model.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Reconciling with Theoretical Frameworks}
The empirical findings can be evaluated against foundational theoretical models in accounting and financial economics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Divergence and Resonance with \citet{kwon2020}:}
    Comparing our results with the post-merger valuation framework of \citet{kwon2020} reveals an important divergence alongside a shared insight:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Where our results resonate:} In line with Kwon and Wang, our findings are consistent with the perspective that corporate amalgamations can introduce accounting noise and structural disruption that attenuate the valuation relevance of earnings. Following major mergers, financial statement comparability is disrupted and historical earnings predictability may diminish; the point estimate for the earnings shift is negative ($\hat{\delta}_E = -3.124$) but statistically insignificant ($p = 0.448$), so it does not establish that equity markets discount reported earnings.
        \item \textit{Where our results diverge:} In Kwon and Wang's framework, when earnings become noisy following mergers, book value may assume an anchoring role. In this sample, the book-value slope point estimate changes from $\hat{\beta}_B=0.100$ to an implied post-consolidation estimate of $-1.658$, but the post estimate is statistically insignificant ($p=0.161$). The result does not establish whether book value served as an asset anchor.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The \citet{ohlson1995} Residual Income Framework:}
    The Ohlson model specifies firm equity value as the sum of contemporaneous book value and capitalized expected future abnormal earnings:
    \begin{equation}
    P_t = B_t + \sum_{\tau=1}^{\infty} R^{-\tau} \mathbb{E}_t [\tilde{x}_{t+\tau}^a]
    \end{equation}
    Our baseline estimates in the pre-consolidation period display positive point estimates for both components ($\hat{\beta}_E = 5.58, \hat{\beta}_B = 0.10$). However, the post-consolidation regime exposes the limitations of linear valuation models during periods of banking restructuring. When clean-surplus accounting is complicated by merger reserve adjustments, swap accounting, and dividend halts, the linear pricing model is estimated imprecisely, producing wide confidence intervals and attenuated point estimates; a structural breakdown of the pricing relation is not established.
    \item \textbf{Dividend Signaling Theory \citep{bhattacharya1979,john1985}:}
    Under dividend signaling theory, cash distributions provide a costly and credible signal verifying reported earnings quality. In the Nepalese banking sector, where retail equity investors face informational opacity regarding loan classifications, cash dividends historically functioned as an observable verification of accounting profits. When regulatory capital constraints coincided with the elimination of cash dividends post-consolidation, the absence of this verification signal coincided with the attenuation of the earnings slope; the sample itself shows no dividend--price association ($r = 0.082$, $p = 0.679$), so this is a theoretical mechanism, not a corroborated finding.
    \item \textbf{Banking Opacity and Regulatory Forbearance \citep{morgan2002,flannery2004}:}
    Commercial banks are recognized in financial economics as opaque institutions due to the proprietary nature of loan assets and the discretion inherent in loan provisioning. In Nepal, regulatory forbearance provisions---allowing consolidated banks extended timelines to meet regulatory ratios---may have increased informational opacity. When market participants cannot independently verify loan portfolio quality, they may discount reported accounting metrics, attenuating valuation slopes.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Comparative Context with Nepalese and International Empirical Studies}
Evaluating the empirical findings of this dissertation against prior literature reveals important methodological and empirical distinctions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Methodological Differences with Prior Nepalese Studies:}
    Prior empirical literature on Nepalese commercial banks has predominantly reported statistically significant positive coefficients for accounting fundamentals \citep{bhattarai2014,joshi2023}. In contrast, our two-way fixed effects baseline yields statistically insignificant slopes ($\hat{\beta}_E = 2.09, p = 0.616; \hat{\beta}_B = 0.22, p = 0.908$). This divergence reflects four critical methodological factors:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Exclusion of Circular Price-to-Earnings Multiples:} Several prior Nepalese studies included the Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratio as an explanatory regressor predicting share price \citep{bhattarai2014,chhetri2023,giri2024,joshi2023,pant2024}. As demonstrated mathematically in Chapter~II, because $\text{P/E} = \mathit{MPS} / \mathit{EPS}$, regressing $\mathit{MPS}$ on P/E introduces the dependent variable into the regressor, generating mechanical circularity and inflated statistical significance. This dissertation strictly excludes P/E.
        \item \textit{Omission of Macro-Year Fixed Effects in Pooled Models:} Prior studies frequently relied on pooled OLS, omitting fiscal-year dummies. As Table~\ref{tab:spec_comp} illustrates, pooled OLS yields an EPS point estimate of $4.882$ compared with $2.095$ in two-way fixed effects. Year fixed effects absorb common fiscal-year price variation, but the two-way LSDV slope is conditional on both bank and year indicators and is not a purely within-year estimate. The pooled estimate is imprecise ($t=1.58$, $p=0.128$), as is the Model~1 estimate; this comparison does not establish that omitted year shocks explain the pooled literature's results.
        \item \textit{Standardized Reporting-Deadline Measurement:} Bhattarai (2014) measured share prices at fiscal year-end (Ashadh-end / mid-July), before audited reports were published. This study measures prices on or after the statutory deadline under Section 59(2) of BAFIA, operationalized at the end of Chaitra (mid-April) \citep{bafia2017}. This reduces temporal mismatch but does not verify the public release date for each bank-year or eliminate possible information leakage.
        \item \textit{Sample Harmonization:} In reconciling prior samples, \citet{bhattarai2014} analyzed nine commercial banks over 2006--2014, while \citet{joshi2023} analyzed seven commercial banks over 2012/13--2021/22. Both examined pre-consolidation periods under pooled structures; whether that pooling explains their higher reported significance is not established by this study's comparison, since the pooled EPS slope here is itself insignificant ($p = 0.128$).
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Alignment with Recent Methodological Studies:}
    Our findings closely parallel more recent Nepalese studies that document valuation instability:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \citet{chaudhari2025} examined ten commercial banks over 2014/15--2023/24 without P/E circularity and documented strong, statistically significant positive coefficients for EPS and DPS, a statistically significant negative association for ROA, while among the macroeconomic variables inflation was significant at the 5\% level ($p = 0.037$) and interest rates and GDP were insignificant.
        \item \citet{pant2024} investigated seven commercial banks through FY 2023/24 and documented a negative, statistically insignificant BVPS coefficient, consistent with our post-consolidation findings.
        \item \citet{timalsina2026} evaluated 13 commercial banks over FY 2011/12 to 2023/24 and documented statistically significant positive associations of EPS, DPS, P/E, firm size, and inflation with share prices, with interest rates exerting a significant negative effect.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Emerging Market Literature Context:}
    In broader emerging and frontier markets, \citet{almumani2014} in Jordan found that EPS, BVPS, and price-earnings ratios were the significant positive determinants of market price, with EPS showing the highest bivariate correlation ($r = 0.84$), and \citet{malhotra2013} in India found that book value per share, earnings per share, and price-earnings multiples significantly influenced market prices. These studies report an EPS/BVPS price relationship in comparable markets, though neither tested whether value relevance changes across a consolidation regime.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Methodological Context: Transition Dynamics and Structural Discontinuities}
The sensitivity analyses conducted in Section~\ref{subsec:robustness_suite} provide important methodological insights for evaluating corporate restructuring in frontier capital markets:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Entity-Boundary Timing and Sensitivity Check R6:}
    The most salient sensitivity finding arises from specification R6, which excludes FY 2078/79 ($N = 23, \text{residual df} = 10$). In that specification, the joint Wald test of regime shift achieves statistical significance at the 5\% level:
    \begin{equation}
    F(2, 10) = 5.82, \quad p = 0.021
    \end{equation}
    This result reflects an entity-timing sensitivity: FY 2078/79 accounts represent pre-merger operations while April 2023 prices reflect post-merger entities. The baseline joint test does not reject at 5\% ($p=0.085$). Excluding the year yields an unadjusted $p=0.021$, but the result is not significant after Holm adjustment across the nine joint tests ($p=0.190$). It is reported as an association across calendar regimes, not as a causal merger effect.
    \item \textbf{The 5-Day Freshness Rule and Trading Suspensions:}
    The exclusion of HBL and NIMB for FY 2077/78 reflects extended merger-related trading suspensions. The two observations exceed the five-day operational cutoff and are omitted without imputation. Because their delays coincide with merger-related suspensions, the exclusion may be informative; including them changes the baseline Model~2 joint test to $F(2,16)=4.762$ ($p=0.024$), compared with $F(2,14)=2.954$ ($p=0.085$) in the 28-row sample.
    \item \textbf{BAFIA Section 59(2) Reporting Deadlines as an Empirical Anchor:}
    Anchoring share price observations to the nine-month statutory reporting deadline under Section 59(2) of BAFIA, operationalized at the end of Chaitra (mid-April), provided a reproducible observation rule. It reduces date-selection ambiguity but does not verify each bank-year public release date or eliminate possible information leakage.
\end{itemize}

In summary, the estimated slopes are consistent with lower earnings and book-value associations after consolidation, but the estimates are imprecise and the baseline joint test does not reject at 5\% under HC3. The unadjusted R6 result is not significant after Holm adjustment across the nine joint tests and depends on the sample definition. These analyses do not establish a causal merger effect.
