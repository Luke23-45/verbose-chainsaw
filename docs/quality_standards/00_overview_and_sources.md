# Thesis quality standards — overview and sources

Directory purpose: the official *Master's Degree Dissertation Guidelines
(2019, FOM, TU)* governs FORMAT (structure, margins, spacing, APA). It
says little about CONTENT QUALITY — what makes examiners judge a thesis
as strong. This directory collects what top universities and the
examiner-preparation literature say about content quality, and maps each
standard onto THIS thesis with concrete file and section pointers.

Precedence rule: where an external standard conflicts with the TU FOM
guideline or the supervisor's instruction, the guideline and the
supervisor win. These files apply only where the guideline is silent.

## How to use this directory

Layer 1 — standards with audit:

1. `01_chapter_function_standards.md` — what each chapter must *do*
   (function, not format), with pointers to where this thesis already
   complies and where the risk sits.
2. `02_claims_hedging_and_style.md` — claim–evidence proportionality,
   hedging vocabulary, tense, paragraph discipline, banned phrasings.
3. `03_statistical_reporting_standards.md` — how to report models,
   tests, and uncertainty at the standard of the APA statistical-
   reporting reform literature (Wilkinson/TFSI 1999 and successors).
4. `04_examiner_checklist.md` — the assessment criteria actual thesis
   examiners apply, converted to a pass/fail checklist for this thesis.

Layer 2 — deep-dive methods (fetched primary sources, worked into the
thesis's own text):

5. `05_introduction_cars_argument.md` — Swales' CARS model audited
   move-by-move against Chapter I, with the niche-statement rules.
6. `06_literature_synthesis_method.md` — the synthesis-matrix method
   (FIU) applied to Table 2.1; the "sources converse" test; the one-
   claim-two-studies rule; the three profile paragraphs to upgrade.
7. `07_discussion_interpretation_moves.md` — Guelph's funnel mapped onto
   §4.3; the four discussion moves with the thesis's own best
   instances; the interpretation vocabulary.
8. `08_inference_and_data_display.md` — the ASA six principles verbatim
   and what each binds; NHST language rules; APA number conventions
   (including the documented leading-zero deviation); Tufte's display
   principles applied to the thesis's tables and figures.
9. `09_examiner_read_path_defense.md` — the read path, the ten viva
   questions this thesis will attract with section-pointer answers, the
   McGill self-rating, and the pre-viva sweep.
10. `10_phrasebank_thesis_templates.md` — Manchester Phrasebank-style
    sentence frames pre-filled with this thesis's variables, numbers,
    and institutions; the anti-slop rule for using them.

## Sources (all consulted 2026-11-03)

### Argument and chapter structure
- Harvard College Writing Center — Thesis:
  https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/thesis — a thesis must be
  *arguable*, appear early, and "telegraph" how the argument proceeds.
- Harvard College Writing Center — Conclusions:
  https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/conclusions — even research
  papers do not need exhaustive summary at the end; conclusions draw
  meaning, not inventory.
- Harvard College Writing Center — Anatomy of a Body Paragraph:
  https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/anatomy-body-paragraph — every
  body paragraph = topic sentence + evidence + analysis.
- Harvard Dept. of History of Art and Architecture — Writing a Thesis:
  https://haa.fas.harvard.edu/writing-thesis — a meaningful question, a
  well-reasoned structured argument, appropriately marshalled evidence.

### Discussion sections
- PLOS Author Resources — How to Write Effective Discussions and
  Conclusions: https://explore.plos.org/author-resources/how-to-write-effective-discussions-and-conclusions
  — open with the principal findings in plain terms, then why they
  matter.
- University of Guelph, Writing in the Sciences — Discussion:
  https://writinginthesciences.uoguelph.ca/discussion — the funnel:
  answer the specific research question first, then move outward to
  broader implications.
- Charles Darwin University — Writing a Discussion Section:
  https://www.cdu.edu.au/library/language-and-learning-support/study-skills/research-communication/writing-discussion-section
  — draw connections between findings, existing theory, and other
  research.
- Oxford Lifelong Learning — Writing Discussion Sections:
  https://lifelong-learning.ox.ac.uk/writing-discussion-sections —
  interpret findings, evaluate significance, connect to wider literature.
- ExpertJournals — Academic Writing Tips:
  https://expertjournals.com/academic-writing-tips-for-the-discussion-section-of-a-research-paper
  — split the discussion into theoretical contribution, practical
  implications, limitations.

### Hedging and claim discipline
- City University of Hong Kong, ELSS — Critiquing Hedging Devices:
  https://www.lc.cityu.edu.hk/ELSS/Resource/Critiquing%20Hedging%20Devices
  — hedging makes claims proportionate to the evidence available.
- The Use of Cautious Language in Academic Research Papers:
  http://www.rce.feaa.ugal.ro/images/stories/RCE2016/CarmenOpritMaftei.pdf
  — hedging expresses claims "with due caution, modesty, and humility".

### Literature review as synthesis
- FIU Writing Center — Writing a Literature Review and Using a Synthesis
  Matrix (PDF):
  https://case.fiu.edu/writingcenter/online-resources/_assets/synthesis-matrix-2.pdf
  — "a literature review is not a summary"; organize by theme, build a
  synthesis matrix of sources × themes.
- Texas A&M University–Corpus Christi — Literature Reviews (PDF):
  https://www.tamucc.edu/academics/casa/assets/documents/literaturereview.pdf
  — organize around ideas, not the sources themselves.
- University of Oregon Libraries — How to Write a Literature Review:
  Synthesize: https://researchguides.uoregon.edu/litreview/synthesize —
  show how sources "converse" with each other.
- Editage — Literature synthesis tips:
  https://www.editage.com/blog/how-to-synthesize-literature-in-research-literature-synthesis-examples-and-tips
  — one claim per paragraph, at least two studies cited per claim.

### Statistical reporting
- Wilkinson, L., & the Task Force on Statistical Inference (1999).
  Statistical Methods in Psychology Journals: Guidelines and
  Explanations. *American Psychologist, 54*(8), 594–604 — the landmark
  reform paper: report effect sizes and confidence intervals, exact
  p values, sufficient descriptive detail for replication, and do not
  let NHST p values carry interpretation alone.
- APA Style — Numbers and Statistics Guide (official):
  https://apastyle.apa.org/instructional-aids/numbers-statistics-guide.pdf
- Purdue OWL — Numbers and Statistics:
  https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/apa_style/apa_formatting_and_style_guide/apa_numbers_statistics.html
- Scribbr — Reporting Statistics in APA Style:
  https://www.scribbr.com/apa-style/numbers-and-statistics

### What examiners actually do

  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191491X18303808
- McGill University — Evaluation of a Written Thesis (official examiner

- Pat Thomson — How an examiner reads a thesis:
  https://patthomson.net/2017/04/03/how-an-examiner-reads-a-thesis

### Rhetorical structure of introductions (fetched/verified)
- Swales, J. (1990). *Genre Analysis: English in Academic and Research
  Settings*. Cambridge UP — the CARS model: Move 1 establish a
  territory, Move 2 establish a niche, Move 3 occupy the niche.
- Swales, J., & Feak, C. (2012). *Academic Writing for Graduate
  Students* (3rd ed.). University of Michigan Press — the working
  textbook version of the moves. (Full-text PDF of the textbook is
  widely mirrored; consulted for the moves breakdown.)
- Secondary analyses: Msuya (2020), Athens Journal of Philology:
  https://www.athensjournals.gr/philology/2020-7-4-2-Msuya.pdf

### Statistical inference reform (fetched in full)
- Wasserstein, R. L., & Lazar, N. A. (2016). The ASA Statement on
  p-Values: Context, Process, and Purpose. *The American Statistician,
  70*(2), 129–133 — six principles:
  https://www.amstat.org/asa/files/pdfs/p-valuestatement.pdf
  (Berkeley mirror: https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/Real_World/ASA_statement.pdf)
- ASA (2021) follow-up Task Force statement:
  https://magazine.amstat.org/blog/2021/08/01/task-force-statement-p-value

### Data display
- Tufte, E. R. (2001). *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*
  (2nd ed.). Graphics Press — data-ink maximization, chartjunk, the lie
  factor; chapter extracts:
  http://www.eng.uwaterloo.ca/~jzelek/teaching/syde361/tufte-ch4-6-v3.pdf

### Phrase-level craft
- The University of Manchester, Academic Phrasebank
  (https://www.phrasebank.manchester.ac.uk) — Discussing findings:
  https://www.phrasebank.manchester.ac.uk/discussing-findings ; Morley,
  J. (2021) compiled edition PDF available via the site. —
  examiners often read the conclusions first, hunting for the stated
  contribution.
