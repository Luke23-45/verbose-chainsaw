# 06 — Literature review as synthesis: method, not summary

Primary sources: FIU Writing Center synthesis-matrix guide ("a literature
review is not a summary ... organize it by theme", with the sources ×
themes grid method); TAMU-CC ("organized around ideas, not the sources
themselves"); University of Oregon ("show how the sources converse with
each other"); Editage ("one claim per paragraph, at least two studies per
claim"); Manchester Academic Phrasebank, "Discussing findings"
(https://www.phrasebank.manchester.ac.uk/discussing-findings).

---

## 1. The architecture this thesis uses is already the recommended one

The FIU method asks for three layers:

| Layer | FIU term | Where it lives in this thesis |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence layer | the matrix (sources × attributes) | Table 2.1 — 22 studies × sample/period/method/variables/findings/**critique** |
| Theme layer | organized by ideas | §2.4.5's four thematic debates |
| Gap layer | the review's product | §2.5's five gaps, each with cited evidence |

A reviewer checking "synthesis or summary?" looks for exactly these
three layers. The thesis has all three. The deep-audit points below are
about the *quality of the joins* between layers.

## 2. The synthesis matrix, kept alive

Table 2.1 is a synthesis matrix, not decoration. Two rules keep it one:

1. **Every cell must be checkable against the row's study.** The current
   state after the 2026-11-02 fixes: observation counts only where
   studies report them; no unsourced periods. Remaining cell-level gaps
   (six counts, Lamsal years) stay marked pending the papers — an empty
   checkable cell beats a filled invented one.
2. **The Critique column must stay evidence-tied.** Current entries come
   from the thesis's own sections (circular P/E from §2.2.6; static
   pooling from the Gurung & Dahal profile). If a new study is added,
   its critique entry must name the thesis section that supports it —
   or not appear.

## 3. Making the sources converse (the Oregon test)

The strongest synthesis move is a sentence in which two studies respond
to each other. The thesis already does this at its best moments:

- The EPS-dominant vs BVPS-dominant debate (§2.4.5 item 1) sets
  Bhandari/Chaudhari/Niroula/Pradhan & Dahal *against* Chhetri/Silwal
  and explains the divergence institutionally (2015 capital mandate →
  bonus expansion → balance-sheet benchmarking). This is a conversation,
  not a list.
- The Gurung & Dahal footnote distinguishes two easily confused author
  teams and states which is the methodological precedent — a
  conversation with the reader's likely confusion.

The upgrade targets are the three profile paragraphs that currently end
on bare findings (Niroula 2021, Gyawali 2022, Joshi 2023). The Oregon
standard: the profile's last sentence should place the study in a
conversation. Model rewrite (Niroula), showing the pattern without
inventing facts — it uses only findings already stated:

> Current: "...book value per share (BVPS) and return on equity (ROE)
> exhibited negative coefficients that failed to attain statistical
> significance, highlighting growing empirical ambiguity regarding the
> pricing of balance-sheet net worth."
>
> Pattern: keep the sentence, then add the conversational close —
> "Niroula's negative, insignificant BVPS coefficient sits at the
> opposite pole from Silwal and Napit's (2019) finding that net asset
> backing was the primary benchmark for Nepalese bank investors, a
> divergence §2.4.5 takes up."

The same one-sentence close works for Gyawali (ROA insignificant →
against Chaudhari's significant-negative ROA) and Joshi (all-four-
significant → against Chhetri's insignificant EPS). No new claims —
each close only cites findings the thesis already reports.

## 4. The one-claim-two-studies rule (Editage) in the synthesis sections

§2.4.5's debate paragraphs already cite 4–6 studies per claim. The rule
to preserve: **a claim about the literature is only as strong as the
number of studies cited for it.** If an edit trims citations from a
debate paragraph, the claim's scope sentence must shrink with it (e.g.,
"studies by X, Y, and Z" → only the names that remain).

## 5. Review-coverage honesty (the examiner's spot-check)

Examiners test the review by picking a study the thesis cites and asking
"what did it actually find?" — and by picking a study it *should* have
cited and asking "where is it?". Defenses already in place:

- Every empirical claim in §2.4 traces to a source in references.bib
  (checked in the citation-vs-bibliography loop).
- The selection criterion for Table 2.1 is stated explicitly, and the
  four excluded non-MPS studies are now named with the reason
  (§2.4.5 intro sentence added 2026-11-02). This is the answer to
  "where is study X?" — the criterion decides, not omission.

## 6. The Phrasbank pattern for profile paragraphs (reference set)

Manchester's "Discussing findings" frames the standard comparison moves;
the thesis's equivalents, drawn from its own text:

- Agreement: "In line with Kwon and Wang, our findings are consistent
  with the perspective that corporate amalgamations can introduce
  accounting noise..." (§4.3.3)
- Divergence: "Where our results diverge: In Kwon and Wang's framework,
  when earnings become noisy following mergers, book value typically
  assumes an anchoring role... In our Nepalese commercial banking
  sample, book value conspicuously did not serve as an asset anchor."
- Concession + rebuttal: "While recent work by Gurung and Dahal
  introduces bank fixed effects..., almost all existing studies estimate
  static, full-period average coefficients."

These are the three moves to reuse whenever a new study or comparison is
added. They come from the thesis, so they are voice-consistent.
