## 1. Guelph's funnel, and where this thesis sits in it

Guelph's seven-step discussion structure: (1) restate the research
question/aim in context, (2) give the answer, (3) place the answer in
the relevant literature, (4) state the significance/implications,
(5) note the caveats, (6) identify future directions. The funnel image:
specific answer → broader meaning.

§4.3 maps onto this almost exactly:

| Guelph step | Thesis section |
|---|---|
| Re-question + answer | §4.3.1 (pre-consolidation regime: what pricing looked like) |
| Answer (regime difference) | §4.3.2 (the shifts, with the governing causal-language disclaimer) |
| Literature placement | §4.3.3 (Kwon & Wang resonance + divergence; Ohlson; signaling; opacity) |
| Comparative literature | §4.3.4 (methodological reasons for divergence from Nepalese studies) |
| Caveats/limits | §4.3.5 (transition dynamics, freshness rule, BAFIA anchor) |

The one inversion vs Guelph: the thesis discusses *before* Chapter V's
implications rather than folding implications into the discussion —
correct under the TU guideline (Ch. V owns implications). Note this
deliberate divergence so an examiner's "where are the implications?"
is pre-answered by §4.3.5's closing and §5.4.

## 2. The four discussion moves (with the thesis's own best instances)

**Move A — interpret, don't re-report.** Oxford's test: a discussion
sentence should be deletable from Results without loss, and vice versa.
The thesis's interpretation sentences are genuinely interpretive:
"Rather than serving as an asset anchor, increases in book equity
following the merger wave were weakly associated with lower share
prices" (§4.3.2 item 4) — this says what the number *means*, not what
it *is*. Violation pattern to avoid (present historically, since fixed):
opening discussion subsections by reprinting full SE/t/p apparatus.

**Move B — confront the theory.** CDU: connect findings to theory *and*
to other research, including the uncomfortable connection. The thesis's
strongest passage is §4.3.3 item 1's two-part Kwon & Wang treatment:
resonance (earnings noise) stated first, then divergence (book value did
NOT anchor) with the banking-specific reason (resolution regimes, not
orderly liquidation). This is the deep-discussion standard: the
divergence is explained, not hidden. Protect this passage in edits.

**Move C — locate the mechanism honestly.** The accepted pattern is
§4.3.2's: mechanism named → "Plausible Mechanism" label → cited
contextual support → explicit sentence that the mechanism is not
estimated. The TFSI discipline (file 03) and the hedging ladder (file
02) both bind here: a mechanism sentence without the "not estimated"
closing is a causal claim the design cannot support.

**Move D — comparative context with a reason.** §4.3.4 does not just
list prior Nepalese findings; it gives four methodological reasons for
the divergence (circular P/E; no year FE; Ashadh-end timing; sample
composition). PLOS's standard is exactly this: findings + why they
differ. The pattern to keep: every "prior studies found X, we find Y"
sentence must carry at least one reason from that list.

## 3. Interpretation vocabulary (Phrasebank-consistent, thesis-tested)

Safe interpretive frames already used in this thesis — reuse these:

- "is consistent with" — the workhorse; attaches an estimate to a
  mechanism without causation
- "coinciding with" / "coincides with" — temporal association
- "suggestive of" / "suggestive structural tendencies" — §4.3.2's
  governing frame
- "plausible economic interpretation" — §4.3.2 item 4's opening
- "cannot therefore be presented as an established empirical law" —
  §4.3.2 item 4's own self-limit
- "documented as an empirical association across calendar periods,
  rather than as a causal merger effect" — the closing frame of §4.3.5

Frames to avoid (assertion without warrant):

- "demonstrates that the consolidation caused", "proves", "validates"
- "clearly indicates" / "strongly suggests" — intensifiers that add
  rhetoric, not warrant
- "it is obvious that" — never

## 4. Significance claims (Oxford: evaluate the significance, honestly)

Oxford asks the discussion to evaluate *how much the finding matters*.
The thesis does this on two axes and should keep both:

1. **Statistical honesty**: the shift is 10% in the baseline, 5% after
   R6 — stated with the level every time (file 03's threshold rule).
2. **Economic magnitude**: the earnings multiple halves (5.58 → 2.45);
   DPS falls 76.9%; dispersion collapses 72%. These are the "why it
   matters" numbers. The rule: every significance paragraph should
   contain at least one magnitude and one level, in that order.
