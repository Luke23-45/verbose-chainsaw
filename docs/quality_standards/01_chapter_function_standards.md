# 01 — Chapter function standards

What each chapter of THIS thesis must *accomplish*, judged against
top-university writing guidance. Format (order, length, headings) is
already fixed by the TU FOM guideline; this file is about function.

The recurring standard across all sources: a thesis is an **argument**,
not a report of activity. Harvard's standard — an arguable claim,
telegraphed early, supported by claim–evidence–analysis paragraphs —
applies to a dissertation exactly as to an essay.

---

## Chapter I — Introduction: sell the question, then the design

Required functions (Harvard Thesis; Harvard HAA):

1. **An arguable central claim, positioned early.** For an empirical
   thesis the claim is the research question + why it is open. Status in
   this thesis: *met.* Section 1.1 opens on the stability question
   across reorganizations (not generic theory), and §1.2's four numbered
   problems + §1.2's closing RQ1–RQ3 give the "telegraph" Harvard asks
   for. The Chapter I preview paragraph (§1.1, final paragraph) states
   the result before the reader commits — this is top-tier practice
   (the reader should know the destination up front).
2. **Every background paragraph earns its place by constraining the
   design.** Harvard's test for body paragraphs: topic sentence,
   evidence, analysis. Status: *met with one watch item.* §1.1's NEPSE
   history and the consolidation narrative each feed a design choice
   (the MPS_RD window; the 2023 divide; the five series). Watch item:
   paragraph 2 of §1.1 carries history (1976/1993/1994) before the
   puzzle — acceptable, but keep it compressed so the arguable claim
   stays visible in the first two pages.
3. **The problem statement must end in research questions.** Status:
   *met* — RQ1–RQ3 close §1.2, each traceable to one of the four numbered
   problems, and each maps to a model or robustness specification.
4. **Objectives use performable action verbs.** Status: *met* — estimate,
   test, evaluate (guideline-conformant and verb-verified).
5. **Hypotheses are stated so that Chapter V can answer them by name.**
   Status: *met* — H1–H4 with H0/Ha, two-tailed at α = 0.05, and Table 11
   in Chapter V returns explicit decisions. The H1–H4 lifecycle is closed.

Watch items (content-level, from the standards, not yet acted on):
- §1.5 rationale point 3 uses "catastrophic multicollinearity" —
  emotive adjective; the standard (see file 02) is proportionate
  language. Candidate: "severe multicollinearity".
- The signposting sentence opening §1.7 ("This dissertation is organized
  into five chapters") should match the final chapter/section names
  exactly if any chapter title changes.

## Chapter II — Literature review: synthesis, not an annotated list

The single most common examiner complaint about master's literature
chapters is source-by-source summary (FIU; TAMU-CC; U Oregon).

1. **Organize around ideas, not sources.** Status: *largely met.* §2.2 is
   organized by theory (RIV → M&A dynamics → dividend models →
   signaling/agency → EMH → circularity), not by author; §2.4.5's four
   thematic debates are genuine synthesis; §2.5's five gaps are claims
   with cited evidence, which is what "establish the gap with evidence"
   means in practice.
2. **One claim per paragraph, multiple studies per claim.** Status:
   *met in the synthesis sections; partially met in the empirical
   sections.* §2.4.1–2.4.4 are intentionally study-by-study profiles
   (one paragraph per study) — this is defensible as the evidence base
   that the synthesis sections then organize, PROVIDED each profile
   paragraph states the study's finding relative to the debate, not just
   its result. The profiles that already do this: Bhattarai (ends on the
   circularity point), Gurung & Dahal (ends on the static-pooling point).
   The ones to watch: Niroula, Gyawali, Joshi — ending on findings only.
3. **The synthesis matrix is itself a synthesis tool.** Table 2.1 now
   carries a Methodological Critique column — this is the FIU synthesis
   matrix idea embedded in the thesis. Keep it honest: a dash means "no
   additional issue identified", not "no issues exist".
4. **Close the loop to the design.** §2.5's five gaps each name the
   design element that answers them, and the closing paragraph maps
   Ohlson + Kwon, MPS_RD, P/E exclusion, and the R1–R8 suite onto the
   gaps. Status: *met.*

## Chapter III — Methodology: justify, don't describe

Top-tier methodology sections defend choices against alternatives
rather than narrating procedures.

1. **Every design choice names its alternative and the reason for
   rejection.** Status: *strongly met.* §3.2 names observational/
   non-experimental and disclaims causality; §3.3.2 defends purposive
   against probabilistic sampling (with the 25.0% count-proportion
   non-claim); §3.3.4's freshness rule states the imputation alternative
   and rejects it; §3.6.3 states why HC3 over cluster-robust SEs (G = 5
   bias); §3.5.3 excludes P/E on algebraic grounds. This
   choice-alternative-rejection pattern is the methodology gold standard.
2. **Disclose limits in the chapter where the choice is made, not only
   in Chapter I.** Status: *met* — the aggregator/NEPSE-unverified
   disclosure appears in §3.4.1/§3.4.3 where the price source is chosen.
3. **Reproducibility.** The own-current rule, the 5-day rule, the
   parameter counts (K = 12/14), and the df arithmetic (16/14) are all
   stated explicitly — an independent researcher can re-derive every
   number. This is the Wilkinson/TFSI replication standard (see file
   03). Status: *met.*

## Chapter IV — Results and Discussion: analysis, then interpretation

1. **Results report; Discussion interprets.** The distinction from
   Guelph's funnel and PLOS's guidance: Results answer "what did the
   analysis produce"; Discussion answers "what does it mean and why does
   it matter". Status: *met structurally* (§4.2 vs §4.3) — and §4.3.2's
   governing sentence ("interpreted not as proven causal effects, but as
   suggestive structural tendencies") is exactly the interpretation
   discipline the sources ask for.
2. **Open the Discussion with the principal findings in plain terms.**
   Status: *met* — §4.3.1 opens with the pre-consolidation regime
   statement and the earnings-vs-book magnitudes before any mechanism.
3. **Compare with prior literature explicitly, agreement AND
   disagreement.** Status: *met* — §4.3.3 states where the result
   resonates with Kwon & Wang and where it diverges (the non-anchoring
   of book value) with a banking-specific reason; §4.3.4 lists the
   methodological sources of divergence from prior Nepalese studies.
4. **Limitations inside the Discussion, not only in Chapter I/III.**
   Status: *met* — small-sample df and HC3 conservativeness are stated
   at the point of interpretation (§4.3.1 opening), and §4.3.5 handles
   the institutional limits.
5. **Every inferential statement carries its statistic, df, and exact
   p.** Status: *met* throughout §4.2 (see file 03 for the standard and
   the few places where rounding choices are watch items).

## Chapter V — Summary, Conclusion, Implications: the contribution chapter

Pat Thomson's examiner observation: examiners often read this chapter
first, hunting for the stated contribution. It must therefore stand
alone.

1. **Summarize to answer the research questions, not to inventory the
   thesis.** Status: *met* — §5.2.1 restates RQ1–RQ3, §5.2.3's findings
   are numbered claims, and §5.3.1 answers each RQ directly by number.
   Harvard's "conclusions are not exhaustive summary" rule is satisfied
   because the summary is organized around questions and findings, not
   chapter recitation.
2. **Conclusions draw ONLY from the findings.** Status: *met* — every
   conclusion sentence in §5.3 carries its estimate or test; the
   sensitivity-dependent framing ("rather than conclusive in the
   baseline") prevents the conclusions outrunning the evidence.
3. **Implications must be actionable and specific to the findings.**
   Status: *met* — §5.4's items cite the specific result that grounds
   each recommendation (DPS collapse → dividend guidelines; 54-day halt
   → trading-continuity rules; R6 sensitivity → entity-boundary timing).
4. **Decisions on hypotheses by name.** Status: *met* — Table 11 and
   §5.3.6 decide H1–H4, the joint test, and the R6 sensitivity row
   explicitly.

## Cross-chapter standards (the examiners' structural checks)

From Golding et al. (2014) and Man (2020), the structural checks
examiners perform — and this thesis's status:

1. **Consistency of key numbers across chapters.** Examiners sample a
   number in Chapter I and hunt for it in IV and V. Status: *verified* —
   the phase reviews recomputed every headline number; the preview
   p-values in Chapter I match Chapter IV exactly (this was the one
   historical failure, fixed in commit 7767752 and re-verified).
2. **Consistency of terminology.** One concept, one name: MPS^RD,
   consolidation wave, entity-timing mismatch, eight-specification suite
   (R1–R2 baselines plus six robustness variants R3–R8). Status: *met*
   after the suite-naming fixes; re-grep before submission.
3. **No claim strengthening between chapters.** A later chapter may not
   upgrade an earlier claim ("verified stability" vs R6 was the
   historical case, fixed). Status: *met*; keep the rule in file 02 when
   editing.
4. **The contribution is stated, not implied.** The thesis's contribution
   is: first regime-interaction evaluation of EPS/BVPS value relevance
   across Nepal's 2023 consolidation wave, with a reproducible
   post-deadline price protocol. It appears in §1.1, §2.5, and §5.3 —
   all three should use the same formulation. Status: *met*; verify the
   wording stays aligned in any edit.
