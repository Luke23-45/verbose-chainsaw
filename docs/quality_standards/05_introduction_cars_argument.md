# 05 — The introduction as a research space: Swales' CARS, applied

Primary sources: Swales, *Genre Analysis* (1990) and Swales & Feak,
*Academic Writing for Graduate Students* — the **CARS model** (Create a
Research Space), the standard rhetorical analysis of research
introductions; Harvard College Writing Center, "Thesis"
(https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/thesis): "Your thesis is the
central claim in your essay—your main insight or idea about your source
or topic. Your thesis should appear early in an academic essay, followed
by a logically constructed argument supporting the central claim."

---

## The three moves

| Move | Function | Typical steps |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Establishing a territory** | Show the topic matters and what is known | (a) claim centrality; (b) review previous research |
| **2. Establishing a niche** | Show what is missing or contested | (a) counter-claim; (b) indicate a gap; (c) raise a question |
| **3. Occupying the niche** | State what THIS work does | (a) outline purposes; (b) announce findings; (c) indicate structure |

Swales' diagnostic: a weak introduction does Move 1 at length, does
Move 2 in one underlined sentence, and rushes Move 3. A strong one keeps
the proportions roughly balanced and makes the niche the pivot of the
whole chapter.

## Chapter I, audited move by move

**Move 1 (establishing the territory) — §1.1 and §1.2 paragraphs 1–2.**
The thesis claims centrality concretely ("A central question in
financial economics and accounting valuation is whether the empirical
relationship ... remains stable across structural corporate
reorganizations") and reviews the territory through the value-relevance
literature and the Nepalese institutional history. This is textbook
Move 1(a) + 1(b). The strength: every piece of territory reviewed is
load-bearing for the design (NEPSE history → the 2023 divide; capital
mandate → bonus/dilution → R7; BAFIA → MPS_RD).

**Move 2 (establishing the niche) — §1.2's four numbered problems.**
The thesis does not merely "indicate a gap"; it builds the gap from four
independent arguments: (1) no regime-shift analysis, (2) P/E circularity
with the algebra shown, (3) look-ahead bias in price timing, (4)
small-cluster fragility. This is stronger than the typical single-sentence
niche. The niche is then restated as research questions — RQ1–RQ3 — so
the gap is operational, not rhetorical.

**Move 3 (occupying the niche) — §1.3–§1.7.** Purposes (objectives),
announcement of principal findings (the preview paragraph — Swales' step
3(b) "announcing principal findings" is rare in student theses and is
present here, with correct HC3 values), and structure (§1.7). All three
steps present.

**Verdict: Chapter I is a complete CARS introduction.** The proportion
watch item from file 01 stands: Move 1 runs long (institutional history),
so keep §1.1 paragraph 2 compressed on any revision pass.

## The Harvard thesis-statement test applied

Harvard: a thesis should be *arguable* and should "telegraph" the
argument. For an empirical thesis the arguable claim is:

> Whether the value-relevance of EPS and BVPS shifted across Nepal's
> 2023 banking consolidation wave, measured with a reporting-deadline
> price protocol — and the answer is sensitivity-dependent.

Three telegraph tests, all currently passing:

1. **The preview paragraph (§1.1 final) states the result** — positive
   but insignificant baseline slopes, 10% joint shift, 5% under R6. An
   examiner reading only page 1 knows the destination. (Harvard: the
   thesis "telegraphs" the argument's shape.)
2. **§1.7 announces the structure** — the reader knows where each piece
   of evidence lives.
3. **The objectives are verbs, not topics** ("to estimate", "to test",
   "to evaluate") — objectives that say "to know" or "to understand"
   telegraph nothing. Status: conformant.

## The niche-statement discipline (guard this during edits)

The niche is the thesis's most scrutinized claim. Three rules:

1. **Scope the negative claim to what was reviewed.** The fixed
   formulation is in place: "No empirical study identified in the
   literature reviewed in Chapter II has formally evaluated..." — never
   write "no study has ever" or "no research exists".
2. **Each niche argument carries its own evidence.** Problem 2 proves
   circularity algebraically (P/E = MPS/EPS substituted into the
   regression); problem 4 proves the df arithmetic (28 − 14 = 14) and
   cites the cluster-SE literature. An examiner cannot dismiss these as
   opinion.
3. **The niche must survive the counter-argument.** The obvious
   counter-claim — "Gurung & Dahal (2025) already use fixed effects, so
   the gap is small" — is pre-answered in §2.4.4 and §2.5: their design
   is bank-FE but static full-sample, with no interaction test. This
   counter-claim/answer pair should stay explicit; it is the strongest
   single defense of the niche.

## What would break the introduction (do not do in edits)

- Moving the preview paragraph later or removing its numbers ("results
  shown in Chapter IV" instead of the actual estimates).
- Adding new territory (e.g., a global banking-crisis digression) that
  constrains nothing in the design.
- Collapsing the four problems into prose paragraphs — the numbered list
  is the niche's architecture.
- Replacing "consolidation wave" with inconsistent synonyms (the
  terminology consistency rule in file 01).
