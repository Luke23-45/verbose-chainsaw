- **Location:** `src/thesis/chapters/chapter_01_introduction.tex`, line 30 (Problem Statement) vs line 13 (Background) and line 171 (Limitations).

### A-05 — The abstract claims more than Chapter I supports ("resolves"; unqualified look-ahead-bias reduction)
- **Location:** `src/thesis/front/abstract.tex`, line 15 vs `src/thesis/chapters/chapter_01_introduction.tex`, lines 15, 148, 185–186.
- **Severity:** Major. **Revision scale:** Narrow. **Status:** VERIFIED (read both files; compared wording).
- **Evidence (two parts):**
  1. Abstract: *"the study resolves methodological limitations in prior Nepalese literature by eliminating circular price-to-earnings (P/E) regressors, reducing look-ahead bias via on-or-after-deadline prices under Section 59(2) of BAFIA…"*. Chapter I line 15 says only *"This dissertation directly addresses these theoretical, institutional, and empirical gaps"* — "addresses", not "resolves" — and the word "resolves" occurs nowhere in Chapter I.   2. Look-ahead bias: Chapter I itself qualifies the claim twice. ### A-06 — Chapter I Limitations omit the estimator-dependence of the headline conclusion
- **Location:** `src/thesis/chapters/chapter_01_introduction.tex`, lines 164–187 (Limitations) vs `src/thesis/front/abstract.tex`, line 17 and `src/thesis/chapters/chapter_05_summary_and_conclusion.tex`, lines 100–101.

- **Evidence:** the abstract states the joint-test result is *"a result specific to that estimator"*, and Chapter V (§5.2, Summary of Major Empirical Findings) states: *"The same test rejects at 5% under the classical, HC0, HC1 and HC2 covariances for all eight specifications R2–R9…, so the baseline non-rejection is specific to the HC3 estimator."* Chapter I's Limitations discuss finite-sample fragility honestly (line 177: *"HC3 adjusts the estimated covariance for heteroskedasticity and leverage; it does not make small-sample inference automatically reliable. ## 7. Argument, purpose, and validity/reliability (assessment)

- **Can a reader tell what the author set out to do and why?** Yes. The general objective (line 52), three research questions (lines 44–47), four hypotheses plus joint test (lines 79–136), and the four-gap problem statement (lines 21–41) form a coherent chain: prior Nepalese work is static, circular (P/E), mistimed (Ashadh-end prices), and silent on degrees of freedom — so test EPS/BVPS relevance before/after the 2023 wave with a deadline-based price, no price-derived regressors, and HC3 inference. This is the chapter's central strength. The chain breaks only at A-02 (RQ3 narrower than Objective 3) and A-01 (unexplained sampling exclusion).
- **Is the approach justified for a reader who does not share the author's methodological preferences?** Largely yes, with one exception. The chapter justifies two-way fixed effects, HC3, and the MPS^RD window in terms a sceptic can follow (the df arithmetic at lines 39/177; the P/E algebra at lines 35–36; the BAFIA deadline logic at lines 37–38). But the pre-specification of HC3 — the choice on which the headline conclusion turns — is asserted ("pre-specified HC3 covariance", line 17) rather than justified in Chapter I: no reason is given here for preferring HC3 over the classical/HC0/HC1/HC2 estimators that would reverse the joint test (A-06). A sceptical reader is entitled to ask why HC3, and Chapter I does not answer.
- **Validity/reliability handling.** Chapter I handles *measurement validity* (temporal alignment via the statutory deadline; exclusion of circular regressors) and *statistical-conclusion validity* (df accounting, small-cluster warning, exploratory interpretation) explicitly and well. It handles *data reliability* only implicitly: audited statements are described as "complete for all 30 target bank-year observations" (line 174) and sources (NRB, SEBON, NEPSE, bank reports, aggregators) are named in the Acknowledgements, but Chapter I contains no discussion of source reliability, cross-checking between sources, or price reliability beyond the 5-day freshness rule (thin trading outside suspensions is never mentioned). *External validity* is handled honestly (line 171, no generalisation). *Internal/causal validity* is handled honestly (lines 179–181: calendar regimes, no causal identification of merger effects). The net position is unusually candid for a master's introduction; the gaps are the missing estimator-dependence (A-06), power (A-07), and the 2-of-30 publication-date qualification's absence from the abstract (A-05).
