## 1. Method

1. Read `chapter_02_literature_review.tex` in full (lines 1–408) and `references.bib` in full (72 entries).
2. Counted sections, citations, and Table 2.1 rows by script (regex over the `.tex` source); hand-verified the counts that matter (the "fifteen studies" claim, the 19-study P/E list) by manual enumeration.

## 4. Coverage, synthesis, and framework — assessment

**(a) Coverage and currency.** Cited works in Chapter II span 1959 (Gordon) to 2026 (Timalsina; Niraula; Bhandari; Acharya). The Nepalese empirical corpus is 2003–2026 and includes work published in 2026, so the review is current for its Nepalese core. No post-2020 international value-relevance study is cited, and the field's review literature is absent (B-12). That is a gap in framing, not in the Nepalese evidence base, which is the chapter's actual subject. No obviously missing Nepalese study was identified: the chapter covers the published Nepalese MPS literature from Pradhan (2003) through the 2024–2026 wave, including the two merger-performance studies (Adhikari et al. 2023, both) and the 2026 merger study (Acharya et al. 2026).

**(b) Critical synthesis vs list of summaries.** Synthesis is present and genuine. §2.4.5 ("Synthesis of Empirical Literature", L285–303) does four things a list cannot: it counts the evidence ("Among the fifteen studies in Table 2.1 that test both EPS and BVPS…" — verified, see §5); it names the poles of the disagreement (Niroula 2021 vs Silwal 2019 vs Giri 2024; Joshi/Chhetri/Gurung finding both significant); it disqualifies studies from the comparison on stated grounds (Bhandari 2026 and Chaudhari 2025 did not include BVPS; Silwal 2019 did not test EPS); and it offers an institutional-temporal interpretation (2015 capital mandate → balance-sheet anchoring; stabilisation → earnings; 2023 wave → balance-sheet again).
