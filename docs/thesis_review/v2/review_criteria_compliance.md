These are the only outcome-adjacent requirements found. **Status: VERIFIED** (full-text read + targeted search).

**Proxy used (labelled as such, never as TU's rules).** Because the governing document supplies format and structure but no examiner criteria or outcome categories, I opened a published set of examiner outcome rules from a comparable institution for the same level and thesis type: University of Essex, *Principal Regulations for Research Degrees 2025–26* (https://www.essex.ac.uk/-/media/documents/about/governance/principal-regulations-research-degrees.pdf — opened in full via browser, read pp. 3, 8, 12–14). Essex confers "Masters by Dissertation (MA or MSc)" and states "'Thesis' includes dissertations submitted for the degree of Masters by Dissertation" (reg. 4.1, fn. 1). Its outcome categories (reg. 4.33) are: (a) pass with no corrections; (b) pass subject to typographical/presentational corrections (usually within four weeks); (c) pass subject to **minor corrections** within three months — "Minor corrections should not require the candidate to conduct further research or undertake substantial further work. These include typographical errors, clarifying points, rephrasing, editing/adding paragraphs, correcting references, etc."; (d) pass subject to **major corrections** within six months — "Major corrections require the candidate to undertake substantial further work … include more extensive editorial revisions, the addition of substantial new material, re-writing of substantial parts of the thesis, re-analysis of existing data, etc."; (e) referral for re-examination (up to 12 months); (k) fail. Essex also caps the abstract/summary at 300 words (reg. 4.19). I use only the **minor/major distinction** from Essex as a yardstick for revision scale in §6–§8, and I label every such use "proxy (Essex)". TU FOM's own process (viva + 25/75 scoring, no published correction categories) is different, so no Essex outcome is attributed to TU.
