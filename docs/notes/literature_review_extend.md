## 4. Methodological Depth: Why the Four Selected Fundamentals Form a Complete Closed System

1. **Earnings Per Share (EPS) — Flow of Profitability**: Represents the net residual return generated per unit of equity. Under Ohlson (1995), current earnings are the anchor for future abnormal earnings expectations.
2. **Book Value Per Share (BVPS) — Stock of Capital & Net Asset Backing**: Provides the liquidation and capital floor. In commercial banks governed by strict capital adequacy standards (NRB Basel III), book equity directly determines risk-weighted asset expansion capacity.
3. **Dividend Per Share (DPS) — Costly Liquidity & Solvency Signal**: Under asymmetric information (Bhattacharya, 1979; John & Williams, 1985), reported dividends represent cash that cannot be fabricated by accounting discretion, resolving shareholder agency friction (Jensen, 1986).
4. **Return on Assets (ROA) — Asset Management & Intermediation Efficiency**: Net income scaled by total assets. Commercial banks operate with extreme balance-sheet leverage (~10:1 to 12:1 asset-to-equity ratio); ROA captures how effectively management deploys interest-bearing deposits and loan portfolios before leverage multiplies it into equity returns.
5. **Formal Econometric Defense for Excluding P/E**:
   Earlier empirical literature (e.g., Bhattarai 2014; Giri 2024; Chhetri 2023; Timalsina 2026) regressed MPS on P/E alongside EPS. Because $P/E \equiv MPS / EPS$, regressing $MPS$ on $MPS/EPS$ embeds the dependent variable on the right-hand side of the equation. This creates:
   - Mechanical endogeneity and high multicollinearity.
   - Artificially inflated $R^2$ statistics.
   - Spurious regression coefficients that obscure the true fundamental pricing power of accounting disclosures.
   This study eliminates $P/E$ to maintain strict regressor independence.
