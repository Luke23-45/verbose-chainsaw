1. Background of the study

Modern banking is an essential industry that operates within the periphery of the national
as well as the international financial system. Financial systems allow funds to be
allocated, invested, or moved between economic sectors. These are the core subjects of
the credit performances and that activity involves credit risk to the financial institutions.
Therefore, the health of national as well as global economy largely depends on a highly
regulated financial system. Banking as a vital section of such system, Rose (2002)
mentioned that it is one of the most heavily regulated businesses in the world.

Commonly, it can be argued that commercial banks are among the most important
financial institution in the economy. Many authors use to argue that no institution has
shaped the economic development of the world more than the bank. The general role of
commercial banks is to provide financial services to general public, business, and
companies, ensuring economic and social stability and sustainable growth of the
economy. Therefore, bankers must have to work within the financial system to supply
loans, accept deposits, and provide many other financial services to their customers.
They must do so within a climate of extensive regulation, design primarily to protect the
public interest (Rose, 2002). Regulation of banks aims at increasing the safety of the
banking sector. One of the most important instruments is a capital adequacy regulation
which relates a bank’s equity to its exposure to risk on the asset side of the balance sheet.

The primary function of a commercial bank is the credit management. The activities
associated with lending by the commercial banks relate to their credit performance. The
Management of credit performance is not an easy job. There are incredible risks
associated with the credit performance of commercial banks. Such risk is popular as
“Credit Risk” in the banking industry. It is a situation of unexpected changes in the credit
quality of counterparty. It is by far the most significant risk faced by banks and the
success of their business depends on accurate measurement and efficient management of
this risk. It is a risk of financial loss if a borrower or counterparty fails to honor
commitments under an agreement and any such failure has an adverse effect on the
financial performance of the bank. The capital adequacy is important to strengthen
solvency of the banks and their protection from the adverse events. These frames of
references are widely researched issues in the academia.
2. Statement of the problem

Different researchers have examined the impact of credit performances and related risks
of the commercial banks on their return in varying dimensions with the sample from
different country context. It is apparent that there are different measures of credit
performances as well as banks return or profitability. Accordingly, the use of variables in
the available work addressing credit performances and banks’ profitability is also not
consistent. As a result, there is a scant of unanimity in findings.

For instance, Kithinji (2010) found the bulk of the profits of commercial banks are not
influenced by the credit risk, suggesting that additional variables other than the credit
risk related variables impact on profits. On the other hand, based on the data from 2010
to 2015 of the selected sample commercial banks in Nepal, Bhattarai (2016) concluded
that there is a significant relationship between bank performance and credit risk
indicators. These are some of the examples of contrasting results that are available in the
academia.

Furthermore, there is no evidence of study by taking the recent data, specifically related
to the credit performances, of privately owned Nepalese commercial banks. Such a
situation is the main reason of motivation for this study and basis of the identification of
the problem. Hence, this study focuses to fill a gap, examining the impact of credit
performances and their impact on the return of privately owned commercial banks in
Nepal. Accordingly, following will be the specific research questions of this study.

    I.   What is the position of credit performance in the privately owned commercial
         banks operating in Nepal?
   II.   What is the existing status of profitability among the privately owned
         commercial banks of Nepal?
  III.   Does credit performance really matter to the commercial banks’ profitability?


3. Objectives of the study

The main objective of this study will be to investigate the impact of credit performance
on the return of commercial banks in Nepal. Accordingly, the specific objectives will be
as follows:
    I.    To assess the credit performance of the privately owned commercial banks
          operating in Nepal.
   II.    To examine the status of profitability that privately owned commercial banks of
          Nepal are enjoying as a return.
  III.    To analyze the impact of credit performance factors, like, credit-deposit ratio
          (CDR), non-performing loan (NPL), and capital adequacy ratio (CAR) on the
          profitability of the commercial banks of Nepal.


4. Hypotheses

Based on the preliminary survey of literature on the subject, this study will be oriented
toward testing the hypothesis as mentioned below:

H1: Credit deposit ratio has a significant and positive effect on profitability of
commercial banks.

H2: Non-performing loan ratio has a significant and negative effect on profitability of
commercial banks.

H1: Capital adequacy ratio has a significant and positive effect on profitability of
commercial banks.

6. Review of Literature

Tarbert (2001) had discussed in wider perspective about capital adequacy and Basel
Capital Accord and stated that adequate capital is a necessary condition of solvent banks
and solvent banks are fundamental to the world economy. Adequate bank capital is thus
essential for a sound economy. Bank capital is a vital component of economic stability; it
has become a subject of concern for financial regulatory bodies around the world.

As stated by Rose (2002), banks are financial service firms, producing and selling
professional management of the public funds and performing many other roles in the
economy. Basically, they try to maximize profits through loans and advances from the
deposits. These activities are popularly known as bank credit. Commercial banks create
credit by advancing loans and purchasing securities. However, commercial banks cannot
use the entire amount of public deposits for lending purposes. They are required to keep
a certain amount as reserve with the central bank. This is commonly known as cash
reserve ratio (CRR). The higher the cash reserve ratio, the lesser is the amount of money
available to banks for lending and investing activities.

On the other hand, the capital adequacy ratio (CAR) sets standards for banks by looking
at a bank’s ability to pay liabilities, and respond to credit risks and operational risks.
Capital adequacy plays an important role in determining banking activities. There are
many empirical evidences on the subject of the relationship between capital requirements
and bank credit performances. Capital adequacy plays an important role in reducing the
number of banks failures and losses to depositors. It plays a crucial role in reducing
different risk components in the banking industry, and it is necessary to reduce a moral
hazard and competitiveness.

Rose (2002) mentioned that credit risk is the danger of default by borrower to whom a
bank has extended credit. Therefore, among others, non performing loan ratio and credit
to deposit ratio are widely used indicators of bank credit risk. Bank capital and risk are
intimately related each other. The main sources of credit risk include limited institutional
capacity, inappropriate credit policies, volatile interest rates, low capital and liquidity
levels, massive licensing of banks, poor credit assessment.

Using the ROA and the ROE as proxies for bank profitability for the period of 1998 to
2007 from Kenyan banks, Mathuva (2009) revealed that bank profitability is positively
related to the core capital ratio and the tier 1 risk-based capital ratio. This implies that an
increase in the capital may raise expected earnings by reducing the expected costs of
financial distress, including bankruptcy. While on the other hand, the study also found
that there exist a negative relationship between the equity capital ratio and profitability.
Therefore, the most important finding of Mathuva (2009) was that there were different
effects of the various measures of capital adequacy on the profitability of the bank.

Bhattarai (2016) examined the effect of credit risk on the performance of Nepalese
Commercial Banks using pooled data of 14 commercial banks for the period of 2010 to
2015. The author found that non-performing loan ratio has a negative effect on bank
performance, whereas the cost per asset has a positive effect on bank performance.
Moreover, the author concluded that there is a significant relationship between bank
performance and credit risk indicators.

Pradhan and Shrestha (2017) examined the impact of capital adequacy and bank
operating efficiency of the financial performance of Nepalese commercial banks using
data from the period of 2005/06 to 2012/13. The result showed that total deposits to total
asset and banks operating efficiency was the major variables determinant of financial
performance of commercial banks in Nepal. Similarly, bank operating efficiency, loan
ratio, total deposit to total assets, loan loss provision to total equity have a significantly
positive impact on financial performance of commercial banks. Loan loss provisions to
total loan, core capital ratio, risk-weighted ratio, total capital ratio have negative impact
on financial performance of Nepalese commercial banks.

In the study, about the effect of capital adequacy and cost income ratio on the
performance of Nepalese commercial banks by Pradhan and Parajuli (2017) found
evidence for a positive relationship of bank size with ROA. It means, larger the banks,
higher would be the ROA. Similarly, the study revealed that there was a positive
relationship of debt to equity ratio with ROA. On the other hand, the study observed that
there was a negative relationship of capital adequacy, cost income ratio, equity capital to
total assets ratio and liquidity ratio with ROA. This means that the higher the capital
adequacy lower would be ROA. Similarly, the study observed that the higher the equity
capital to total assets, lower would be the ROA. The result also showed that there is a
positive relationship of capital adequacy, equity capital to total assets, bank size and debt
to equity ratio with ROE. The study also indicated that higher the equity capital to assets,
higher would be return on equity. Similarly, the study found that larger the bank, higher
would be the ROE.

The aforesaid review represents only a preliminary survey of the relevant issue. On the
basis of a review, it can be concluded that still there are some unsolved research issues
on the proposed subject. Moreover, there is a scant of studies based on recent data. As
many researchers emphasized the effects of credit performances relating the credit risk in
different country context or with different variables. Hence, there exists a research gap
and this study focuses to contribute to the existing knowledge in the subject based on the
recent data. However, the previous studies cannot be ignored because they provide the
foundation for the present study.

7. Research Methodology

The research methodology is a way to solve the research problem systematically and to
fulfill the research objectives accordingly. This study plans the following methodological
aspects.

7.1 Research design
A research design is the logical and systematic planning that specifies the procedures for
collecting and analyzing data and information. To attain the purpose of this study,
descriptive research design will be considered as an appropriate one. On the other hand,
causal-comparative research method will be followed. The overall study plan will based
on the quantitative approach of research.

7.2 Population and sampling procedure
This study will be based on the data collected from the annual reports of the sample
commercial banks. The population of the study will be the “A” class commercial banks
licensed by Nepal Rastra Bank. It is at present twenty-seven. Whereas, the study focuses
on the purely private-sector commercial banks listed in the NEPSE. Presently, it is
twenty-four, and the sampling frame will be considered accordingly. Based on the non-
random purposive sampling, twenty banks will be selected as a sample to collect data
from the fiscal year 2015/016 to 2019/020 for each. The rationale behind the purposive
sampling is to assure the heterogeneity in the sample.

7.3 Nature and sources of data collection
To fulfill the research objectives, most of the data will be collected from the secondary
sources. The required data, as demanded by the study, will be collected through the
published annual reports of the sample banks. On the other hand, some supporting
information has been collected from the website of Nepal Rastra Bank and other official
websites. The negligible information will be acquired from the primary sources.

The data will include time-series and cross-sectional data, i.e. pooled data set. The 5-
years data (fiscal year 2072/2073 to 2076/2077) of sample banks will be considered, as a
balanced panel data, for the study. Therefore, the total number of observation for the
study will be one hundred.

Dependent variables
The important aspect of the study will be to analyze an impact of credit performance on
the profitability. Therefore, dependent variables will be the proxies of profitability.
Among the different aspects of profitability, as proxies, earnings per share and return on
assets will be considered.

Return on assets (ROA): Return on assets is an indicator of how profitable a company
is relative to its total assets. It gives a manager, investor, or analyst an idea as to how
efficient a company's management is using its assets to generate earnings. Thus, ROA is
primarily an indicator of managerial efficiency.

Earnings per share (EPS): Earnings per share (EPS) are a company's net profit divided
by the number of common shares it has outstanding. It indicates how much money a
company makes for each share of its stock.

Independent variables
Credit deposit ratio (CDR): It is also known as the loan-to-deposit ratio. It is used to
assess a bank's credit risk as well as liquidity by comparing a bank's total loan to its total
deposits for the same period.

Non-performing loan ratio (NPLR): A nonperforming loan ratio is the ratio of the
amount of nonperforming loans in a bank's loan portfolio to the total amount of
outstanding loans the bank holds. A nonperforming loan is a loan in which the borrower
is the default and hasn't made any scheduled payments of principal or interest for some
time. Any definition of a nonperforming loan will depend on the loan's particular terms
and agreement.

Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR): Capital adequacy ratios are a measure of the amount
of a bank's capital expressed as a percentage of its risk weighted credit exposures. In
other words, it is the ratio of a bank’s capital in relation to its risk weighted assets and
current liabilities.

7.5 Methods of analysis
To analyze the data of the variables, descriptive statistics will be used. Under the
descriptive statistics, the central tendency will be measure through arithmetic mean. To
understand the consistency, standard deviation and coefficient of variation will be used.
Similarly, to observe the data nature in terms of normality, skewness and kurtosis will be
considered. In addition, to analyze the phenomena, statistical graphics will also use as
per the data nature. Correlation coefficients between the variables will also examine
through the correlation matrix.

Similarly, to draw the statistical inferences multiple regressions will be applied. It is
because; this study intends to draw the inferences about the effects of credit performance
on the return of the privately owned commercial banks in Nepal. All the statistical works
will be performed through Microsoft Excel and SPSS version 20.0.

Models
The study will intend toward analyzing the effect of credit performances on profitability
of the selected group of commercial banks in Nepal. Therefore, to analyze the impact and
basic relationship functions, the regression equation will base on the model specified as:

Y = β0 + β Xit+εit

Where Y represents the dependent variable; β0 is the constant; β represents the
coefficient of explanatory variables; Xit is the vector of explanatory variables, and εit is
the error term. Adapting the basic model, following equations will be estimated.

EPSit = β0 + β1CDRit + β2NPLit + β3CARit + εit
ROAit = β0 + β1CDRit + β2NPLit + β3CARit + εit

Where,
EPSit represents earning per share of bank i in year t;
ROAit represents Return on assets of bank i in year t;
CDRit represents credit deposit ratio of bank i in year t;
NPLit represents non performing loan of bank i in year t;
CARit represents capital adequacy ratio of bank i in year t;
β0 is the intercept (constant); β1, β2, and β3 represent the corresponding slope which
addresses the impact coefficients and εit represents the error term..
