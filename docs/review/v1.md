## 3. Gaps in detail

### F-01 — Figure captions are above the figures (Major, VERIFIED)

**Rule.** "Figures must have numbers and captions under the figures. Tables have their titles and numbers above the tables. Figures have their titles and numbers below the figures." (Guideline p. 21; repeated in Appendix C, p. 28: "Give the title to the figure that is descriptive of the content of the figure. Put them below the figure.")

**Current thesis.** The document class sets captions above for every figure:

- `config/preamble.tex:74` — `\captionsetup[figure]{labelfont=it,textfont=normalfont,position=above}`
- Every chapter also writes `\caption` *before* `\includegraphics`, e.g. `chapters/chapter_04_results_and_discussion.tex:86–91` (Figure 2), `chapters/chapter_03_research_methodology.tex:179–181` (Figure 1), `appendices/appendix_a.tex:90–95` (Figure 9) and `appendices/appendix_c.tex:10–21` (Figures 10–11).

**Evidence in the compiled PDF.** On printed p. 68 (PDF 83) the caption block "Figure 2: Variable Trends…" sits at y = 76 pt and the plot occupies y = 101–357 pt, i.e. the caption is above the image. The same order holds on printed pp. 53, 70, 76, 83, 85, 116, 119 and 120 (captions at y = 76–470 pt, figure content starting 23–43 pt lower).

**Fix.** Remove `position=above` from the figure caption setup (or set `position=bottom`), and move each `\caption{…}\label{…}` pair after its `\includegraphics` in Chapters III–IV and Appendix C. The note blocks that currently follow the image stay below the image; only the caption moves.

### F-02 — Chapter III omits the instrument of data collection (Major, VERIFIED)

**Rule.** The chapter "includes (i) research design, (ii) population and sample, and sampling design, (iii) nature and sources of data, **and the instrument of data collection**, (iv) methods of analysis, and (v) research framework and definition of variables." (Guideline p. 13.) "Also specify the sources, methods and instruments of data collection." (same page)

**Current thesis.** `chapters/chapter_03_research_methodology.tex:135–172` is titled "Nature and Sources of Data, and Data Collection Procedure" and describes the two data streams, the sources, and an audit/extraction/reconciliation protocol. It never names or describes an instrument (for secondary data, the natural instrument is the structured extraction sheet / data register). The word "instrument" does not appear anywhere in Chapter III. Appendix D later prints a data register, but Chapter III does not present it as the study's instrument.

**Fix.** Add a short subsection to 3.4 that defines the instrument explicitly — e.g. a bank-year extraction matrix with one row per bank per fiscal year and fixed fields (EPS, equity, shares outstanding, DPS, closing price, trade date, source locator), cross-referenced to Appendix D (Table 14 register) — and state that audited annual reports and NEPSE closing prices were transcribed into it under the protocol already described.

### F-04 — Chapter II section order (Minor, VERIFIED)

### F-08 — Lower-cased proper nouns in the reference list (Major, VERIFIED)

**Rule.** "Faculty of Management follows the APA style of citation and referencing… check Publication Guidelines of the American Psychological Association, (sixth edition)." (Guideline pp. 12, 16.) APA 6 retains capitalisation of proper nouns in article titles.

**Current thesis.** `references.bib` stores titles in title case, and `apacite` sentence-cases them on output without protecting proper nouns, so the References pages print, among others:

- printed p. 109 — Adhikari, Kavanagh & Hampson (2023a), "…using **nepalese** data"; Almumani (2014), "…listed banks in **amman** stock exchange".
- printed p. 110 — Bhattarai (2014), "…share price of **nepalese** commercial banks"; Chhetri (2023), "…commercial banks in **nepal**".
- printed p. 111 — Gyawali (2022), "…stock price of **nepalese** commercial banks".
- printed p. 113 — Sharma (2011), "…equity share prices in **india**".

Count on printed pp. 109–113: **nepal** 9, **nepalese** 14, **amman** 1, **india** 1 = **25 occurrences** (URLs excluded; "Kathmandu" appears twice only as a publisher place and is correctly capitalised, so it is not counted).

**Fix.** Brace-protect the proper nouns in `references.bib`, e.g. `{Nepal}`, `{Nepalese}`, `{Amman}`, `{India}`, then rebuild. apacite keeps braced words verbatim in its sentence-casing pass.

### F-10 — Table notes do not expand every abbreviation in the table (Minor, VERIFIED)

**Rule.** "Notes, if any, should be provided below the table. If abbreviations have been used in the table, they must be explained below the table." (Guideline p. 28.)

**Current thesis.** Table 1 (Chapter II, pp. 37–40) is exemplary: its note defines MPS, EPS, BVPS, DPS, ROA, ROE, DPR, DY, P/E, OLS, RIV, M&A, BSE, EY and MTB (`chapter_02_literature_review.tex:378`). The later tables do not follow that standard:

- **Table 3** (descriptives, p. 66; `chapter_04_results_and_discussion.tex:37–71`) uses EPS, BVPS, DPS and ROA in the row labels; the note expands none of them (it expands only "NPR" and the MPS^RD timing rule).
- **Table 4** (correlations, p. 68; `:103–127`) uses EPS, BVPS, DPS and ROA; the note expands only MPS^RD (and P/E, which the table does not show).
- **Tables 5–10** and most **Appendix E** tables use EPS, BVPS and ROA (and, in places, HC3, LSDV, OLS and VIF) without defining those abbreviations in the table's own note; the text defines them earlier, but the guideline's requirement is attached to each table.

**Fix.** Either add a one-line "Abbreviations: EPS = …; BVPS = …; DPS = …; ROA = …" to each affected note, or adopt a short standard note block reused across the results and appendix tables. This is a small, mechanical change.

### F-11 — Heading size is 14.3 pt against a 12 pt body (Advisory, VERIFIED)

**Rule.** "Use uniform typeface and font size. Use Times New Roman, with 12-point font size." (Guideline p. 21.)

**Current thesis.** Body text is Times New Roman 12 pt (measured), but chapter, References and Appendix headings are 14.3 pt bold:

- `config/preamble.tex:94–101` (`\titleformat{\chapter}[display]{…\large\bfseries…}`) and `:309–313` for appendix headings.
- Measured on printed pp. 1, 17, 44, 109 and 114: "CHAPTER I/II/III", "REFERENCES", "APPENDIX A" at 14.3 pt, body at 12.0 pt.

**Judgement.** The 14 pt bold chapter heading is the FOM/college convention used by the reference dissertations, and the "uniform font" clause is normally read as governing the text face. No change recommended without supervisor direction; if a strict reading is enforced, switch `\large` to `\normalsize` in the two `\titleformat` blocks.

## 5. Judgement calls and non-gaps

1. **References spacing.** The guideline says both "Allow 1.5-line space after every line in the … references" and "Single spacing should be used only in … references" (p. 20). The thesis uses single spacing. This is one of two permitted readings, not a defect.
2. **14.3 pt headings (F-11).** Universally used in FOM dissertations; the "uniform 12-point" clause most plausibly governs body text. Listed for completeness only.
3. **Sans-serif figure text (F-12).** APA 6 explicitly expects sans serif inside figures, so the current output is the APA-conforming choice even though it is not Times New Roman.
4. **Extra Chapter I/V sections (F-06, F-07).** The guideline explicitly permits altering/adding sections "upon the advice" of the supervisor (p. 9). These are deviations to clear with the supervisor, not automatic defects.
5. **Front matter.** Deliberately not assessed: the instruction was to skip everything before Chapter I. Nothing in this file should be read as approving or condemning the title page, certification, approval sheet, acknowledgements, contents, lists, abbreviations or abstract.
