```
A share price represents the financial value at which a unit of ownership in a publicly traded firm is exchanged on an organized stock market. Equity valuation rests on expected cash distributions, balance-sheet net asset backing and earnings performance, while signaling theory holds that published earnings and dividends convey credible information on financial condition in markets with information asymmetry (Y. R. Bhattarai, 2014; Mosley & Singer, 2008).
```

Para 2:
```
Guided by these theoretical frameworks, this study examines four core firm-specific accounting fundamentals as explanatory variables of market price per share (MPS): earnings per share (EPS), dividend per share (DPS), book value per share (BVPS), and return on assets (ROA). While earlier empirical studies frequently incorporated the price-earnings (P/E) ratio as a regressor, P/E is mathematically constructed using the dependent variable itself (P/E = MPS/EPS). Including a regressor that embeds the dependent variable creates direct definitional dependence and is therefore inappropriate as an explanatory regressor when MPS is the dependent variable. Consequently, this study deliberately restricts its explanatory regressors to financial accounting measures that exist independently of secondary market price.
```

```
Earnings per share
```

Para (EPS):
```
Earnings per share (EPS) reflects the net profitability attributable to each ordinary equity share, calculated as profit attributable to ordinary equity holders divided by weighted average ordinary shares outstanding. As a direct indicator of earning power, EPS is theoretically expected to have a strong positive association with stock prices. This positive relationship is widely corroborated both in international equity markets (Almumani, 2014; Malhotra & Tandon, 2013) and across Nepalese commercial banking literature (Gyawali, 2022; Niroula, 2021). At the regulatory level, Bhattarai (2024) established that interest spread rates and capital adequacy enhance bank EPS, while loan loss provisions and broad money growth constrain it.
```
