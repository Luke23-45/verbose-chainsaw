Para 1:

```
The stock market plays a vital role in economic development by promoting capital formation and fostering sustainable economic growth. The trading of securities facilitates capital allocation between savers and investors through fund pooling, risk sharing, and wealth transfer (Mosley & Singer, 2008).
```

Para 2:
```
In Nepal, the organized stock market began with the conversion of the Securities Exchange Centre into Nepal Stock Exchange (NEPSE) Limited in 1993, with floor trading commencing in 1994. NEPSE remains the sole stock exchange in the country and has grown substantially in both market capitalization and the number of listed securities (Nepal Rastra Bank, 2023).
```

Para 3:
```
Returns from equity investments vary with firm performance and stock price movements driven by technical, fundamental, and market factors (Y. R. Bhattarai, 2014). Firm-specific accounting fundamentals, principally earnings per share (EPS), dividend per share (DPS), book value per share (BVPS), and return on assets (ROA), have been widely examined in relation to equity valuation both across emerging markets (Sharma, 2011) and within the Nepalese commercial banking sector (Y. R. Bhattarai, 2014; Pradhan & Dahal, 2016).
```

Para 4:
```
The Nepalese banking sector has undergone substantial structural transformation through the merger and acquisition wave of 2013–2020 and the major consolidation of early 2023: Global IME Bank absorbed Bank of Kathmandu, Kumari Bank absorbed Nepal Credit and Commerce Bank, Nepal Investment Bank merged with Mega Bank to form Nepal Investment Mega Bank, and Himalayan Bank acquired Civil Bank (Adhikari, Kavanagh, & Hampson, 2023b). Across the broader financial sector, 239 banking and financial institutions entered merger processes and 177 ceased to exist in their own names, with the commercial-bank count falling from 32 in 2014 to 20 by mid-2023. Sector-wide market price per share fell by roughly 46% in the periods following the mergers while earnings and dividend per share were statistically unchanged (Adhikari, Kavanagh, & Hampson, 2023a; Adhikari et al., 2023b). Most existing empirical studies on share prices rely on pre-merger datasets that do not reflect the restructured banking environment. Furthermore, conflicting findings on ROA and BVPS across recent studies underscore the need to re-examine the associations between accounting fundamentals and share prices across a study window spanning the period before, during, and after the major 2023 consolidation.
```
