# Rationale of the Study

## Draft

The rationale for this study rests on the absence of reliable, current evidence
on what determines bank share prices in Nepal, and on the practical decisions
that turn on that evidence.

For individual investors, share price is the only observable measure of the
return available on an equity holding, and it is the point at which an
investment decision is actually taken. If the firm-specific factors that
systematically co-move with share price cannot be identified, then judgements
about whether a bank is attractively valued rest on intuition rather than
analysis. This study offers investors an empirically grounded basis for the
comparison of banks within a sector they can observe directly.

For the banking sector, the analysis has a complementary use. Return on assets
and book value per share are standard measures of profitability and capital
adequacy. Examining how these relate to market valuation indicates whether the
market's pricing of bank equity tracks underlying operating performance or
instead reflects something else. The result is relevant to how individual banks
understand their own market position, and to NRB in considering the
transparency of bank-level performance reporting.

For policymakers and market institutions, the value is in the substitution of
current evidence for stale evidence. The principal prior study on this question
in the Nepali context drew on data ending several years ago. Since that period,
merger activity among commercial banks has altered which legal entities are
listed, and regulatory change has affected bank capital. Estimates derived from
the earlier period may no longer describe the sector. Producing an updated
estimate is a precondition for any policy discussion that relies on the earlier
figures, and it also identifies whether the inconsistencies found in the prior
literature persist under a common specification.

For the research literature, the study contributes updated evidence on a
question on which findings are documented as mixed and inconsistent. Resolving
even part of that inconsistency, or demonstrating that it persists under a
cleaner specification, is a modest but genuine addition. The specification
chosen for the present work is also stated more explicitly than in the earlier
attempt, which makes the results interpretable and comparable with future work.

Finally, the study provides a reference point for subsequent research. It
identifies which firm-specific measures are worth examining in bank share
pricing, and sets out a specification and sample that a later study can extend
to other sectors, other periods, or a longer horizon.
